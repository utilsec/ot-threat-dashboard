Volt Typhoon’s “Silent Train”: Lumen exposes the throwaway relay chains the China-linked group uses to exploit SD-WAN and IT-management platforms — and lists rail among the sectors targeted

Lumen’s Black Lotus Labs published research on 28 October describing “Silent Train”, the framework it says the China-linked group Volt Typhoon has used since November 2023 to reach targets without exposing its own infrastructure. Each operation runs through a fresh chain of typically four to six hops — rented servers outside the US, then compromised small-office routers, IoT devices and enterprise appliances abroad and finally inside the US — which operators can exploit and assemble in under two minutes and clean up afterwards; Lumen counts more than 27 device classes, among them TP-Link routers, Netgear Orbi devices, Fortinet appliances and CyberPanel servers. CyberScoop reports that the group leaned on the framework more after the FBI and Justice Department disrupted its KV botnet in early 2024. The chains are aimed at management and control-plane platforms: Lumen ties the group to exploitation of Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN CVE-2026-20127 (CVSS v3.1 10.0, Cisco), seen by late 2025 and continuing after Cisco’s February disclosure; a VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day later designated CVE-2026-16812; N-able N-central CVE-2025-8875 and CVE-2025-8876; Versa Director in mid-2024; and, with moderate confidence, a Quest KACE zero-day that hit more than 30 victims. Lumen’s key findings name rail among the critical-infrastructure sectors targeted, alongside water, energy, transportation, maritime, aviation and emergency services, and it says it has notified hundreds of victims over three years. It names no victim and gives no count by sector, and neither the report nor coverage by CyberScoop and Zero Day describes access to OT or any operational effect. Black Lotus Labs’ Mike Horka told Zero Day that the researchers agree with US officials’ description of the activity as pre-positioning but see espionage in it too, and that the operators often return using credentials stolen on an earlier visit. These are not the SD-WAN Manager and VeloCloud flaws on this page’s other cards, CVE-2026-76504 and CVE-2026-114625, which no one has attributed to Volt Typhoon; what connects them is that the same orchestration products keep being targeted. It leads because it is a confirmed state campaign whose target list names rail, and the platforms it goes after — SD-WAN orchestrators and IT-management consoles — are the ones that configure the networks at stations, depots, yards and wayside sites (editorial assessment).

High Advisory — Joint ransomware advisory AA26-293A on an active group; cross-sector, no transport victims named

CISA, the FBI and partners say SafePay ransomware has hit at least 600 organizations since August 2024 — through stolen credentials, VPN logins and old firewall flaws

The FBI and CISA, with the US Secret Service, Germany’s LKA Rheinland-Pfalz, the UK’s National Crime Agency and the North West Regional Organised Crime Unit, published joint advisory AA26-293A on 20 October. It says SafePay, a closed group that does not use affiliates, has affected at least 600 organizations from multiple critical-infrastructure sectors since August 2024, in North and South America, Europe and Australia, and it gives indicators and techniques observed from November 2024 to August 2026. The coverage we read names no transport victim. The rail relevance is our editorial assessment: ransomware outages at rail operators typically begin in IT, and remote-access VPNs and NAS-based backups sit in the corporate networks that dispatch, maintenance and passenger-information systems depend on.

High Incident — Active credential-theft campaign against boundary firewalls, agency-confirmed, all critical-infrastructure sectors

The FBI and Secret Service say FortiBleed credential theft against FortiGate firewalls is still running — across every critical-infrastructure sector, with no CVE to patch

The FBI and the US Secret Service issued a joint advisory on Tuesday 6 October warning that the FortiBleed campaign against internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways remains an active threat. It is not a software vulnerability and has no CVE: per the agencies, it exploits reused or leaked credentials and legacy SHA-256 password storage. Researchers describe credential stuffing and spraying from leak dumps and infostealer logs, offline cracking of extracted password hashes on a GPU cluster, and a Go-based sniffer that passively captures authentication traffic across 24 protocols. Some owners were locked out after the attackers created their own administrator accounts and deleted or reset the legitimate ones. The agencies say compromised firewalls have served as ransomware entry points, and the FBI advisory links the access to Payload ransomware. Cybersecurity Dive reports that targets span all 16 US critical-infrastructure sectors — a list that by definition includes transportation systems — naming energy, communications, and food and agriculture among them. The most-cited scale figure, more than 86,644 working device credentials across 194 countries, is SOCRadar’s count as of 19 June, not a new tally; SOCRadar says it has confirmed at least 12 ransomware deployments stemming from the access and describes the operator as a Russian-speaking access broker, which is a researcher assessment, not agency attribution. No OT system compromise and no rail victim has been described. The rail relevance is our editorial assessment: FortiGate is one of the most common firewalls at the IT/OT boundary of railroads and transit agencies, and a stolen administrator credential there is a way to rewrite segmentation without exploiting anything.

High Incident — Food & retail sector OT attack in France; carried here as a cross-sector lesson, not a rail incident

Attackers took over web-managed refrigeration controllers at about 700 French supermarkets — a food-sector attack, and a direct lesson for contractor-managed station and depot plant

From the night of 11–12 October, attackers remotely took control of internet-reachable refrigeration management controllers in French supermarkets and forced them into continuous or extended defrost, with setpoints of up to about 50 °C, spoiling stock; the Paris prosecutor reports outbreaks of fire at some sites. A second wave on the night of 12–13 October hit stores with Dixell controllers. Finance Minister Roland Lescure told Parliament on 13 October that about 700 of France’s roughly 10,000 supermarkets were affected, and Carrefour, Coopérative U, E.Leclerc, Intermarché/Netto and Monoprix have confirmed impact. CERT-FR’s alert CERTFR-2026-ALE-012 named Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC controllers on 12 October, added Copeland and Dixell xweb on 13 October, and in its 16 October update says attack campaigns are probably still ongoing. As of 31 October CERT-FR’s alert was still at version 3 of 16 October, and no new attack nights, attribution or arrests had been reported since 13 October. Danfoss and Copeland say most affected units are older, end-of-life models. No group has claimed the attack, no ransom has been demanded and the entry route has not been established; the national cyber agency ANSSI has mobilised, and the Paris prosecutor’s cybercrime section has opened an inquiry. Reports of targeting in other European countries come from one security firm, Sekoia, and have not been confirmed by any foreign authority. On 29 October Reuters, citing four unnamed officials, reported that US investigators are treating freezer failures at no fewer than six US military commissaries since late August — units forced into defrost, at bases including Fort Huachuca and Fort Irwin — as a suspected cyberattack; Army and Air Force investigators, including an Army cybercrime unit, and US Cyber Command are involved, and one official said investigators had spoken to French police. The Pentagon says it is working with interagency and law-enforcement partners and has not confirmed a cause, and Reuters could not establish whether the commissaries used Danfoss equipment. Separately, Claroty’s Team82 showed on 13 October that an AI coding agent, steered by a researcher, reproduced a previously disclosed Danfoss exploit chain to root in about 28 minutes; it does not say those flaws were used in France. This is not a rail incident and it does not count toward this page’s threat level. It is here as our editorial lesson: stations, depots and tunnels run the same kind of contractor-supported, web-managed plant — HVAC, ventilation, pumping, lighting — and this attack needed little more than reachability and outdated controllers.

High Threat Intelligence & Research — Mass exploitation of an enterprise application, transportation among sectors hit

Mandiant says ShinyHunters is back exploiting Oracle PeopleSoft by encoding one letter past WAF rules — transportation is among the sectors with compromised systems

Google Threat Intelligence and Mandiant reported on 25 September a renewed mass-exploitation campaign by UNC6240, the group known as ShinyHunters, against CVE-2026-35273, an unauthenticated remote code execution flaw in the PeopleSoft Environment Management Hub (the /PSEMHUB/ path), rated CVSS v3.1 9.8. The flaw was first exploited as a zero-day against academic institutions in late May and early June, and Oracle has had a fix out since June. The new wave requests /%50SEMHUB/ instead: WAF rules that match the literal path miss it, and WebLogic decodes and routes the request anyway, reaching, in Mandiant’s words, “systems whose operators may have believed their WAF rules had mitigated the exposure”. Mandiant counts “dozens of systems” compromised globally across higher education, technology and IT services, healthcare, agriculture, transportation and government, names no victims, and says it has notified more than 100 organisations whose addresses matched vulnerable endpoints. The tooling includes JSP web shells, Neo-reGeorg tunnels, a SIDEEYE backdoor delivered in a trojanised Light Alloy media-player installer, and MeshAgent. CISA has listed CVE-2026-35273 in the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue since 12 June 2026 and marks it as known to be used in ransomware campaigns. Since then the FBI has said, on 6 October, that a breach of its own jobs portal resulted from a contractor that failed to apply a patch explicitly issued for the platform — which Reuters, citing unnamed sources, identified as Oracle PeopleSoft — and on 9 October it announced another arrest of a suspected ShinyHunters member, saying the group has breached more than 140 organisations. Oracle’s October 2026 quarterly Critical Patch Update has since been released. The rail relevance is our editorial assessment: many transit authorities and railroads run PeopleSoft for HR, payroll and finance, and a web shell on that tier puts employee records and whatever directory and database credentials the application holds within an attacker’s reach. It is an IT-side risk, not an OT one.
