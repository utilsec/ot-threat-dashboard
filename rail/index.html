High Research — AI-assisted exploit porting against PLCs

Researchers used an AI model to port a working PLC exploit to a sibling controller — then bricked one

Forescout’s Vedere Labs set a large language model the task of taking a working remote-code-execution exploit for one WAGO controller — the 750-852, via CVE-2021-31886, a pre-authentication buffer overflow in its Nucleus FTP server — and re-targeting it at a related model, the 750-831. It worked, but not cheaply or unattended: the final stage alone ran more than eight hours and over $500 in API costs, with a researcher steering throughout. A later session aimed at building a command-and-control implant wrote a payload into flash-mapped memory and permanently bricked the test controller. For rail and transit the relevant detail is fleet uniformity in reverse: wayside, station and depot control estates are assembled from close variants of the same controller families, and an exploit proven on one variant is now materially cheaper to move onto its siblings.

Critical Critical Advisory — Actively exploited, KEV-listed

Cisco firewall management is being exploited by Sandworm and a Qilin affiliate — that is the IT/OT boundary TSA asks you to prove

Cisco and CISA confirmed on 9 September that CVE-2026-20079 in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center is under active exploitation. The flaw is a critical authentication bypass caused by an improper system process created at boot; a remote, unauthenticated attacker sends crafted HTTP requests and gains root on the appliance operating system. Cisco fixed it in early March and published indicators of compromise in late July, then disclosed on 9 September that it had observed exploitation from August. CISA added the CVE to KEV with a 12 September federal deadline. Cisco Talos attributes three clusters to this flaw and the earlier CVE-2026-20316: one dropping a web shell and malicious JAR to steal credentials, one tied to the Russian state actor Sandworm deploying Cyclops Blink, and one linked to Qilin ransomware enumerating endpoints to encrypt. For railroads and transit agencies the consequence is specific. TSA security directives require a documented, tested separation between IT and critical cyber systems — dispatch, signalling support, traction-power SCADA, fare and station systems. FMC is where that separation is enforced, and root there lets an attacker edit it silently.

Britain moves to bar designated high-risk suppliers from critical infrastructure — without naming them publicly

The UK government has tabled late amendments to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill that would let ministers stop critical-sector organisations from using technology suppliers designated as high risk. The Bill, introduced in November 2025 and now near the end of its passage through the Lords, already extends duties to energy, water, transport and health operators and pulls managed service providers, data centres and digital infrastructure into scope. The amendments were tabled on 24 August and reported on 2 September; they follow reporting that a small UK generating station ran manually for four days after a cyberattack. Designations would not necessarily be made public, which is the detail operators should notice: a supplier could be designated without a public list to check against, so the obligation to know your own supply chain moves from good practice to compliance exposure. None of this is in force yet, and the Bill has not completed its passage.

Not an incident — proposed legislation; amendments tabled but not enacted Not a CVE

Info Policy — EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting obligation

From 11 September 2026 exploited vulnerabilities in digital products must be reported within 24 hours

The EU Cyber Resilience Act’s reporting obligations begin on 11 September 2026. Manufacturers of products with digital elements placed on the EU market must notify an actively exploited vulnerability in their product, or a severe incident affecting its security, within 24 hours of becoming aware, with fuller reporting to follow. Railways sit on both sides of this. Operators buying rolling stock, signalling and station systems from European suppliers gain a defined notification obligation to write into contracts; operators or subsidiaries that themselves place connected products on the EU market acquire the obligation directly.
