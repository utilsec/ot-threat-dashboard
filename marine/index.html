Top Story

A cyberattack pushed all three North Carolina Ports facilities into manual gate processing

North Carolina Ports Authority suffered a cyberattack on 4 August that disrupted IT systems and slowed operations at the Port of Wilmington, the Port of Morehead City and the Charlotte Inland Port. The systems-wide outage forced a switch to manual gate processing, delaying truckers and cargo moves across all three facilities. The authority detected and contained the incident the same day and reported operations returning to normal by 7 August, with some delays persisting during IT restoration. No evidence of sensitive data compromise has been reported, and no threat actor has been named. Wilmington alone handles roughly 5,000 container gate moves a week. The incident is a reminder that terminal gates, vessel scheduling, documentation and cargo release are now operational systems in their own right.

High Incident — Freight & logistics

A breach at Ceva Logistics halted shipments from eight European warehouses and rippled into retail

A cyber intrusion disrupted part of Ceva Logistics’ European contract-logistics operation from 29 July, halting shipments at eight warehouses and delaying deliveries for retail customers holding inventory there. Affected customers were notified on 1 August. Personal data including names, addresses, phone numbers, emails and order details was compromised, though Ceva says payment and credential data was not. Dutch retailers Bol and De Bijenkorf, ING, Ace & Tate, football club Ajax and Valve have been reported as affected downstream. The Dutch Data Protection Authority is investigating. No ransomware group has claimed responsibility and Ceva has not disclosed technical details. For terminal and shipping operators, this is the landside failure mode: cargo does not move because the systems that release it are down.

High Incident — GNSS interference

Baltic GNSS interference is accelerating — Bornholm has already logged more disruption in 2026 than in all of 2025

A monitoring station on the Danish island of Bornholm has recorded 30 cases of navigation-signal jamming and spoofing so far in 2026, against 16 for all of 2025 and 11 in 2024. Denmark has reported record numbers and durations of GPS disruption in the Baltic; the cargo ship Saturn lost signal for about a day between Gdańsk and southern Gotland on 29 July, and again on a later voyage as far as Bornholm. A Polish study suggests some of the interference originates from vessels rather than fixed shore sites, which would make the source mobile and harder to avoid. Several European governments attribute the activity to Russia. This is electronic warfare rather than a cyber compromise, but the operational consequence — loss of position, timing and AIS integrity — lands on the same bridge team.

Ongoing interference — confirmed vessel impacts; not a cyber compromise Not applicable
No patch — navigation redundancy, crew procedures, incident reporting Action: Brief bridge teams to treat GNSS loss as expected in the Baltic, Black Sea, Red Sea and Gulf, and confirm crews can fix position without GNSS and detect AIS anomalies. Check what your ECDIS, gyro and timing-dependent systems do on signal loss, and report interference to the relevant coastal authority so the picture stays current.

High Threat Intelligence — Ransomware

Gunra ransomware is entering through unpatched VPNs and firewalls, with transport named in its victim base

The FBI, CISA, DoD Cyber Crime Center, NSA, US Secret Service and the Republic of Korea’s National Police Agency issued #StopRansomware advisory AA26-222A on Gunra, a Conti-derived double-extortion operation that moved to a structured affiliate model in early 2026. Transport is explicitly among its named victim sectors, alongside manufacturing, utilities and government. Affiliates gain access through exposed edge infrastructure — VPN gateways, firewall appliances and RDP — including the Fortinet FortiOS and FortiProxy authentication-bypass flaws CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472. For ports and shipping companies, the relevant exposure is usually the remote-access appliance serving terminal staff, agents and vessel connectivity, which is often maintained on a different cadence from the rest of the estate.

Include appliances managed by a maritime connectivity provider — those are frequently outside the shore IT patch cycle.

High Threat Intelligence — Virtualisation

A suspected China-nexus actor is mass-exploiting a patched vCenter flaw and deploying ransomware

Exploitation of CVE-2026-59310, a directory-traversal flaw in Broadcom VMware vCenter rated CVSS 9.8 and patched on 29 July, has been attributed to a suspected China-nexus actor. German incident-response firm QUIRSO counted roughly 361 victim IP addresses across 47 countries, 343 appearing between 3 and 5 August, and assesses with moderate confidence that a Chinese-speaking operator is responsible. The chain persists through cron, systemd and reverse_ssh, drops a Linux backdoor and in some cases deploys Babuk-derived ransomware. Terminal operating systems, berth planning, gate systems and shore-side vessel-traffic applications are commonly virtualised on a single cluster, so root on vCenter is a plausible route to the same manual-gate scenario North Carolina Ports just lived through.

Actively exploited at scale — ~361 victim IPs across 47 countries Not listed in CISA KEV at generation time
Patch available — Broadcom fix released 29 Jul 2026 Action: Patch vCenter now; if an instance was internet-reachable and unpatched after 3 August, hunt for unexpected cron and systemd units, reverse_ssh binaries and unfamiliar SSH keys before declaring it clean. Then check whether your terminal operating system shares a cluster with general enterprise workloads.

Critical Advisory — HMI gateway

Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway carries an unauthenticated root command-injection flaw rated CVSS 10.0

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an operating-system command-injection vulnerability in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway rated CVSS 10.0. The Net Check feature reachable through the /setting endpoint passes unsanitised input to the cmdPing Socket.io event, letting an attacker run arbitrary commands as root. CISA lists Critical Manufacturing, Energy and Water and Wastewater among affected sectors; Haiwell has released fixed firmware. Ports and terminals should still check, because low-cost cloud HMI gateways of this type turn up in shoreside building management, fuelling, conveyor and bulk-handling installations, usually fitted by a contractor and rarely recorded in the port’s own asset register. No public exploitation has been reported to CISA.

Info Regulation — USCG / MTSA

The USCG cyber rule’s full compliance date is July 2027 — incident reporting is already mandatory

The Coast Guard’s final rule on Cybersecurity in the Marine Transportation System, published under MTSA in January 2025, took effect on 16 July 2025 and phases in from there. The cybersecurity training requirement for personnel with access to IT or OT systems fell due on 12 January 2026, and the full compliance date — Cybersecurity Plan, designated Cybersecurity Officer, and measures to detect, respond to and recover from incidents — is July 2027. One obligation has applied since the effective date and is easy to miss: reportable cyber incidents must be reported to the National Response Center. Given the North Carolina Ports incident this month, US-regulated facilities should confirm their duty officers know the NRC reporting threshold and number without looking them up.
