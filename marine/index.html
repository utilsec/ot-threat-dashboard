Top Story

A cyberattack pushed all three North Carolina Ports facilities into manual processing — and the OT questions were never answered

The North Carolina State Ports Authority detected an unauthorised intrusion on 4 August and isolated core systems, causing a systems-wide outage that delayed gate opening at the Port of Wilmington, the Port of Morehead City and the Charlotte Inland Port. Cargo moved on manual processing while containment ran, and the Authority said normal schedules had been restored by Friday. It activated its cyber contingency plan and worked with NCDOT, the state Department of Information Technology and the US Coast Guard. No group has been named and the entry vector has not been disclosed. Worth saying plainly: the Authority did not state whether vessel operations, cargo-handling equipment or rail interchange were affected, so this cannot be read as either a confirmed OT compromise or a confirmed IT-only event. The instructive part is that a containment decision, not the attacker, is what closed the gate.

High Threat Intel — Virtualisation

CISA added the exploited vCenter flaw to KEV yesterday — 361 hosts compromised across 47 countries

CISA added CVE-2026-59310, a path-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter Server, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August. Broadcom patched it on 29 July; exploitation began roughly five days later. German incident-response firm QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence that a Chinese-speaking actor runs the campaign, counting around 361 affected addresses across 47 countries. The actor gains root on the vCenter appliance, plants a backdoor through cron and a reverse SSH binary, and in at least one case deployed Babuk-derived ransomware. Terminal operating systems, berth planning, gate and yard management and vessel-traffic displays are commonly virtualised on a single cluster ashore; if the management plane falls, every one of those services is reachable at once, which is precisely the scenario that forces a port into manual gate processing.

Confirmed active exploitation — suspected China-nexus actor, ransomware in at least one case In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-59310 added 18 Aug 2026
Patch available — Broadcom fix released 29 Jul 2026 Action: Patch any vCenter hosting terminal operating, gate, yard or VTS-adjacent virtual machines, and check the appliance for unexpected cron entries and a “linuxFile” binary. Restrict the management interface to a dedicated administrative host; a port that virtualises everything on one cluster should treat that cluster as a single point of operational failure.

High Threat Intel — Ransomware

Medusa has passed 500 critical-infrastructure victims, and weaponises new bugs faster than most ports can schedule a patch

CISA and the FBI refreshed their #StopRansomware advisory on Medusa on 18 August. The cumulative victim count passed 500 critical-infrastructure organisations as of April 2026, up from roughly 300 in the March 2025 version — about 200 new victims in a year across healthcare, critical manufacturing, the defense industrial base, government facilities, IT and finance. The operational finding is the tempo: Medusa has been observed weaponising newly disclosed vulnerabilities within about 24 hours, and in some cases using exploits up to a week before public disclosure, because it buys them rather than developing them. Impact is enterprise-IT encryption plus data theft; no control-system involvement is alleged. For a port that is the whole point — encrypting gate, yard and documentation systems is enough to stop cargo without ever touching a PLC.

Medium Research — Ransomware trends

Dragos recorded 1,140 ransomware incidents against industrial organisations in the second quarter of 2026, up 12 percent on the 1,020 in the first quarter. Critical Advisory — HMI gateway

A CVSS 10.0 command injection gives unauthenticated root on Haiwell’s IoT Cloud HMI gateway

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an OS command injection in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway rated 10.0. The Net Check feature reached through the /setting endpoint passes user input from the cmdPing Socket.io event to the operating system without sanitisation, allowing an unauthenticated remote attacker to execute commands as root. Version 3.40.1.12 is affected and Haiwell has published Scada-v3.50.1.19. Devices of this class appear across marine facilities in places procurement rarely tracks: shore power, bunkering skids, ballast and bilge monitoring, cold-store plant rooms at reefer terminals, and lock and bridge machinery. They are usually installed by a contractor, connected for remote monitoring, and never revisited.

Critical Advisory — Siemens

SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced exposes Node-RED with no authentication — maximum-severity remote code execution

Siemens published ten advisories in its August Patch Tuesday round, the most severe being SSA-834709 for SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices running Industrial OS with Node-RED installed. The Node-RED HTTP interface enforces no authentication, so a remote unauthenticated attacker can reach programming nodes that execute system commands and run arbitrary code at maximum privilege. A fixed version is available. In marine settings IoT2050 gateways show up as protocol converters and data collectors between crane, conveyor, shore-power or terminal automation equipment and a monitoring platform — devices the control network trusts implicitly, commissioned by an integrator and then rarely patched.

High Advisory — Remote access

A Windows IKE flaw went into CISA’s KEV catalogue with a three-day federal deadline — and IKE is what terminates ship-to-shore tunnels

CISA added CVE-2026-33824, a double free in the Microsoft Windows IKE service extensions (IKEEXT) that leads to remote code execution, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August with a federal remediation deadline of 21 August. A three-day deadline is CISA’s signal that exploitation is active and broad. The marine relevance is direct: IKE and IPsec terminate the site-to-site links between head office, terminals and remote facilities, and the remote-access tunnels that crane and terminal-automation vendors, classification surveyors and shoreside support use to reach vessel and equipment networks. Code execution at the tunnel endpoint yields access that looks entirely legitimate in the logs, on the exact path that reaches operational equipment.

Medium Regulation — USCG MTSA

An MTSA waiver does not exempt you from the cyber rule, the Coast Guard has confirmed — and 16 July 2027 is the hard deadline

The Coast Guard clarified in late July that existing MTSA waivers do not exempt an operator from the Cybersecurity in the Marine Transportation System final rule — a point that had been read hopefully in some quarters. The rule took effect on 16 July 2025 and its personnel training milestone passed on 12 January 2026. The remaining obligation is the substantial one: by 16 July 2027, MTSA-regulated facilities, US-flagged vessels and Outer Continental Shelf facilities must have designated a cybersecurity officer in writing, completed a comprehensive cybersecurity assessment and submitted a cybersecurity plan for approval. That leaves under a year, and plan approval is not instantaneous. The North Carolina Ports incident, which the Coast Guard was engaged on, is a reminder that the reporting expectations apply well before the planning deadline does.

Low Policy — Risk transfer

A case for a federal cyber reinsurance backstop for US ports, built on concentration risk in terminal systems

The August issue of USNI Proceedings carries an argument for a federal port cyber catastrophe reinsurance programme, modelled on the terrorism risk insurance scheme: private insurers would keep writing primary cover while the federal government backstops catastrophic, systemic losses. The reasoning is concentration risk — a small number of terminal operating system vendors and cargo-handling OT platforms are used across many US ports, so one systemic event is correlated rather than isolated, and commercial policies frequently exclude state-backed attacks, with attribution disputes delaying recovery money when it is needed most. The author also proposes industry-wide standards and annual independent audits building on the Coast Guard rule. This is advocacy, not policy: nothing has been introduced or enacted.

The National Security Science and Technology Strategy, released today, sets out four pillars and names operational technology and industrial control system resilience, along with technology supply chains, as explicit priorities rather than sub-clauses of general cybersecurity. For marine operators the practical read is about funding and framing rather than obligation: nothing here is a requirement, no deadline attaches to it, and it changes nothing you must do this week.
