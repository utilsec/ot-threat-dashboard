Top Story

Investigators reportedly found hackers briefly reached a US-bound tanker’s propulsion system; the FBI reports no disruption

Bloomberg reported on 2 October, citing US officials not authorized to speak publicly, that FBI and Coast Guard investigators found outsiders had gained temporary access to digital systems aboard the supertanker VL Prosperity, including its propulsion system, as it approached Texas this summer. How long the access lasted and what the intruders could control is unclear, and neither agency has confirmed the propulsion finding on the record; the Coast Guard declined to comment. The FBI’s on-record statement says there are no reports of operational disruption, vessel instability, danger to crews or environmental harm. In September, Coast Guard Rear Adm. Amy Grable said investigators found evidence of a malicious cyber actor after assessing the ship’s IT and onboard systems. VL Prosperity was boarded on 21 August in the Atlantic and a second tanker on 24 August in the Gulf of Mexico, per Dragos; no attacker has been named. Bloomberg adds that by September US agencies were tracking cyber threats against nearly 20 vessels worldwide. Engine-speed and cooling claims come only from Iranian media and remain unverified, as does a crew claim that hackers seized tank-pressure controls on the LNG carrier Vivit Africa LNG, which Italy’s Coast Guard recorded as a cargo-monitoring malfunction.

MTSA Rules The tanker cases are being read against rules the Coast Guard already has

Dragos (24 September) used VL Prosperity to walk through the Coast Guard’s MTSA cybersecurity rule: cyber-incident reporting to the National Response Center has been mandatory since 16 July 2025, cyber training for personnel with IT/OT access was due 12 January 2026, and a designated Cybersecurity Officer, completed assessment and approved plan are due by 16 July 2027. Its suggested sequence is asset inventory first, then IT/OT segmentation, then the Cybersecurity Officer and vulnerability remediation.

High Incident — Container terminal operations suspended

Port of Tanjung Pelepas restarted in phases after a cyber incident halted container operations

A cyber incident detected late on 9 September forced Malaysia’s Port of Tanjung Pelepas (PTP), a joint venture of MMC Group and APM Terminals, to suspend container-terminal operations as a precaution; the terminal operating systems were affected. A phased restart began on 10 September with manual processing for export container gate-ins, and a PTP statement reported by WorldCargo News on 15 September said container operations had resumed with system recovery “advancing steadily.” The Direwolf ransomware group listed the port on its leak site on 11 September, claiming about 200 GB of data (per ransomware.live); PTP has not confirmed ransomware or any group, so that remains a claim. No newer development was found for this 6 October edition.

Cisco Secure FMC, a common firewall console at IT/OT boundaries, remains under active exploitation

Info Research — Port ownership and NATO military mobility

FDD report: Chinese-operated port terminals and patchy cyber oversight expose NATO’s European mobility corridors

A Foundation for Defense of Democracies report by Jiwon Ma, published 28 September, argues that foreign ownership and fragmented cyber oversight weaken the ports, railways and roads NATO would use to move forces across Europe. It says Chinese state-linked firms hold stakes in more than 30 European port terminals and, citing a 2023 European Parliament study, that COSCO Shipping, China Merchants and Hutchison Port Holdings own or operate terminals in roughly half of the EU ports considered critical to NATO. It also finds that civilian transport operators lack consistent security standards across NATO, EU and national authorities, and recommends tighter EU investment screening plus authentication and alternative navigation procedures against satellite interference. The “state-linked” characterization is FDD’s; the report describes structural exposure, not an intrusion at any port.

Critical Advisory — Exploited zero-days, CISA KEV

Citrix NetScaler: a third exploited zero-day joins KEV, and patching alone does not evict intruders

Citrix patched two exploited NetScaler ADC and Gateway zero-days on 27 September, both CVSS 9.5 (v4): CVE-2026-88771, unauthenticated command execution that affects all deployments including the default configuration, and CVE-2026-88772, a memory overflow on appliances with DTLS enabled, the default on VPN virtual servers. CISA added both to KEV that day and says exploitation is global. On 3 October Citrix patched a third flaw, CVE-2026-88779 (CVSS 8.7), which affects only SAML SP or IdP configurations, after observing targeted attacks; CISA added it to KEV on 4 October with a 7 October deadline. Citrix and watchTowr describe it as a crash (denial-of-service) bug; one researcher reported signs of code execution, which watchTowr disputes. Responders have found web shells, a rogue admin account and stolen configuration files, and Mandiant attributes the activity only to suspected state-sponsored actors. Victim counts differ by tracker: Mandiant says dozens of organizations, Arctic Wolf at least 78, researcher Kevin Beaumont over 100. Editorial assessment: NetScaler Gateway is a common remote-access front door into port, terminal and OT DMZ networks.

Confirmed by Citrix and CISA — all three flaws exploited in the wild; no actor named In CISA KEV — 88771/88772 added 27 Sep (due 30 Sep); 88779 added 4 Oct (due 7 Oct 2026)
Patched — 14.1-73.41 / 13.1-64.28 or later fix all three; FIPS builds listed Action: Preserve memory and logs, then move every NetScaler serving port, terminal or vessel-operator remote access to 14.1-73.41 or 13.1-64.28 or later; hunt for web shells and unknown admin accounts, revoke sessions and rotate credentials, since an upgrade does not remove an intruder already inside.

High Advisory — Hard-coded cryptographic keys, fix available, no known exploitation

Wärtsilä FOS-Onboard hard-coded keys: CISA advisory names two CVEs and the fixed build

CISA’s advisory ICSA-26-258-02 (15 September) covers two hard-coded cryptographic key flaws (CWE-321) in Wärtsilä FOS-Onboard 5.07.0923.01, a shipboard fleet-optimization system: CVE-2026-81855 in its robot testing framework and CVE-2026-78225 in its deployer-ng Update Controller. CISA’s 15 September advisory rated them 9.1 and 9.0 (CVSS v3.1, network attack vector), but the published scores have differed between sources since, so check the current advisory for the rating. Per CISA, an attacker could deliver an unauthorized update, execute code or extract credentials. The fix is 5.08.4052.01, obtained through Wärtsilä’s FOS download site or patch-deployment service; the CVE records were updated on 24 September to point there. Wärtsilä says the flaws are not exploitable when the product is installed as recommended, and Cydome, which reported them, credits the vendor with a patch. No exploitation has been reported. A hard-coded key is shared across installations, so one recovered key could work fleet-wide.

No known exploitation reported Not in CISA KEV
Fixed — FOS-Onboard 5.08.4052.01 Action: Fleet managers running FOS-Onboard should schedule the upgrade to 5.08.4052.01, restrict the software to trusted onboard network segments until then, and treat the embedded keys as fleet-wide secrets when scoping exposure.

Medium Advisory — Cellular gateway update-signing flaw, fix available

Lantronix G520 cellular gateways: a leaked signing key lets attackers push trusted firmware; update to 2.6.0.7R6

CISA advisory ICSA-26-272-01 (29 September) covers two flaws in Lantronix G520 Series cellular gateways running 2.6.0.4R6_stable, each CVSS 7.5 (v3.1). CVE-2026-91191 is a signature-verification failure in the update mechanism: per the advisory, the publicly distributed SDK contains the production private key, so an attacker could sign malicious packages the gateway will trust. CVE-2026-84409 is a cross-site scripting flaw in how the web interface displays update metadata fetched over plain HTTP. CISA says exploitation could let an attacker replace software and run code with root privileges, and lists Transportation Systems, Energy and Water and Wastewater among affected sectors. Lantronix fixed both in firmware 2.6.0.7R6; no exploitation has been reported. Editorial assessment: cellular gateways like these often link remote port-side equipment and terminal sensors back to control centers.

CISA and the FBI name transportation among sectors exposed through a compromised ICS integrator

A joint FBI/CISA fact sheet on working with third-party ICS integrators, published 24 September, describes foreign cyber actors who compromised a US industrial automation company in March–April 2025 and created nine .zip files of about 800 files, including SCADA-related data, for presumed exfiltration. Info Guidance — Draft OT security guide now covers maritime vessels

NIST’s draft SP 800-82 Rev. 4 brings maritime vessels into its OT security guide; comments close 30 November

NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Revision 4, its Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, on 21 September. The revision expands coverage to maritime vessels, building automation, water and wastewater, and freight rail, and reorganizes the guide around Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, moving risk management under the Govern function and adding Risk Management Framework appendices. It also strengthens asset-management and network-monitoring guidance and adds zero-trust architecture material, reflecting IIoT and cloud connections into OT. Comments are due 30 November 2026. This is voluntary draft guidance; for US-regulated vessels and facilities, the Coast Guard’s MTSA cybersecurity rule sets the binding requirements.
