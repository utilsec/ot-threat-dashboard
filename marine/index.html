Top Story

Russia’s APT28 spent at least two months inside a NATO naval shipbuilder’s IT network, Symantec says; no OT impact reported

Symantec’s Threat Hunter Team reported on 20 October that Swallowtail, the group also tracked as APT28 or Fancy Bear, which the US government links to Russia’s GRU military intelligence, spent at least two months inside the IT network of a naval shipbuilder in a NATO member state. No OT, ICS or production impact is reported. Editorial assessment: shipyards hold vessel designs and support data, so their business networks are an espionage target in their own right.

High Campaign — Credential theft against Fortinet firewalls and SSL VPNs, no CVE

FBI and Secret Service say the FortiBleed credential-theft campaign against Fortinet gateways is still active

In a joint advisory dated 6 October, the FBI and US Secret Service warned that attackers are still scanning internet-facing FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways with credentials gathered earlier in the campaign. No vulnerability is involved: the agencies point to reused or leaked passwords and legacy SHA-256 password storage, and researchers describe offline hash cracking and a tool that sniffs login traffic across 24 protocols. Intruders have created rogue admin accounts and deleted or reset legitimate ones, locking owners out, and the access is resold; SOCRadar says it has confirmed at least 12 ransomware deployments from it. The agencies say targeted organizations span all 16 US critical-infrastructure sectors, a set that includes transportation systems. SOCRadar’s figure of 86,644 working device credentials across 194 countries dates from 19 June and is not a new count. No maritime or OT victim has been named. Editorial assessment: FortiGate appliances commonly guard links between shipping offices, terminals and remote sites.

Active campaign — the FBI and USSS say it remains an active threat; ransomware links and the operator profile are researcher assessments; no OT victim named No CVE — credential abuse, so no CISA KEV entry applies
No patch applicable — credential reset, MFA and modern hash storage Action: Reset FortiGate admin and VPN passwords, end active sessions, enable phishing-resistant MFA, look for unexpected admin accounts (reported names include fortiAdmin, forticloud-sync and fgtsecure), and move admin credentials to PBKDF2 storage (FortiOS 7.2.11 or later). Confirm no firewall policy between business networks and vessel- or terminal-OT networks was changed.

BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day, which Google ties to a suspected China-nexus espionage group, is now in CISA KEV

CISA added CVE-2026-102764 (CVSS v4.0 9.3), a BeyondTrust Remote Support flaw, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 16 October with a 19 October federal deadline. Editorial assessment: OEMs and service providers use remote-support tools like this to reach vessel and terminal workstations.

Ivanti Connect Secure: a three-flaw chain gave attackers root on fully patched VPNs, and one link is still unpatched

Citrix disclosed a fourth critical NetScaler ADC and Gateway flaw on 8 October in bulletin CTX697191: CVE-2026-107406 (CVSS v4.0 9.5), a memory overflow on appliances configured as a SAML service provider or identity provider that Citrix says may lead to remote code execution or denial of service. No exploitation of it has been reported and it is not in KEV, but builds that fixed the September zero-days (14.1-73.37 to 73.41 and 13.1-64.23 to 64.28) stay vulnerable when used as a SAML IdP, so those appliances need another upgrade. Three earlier flaws are exploited and in KEV: CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (both CVSS 9.5, added 27 September) and CVE-2026-88779 (added 4 October), which Citrix and watchTowr describe as a denial-of-service bug. Mandiant links the activity only to suspected state-nexus actors and counts dozens of affected organizations; Arctic Wolf tracks at least 78 targeted organizations, and Shadowserver sees more than 20,000 exposed or potentially vulnerable appliances. Editorial assessment: NetScaler Gateway is a common remote-access front door into port, terminal and OT DMZ networks.

The US Coast Guard’s Navigation and Vessel Inspection Circular 02-26, dated 19 October and reported by Industrial Cyber on 23 October, explains how to comply with the cybersecurity requirements in 33 CFR Part 101 Subpart F for US-flagged vessels, MTSA-regulated facilities and Outer Continental Shelf facilities.
