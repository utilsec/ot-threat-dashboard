Cyberattack forces a systems-wide outage at North Carolina Ports, slowing gate and terminal operations

North Carolina Ports confirmed a cyberattack that triggered a systems-wide outage across its facilities — the Port of Wilmington, the Port of Morehead City and the Charlotte Inland Port — detected on 4 August and disrupting operations into 5 August. Qilin ransomware gang claims the Shipping Association of New York & New Jersey

The Russian-speaking Qilin ransomware crew listed the Shipping Association of New York & New Jersey (SANYNJ) on its dark-web leak site on 8 June, with the entry updated into July. Medium Incident — Ransomware

Resecurity details Anubis ransomware breach of Ancona’s Adriatic port authority

Resecurity published analysis on 11 June of an Anubis ransomware attack on the Central Adriatic Sea Port Authority, which runs the Italian port of Ancona. The intrusion — dated to December 2025 and claimed by Anubis in January — began with a spear-phishing email against port-management staff and moved laterally through insecure Office 365 and Azure cloud accounts; the attackers worked purely through IT weaknesses and did not need to touch operational technology. Stolen material reportedly included contracts, employee records and port safety and security plans, with a $10 million ransom demand. The authority characterised most of the data as public or soon-to-be-public and put overall loss near 2%, but the case shows how an IT-side compromise at a port can still reach sensitive safety and security documentation.

Confirmed breach — IT-side, no OT compromise reported Resecurity / Infosecurity Magazine

High Threat Intel — Nation-State

Federal alert: Iran-affiliated hackers manipulating the PLCs that also run port and terminal OT

Federal agencies sharpened their warning that Iran-affiliated hackers are actively manipulating the programmable logic controllers behind US critical infrastructure — the same controllers that run cranes, fuel, power and water systems inside MTSA-regulated ports and terminals. Advisory AA26-097A, updated 22 July, describes actors reaching internet-exposed Rockwell, Schneider and Siemens gear, stealing project files, and rewriting or deleting control logic, in some cases disabling shutdown and alarm functions without alerting operators. Port facilities are exactly the mixed IT/OT environment the campaign favours: diverse controllers, remote maintenance access and edge devices often reachable from the internet. The activity has caused operational disruption and financial loss across critical-infrastructure sectors. It is opportunistic rather than sophisticated — the way in is weak credentials and exposed controllers, not a novel exploit.

High Research — Vessel OT

Researchers find remote-access flaws in NAVTOR NavBox vessel OT gateway (now patched)

Maritime-security firm Cydome disclosed three vulnerabilities in the NAVTOR NavBox, an onboard gateway that manages navigation and network operations and sits at the IT/OT boundary on many vessels. In version 4.12.0.3, missing authentication on HTTP API endpoints (CVE-2026-2752), a path-traversal flaw (CVE-2026-2753) and an information-disclosure issue (CVE-2026-2754) could allow unauthorised remote access to vessel telemetry, network configuration and sensitive operational data such as ECDIS IP addresses. NavTor acknowledged the findings, confirmed they were fixed in later releases (v4.14.1.2 and v4.16.2.4) and notified affected customers. It is a research finding with no reported in-the-wild exploitation, but it is a rare, concrete look at how a shipboard OT gateway can expose navigation systems.

No known exploitation — research, vendor-confirmed & fixed Cydome / NAVTOR
Fixed in v4.14.1.2 / v4.16.2.4 Action: Fleet operators should inventory NavBox units, confirm firmware is on a fixed release, restrict management-API exposure to onboard networks, and segment the NavBox from ECDIS and other navigation OT so a gateway compromise cannot reach bridge systems.

The US Coast Guard Cyber Command’s fifth annual Cyber Trends and Insights in the Marine Environment (CTIME) report finds reported maritime cyber incidents rose about 17% year over year, with phishing still the leading entry point and operational technology increasingly central — OT featured in 62% of CGCYBER cyber missions, up from 46% the year before. High Active Exploitation — KEV

Cisco Secure FMC zero-day exploited — the edge that fronts port and vessel networks

Cisco disclosed CVE-2026-20316, a static (hard-coded) credential flaw in the web interface of Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC) that is being exploited as a zero-day. An unauthenticated remote attacker can log in with a built-in low-privileged account and read sensitive data, and Cisco warns it can be chained with other FMC bugs to escalate privileges. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on 29 July with a 1 August federal remediation deadline. FMC manages the firewalls that sit on the perimeter and remote-access path in front of many port, terminal and vessel-shore networks, so a foothold there is a foothold toward the OT behind it. CVSS is only 5.3, but active exploitation makes it urgent.

High ICS Advisory — Satcom

CISA flags API flaws in iDirect iQ-Series satellite terminals used across maritime VSAT

CISA advisory ICSA-26-183-01 covers vulnerabilities in ST Engineering iDirect iQ-Series satellite terminals — hardware widely deployed for maritime, energy and defense VSAT connectivity. The lead flaw, CVE-2026-38059 (CVSS 7.5 v3 / 8.7 v4), exposes the terminal’s REST management API without authentication, letting an attacker on the network read the device ID, MAC, firmware version and — critically — the Terminal Private Key identifier used for satellite-network authentication, opening the door to terminal impersonation and reconnaissance. A companion CSRF gap on the reboot endpoint lets a state-changing request through on a session cookie alone. iDirect has released fixed firmware 4.5.2.2. For vessels and offshore assets, this is the satcom link itself.

No known exploitation — advisory CISA ICS — ICSA-26-183-01
Fixed firmware 4.5.2.2 available Action: Identify iDirect iQ-Series, 3315-Series and 9-Series terminals afloat and ashore, update to 4.5.2.2 or newer, restrict management-API network exposure, and rotate/re-key terminals where the private-key identifier may have been exposed.

Info Regulation — USCG

USCG Marine Transportation System cyber rule: reporting and Cyber Security Officer duties in force

The US Coast Guard's Marine Transportation System cybersecurity rule continues to phase in for US ports and MTSA-regulated facilities. Reportable cyber incidents must be reported to the National Response Center (within 12 hours for incidents affecting seaworthiness or security compliance), and facilities must designate a Cyber Security Officer alongside the traditional Facility Security Officer. For operators the near-term work is governance: named accountability, incident-reporting playbooks, and a cyber plan that maps to the rule.

Regulation — no exploitation USCG
Compliance — governance & reporting Action: Confirm a designated Cyber Security Officer, stand up a 12-hour incident-reporting procedure to the NRC, and align the facility cyber plan with the MTS rule's requirements.

Info Regulation — IACS

IACS UR E26/E27 keep raising the cyber bar for newbuild vessels

The IACS Unified Requirements UR E26 (whole-ship cyber resilience) and UR E27 (equipment-level security) apply to vessels with construction contracts signed on or after 1 July 2024, covering identification, protection, detection, response and recovery across integrated IT/OT and third-party equipment. The requirements shift cyber resilience into ship design and supplier obligations, so owners, yards and integrators building 500 GT-plus tonnage need cyber baked into specifications, not bolted on later.
