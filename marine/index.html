Two confirmed tanker intrusions and an unverified LNG-carrier claim keep vessel control-system access in focus

No attribution has been confirmed.

MTSA Rules The tanker cases are being read against rules the Coast Guard already has

Dragos (24 September) used VL Prosperity to walk through what the Coast Guard’s MTSA cybersecurity rule already requires: cyber incidents reportable to the National Response Center since 16 July 2025, cyber training for personnel with IT/OT access by 12 January 2026, and a Cybersecurity Officer, assessment and approved plan by 16 July 2027. Foreign-flagged ships fall under Port State Control instead, but must still report cyber incidents as hazardous conditions.

AIS Integrity One vendor reports Black Sea tankers “appearing” in central Lima

Copenhagen start-up Worldwide AIS Network told Splash247 (25 September) that a receiver in Varna, Bulgaria logged 27 false position reports from four Black Sea tankers, all placed in central Lima, Peru, which short-range VHF AIS cannot physically deliver. It also reported 1,702 impossible position jumps across 379 vessels in the Gulf of Oman on 2–9 September. This is a single-vendor detection with no government confirmation, no vessel names and no known safety impact.

OT Guidance NIST’s OT security guide draft brings maritime vessels into scope

NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Rev. 4, “Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security”, on 21 September. It adds coverage for maritime vessels alongside building automation, water, food and agriculture, freight rail and IIoT/cloud, and is restructured around CSF 2.0. Comments are open until 30 November 2026, a chance for vessel and terminal operators to shape it.

High Incident — Container terminal operations suspended

Port of Tanjung Pelepas restarted in phases after a cyber incident halted container operations

A cyber incident detected shortly before midnight on 9 September forced Malaysia’s Port of Tanjung Pelepas (PTP), a joint venture of MMC and Maersk’s APM Terminals, to suspend container terminal operations and its terminal operating systems as a precaution. A phased restart began on 10 September, initially with manual processing for export container gate-ins, and a PTP spokesperson said on 15 September that operations had resumed with system recovery “advancing steadily.” Maersk called it a cybersecurity incident and said it had no evidence customer data was accessed. The Direwolf ransomware group listed the port on its leak site on 11 September, claiming 200 GB of data (per ransomware.live); PTP has not confirmed ransomware or any group, so that remains a claim. No new development reported as of 27 September.

Critical Incident — Exploited firewall management platform, CISA KEV

Cisco Secure FMC, a common firewall console at IT/OT boundaries, remains under active exploitation

Cisco Talos tracks three clusters exploiting Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center flaws. UAT-12197 used CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated root) to plant web shells. UAT-11823, an APT whose tooling overlaps with Sandworm’s and which deployed a Cyclops Blink variant, used both CVE-2026-20079 and CVE-2026-20316. UAT-11988, a ransomware operator, deployed Qilin after logging in with the CVE-2026-20316 static credentials. Talos names no victims, sectors or countries, and there has been no new development since its 21 September update. Editorial assessment: FMC often manages the firewalls between corporate IT and terminal operating systems, crane and gate networks at ports.

High Research — IT/OT/IoT network convergence

Forescout: only 13% of network segments containing OT devices are OT-only

Forescout’s Vedere Labs analysed 47,700 network segments holding more than 2.5 million devices across 209 organizations (published 22 September; reported by Industrial Cyber 24 September). Only 13% of segments containing OT devices were OT-only; the rest held other device types, and almost half mixed OT with both IT and IoT assets. Editorial assessment: aboard vessels and at terminals, where crew Wi-Fi, cargo systems and navigation networks increasingly share infrastructure, this is the architectural gap that lets one compromised laptop or crew device reach navigation or cargo-control systems.

Research finding — not tied to a specific exploited vulnerability or incident Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: Verify actual network segmentation between crew/passenger IT and navigation, propulsion and cargo-control OT against real traffic, particularly on vessels where connectivity has been added incrementally over the ship’s life.

Medium Threat Intelligence — AI-assisted naval reconnaissance

Anthropic: an Iran-linked actor used its AI model to track US Navy vessels and research maritime VSAT flaws

Anthropic reported on about 11 September that an Iran-linked actor used its Claude model to build a Python OSINT pipeline for identifying and tracking US Navy vessel positions, compiling “targeting handbooks” with personnel rosters, ship and aircraft transponder identifiers and satellite-imagery scripts. The actor also compiled vulnerability data on maritime VSAT terminals, Cisco communications equipment and industrial control products. High Advisory — Hard-coded cryptographic keys, fix available, no known exploitation

Wärtsilä FOS-Onboard hard-coded keys: CISA update rates both flaws 8.3 and names the fixed build

CISA’s ICSA-26-258-02 (15 September; Update A, 24 September) covers two hard-coded cryptographic key flaws (CWE-321) in Wärtsilä FOS-Onboard 5.07.0923.01, a shipboard fleet-optimization system: CVE-2026-81855 in its robot testing framework and CVE-2026-78225 in its deployer-ng Update Controller. Both are rated CVSS 8.3 with an adjacent-network attack vector, so an attacker needs access to the same local network. Update A adds the fixed version, 5.08.4052.01. A hard-coded key is shared by every installation, so a key recovered from one vessel can work across the fleet. No exploitation has been reported.

No known exploitation reported Not in CISA KEV

High Guidance — Third-party ICS integrator remote access

CISA and the FBI name transportation among sectors exposed through a compromised ICS integrator

A joint FBI/CISA fact sheet, “Considerations for Critical Infrastructure Operators Working With Third-Party ICS Integrators” (dated 23 September, publicised 24 September), describes foreign cyber actors who compromised a US industrial automation company in March–April 2025 and staged customer SCADA data, ICS device details and schematics for exfiltration. It names power utilities and transportation entities among the affected customers. It recommends security terms in integrator contracts, least privilege, less internet exposure, and integrator remote access through monitored, logged, on-demand paths. Editorial assessment: shipyards, OEMs and other service providers often hold standing remote access to vessel and terminal control systems, so this applies directly to marine operators.

Not an exploit advisory — access-control guidance grounded in a documented 2025 integrator intrusion Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: Inventory every shipyard, OEM and service provider with remote access to vessel or terminal control systems, and require time-boxed, logged access rather than standing credentials.

Info Policy — New Coast Guard maritime cybersecurity office

Coast Guard announces a dedicated Office of Maritime Cybersecurity Policy

The US Coast Guard announced on 31 August that it has established an Office of Maritime Cybersecurity Policy under the Director of Inspections and Compliance (CG-5PC). It arrives as the Coast Guard’s MTSA cybersecurity rule phases in: cyber incidents have been reportable to the National Response Center since July 2025, and owners and operators need a designated Cybersecurity Officer, a cybersecurity assessment and an approved plan by 16 July 2027 (per Dragos’ 24 September summary of the rule).
