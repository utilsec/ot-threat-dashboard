Top Story

Coast Guard confirms malicious cyber activity on a US-bound tanker, while an LNG-carrier control-system claim stays unverified

The US Coast Guard and FBI boarded two US-bound tankers in late August, VL Prosperity on 21 August and the LPG carrier Kohaku on 24 August, after indications that their networks were compromised. Rear Adm. Amy Grable said investigators found malicious cyber activity on VL Prosperity’s IT and operational systems, yet the Coast Guard reports no operational disruption, vessel instability, danger to crews or environmental impact. Claims that attackers raised engine speed and cut communications for about 30 hours come from Iranian media and are unconfirmed. No attribution has been confirmed, though US officials are examining an Iran link. Separately, LNG carrier Vivit Africa LNG lost access to some control systems in early September; its crew alleges hackers took control, but Italy’s Coast Guard recorded only a cargo-monitoring malfunction of undetermined cause. Bloomberg reports nearly 20 ships are being tracked.

MTSA Rules The tanker cases are being read against rules the Coast Guard already has

Dragos (24 September) used VL Prosperity to walk through the Coast Guard’s MTSA cybersecurity rule: cyber-incident reporting to the National Response Center has been mandatory since 16 July 2025, cyber training for personnel with IT/OT access was due 12 January 2026, and a designated Cybersecurity Officer, completed assessment and approved plan are due by 16 July 2027. Its suggested sequence is asset inventory first, then IT/OT segmentation, then the Cybersecurity Officer and vulnerability remediation.

Copenhagen start-up Worldwide AIS Network told Splash247 (25 September) that a receiver in Varna, Bulgaria logged 27 position reports placing four Black Sea tankers on the same 1 km circle in Lima, Peru, about 12,000 km away. GNSS & Insurance A P&I club moves to authenticate ship positions as GNSS interference spreads

Seatrade Maritime (28 September) reports that P&I club NorthStandard has partnered with SGM Technology on PntGuard, a platform that uses encrypted Iridium satellite signals, roughly a thousand times stronger than GPS, to authenticate positions and alert crews when GNSS fails. The article places interference in the Strait of Hormuz, Bab al-Mandab, the Baltic, the Black Sea and the Mediterranean, and notes the UK Maritime and Coastguard Agency’s eLoran proposal as a terrestrial backup. This is an industry response to electronic-warfare interference, not a confirmed cyber-OT compromise.

High Incident — Container terminal operations suspended

Port of Tanjung Pelepas restarted in phases after a cyber incident halted container operations

A cyber incident detected late on 9 September forced Malaysia’s Port of Tanjung Pelepas (PTP), a joint venture of MMC Group and APM Terminals, to suspend container-terminal operations as a precaution; the terminal operating systems were affected. A phased restart began on 10 September with manual processing for export container gate-ins, and a PTP statement reported by WorldCargo News on 15 September said container operations had resumed with system recovery “advancing steadily.” The Direwolf ransomware group listed the port on its leak site on 11 September, claiming about 200 GB of data (per ransomware.live); PTP has not confirmed ransomware or any group, so that remains a claim. No newer development was found as of 29 September.

Critical Incident — Exploited firewall management platform, CISA KEV

Cisco Secure FMC, a common firewall console at IT/OT boundaries, remains under active exploitation

Cisco Talos tracks three clusters exploiting Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center flaws. UAT-12197 deployed web shells and harvested credentials via CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated authentication bypass to root code execution). UAT-11823, tied by Talos to Sandworm through tooling overlap, deployed a Cyclops Blink implant. UAT-11988, a ransomware operator, deployed Qilin after reconnaissance and tunneling. CVE-2026-20316 (CVSS 5.3) is a related low-privilege login flaw. Talos names no victims, sectors or countries, and its post was last updated 21 September. Editorial assessment: FMC often manages the firewalls between corporate IT and terminal-operating-system, crane and gate networks at ports.

Medium Threat Intelligence — AI-assisted naval reconnaissance

Anthropic: an Iran-linked actor used its AI model to track US Navy vessels and research maritime VSAT flaws

Anthropic reported on 11 September that an Iran-linked actor used its Claude model to build Python pipelines for collecting open-source information on US naval positions, and to compile “targeting handbooks” with personnel rosters drawn from military photos, transponder identifiers and satellite-imagery scripts. The actor also directed the model to compile vulnerability research on shipboard systems, including maritime VSAT terminals, Cisco communications equipment and industrial control products. Anthropic banned the account, built new detection and shared intelligence with government authorities. The targets were naval, not commercial shipping, and the report describes reconnaissance and research, not intrusion into any vessel. No newer development was found as of 29 September.

Critical Advisory — Zero-days exploited before a fix, CISA KEV

Citrix NetScaler RCE zero-days (CVSS 9.5 each) exploited before patches; CISA adds both to KEV

Citrix disclosed two NetScaler ADC and Gateway flaws on 27 September, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772, both CVSS 9.5 (v4), after watchTowr warned publicly on 26 September; both were exploited in the wild before a fix existed. CVE-2026-88771 is an unauthenticated command-injection flaw affecting NetScaler deployments generally, with no workaround reported; CVE-2026-88772 needs DTLS enabled, the default on VPN virtual servers. CISA added both to KEV on 27 September with a 30 September federal deadline and said exploitation is global. Fixed builds include 14.1-73.37 and 13.1-64.23 plus FIPS/NDcPP equivalents. Editorial assessment: NetScaler Gateway is a common remote-access front door into port, terminal and OT DMZ networks.

Confirmed by Citrix and CISA — active exploitation, including before a patch was public In CISA KEV — added 27 Sep 2026, federal deadline 30 Sep 2026
Patched — 14.1-73.37 / 13.1-64.23 and FIPS/NDcPP equivalents available Action: Identify every NetScaler ADC or Gateway serving port, terminal or vessel-operator remote access, apply the fixed builds immediately, and treat unpatched instances as potentially compromised, since exploitation preceded the fix and Citrix published no indicators.

High Advisory — Hard-coded cryptographic keys, fix available, no known exploitation

Wärtsilä FOS-Onboard hard-coded keys: CISA advisory names two CVEs and the fixed build

CISA’s advisory ICSA-26-258-02 (15 September) covers two hard-coded cryptographic key flaws (CWE-321) in Wärtsilä FOS-Onboard 5.07.0923.01, a shipboard fleet-optimization system: CVE-2026-81855 in its robot testing framework and CVE-2026-78225 in its deployer Update Controller. Published severity scores differ between sources, so check the advisory for the current rating. A hard-coded key is shared across installations, so one recovered key could work fleet-wide.

High Guidance — Third-party ICS integrator remote access

CISA and the FBI name transportation among sectors exposed through a compromised ICS integrator

A joint FBI/CISA fact sheet on working with third-party ICS integrators, published 24 September, describes foreign cyber actors who compromised a US industrial automation company in March–April 2025 and created nine .zip files of about 800 files, including SCADA-related data, for presumed exfiltration. It names power utilities and transportation among the affected customers. It urges least privilege for integrators, secured remote-access points, offline backups and cybersecurity terms in contracts. Editorial assessment: shipyards, OEMs and other service providers often hold standing remote access to vessel and terminal control systems, so the guidance applies directly to marine operators.

No patch applicable Action: Inventory every shipyard, OEM and service provider with remote access to vessel or terminal control systems, and replace standing credentials with time-limited, logged, least-privilege access.

Info Policy — New Coast Guard maritime cybersecurity office

Coast Guard announces a dedicated Office of Maritime Cybersecurity Policy

The US Coast Guard announced on 31 August that it has established an Office of Maritime Cybersecurity Policy (CG-MCP) under the Director of Inspections and Compliance (CG-5PC). It is meant to be the Coast Guard’s central authority for cyber policy for the Marine Transportation System, directing a coordinated compliance and enforcement strategy and acting as the main liaison to industry. It arrives as the MTSA cybersecurity rule phases in: incident reporting has applied since July 2025, and a Cybersecurity Officer, assessment and approved plan are due by 16 July 2027 (per Dragos’ 24 September summary of the rule).

Organizational policy change, not an exploit or incident Not applicable
No patch applicable Action: Confirm your organization reports cyber incidents to the National Response Center as the MTSA rule requires, and that your Cybersecurity Officer designation, assessment and plan are on track for the July 2027 deadline. Questions can go to the Coast Guard’s MTSCyberRule mailbox.
