High Incident — Supply-chain compromise of a managed-service cloud app; China-state aligned actor per Microsoft; energy and chemicals among targeted sectors; no OT impact reported

Microsoft ties the ConnectWise cloud compromise to China-aligned Storm-0940, which used the vendor’s own app identities to reach Microsoft 365 tenants — energy and chemicals are on its target list

Microsoft Threat Intelligence wrote on 21 October that it assesses Storm-0940, an actor it calls China-state aligned, was responsible for the recently disclosed compromise of ConnectWise’s cloud services. The attacker gained control of the identities of several ConnectWise cloud applications, ConnectWise Cloud Backup among them, and used their stolen credentials to sign in to customers’ Microsoft 365 tenants as the trusted app, so the users’ multi-factor authentication never came into play. Microsoft says Storm-0940 drove the campaign with ARTEX, an open-source penetration-testing framework that can use any AI model, and that more than 2,250 Microsoft 365 tenants received sign-in attempts — attempts, not compromises. Huntress says it saw unauthorised sign-ins at 162 tenants it monitors. A separate password spray from 10 to 14 October produced no successful sign-in. Once inside, it ran directory reconnaissance, added credentials to applications and changed roles and groups. ConnectWise, which says it first detected the activity on 14 October, suspended Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick), took its SaaS Security and Cloud Management services offline and disabled the affected app identities, and says it has seen no indication so far that partner backup data was compromised; its chief executive said on 22 October that services will be restored in controlled phases. Microsoft lists aerospace and defence, chemicals, energy, manufacturing and government among the targeted sectors; it names no victims, and no OT impact has been reported. The sector read is ours: a Microsoft 365 tenant holds the email, SharePoint libraries and Teams channels where plant drawings, network diagrams, vendor remote-access details and incident plans tend to live, and many operators and their contractors buy backup through managed service providers.

High Active campaign — FBI and Secret Service warning on FortiGate credential theft; energy among targeted sectors; no CVE; no OT compromise described

FBI and Secret Service: the FortiBleed credential campaign against FortiGate firewalls is still running — and the energy sector is among its targets

In a joint advisory dated Tuesday 6 October (JCSA-20261006-01), the FBI and US Secret Service warned that the FortiBleed campaign against internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways remains an active threat, with attackers still scanning for devices they can log into using credentials obtained earlier. There is no CVE: the agencies describe abuse of reused or leaked credentials and of legacy SHA-256 password storage. Researcher reporting adds credential stuffing and spraying from leak dumps, offline cracking of extracted hashes and a tool, FortigateSniffer, that passively captures authentication traffic across 24 protocols. Intruders have created administrator accounts with names such as fortiAdmin, forticloud-sync and fgtsecure, and some owners were locked out after attackers reset or deleted the legitimate accounts. The agencies say targets span all 16 US critical infrastructure sectors, energy and food and agriculture among them, and the FBI links the access to Payload ransomware; SOCRadar says it has confirmed at least 12 ransomware deployments that began this way. SOCRadar’s figure of 86,644 working device credentials in 194 countries dates from 19 June and is not a new count. Researchers describe the operator as a Russian-speaking initial access broker whose buyers overlap with INC and Lynx affiliates; that is their assessment, not the agencies’. No OT system compromise has been described and no oil and gas victim named. The sector read is ours: FortiGate is one of the most common firewalls at the IT/OT boundary and at remote sites, so a working login on one is a step toward operations.

Confirmed active campaign — the FBI and Secret Service say it continues and that targets span all 16 US critical infrastructure sectors, including energy; ransomware counts and the operator profile are researcher assessments; no OT compromise or oil and gas victim reported No CVE — the advisory describes credential abuse and legacy password storage, not a product vulnerability

Review local accounts and API keys for names you did not create — the reported list includes fortiAdmin, forticloud-sync and fgtsecure — and check whether legitimate accounts were changed or removed. Then walk the path the agencies describe, from a FortiGate login to Active Directory enumeration to ransomware: confirm that a valid VPN session lands in a zone that cannot reach OT, and that access into the OT DMZ needs a second, separate authentication.

High Threat Intelligence — China state-sponsored group’s relay network used to exploit management and control-plane platforms; energy named among targeted sectors; no victim named; no OT intrusion described

Lumen: Volt Typhoon’s “Silent Train” network of throwaway relays has carried attacks on SD-WAN, remote-management and IT-management platforms since 2023 — energy is on the target list, but no OT intrusion is described

Lumen’s Black Lotus Labs published research on 28 October on what it calls Silent Train, a framework Volt Typhoon has used since about November 2023 to gain and keep access to the management and control-plane systems organisations depend on. It builds rapidly rotating chains of compromised home-office routers, IoT devices and enterprise equipment — more than 27 device classes — uses each chain for a target and then cleans up; CyberScoop reports one chain was assembled in under two minutes. Through it, Lumen says, the group exploited management platforms with zero-days: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN CVE-2026-20127 (CVSS v3.1 10.0 per Cisco, in KEV since 25 February), VeloCloud Orchestrator CVE-2026-16812 (in KEV since 27 July), N-able N-central CVE-2025-8875 and CVE-2025-8876 (in KEV since August 2025), Versa Director and, with moderate confidence, Quest KACE. These are earlier flaws than the Catalyst SD-WAN and VeloCloud bugs on this page’s cards (see Cisco SD-WAN Manager and VeloCloud); Lumen does not tie those to the group, but the same platforms keep being targeted. Lumen says it has notified hundreds of victims over about three years and lists water, energy, transportation, maritime, rail, aviation and emergency services among the critical infrastructure affected; one of its maps notes that the targeted states host assets tied to the country’s energy generation. It names no victim, confirms no energy-generation victim and describes no access to industrial control systems. Zero Day reports that Hawaii, California and Texas were recurring focal points, that Lumen researcher Michael Horka describes the activity as a mix of pre-positioning and espionage, and that the actors often come back with stolen credentials.
