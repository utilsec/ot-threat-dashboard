High Critical Advisory — Pipeline leak detection and integrity monitoring

A hard-coded key and MD5 password hashes inside AVEVA Pipeline Integrity Monitor — read access to the project files is enough

CISA published advisory ICSA-26-253-01 on 10 September covering four vulnerabilities in the PIMBoards component of AVEVA Pipeline Integrity Monitor, AVEVA’s real-time leak-detection and integrity product for liquid and gas pipelines. All versions up to and including 2025 SP1 P1 are affected. CVE-2026-81821, a hard-coded cryptographic key (CVSS v3 8.4), lets anyone who can read PIMBoards project files decrypt the sensitive information inside them. CVE-2026-81822, weak MD5 password hashing (CVSS v3 8.4), exposes password hashes to brute force and a potential route to administrator access. CVE-2026-81823, missing authorisation (5.3), allows unauthorised reads of PIMBoards data, and CVE-2026-81824, a cross-site scripting flaw (4.7), lets a malicious link run script in a user’s session. AVEVA found the first two itself; researcher Adham Khairy Ramadan reported the others. The fix is the 2025 SP1 P2 security update, and AVEVA notes that migrating legacy project files to the new cryptography is one-way. No exploitation has been reported. This is the most directly pipeline-specific advisory of the period: whoever can read or administer PIM can see, and potentially change, the leak-detection configuration and dashboards that controllers rely on for safety decisions.

How to read the indicators. Exploitation and CISA KEV tags reflect status at generation time — “No known exploitation / Not in CISA KEV” means none was reported as of this edition, not that risk is absent. Suggested SLAs are starting points (Critical ≈ 7 days, High ≈ 30 days) to adapt to your own risk tolerance. Owner and status notes you enter are saved only in your local browser and are not transmitted anywhere.

Scope. This edition covers oil and gas operational technology: upstream production, wellsite automation and offshore installations; midstream pipelines, compression, gas processing, storage and LNG; and downstream refining, petrochemical units and fuel terminals, plus gas distribution where OT is involved and the maritime interface at energy terminals. Cross-sector vulnerabilities are included where they affect equipment commonly deployed in these environments, and the sector relevance of those items is editorial assessment.
