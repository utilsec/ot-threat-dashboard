Top Story

CERT.PL discloses how Sandworm reached a Polish combined-heat-and-power plant through a private cellular network and stopped its turbine

CERT.PL published a detailed report on 10–11 Aug 2026 describing a 29 Dec 2025 intrusion, attributed to the Russia-linked Sandworm group, against a combined heat and power (CHP) plant that serves roughly 50,000 residents in Poland. The attacker’s entry point was a FortiGate device at a wind farm functioning as both firewall and VPN concentrator. From there they scanned the carrier-managed private APN — the cellular network the operator uses to reach substations and other remote sites — for exposed VNC, HTTP, Modbus and S7 services. That scan found a WAGO PFC200 controller at the CHP plant with a WAN-facing management interface protected only by default credentials. The attacker enabled SSH on the controller and tunnelled from it into the plant’s OT network. Once inside, they stopped a steam turbine and the plant’s process water treatment system; several ICS devices were permanently damaged during recovery. Heat and electricity deliveries to residents were not interrupted, and staff restored operations from backups. CERT.PL says this is the first publicly documented case of a private APN being used as an OT lateral-movement path, and broader reporting links the intrusion to a wider campaign against roughly 30 other Polish energy-sector sites. This is a disclosed past event, not ongoing activity — the intrusion itself is more than seven months old — but the technique is new to defenders and transfers directly to any utility that treats cellular backhaul as an inherently trusted network rather than a segment requiring its own controls.

Confirmed nation-state intrusion — hands-on manipulation of generation OT; a disclosed Dec 2025 event, not ongoing No CVE — WAN-exposed controller management interface secured only by default credentials
No patch — architectural: APN segmentation and field-device hardening Action: Inventory every cellular router, RTU gateway and modem on your private APN and treat it as a routed network requiring the same segmentation as any other WAN link. Disable SSH and web-management interfaces on field controllers that do not need them, replace default credentials estate-wide, and confirm the APN cannot reach anything beyond the specific SCADA endpoints it exists to serve. Then verify you hold offline, hash-verified backups of controller logic for every generation asset — that is what restored this plant.

High Incident — Nation-state

The Iran-linked PLC campaign spreading across a dozen states has put the energy sector on federal agencies’ target list

Since late July 2026, actors have reached internet-exposed Rockwell Automation/Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 controllers at water and wastewater utilities in at least 12 states, changing device IP addresses and setting unauthorised passwords to lock operators out of monitoring and control. Confirmed effects include 30+ affected community systems in Minnesota, a brief state of emergency in Maple Plain, a pump failure and conservation order in Braham, a boil-water advisory after a pump-station failure in Clayton County, Georgia, and incidents in Michigan, South Dakota, New Jersey and Alabama. Officials say drinking-water safety itself was not compromised. A joint advisory (AA26-097A) from FBI, CISA, NSA, EPA, DOE, USCYBERCOM Cyber National Mission Force and Treasury assesses the activity to Iranian-affiliated APT actors, naming CyberAv3ngers (aka Shahid Kaveh Group), and lists Energy alongside Water and Government Facilities among targeted sectors. No CVE was exploited — actors used leased third-party infrastructure and the manufacturers’ own PLC programming software to reach controllers left internet-accessible with default or weak credentials. For power operators the relevance is direct: the same MicroLogix family and the same failure pattern — an internet-exposed controller secured only by a factory or shared password — shows up in substation RTUs, DER aggregator gateways and remote hydro or generation sites, not just water utilities. The newest individual incidents (New Jersey, Alabama) have not each been separately attributed by name in agency statements; press coverage describes them as Iran-linked.

Active — confirmed OT lockouts at multiple water utilities; Energy named as a targeted sector in the federal advisory No CVE — internet exposure plus default/weak controller credentials

Reset every default or shared controller password, put remote access behind MFA and a brokered jump host, and confirm you would detect an unauthorised IP or password change on a controller today.

Two Linux-kernel flaws in Hitachi Energy’s transformer-monitoring appliance have an unresolved patch status

CISA published an advisory on 13 Aug 2026 for Hitachi Energy’s Asset Performance Management (APM) Edge appliance, used for transformer condition monitoring, covering CVE-2026-43284 (CVSS 8.8, an ESP-in-UDP datagram-splicing flaw) and CVE-2026-43500 (CVSS 7.8, an rxrpc shared-memory fragment-check flaw). Because APM Edge sits on transformer-monitoring networks at substations and generation sites, exploitation could expose or disrupt asset-health data feeding maintenance and protection decisions, though no active exploitation has been reported.

High Advisory — AVEVA

AVEVA patches a code-execution flaw in Enterprise SCADA that needs only operator-level access to trigger

CISA published ICSA-26-225-01 on 13 Aug 2026 for CVE-2025-7639 (CVSS 7.1) in AVEVA Enterprise SCADA and HMI, an untrusted-deserialization flaw that lets a user holding only operator-level privilege execute arbitrary code. AVEVA Enterprise SCADA is widely deployed as the master station/HMI layer in power transmission and distribution control centres. Fixes are available: Enterprise SCADA v2025 P1 and later, v2024 SP1 P2 and later, v2023 SP1 P1 and later, v2022 SP2 P3 and later, v2021 SP2 P6 and later; HMI v2024 R2 HF7 and later. No public exploitation has been reported, but because the bug needs only operator-level rather than administrator access, it lowers the bar for an insider or a compromised operator workstation to reach code execution on the SCADA master.

High Advisory — CISA KEV

A Cisco firewall flaw exploited to crash ASA and FTD devices got a three-day federal patch deadline

CVE-2026-20349 (CVSS 8.6) is a heap-inspection denial-of-service flaw in the Remote Access SSL VPN service on Cisco ASA and FTD, actively exploited to crash devices; CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 Aug 2026 with a 14 Aug federal remediation deadline. Hotfixes are available across the affected 9.x, 7.x and 10.x branches; Cisco has published no workaround. ASA and FTD are commonly deployed as the IT/OT boundary device at power utilities — the firewall separating the corporate network and remote-access VPN from the control-centre or substation network — so an exploited crash can take down segmentation and remote engineering access at the same time. This is a denial-of-service flaw, not remote code execution; exploitation crashes the device rather than granting an attacker control of it.

Info Research — Ransomware

Dragos counted 1,140 industrial ransomware incidents in Q2 2026 — and found no case of direct control-system manipulation

Dragos’s Q2 2026 Industrial Ransomware Analysis, published 10 Aug 2026, recorded 1,140 ransomware incidents against industrial organisations, up 12% on Q1; manufacturing accounted for 747 of them, with transportation and logistics (95, including 6 aviation) and food and beverage (70) also significant. The most active groups were Qilin, Akira, The Gentlemen, DragonForce and LockBit 5.0. The key finding for OT owners: most intrusions cascaded into OT disruption through IT-side encryption of shared infrastructure, not through direct manipulation of control systems — Dragos observed no Stage 2 ICS Kill Chain activity in the quarter’s incidents. For power specifically, that means the realistic ransomware failure mode is losing the IT systems generation and transmission operations depend on — engineering workstations, historians, outage-management and market-settlement systems — rather than an attacker directly touching a turbine controller.

Medium Research — Ransomware

CISA and international partners detail Gunra, a Conti-derived ransomware operation hitting government and critical infrastructure

A joint #StopRansomware advisory (AA26-222A), published 10 Aug 2026 by CISA, the FBI and international partners, details Gunra, a Conti-derived ransomware-as-a-service operation using double extortion against government and critical-infrastructure targets. Initial access is typically through unpatched internet-facing VPN gateways and exposed RDP; the advisory cites Fortinet flaws CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472 as observed entry points. Gunra gives victims a five-to-seven-day payment deadline before publishing stolen data, an aggressive timeline compared with many peer groups. No Gunra victim in the power sector is named in the advisory, but the entry vectors described — exposed VPN and RDP — are the same perimeter weaknesses that repeatedly show up in utility IT-network compromises that later disrupt OT-dependent business systems.

Info Policy — Federal legislation

A new federal bill to mandate water-utility cybersecurity is a preview of the scrutiny facing exposed OT everywhere

Senators Klobuchar and Schiff introduced the Water Cyber Shield Act on 13 Aug 2026 in direct response to the multistate water-utility attacks, giving EPA authority to require mandatory cybersecurity assessments, directing $300 million a year in state revolving funds, and adding a cyber-risk requirement to resilience planning for large water systems. The bill targets water utilities specifically and creates no new obligation for power generation, transmission or distribution operators. Its relevance to power is indirect but real: the failure pattern that triggered it — internet-exposed legacy controllers secured only by default or shared credentials — is not unique to water, and it is the same exposure class documented across Rockwell/Allen-Bradley MicroLogix deployments generally, including at remote generation and DER sites. Utilities that operate combined water and electric service, and state public utility commissions that regulate both, are likely to feel any resulting assessment requirements first, ahead of a possible template for other under-regulated OT sectors.
