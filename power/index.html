Why: Upper Medium. Top Story

Attackers crossed from a wind farm into an unrelated Polish CHP plant over a private cellular APN — and stopped its steam turbine

CERT Polska has disclosed that attackers reached the control network of a Polish combined heat and power plant serving roughly 50,000 residents by pivoting through a private cellular APN, and stopped a steam turbine and the plant’s process-water treatment system. The intrusion started somewhere else entirely: a compromised FortiGate firewall at a wind farm, and a Teltonika cellular router on that network which reached a private APN operated by the distribution system operator. Because devices on that APN were permitted to talk to one another, the attacker crossed from one generator’s network into another operator’s controllers. CERT Polska believes this is the first observed real-world use of a private APN as a route into an industrial control network. Plant staff restored the affected systems quickly and customers saw no loss of supply.

Confirmed OT-disruptive incident — turbine and process-water treatment stopped No new CVE — compromised edge firewall plus a flat private cellular network
No patch — architectural: segment the APN, treat carrier networks as untrusted Action: Ask your carrier this week for the routing and isolation policy on every private APN your sites use, and confirm devices on it cannot reach each other laterally. Inventory cellular routers at remote generation, substation and DER sites, and put a firewall with an explicit allow-list between each one and the control network it serves.
Suggested SLA: Private APN isolation review and cellular router inventory — 30 days Owner:

High Incident — Nation-state / PLCs

The Iranian-affiliated PLC campaign is an energy-sector problem too, and at one victim the actors disabled safety shutdown logic

Joint advisory AA26-097A — issued 7 April by the FBI, CISA, NSA, EPA, DOE, US Cyber Command’s Cyber National Mission Force and Treasury, and substantially expanded on 22 July — covers Iranian-affiliated actors reaching internet-exposed programmable logic controllers across US water, energy and government facilities. Two details matter for power operators. First, the observed manufacturer scope now runs beyond Rockwell Automation to Schneider Electric and Siemens controllers, so a utility that standardised away from MicroLogix is not automatically outside it. Second, at one confirmed US victim the actors modified ladder logic to disable safety shutdown and alarm functions, letting unsafe conditions develop without alerting operators. The agencies also documented project-file exfiltration and Dropbear SSH persistence. No CVE is involved: the entry path is exposure plus default, shared or absent credentials.

Confirmed active campaign — federal assessment of Iranian-affiliated (IRGC-CEC) actors No CVE exploited — internet-exposed controllers with weak or default credentials
No patch — exposure removal, credential hygiene and logic-integrity verification Action: Pull a current copy of the logic from every controller that governs a protection, interlock or shutdown function and diff it against your last known-good baseline, with particular attention to reusable add-on instructions. If no baseline exists, capturing one this month is the highest-value item on this page.

High Incident — Municipal utilities

The multistate utility PLC attacks have reached at least 12 states — and many of those utilities also run the electric system

Water and wastewater systems in at least 12 states have reported operational-technology intrusions since late July, with more than 30 Minnesota community systems affected, a boil-water advisory in Clayton County, Georgia after a pump-station failure, and confirmed incidents in Michigan, South Dakota, New Jersey and Alabama. This belongs on a power page for a structural reason: a large share of the affected utilities are combined municipal utilities that run water, wastewater and electric distribution, frequently on shared network infrastructure, shared cellular contracts and a single small IT team. The technique used against the water side — find a controller answering from the internet, change its IP address, set a password the operator does not have — works identically against a recloser controller, a substation gateway or a distribution RTU.

Confirmed OT-disruptive campaign — operator lockout and physical consequences No CVE exploited — exposure and credential weakness
No patch — exposure reduction and network separation between utility departments Action: If you are a combined utility, confirm this week that the electric OT network is genuinely separated from the water and wastewater OT network, not merely on different VLANs behind the same firewall with the same admin credentials. Re-run an external scan across every public address and cellular range the whole utility owns, not just the electric department’s.
Suggested SLA: Cross-department OT segmentation review and external exposure scan — 14 days Owner:

High Incident — Extortion / Engineering data

Clop has named 43 PTC Windchill victims including Shell and GE, and what it took is engineering data

The Clop extortion group has listed 43 organisations on its leak site after a mass data-theft campaign against internet-exposed PTC Windchill and FlexPLM product-lifecycle instances, exploiting CVE-2026-12569, an improper input validation flaw. Ransom-ISAC assesses with high confidence that Clop affiliates ran it as a zero-day in early June, weeks before PTC began issuing patches on 17 June. Clop claims 89 GB from Shell, including engineering drawings, facility test reports and project plans; Philips has confirmed an enterprise server breach it says was contained, and GE says it is assessing the claim. BleepingComputer reports Clop built a custom web shell for the campaign. For energy operators the exposure is not the plant floor but the design record — drawings, test reports and vendor configuration that make a later, targeted intrusion far easier.

High Threat Intel — Virtualisation

CISA added the exploited vCenter flaw to KEV on 18 August — the campaign has hit 361 hosts in 47 countries

CISA added CVE-2026-59310, a path-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter Server, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August. Broadcom patched it on 29 July; exploitation started roughly five days later. German incident-response firm QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence that a Chinese-speaking actor is behind the campaign, based on Chinese-language artefacts in the attacker’s scripts and tooling, and counts about 361 affected addresses across 47 countries, with Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran and France most represented. The actor obtains root on the vCenter appliance, installs a backdoor via cron and a reverse SSH binary, and in at least one case deployed Babuk-derived ransomware. Utilities that host EMS, historian or engineering-workstation virtual machines on vCenter should treat this as an OT-adjacent exposure, not an IT one.

Confirmed active exploitation — suspected China-nexus actor, ransomware in at least one case In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-59310 added 18 Aug 2026
Patch available — Broadcom fix released 29 Jul 2026 Action: Patch or isolate any vCenter instance that manages virtual machines supporting EMS, SCADA, historian or engineering workstations, and check for unexpected cron entries and a “linuxFile” binary on the appliance. Management interfaces should not be reachable from the corporate network, let alone the internet.

High Threat Intel — Ransomware

A joint advisory names utilities among Gunra ransomware’s targets, and the way in is unpatched edge devices

CISA, the FBI, NSA, DC3, the Secret Service and South Korea’s National Police Agency issued #StopRansomware advisory AA26-222A on 10 August covering Gunra, a double-extortion operation built on leaked Conti source code that moved to a ransomware-as-a-service affiliate model in 2026. The sector list explicitly includes utilities, critical manufacturing, transportation and government facilities. What makes it actionable rather than merely alarming is the initial access: Gunra affiliates are exploiting CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472 in internet-facing FortiOS and FortiProxy devices — both long-patched authentication bypasses that grant super-admin and defeat multi-factor authentication. Nothing novel is required. The advisory ships detection content and indicators, which makes this a straightforward hunt rather than a research project.

Critical Advisory — Siemens

SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced ships a Node-RED interface with no authentication — unauthenticated code execution at maximum severity

In its August Patch Tuesday round Siemens published ten advisories, the most serious of which (SSA-834709) covers SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices running Industrial OS with Node-RED installed. The Node-RED HTTP interface does not enforce authentication, so a remote unauthenticated attacker can reach programming nodes that execute system commands and create flows that run arbitrary code on the device with maximum privileges. Siemens rates it at the top of the scale and has released a fixed version. IoT2050 gateways are common in energy applications as edge data-collection and protocol-translation points between substation or generation equipment and a historian or cloud service, which means a device that is trusted by the control network but frequently managed outside it.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV
Patch available — update to the fixed SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced release Action: Find every IoT2050 in your estate this week, check whether Node-RED is installed, and confirm its HTTP interface is not reachable from anything but a dedicated management host. Update to the fixed version; where an outage window prevents that, block the interface at the network layer as an interim control.

Critical Advisory — HMI gateway

A CVSS 10.0 command injection gives unauthenticated root on Haiwell’s IoT Cloud HMI gateway

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an OS command injection in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway rated 10.0. The Net Check feature reached through the /setting endpoint passes user-supplied input from the cmdPing Socket.io event to the operating system without sanitisation, so an unauthenticated remote attacker can run arbitrary commands as root. Version 3.40.1.12 is affected and Haiwell has published Scada-v3.50.1.19 as the fix. Haiwell gear turns up in smaller generation sites, DER installations and balance-of-plant systems where it was chosen on cost, and it is exactly the class of device that ends up on a cellular connection for remote monitoring — the same pattern behind this month’s utility intrusions.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV
Patch available — Haiwell Scada-v3.50.1.19 Action: Search your asset inventory for Haiwell devices, including anything installed by a packager or balance-of-plant contractor rather than bought directly. Update to Scada-v3.50.1.19 and, regardless of version, make sure the gateway is not answering from the internet or from a cellular carrier address.

High Advisory — Remote access

Cisco ASA and Firepower Threat Defense joined CISA’s KEV catalogue on 11 August

CISA added CVE-2026-20349, a heap inspection vulnerability in Cisco Secure Firewall ASA and Firepower Threat Defense, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 11 August, alongside a Windows Ancillary Function Driver use-after-free and a Metabase SQL injection flaw. The KEV listing means confirmed exploitation in the wild, not theoretical risk. ASA and FTD appliances sit at the boundary of many utility networks and terminate the VPN tunnels that vendors, integrators and on-call engineers use to reach EMS, SCADA and substation networks. A compromise there does not just expose data; it hands an attacker the same access path your own engineers use, with the same reach into the control environment.

Medium Regulation — NERC CIP

CIP-012-2 is in force, CIP-003-9 has been enforceable since April, and the 2026 roadmap points at low-impact and cloud

Two compliance dates have already passed this year and both change day-to-day practice. CIP-003-9 became enforceable on 1 April, extending governance expectations to low-impact BES cyber systems with an explicit focus on vendor remote access and supply chain. CIP-012-2 took effect on 1 July, tightening protection of real-time operational data exchanged between control centres. Looking ahead, NERC’s CIP Roadmap published in January sets out near-term standards work and widens the scope conversation to low-impact systems, cloud services, telecommunications and DER aggregators, while FERC has directed NERC to extend internal network security monitoring under CIP-015 to EACMS and PACS. Penalties remain up to $1 million per violation per day, but the operational point is that low-impact sites and vendor access are where the next audit attention lands.

Low Policy — Investment incentives

A Senate letter asks Treasury to stop the tax code penalising OT security spending

Senator Tom Cotton wrote to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on 5 August asking that federal tax guidance be adjusted so it encourages rather than discourages modernisation of the operational technology that runs critical infrastructure, citing the Minnesota water attacks and the Iranian PLC campaign as the prompt. Three specific asks: confirm that security software for industrial control systems qualifies as research under Section 41; use Section 7701(e) to create a safe harbour so cybersecurity monitoring agreements with public utilities are treated as services rather than long-term equipment leases; and extend the existing lease exception to service providers, since a firm that retains ownership of security equipment and sells monitoring gets no equivalent protection today. Nothing has changed yet, but the financing friction behind slow OT security adoption is now on the record.
