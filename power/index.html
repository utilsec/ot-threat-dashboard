Top Story

Attackers crossed from a wind farm into an unrelated Polish CHP plant over a private cellular APN — and stopped its steam turbine

CERT Polska has disclosed that attackers reached the control network of a Polish combined heat and power plant serving roughly 50,000 residents by pivoting through a private cellular APN, and stopped a steam turbine and the plant’s process-water treatment system. The intrusion started somewhere else entirely: a compromised FortiGate firewall at a wind farm, and a Teltonika cellular router on that network which reached a private APN operated by the distribution system operator. Because devices on that APN were permitted to talk to one another, the attacker crossed from one generator’s network into another operator’s controllers. CERT Polska believes this is the first observed real-world use of a private APN as a route into an industrial control network. Plant staff restored the affected systems quickly and customers saw no loss of supply.

No patch — architectural: segment the APN, treat carrier networks as untrusted Action: Ask your carrier this week for the routing and isolation policy on every private APN your sites use, and confirm devices on it cannot reach each other laterally. Inventory cellular routers at remote generation, substation and DER sites, and put a firewall with an explicit allow-list between each one and the control network it serves.

High Incident — Nation-state / PLCs

The Iranian-affiliated PLC campaign is an energy-sector problem too, and at one victim the actors disabled safety shutdown logic

Joint advisory AA26-097A — issued 7 April by the FBI, CISA, NSA, EPA, DOE, US Cyber Command’s Cyber National Mission Force and Treasury, and substantially expanded on 22 July — covers Iranian-affiliated actors reaching internet-exposed programmable logic controllers across US water, energy and government facilities. Two details matter for power operators. First, the observed manufacturer scope now runs beyond Rockwell Automation to Schneider Electric and Siemens controllers, so a utility that standardised away from MicroLogix is not automatically outside it. Second, at one confirmed US victim the actors modified ladder logic to disable safety shutdown and alarm functions, letting unsafe conditions develop without alerting operators. The agencies also documented project-file exfiltration and Dropbear SSH persistence. No CVE is involved: the entry path is exposure plus default, shared or absent credentials.

Confirmed active campaign — federal assessment of Iranian-affiliated (IRGC-CEC) actors No CVE exploited — internet-exposed controllers with weak or default credentials
No patch — exposure removal, credential hygiene and logic-integrity verification Action: Pull a current copy of the logic from every controller that governs a protection, interlock or shutdown function and diff it against your last known-good baseline, with particular attention to reusable add-on instructions. If no baseline exists, capturing one this month is the highest-value item on this page.

High Threat Intel — Virtualisation

CISA added the exploited vCenter flaw to KEV on 18 August — the campaign has hit 361 hosts in 47 countries

CISA added CVE-2026-59310, a path-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter Server, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August. Broadcom patched it on 29 July; exploitation started roughly five days later. German incident-response firm QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence that a Chinese-speaking actor is behind the campaign, based on Chinese-language artefacts in the attacker’s scripts and tooling, and counts about 361 affected addresses across 47 countries, with Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran and France most represented. The actor obtains root on the vCenter appliance, installs a backdoor via cron and a reverse SSH binary, and in at least one case deployed Babuk-derived ransomware. Utilities that host EMS, historian or engineering-workstation virtual machines on vCenter should treat this as an OT-adjacent exposure, not an IT one.

Confirmed active exploitation — suspected China-nexus actor, ransomware in at least one case In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-59310 added 18 Aug 2026
Patch available — Broadcom fix released 29 Jul 2026 Action: Patch or isolate any vCenter instance that manages virtual machines supporting EMS, SCADA, historian or engineering workstations, and check for unexpected cron entries and a “linuxFile” binary on the appliance. Management interfaces should not be reachable from the corporate network, let alone the internet.

High Threat Intel — Exposure research

Roughly 6,300 ICS and building-automation devices around US data centres answer from the internet — and the newer sites are the worse offenders

Trend Micro’s TrendAI research, published on 18 August, identified about 6,300 internet-reachable industrial control and building-automation devices in and around data centre facilities across the United States. The counter-intuitive finding is the distribution: exposure concentrates in newer builds rather than legacy estate, which the researchers attribute to the volume of connected power, cooling and monitoring equipment commissioned at speed. The devices in scope govern power distribution and cooling. For a utility the interest is not the tenant’s risk but the load: hyperscale and colocation sites are very large, tightly clustered interconnected loads whose controllers sit outside the utility’s own visibility, so an abrupt or coordinated change in their behaviour is a grid-operations question as much as a data-centre one. This is exposure research, not evidence of compromise.

High Threat Intel — Ransomware

CISA and the FBI put Medusa past 500 critical-infrastructure victims — and say it weaponises new flaws inside 24 hours

The updated #StopRansomware advisory AA25-071A, reissued on 18 August by CISA and the FBI, puts Medusa’s cumulative critical-infrastructure victim count above 500 as of April 2026, up from roughly 300 in the March 2025 version — about 200 new victims in a year. Named sectors are healthcare, critical manufacturing, the defense industrial base, government facilities, IT and financial services; impact is enterprise encryption plus data theft, and no control-system involvement is alleged. The operationally useful finding is the tempo: the agencies assess Medusa turns newly disclosed vulnerabilities into working access within about 24 hours, and has been observed using exploits up to a week before public disclosure, because it buys exploits rather than developing them. For a utility that plans patching around monthly change windows, that is the number to argue with — the exposure window on an internet-facing appliance is now shorter than most emergency-change processes.

Confirmed active campaign — 500+ critical-infrastructure victims, federal advisory No single CVE — opportunistic use of newly disclosed and pre-disclosure exploits
No single patch — patch tempo, MFA and segmentation are the controls Action: Measure your real time-to-patch on internet-facing remote access, VPN and file-transfer systems and compare it against 24 hours. Where you cannot meet that, compensate: remove the exposure, put phishing-resistant MFA in front of it, and make sure the IT-side blast radius stops well short of EMS, historian and outage-management systems.

Critical Advisory — Siemens

SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced ships a Node-RED interface with no authentication — unauthenticated code execution at maximum severity

In its August Patch Tuesday round Siemens published ten advisories, the most serious of which (SSA-834709) covers SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices running Industrial OS with Node-RED installed. The Node-RED HTTP interface does not enforce authentication, so a remote unauthenticated attacker can reach programming nodes that execute system commands and create flows that run arbitrary code on the device with maximum privileges. Siemens rates it at the top of the scale and has released a fixed version. IoT2050 gateways are common in energy applications as edge data-collection and protocol-translation points between substation or generation equipment and a historian or cloud service, which means a device that is trusted by the control network but frequently managed outside it.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV

Update to the fixed version; where an outage window prevents that, block the interface at the network layer as an interim control.

Critical Advisory — HMI gateway

A CVSS 10.0 command injection gives unauthenticated root on Haiwell’s IoT Cloud HMI gateway

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an OS command injection in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway rated 10.0. The Net Check feature reached through the /setting endpoint passes user-supplied input from the cmdPing Socket.io event to the operating system without sanitisation, so an unauthenticated remote attacker can run arbitrary commands as root. Version 3.40.1.12 is affected and Haiwell has published Scada-v3.50.1.19 as the fix. Haiwell gear turns up in smaller generation sites, DER installations and balance-of-plant systems where it was chosen on cost, and it is exactly the class of device that ends up on a cellular connection for remote monitoring — the same pattern behind this month’s utility intrusions.

High Advisory — OT monitoring

Six flaws in CISA’s own Malcolm OT traffic-analysis suite — the worst gives arbitrary code execution

ICSA-26-230-01, published on 18 August, documents six vulnerabilities plus a resource-exhaustion condition in Malcolm, the open-source network traffic analysis suite CISA itself publishes for OT monitoring. The most serious is CVE-2026-55676 (CVSS 8.8), an unrestricted file upload that allows arbitrary code execution; the others are CVE-2026-63177 (7.1, path traversal during archive extraction), CVE-2026-63133 and CVE-2026-19671 (6.5 each), and CVE-2026-63134 and CVE-2026-19670 (5.4 each, authorisation bypasses via unnormalised URI paths and percent-encoding). Compression-bomb inputs can also exhaust resources. Affected releases run below 26.06.1 and 26.07.0 and up to 26.07.1, with fixes in 26.07.0 and 26.08.0 depending on the CVE. This matters more than a typical utility-software advisory because Malcolm is a common low-cost way for utilities to get passive visibility: it usually sits inside the OT network on a SPAN or TAP feed, seeing everything, which makes it a high-value host rather than a peripheral tool. No exploitation is reported.

High Advisory — Remote access / KEV

A Windows IKE double free is in KEV with a 21 August deadline — and IKE is what terminates your site-to-site tunnels

CISA added four vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August, all with a 21 August federal remediation deadline. The one that matters most for utility networks is CVE-2026-33824, a double free in the Microsoft Windows IKE service extensions (IKEEXT) leading to remote code execution. A three-day deadline is CISA’s signal for active, broad exploitation. The relevance is architectural rather than exotic: IKEEXT is the Windows component that negotiates IPsec, and IPsec is what terminates a large share of utility site-to-site links to substations, remote generation and control-centre backups, as well as vendor and engineer remote access. The same KEV batch added the VMware vCenter path traversal already covered on this page, a SharePoint authentication-bypass flaw and a macOS Screen Sharing authentication flaw.

Confirmed active exploitation — basis for the KEV listing In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-33824 added 18 Aug 2026, federal deadline 21 Aug 2026
Patch available — apply the Microsoft update to affected Windows hosts Action: Identify every Windows host that terminates or brokers an IPsec tunnel — RRAS servers, jump hosts, control-centre gateways — and patch those first rather than working alphabetically through the estate. Where an outage window blocks patching, restrict which peers can reach UDP 500 and 4500 and review IKE negotiation logs for sessions that do not match a known peer.

Medium Regulation — NERC CIP

CIP-012-2 is in force, CIP-003-9 has been enforceable since April, and the 2026 roadmap points at low-impact and cloud

Two compliance dates have already passed this year and both change day-to-day practice. CIP-003-9 became enforceable on 1 April, extending governance expectations to low-impact BES cyber systems with an explicit focus on vendor remote access and supply chain. CIP-012-2 took effect on 1 July, tightening protection of real-time operational data exchanged between control centres. Looking ahead, NERC’s CIP Roadmap published in January sets out near-term standards work and widens the scope conversation to low-impact systems, cloud services, telecommunications and DER aggregators, while FERC has directed NERC to extend internal network security monitoring under CIP-015 to EACMS and PACS. Penalties remain up to $1 million per violation per day, but the operational point is that low-impact sites and vendor access are where the next audit attention lands.

Medium Regulation — Bulk electric system

A new Senate bill would put quantum risk to the bulk electric system inside FERC’s reliability remit — with OT written into scope

The Quantum Grid Utility Assurance and Resilient Defense Act, or Quantum-GUARD Act, was introduced on 18 August by Senators Chris Coons and Mike Rounds. It would direct FERC to assess quantum-related vulnerabilities using its existing reliability authority, require the Department of Energy to study quantum risk to the bulk electric system with operational technology explicitly in scope alongside IT, and establish a DOE test environment for migrating to post-quantum cryptography. The status matters: it has been introduced, not enacted, and creates no obligation today. The practical significance for operators is that a federal proposal is now naming OT rather than treating cryptographic migration as an IT project — which is where the hard problems sit, given protection relays, RTUs and substation gateways with fifteen-to-twenty-year service lives and firmware that cannot readily accept new algorithms.

Low Policy — National strategy

The new National Security Science and Technology Strategy names OT/ICS resilience as a priority

The National Security Science and Technology Strategy was released on 19 August and sets out four pillars, with operational technology and industrial control system resilience and technology supply chains named as explicit priorities rather than folded into a general cybersecurity heading. It is a strategy document: it directs federal research and coordination priorities and creates no operator obligation, no reporting requirement and no compliance date. Its value to a utility is as a signal of where federal attention and funding are being pointed, and as language you can borrow when making an internal case — a national strategy naming control-system resilience and supply chain is useful supporting material for a programme that has to be argued for annually.
