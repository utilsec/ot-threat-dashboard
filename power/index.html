Water utilities in 12 states lost control of exposed PLCs — the same advisory says energy is in scope

The campaign that began around 27 July against internet-facing water controllers has now been reported in at least 12 states. The FBI and EPA public-service alert of 30 July confirms attackers reached exposed Rockwell Automation/Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 PLCs, changed IP addresses and passwords to lock operators out of monitoring and control, and in at least one case modified controller logic; reported physical effects include pressure loss and localized flooding. High Research — Exposure

4,407 industrial controllers found answering the public internet on EtherNet/IP

A Shodan query run on 3 August returned 4,407 devices exposing port 44818, with roughly 65% in the United States, 12% in Canada and 3% in Spain. Of 22 controllers located in cities where utilities reported incidents, 19 were running firmware susceptible to CVE-2017-16740, a Modbus TCP buffer overflow affecting Rockwell MicroLogix 1400 Series B and C on firmware 21.002 and earlier, fixed in revision 21.003. Power utilities tend to assume this is a small-water-system problem, but the exposure pattern — a controller or gateway reachable over a cellular link at an unstaffed remote site, installed by a contractor and never entered in the asset register — describes substation auxiliaries, recloser controllers, DER skids and district-heating plant just as well.

High Campaign — Ransomware

Six agencies warn on Gunra ransomware: affiliates entering through VPN gateways and firewalls, utilities among victims

Joint advisory AA26-222A, published 10 August by CISA, the FBI, DC3, NSA, the Secret Service and South Korea’s National Police Agency, covers Gunra ransomware. Gunra appeared in April 2025 built on leaked Conti source code and has since become a ransomware-as-a-service operation recruiting affiliates through dark-web forums. The advisory says affiliates break in through exposed edge infrastructure — VPN gateways, firewall appliances and RDP-reachable systems — and run double extortion, exfiltrating before encrypting. Named victim sectors include utilities, manufacturing, transport and government. For a generation or transmission operator the realistic damage path is not the EMS but everything that supports it: outage management, work orders, settlements, engineering file shares and the virtualization layer they all run on.

High Advisory — Nation-State

AA26-097A update details how PLC project files are being rewritten to disable shutdown and alarm logic

The 22 July update to joint advisory AA26-097A is the clearest public description yet of the tradecraft. Iran-affiliated actors connect to internet-exposed controllers using the vendors’ own engineering software — Rockwell Studio 5000 Logix Designer, Schneider EcoStruxure Control Expert and Siemens TIA Portal — over ports 44818, 2222, 102, 502 and 22, from leased third-party-hosted infrastructure. In one US case the FBI found a downloaded project file that preserved ladder logic for downstream function while adding logic that overrode the instruction sets maintaining safe operating parameters, and disabled shutdown and alarm logic so equipment could enter unsafe states without notifying operators. Investigators cite attacks on Rockwell CompactLogix and Micro850, Schneider Modicon M340 (BMX P34) and Siemens S7-1200. The advisory says energy is among the affected sectors.

Active — confirmed hands-on manipulation of control logic; energy listed among affected sectors CISA/FBI/NSA/EPA joint advisory AA26-097A — refreshed IOCs and detection guidance
No single patch — segmentation, credentials and logic integrity Action: Build a list of every PLC in your fleet from the named families and confirm none is reachable without going through a monitored jump host; restrict engineering software to hardened workstations with named accounts; export current project files and diff them against known-good backups, looking specifically for added add-on instructions and removed trip, interlock or alarm rungs; add a periodic logic-integrity check to your maintenance cycle and hunt against the July indicators.
Suggested SLA: Engineering-workstation and exposure review — 7 days; project-file integrity diff on protection-relevant logic — 14 days Owner:

Medium Report — Ransomware Trends

Dragos: industrial ransomware up 12% in Q2, with zero cases of direct ICS manipulation

Dragos’ Q2 2026 analysis, published 11 August, recorded 1,140 ransomware incidents at industrial organizations, up from 1,020 in Q1. Manufacturing absorbed 747 of them; ICS-adjacent firms including engineering companies and system integrators accounted for 117, and transportation and logistics 95. North America led with 514 incidents, Europe 316. The most useful finding for a utility is the absence: Dragos observed no case in the quarter where a ransomware operator directly manipulated an industrial control system, yet operations were still disrupted through IT, ERP and virtualization systems. The 117 integrator and engineering-firm victims are worth a second look — those are the organizations holding your relay settings, one-lines and remote-access credentials.

Critical Advisory — CVSS 10

CVSS 10 unauthenticated root command injection in Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway (ICSA-26-225-02)

CISA published 14 ICS advisories on 13 August, led by CVE-2026-19188 in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway at CVSS 10.0. The device’s ‘Net Check’ feature passes user-supplied input from the cmdPing Socket.io event to the operating system without sanitizing it, allowing an unauthenticated remote attacker to run arbitrary OS commands as root with no user interaction. Version 3.40.1.12 is affected, and CISA lists energy among the deployment sectors alongside critical manufacturing and water. These combined HMI/gateway/DTU units are inexpensive remote-monitoring boxes that turn up on solar and storage skids, small hydro, district energy and unstaffed pump or valve stations — precisely the assets least likely to appear in a NERC CIP asset list and most likely to have a cellular connection.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV
See vendor advisory — isolate immediately if internet-reachable Action: Search procurement and integrator records, not just the CMDB, for Haiwell gateways and cloud HMI units at generation, DER and remote-station sites; take any internet- or cellular-reachable unit offline or restrict it to a management VLAN today; apply vendor firmware when published; where the device cannot be updated, plan replacement, because unauthenticated root execution leaves no meaningful compensating control on the device.

High KEV — Actively Exploited

Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD zero-day added to CISA KEV after denial-of-service exploitation

CISA added three vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August. CVE-2026-20349 is a heap-inspection flaw in Cisco Secure Firewall Adaptive Security Appliance and Firewall Threat Defense that Cisco patched after exploitation as a zero-day for denial-of-service attacks; the others are a use-after-free in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock (CVE-2026-68820) and SQL injection in Metabase (CVE-2026-72898). Medium Compliance — NERC CIP

CIP-015-1 internal network security monitoring: 1 October 2028 for high impact and medium with ERC

FERC approved CIP-015-1 on 26 June 2025, requiring internal network security monitoring inside the electronic security perimeter. High-impact BES Cyber Systems and medium-impact systems with external routable connectivity must comply by 1 October 2028; all other BES Cyber Systems with ERC have until 1 October 2030. The requirements cover monitoring network data feeds, detecting anomalous activity, evaluating what is detected, protecting the monitoring data itself and retaining investigation records. FERC also directed NERC to revise the standard to bring external EACMS and PACS into scope. Read against this week’s advisories the timing is pointed: INSM is precisely the capability that would surface an engineering workstation talking to a controller it has no business talking to.

Not applicable — regulatory requirement FERC-approved standard — enforceable on the dates shown
Not applicable — multi-year monitoring architecture and budget work Action: Scope which BES Cyber Systems fall into the 2028 and 2030 tiers and get sensor placement into the next capital cycle rather than the last one; decide now whether monitoring data stays inside the ESP or is exported, because that choice drives the retention and protection design; pilot INSM at one high-impact control center to learn the alert volume before you own it fleet-wide; assume EACMS and PACS will be added and design for it.
