Top Story

Citrix confirms NetScaler zero-days exploited worldwide; both flaws are in CISA KEV with a 30 September deadline

Citrix confirmed on 27 September that two critical flaws in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (both CVSS 9.5), were exploited in the wild before patches existed. CISA added both to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a 30 September federal deadline. CVE-2026-88771 lets an unauthenticated attacker run commands on default configurations; CVE-2026-88772 is a memory flaw reachable when DTLS is enabled, the default on VPN virtual servers. Citrix says multiple customers worldwide were hit but names no sector, and no power-sector compromise has been reported. NetScaler is a common remote-access front door for utility corporate networks, contractors and integrators, so a compromised appliance could become the first hop toward EMS, SCADA and substation networks. CISA advises checking for compromise before patching.

CISA added two Check Point flaws to KEV on 22 September. High Incident — Confirmed customer-data breach at a US electric & gas utility

CenterPoint Energy confirms customer data was taken; a hacker claims about 7.5 million records and threatens “infrastructure” next

CenterPoint Energy told the SEC on 15 September that an unauthorized third party obtained personal information relating to a portion of its customers through one of its externally facing systems. Three days earlier a threat actor had posted a 2.5 GB archive on a cybercrime forum, claiming about 7.5 million records and adding: “next time we won’t simply pull data, we’ll start attacking the main infrastructure.” CenterPoint says the incident has not affected the delivery of electric or gas service; it has not confirmed the record count, and no vulnerability, intrusion vector or actor identity has been disclosed. SecurityWeek cautions that hackers often exaggerate and that the data’s validity is unconfirmed, and notes this is not the first CenterPoint data incident. This reads as an IT-side data breach, not an OT compromise — but the actor’s stated intent is a reason for other utilities to check that customer-facing systems are properly segmented from anything that touches control-system networks.

Confirmed — CenterPoint’s SEC disclosure confirms unauthorized access to customer data; the 7.5 million record count and the escalation threat are the actor’s unverified claims Not KEV-related — no CVE or exploited product identified
Not applicable — no vulnerability disclosed Action: Re-verify that customer-facing web and billing systems sit on a network segment with no path to OT, SCADA or DER management systems; review externally facing application logs for the access pattern CenterPoint has not yet disclosed; and use the actor’s claim as a prompt to test incident-response coordination between IT security and OT operations, not as evidence of a confirmed capability.

High Threat Intelligence — Joint CISA/FBI guidance drawn from a compromised ICS integrator

CISA and FBI: a compromised ICS integrator held utilities’ SCADA data — limit what yours can reach

CISA and the FBI released a joint fact sheet on 24 September, “Considerations for Critical Infrastructure Operators Working With Third-Party ICS Integrators.” Its motivating case: in March–April 2025, foreign actors inside a US industrial automation company that does system integration and SCADA programming for customers including power utilities and transportation entities searched for “customers” and “SCADA” and staged nine archives of about 800 files — customer SCADA information, ICS device details and schematics — for exfiltration. The agencies do not name the company, the actor or its country, and do not say any utility was breached. Their advice: write security and supply-chain requirements into integrator contracts, apply least privilege, route remote access through monitored, on-demand channels, know what data integrators hold and where, and be able to operate if an integrator is compromised.

Not an exploit advisory — a joint fact sheet grounded in a documented 2025 intrusion at an automation integrator Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: Inventory every integrator, OEM and contractor with remote access to control-system networks; convert standing VPN or remote-desktop accounts to time-boxed, approval-gated and logged sessions; ask each integrator which of your SCADA data, schematics and credentials it stores and where; add security clauses to contracts at renewal; and rehearse manual operation for the case where an integrator is compromised.
Suggested SLA: Integrator access inventory — 14 days; conversion to on-demand access — 30 days; contract-language review — 60 days Owner:

Critical Advisory — Protection relay authentication bypass, no known exploitation

A predictable session ID lets an attacker bypass authentication on Siemens’ Reyrolle 7SR5 protection relay

Siemens published advisory SSA-142885 on 8 September as part of that month’s ICS Patch Tuesday batch. It covers 15 vulnerabilities in the Reyrolle 7SR5 protection relay, with a top CVSS v3.1 score of 9.8: authentication bypass through predictable session identifiers, plus denial of service, privilege escalation and, for someone with physical access, memory corruption and unsigned code execution in maintenance mode. All versions before V2.70 are affected. No in-the-wild exploitation or public exploit code has been reported. Protection relays like this trip breakers to protect equipment during faults; an attacker with unauthenticated access could, in principle, alter settings or block a legitimate trip, though doing so would first require network reach to the relay.

No known exploitation — no public exploit code reported Not in CISA KEV
Patched — Reyrolle 7SR5 V2.70 and later Action: Inventory Reyrolle 7SR5 relays and schedule the V2.70 upgrade at the next protection-system outage window; in the interim, confirm the relay’s web interface is not reachable from any network beyond the protection LAN and follow Siemens’ network-protection guidance.

High Advisory — Substation RTU privilege escalation, no known exploitation

Schneider Electric PowerLogic T300 substation RTU has a root privilege-escalation flaw, CVE-2026-77120 (CVSS 8.7)

Schneider Electric’s September ICS Patch Tuesday release on 9 September included a high-severity OS command-injection flaw (CWE-78), CVE-2026-77120, in the PowerLogic T300 — the platform formerly sold as the Easergy T300 RTU — in versions 2.9.8-5620 and earlier. Exploitation needs authenticated SSH access with high privileges, after which an attacker could escalate to root and run administrative functions; the advisory is SEVD-2026-251-02. The same release covered a CVSS 9.2 authentication flaw in Modicon M580 and M580 Safety controllers (CVE-2026-3869), which matters to plant automation at generation sites. Neither is reported as exploited or in KEV. The T300 flaw is a post-access risk rather than a remote entry point, so the priority is controlling who holds SSH credentials on substation RTUs.

High Advisory — Time-series database used for industrial telemetry, unauthenticated crash

One malformed packet can crash TDengine, a database used for industrial and utility telemetry (CVE-2026-42542, CVSS 7.5)

Ridge Security disclosed CVE-2026-42542, an integer-underflow flaw in the RPC message handler of TDengine, a time-series database used to store industrial, IoT and energy telemetry. A single crafted packet to the default TCP 6030 port can crash the database process without credentials, so an attacker on the network could blind operators to historian and monitoring data. TDengine 3.4.0.0 through 3.4.1.5 is affected and 3.4.1.6 fixes it. Ridge reports no in-the-wild exploitation and no public exploit code, and is withholding its proof of concept. For a generation or transmission operator the exposure depends on whether historians, vendor monitoring products or cloud analytics platforms embed TDengine — a question worth asking vendors, since it is often bundled rather than installed directly.

No known exploitation — Ridge Security reports no in-the-wild telemetry and no public exploit code Not in CISA KEV
Patched — TDengine 3.4.1.6 and later Action: Find TDengine instances, including those bundled inside OEM monitoring, historian or analytics products; upgrade to 3.4.1.6 or later; block TCP 6030 from anything outside the systems that need it; and alert on repeated taosd crashes or restart loops.
Suggested SLA: Discovery across estate and vendors — 14 days; upgrade or vendor confirmation — 30 days Owner:

Medium Policy — DOE request for information on foreign equipment in the bulk-power system

DOE asks the industry how to secure the bulk-power system from foreign-equipment risk; comments are due 9 October

Executive Order 14421, signed 26 August, declares a national emergency over the security of the US bulk-power system and authorizes the Department of Energy to prohibit, condition or unwind transactions involving foreign-produced bulk-power equipment tied to covered foreign entities. Coverage includes transmission at 69 kV and above, generation needed for reliability, and substation, control-room and generating-station equipment such as transformers, inverters, battery storage and industrial control systems; DOE may also set conditions on previously installed equipment and must issue implementing rules within 120 days. On 9 September DOE published a Federal Register request for information asking utilities, manufacturers, software developers and integrators about equipment definitions, foreign-entity identification, risk criteria, existing-equipment mitigation, remote access and domestic supply. Responses are voluntary and due 9 October 2026.

Not an exploit advisory — a federal request for information under an executive order Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: Begin inventorying bulk-power equipment, software, firmware and vendor remote-access paths by manufacturer and country of origin; decide by early October whether to file comments through your trade association or directly; and track DOE’s rulemaking, since existing installed equipment is within the order’s reach.
Suggested SLA: Comment decision — before 9 Oct 2026; equipment-origin inventory — 60 days Owner:

Info Regulation — FERC approves NERC CIP-014-4 (transmission physical security)

FERC approves NERC CIP-014-4; the revised transmission physical-security standard takes effect on 1 October 2028

FERC issued an order on 10 September approving NERC Reliability Standard CIP-014-4, published in the Federal Register on 15 September. It consolidates the 30- and 60-month reassessment cycles into a single 36-month cycle, brings jointly owned transmission facilities into scope, requires technically justified thresholds and both steady-state and dynamic simulations, and adds a review of facilities within 1,500 feet of applicable stations. CIP-014-3 retires when the new version takes effect on 1 October 2028; FERC estimates the change affects about 344 transmission owners. The standard is about physical attack, not cyber, but it determines which transmission stations and substations are treated as critical — the same list many owners use to prioritize security work more broadly.

Not an exploit advisory — an approved reliability standard with a forward-looking effective date Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: Transmission owners and operators: gap-assess current CIP-014 methodology against the new 36-month cycle, simulation, 1,500-foot proximity and joint-owner coordination requirements, and plan budget and staffing ahead of 1 October 2028.
Suggested SLA: Methodology gap assessment — 6 months; implementation plan — 12 months Owner:
