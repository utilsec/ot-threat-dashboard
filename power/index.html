A third Citrix NetScaler zero-day is under attack as exploitation of the first two widens; patching does not evict an intruder

Citrix confirmed on 27 September that two NetScaler ADC and Gateway flaws were exploited before patches existed: CVE-2026-88771, which lets an unauthenticated attacker run commands on default configurations, and CVE-2026-88772, a memory flaw reachable when DTLS is enabled, the default on VPN virtual servers (both CVSS v4 9.5). Mandiant counts “dozens” of victims in government, technology, finance, telecom, education and professional services; Arctic Wolf counts at least 78, and researcher Kevin Beaumont more than 100 by his own tracking. Mandiant calls the actors suspected state-sponsored and names no group. Intruders planted web shells, created a rogue superuser account and stole appliance configurations, and Mandiant warns that upgrading alone does not remove that access. On 4 October CISA added a third flaw, CVE-2026-88779 (CVSS v4 8.7), after Citrix reported targeted attacks causing denial of service on SAML-configured appliances. watchTowr reproduced it as a crash-only bug and suspects it is used to knock appliances over to speed exploitation of CVE-2026-88771; one researcher reported signs of code execution on a honeypot, which Citrix has not confirmed. No power-sector victim has been named, but NetScaler often fronts contractor and field-staff access at utilities, so an unhunted appliance is a possible first hop toward EMS, SCADA and substation networks.

Critical Incident — Exploited SD-WAN management zero-day, CISA KEV

Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager authentication bypass CVE-2026-76504 was exploited as a zero-day; there is no workaround

Cisco disclosed CVE-2026-76504 (CVSS 9.8) on 30 September: Catalyst SD-WAN Manager mishandles URI encoding, so a crafted HTTP request can bypass an API authentication rule and give a remote, unauthenticated attacker administrative access. Cisco says its PSIRT became aware of active exploitation in September, and CISA added the flaw to KEV the same day with a 3 October federal deadline, now passed. Cisco revised the advisory on 2 October. There is no workaround; a temporary Live Protect shield gives partial protection but may block some legitimate users. BleepingComputer counts this as the fifth exploited SD-WAN zero-day of 2026. No actor, victim or sector has been named. SD-WAN Manager pushes policy to the overlay that many utilities use to link offices, plants and substations, so administrative access to it is a position from which traffic paths toward field sites could be changed (editorial assessment).

High Incident — Exploited email-security gateway zero-day, CISA KEV

Fortinet FortiMail flaw CVE-2026-104286 was exploited before a fix existed; Fortinet’s 5 October update now lists fixed releases

Fortinet published advisory FG-IR-26-175 on 1 October for CVE-2026-104286 (CVSS 9.8), a path-traversal flaw in FortiMail that lets an unauthenticated attacker write arbitrary files on the appliance with crafted HTTP or HTTPS requests. Fortinet says the flaw has been reported exploited in the wild, and CISA added it to KEV on 1 October with a 4 October deadline. No fixed build was available at disclosure; Fortinet’s 5 October update lists 8.0.2, 7.6.7 and 7.4.9, and tells 7.2 users to move to the 7.4 branch or later. Fortinet still urges its workarounds: turn off the IBE feature, restrict the webmail interface to trusted networks, and block POST requests to /ibe paths that contain “../”. No actor or victims have been disclosed. FortiMail sits on the corporate side rather than in control networks, but a compromised mail gateway is a trusted platform for phishing operations and engineering staff (editorial assessment).

High Incident — Confirmed customer-data breach at a US electric & gas utility

CenterPoint Energy confirms customer data was taken; a hacker claims about 7.5 million records and threatens “infrastructure” next

CenterPoint Energy told the SEC on 15 September that an unauthorized third party obtained personal information relating to a portion of its customers through one of its externally facing systems. Three days earlier a threat actor had posted a 2.5 GB archive on a cybercrime forum, claiming about 7.5 million records and adding: “next time we won’t simply pull data, we’ll start attacking the main infrastructure.” CenterPoint says the incident has not affected the delivery of electric or gas service; it has not confirmed the record count, and no vulnerability, intrusion vector or actor identity has been disclosed. SecurityWeek cautions that hackers often exaggerate and that the data’s validity is unconfirmed, and notes this is not the first CenterPoint data incident. This reads as an IT-side data breach, not an OT compromise — but the actor’s stated intent is a reason for other utilities to check that customer-facing systems are properly segmented from anything that touches control-system networks.

High Threat Intelligence — Joint CISA/FBI guidance drawn from a compromised ICS integrator

CISA and FBI: a compromised ICS integrator held utilities’ SCADA data — limit what yours can reach

CISA and the FBI released a joint fact sheet on 24 September, “Considerations for Critical Infrastructure Operators Working With Third-Party ICS Integrators.” Its motivating case: in March–April 2025, foreign actors inside a US industrial automation company that does system integration and SCADA programming for customers including power utilities and transportation entities searched for “customers” and “SCADA” and staged nine archives of about 800 files — customer SCADA information, ICS device details and schematics — for exfiltration. The agencies do not name the company, the actor or its country, and do not say any utility was breached. Their advice: write security and supply-chain requirements into integrator contracts, apply least privilege, route remote access through monitored, on-demand channels, know what data integrators hold and where, and be able to operate if an integrator is compromised.

Not an exploit advisory — a joint fact sheet grounded in a documented 2025 intrusion at an automation integrator Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: Inventory every integrator, OEM and contractor with remote access to control-system networks; convert standing VPN or remote-desktop accounts to time-boxed, approval-gated and logged sessions; ask each integrator which of your SCADA data, schematics and credentials it stores and where; add security clauses to contracts at renewal; and rehearse manual operation for the case where an integrator is compromised.

Critical Advisory — Protection relay authentication bypass, no known exploitation

A predictable session ID lets an attacker bypass authentication on Siemens’ Reyrolle 7SR5 protection relay

Siemens published advisory SSA-142885 on 8 September as part of that month’s ICS Patch Tuesday batch. It covers 15 vulnerabilities in the Reyrolle 7SR5 protection relay, with a top CVSS v3.1 score of 9.8: authentication bypass through predictable session identifiers, plus denial of service, privilege escalation and, for someone with physical access, memory corruption and unsigned code execution in maintenance mode. All versions before V2.70 are affected. No in-the-wild exploitation or public exploit code has been reported. Protection relays like this trip breakers to protect equipment during faults; an attacker with unauthenticated access could, in principle, alter settings or block a legitimate trip, though doing so would first require network reach to the relay.

No known exploitation — no public exploit code reported Not in CISA KEV

High Advisory — Substation RTU privilege escalation, no known exploitation

Schneider Electric PowerLogic T300 substation RTU has a root privilege-escalation flaw, CVE-2026-77120 (CVSS 8.7)

Schneider Electric’s September ICS Patch Tuesday release on 8 September included a high-severity OS command-injection flaw (CWE-78), CVE-2026-77120, in the PowerLogic T300 — the platform formerly sold as the Easergy T300 RTU — in versions 2.9.8-5620 and earlier. Exploitation needs authenticated SSH access with high privileges, after which an attacker could escalate to root and run administrative functions; the advisory is SEVD-2026-251-02. The same release covered a CVSS 9.2 authentication flaw in Modicon M580 and M580 Safety controllers (CVE-2026-3869), which matters to plant automation at generation sites. Neither is reported as exploited or in KEV. The T300 flaw is a post-access risk rather than a remote entry point, so the priority is controlling who holds SSH credentials on substation RTUs.

Medium Advisory — Protection-relay engineering software, local privilege escalation, no fixed version

ABB’s PCM600 relay-engineering tool has two local flaws and no fixed version yet, only configuration workarounds

CISA published advisory ICSA-26-274-03 on 1 October for ABB’s Protection and Control IED Manager PCM600, versions 2.14 and earlier; ABB’s own advisories are dated 28 September. CVE-2026-15952 (CVSS v3.1 6.4, v4 7.1) is an incorrect permission assignment: PCM600’s Scheduler Service runs as LocalSystem while granting permissions to standard users, so an attacker with local access and valid credentials could escalate privileges. CVE-2026-15953 (v3.1 5.0, v4 5.6) is a path traversal in archive processing that could write files outside the intended directory; it needs local access and user interaction. CISA lists the Energy sector and reports no known public exploitation. ABB has released no fixed version, only mitigations. Neither flaw is remotely exploitable, but PCM600 is the tool engineers use to manage protection and control IEDs, so the workstations it runs on deserve the same care as the relays (editorial assessment).

No known exploitation — CISA reports none; both flaws need local access Not in CISA KEV
No fixed version — ABB mitigations only (run the Scheduler Service under the PCM600 user account) Action: Find PCM600 installations at 2.14 or earlier. Medium Policy — DOE request for information on foreign equipment in the bulk-power system

DOE’s request for information on foreign-equipment risk in the bulk-power system closes this Friday, 9 October

Executive Order 14421, signed 26 August, declares a national emergency over the security of the US bulk-power system and authorizes the Department of Energy to prohibit, condition or unwind transactions involving foreign-produced bulk-power equipment tied to covered foreign entities. Coverage includes transmission at 69 kV and above, generation needed for reliability, and substation, control-room and generating-station equipment such as transformers, inverters, battery storage and industrial control systems; DOE may also set conditions on previously installed equipment and must issue implementing rules within 120 days. On 9 September DOE published a Federal Register request for information asking utilities, manufacturers, software developers and integrators about equipment definitions, foreign-entity identification, risk criteria, existing-equipment mitigation, remote access and domestic supply. Responses are voluntary and due 9 October 2026; DOE says the request is for information and planning and is not itself a proposed rule.

No patch applicable Action: Begin inventorying bulk-power equipment, software, firmware and vendor remote-access paths by manufacturer and country of origin; decide this week whether to file comments through your trade association or directly, since the window closes on 9 October; and track DOE’s rulemaking, since existing installed equipment is within the order’s reach.

Info Policy — DOE cybersecurity technical assistance for small electric utilities

DOE launches a $100 million cybersecurity technical-assistance partnership for cooperative, municipal and small electric utilities

DOE’s Office of Cybersecurity, Energy Security, and Emergency Response (CESER) announced on 1 October the $100 million Rural and Municipal Utility Cybersecurity (RMUC) Advanced Cybersecurity Technical Assistance partnership, administered by DOE’s partnership intermediary ConnectWerx. Applicant organizations must have formal partnerships with at least six qualifying electric utilities: cooperatives, municipal utilities or small investor-owned utilities. Funded work covers assessments and planning, workforce training and certification, exercises, tool implementation, verification and validation, and threat-information sharing. DOE’s announcement gives no application deadline and points applicants to ConnectWerx for details. It creates no new requirement, but for utilities without in-house OT security staff it is a route to outside assessment and exercise help.
