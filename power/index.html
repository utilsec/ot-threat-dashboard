Top Story

Federal alert sharpens: Iran-affiliated hackers are actively manipulating the PLCs that run US energy infrastructure

Federal agencies have sharpened their warning that Iran-affiliated hackers are actively manipulating the programmable logic controllers that run US energy infrastructure. Joint advisory AA26-097A, updated 22 July, confirms actors have reached internet-exposed Rockwell, Schneider Electric and Siemens controllers, use the vendors’ own engineering software to steal PLC project files, and rewrite or delete control logic — in some cases disabling the shutdown and alarm functions that keep equipment inside safe limits, without alerting operators. The risk is no longer theoretical: coordinated attacks on exposed PLCs have now disrupted water utilities in at least 12 states, with loss of pressure, localized flooding and sustained manual operation confirmed. For grid and generation operators the read-across is direct — the same brands (including the Rockwell MicroLogix controllers seen in these intrusions) sit in substations, plants and remote co-op sites, and a controller that suppresses its own alarms undermines the protection logic crews rely on.

High Incident — Cross-Sector

Coordinated OT attacks on exposed PLCs spread to water utilities in at least 12 states — the exposed-controller playbook, proven

What began with 30-plus Minnesota systems in late July has grown into a coordinated campaign against internet-exposed programmable logic controllers at water and wastewater utilities in at least 12 states, including Georgia (Clayton County), Michigan and South Dakota. Attackers have targeted Rockwell Automation MicroLogix 1100/1400 controllers and similar exposed devices; the FBI and CISA confirm real operational impact at some sites — loss of pressure, localized flooding, boil-water notices and sustained manual operation — though drinking water has stayed safe and disruptions were largely contained to hours. Federal agencies have not officially attributed the activity, but multiple reports and a non-public WaterISAC assessment align it with the Iranian OT-targeting patterns described in AA26-097A. For power operators this is the clearest proof yet that the same internet-exposed-controller pattern — researchers estimate roughly 10,000 Rockwell, Siemens and Schneider PLCs are internet-reachable — produces genuine multi-environment OT disruption, and the grid runs the same brands.

High Incident — Nation-State

FSB Center 16 router campaign (AA26-194A): the exposed edge gear sitting in front of substation networks

A joint advisory from NSA, CISA, the FBI and roughly a dozen partner nations (AA26-194A, 13 July) details how Russia’s FSB Center 16 — tracked as Static Tundra, Berserk Bear, Energetic Bear and Dragonfly — scans for network devices exposing SNMP with default community strings and abuses Cisco Smart Install to copy device configurations. Energy and state/local government are named explicitly. The same internet-facing edge routers, often with SNMP left open, sit directly in front of substation SCADA and control-center networks, and a stolen config is a ready-made map of the network before an intruder touches a relay or RTU.

High Active Exploitation — KEV

Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center zero-day exploited — hard-coded password on the utility IT/OT boundary (CVE-2026-20316)

CISA added CVE-2026-20316 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 29 July after confirming active exploitation, setting a 1 August federal remediation deadline. The flaw is a hard-coded password tied to a low-privilege account in the web interface of Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC), letting an unauthenticated remote attacker log in; Cisco rates the impact high because that access can be chained with other FMC flaws to escalate. FMC is the console that governs the firewalls segmenting utility IT from substation and control-center OT — a foothold there is a foothold on the boundary itself. Fixed builds are available across the 7.0 through 10.0 trains.

Active — exploitation confirmed in the wild CISA KEV — added 29 Jul 2026
Vendor fixes available — patch immediately Action: Inventory all Cisco Secure FMC appliances and upgrade to a fixed build (7.0.9.1, 7.2.11.1, 7.4.7.1, 7.6.5.1, 7.7.12.1 or 10.0.1.1) now; remove FMC management interfaces from internet exposure, restrict them to a dedicated admin network, and review authentication logs for logins via the built-in low-privilege account.

High Research — DER/Inverters

Solar and storage inverters flagged as a remote-sabotage path as the grid leans on DER

Renewed research and industry attention this month centers on the cyber exposure of grid-connected solar inverters and battery storage as distributed energy resources become a larger share of generation. Analysts warn that inverters and their cloud-management platforms can be remotely manipulated — plaintext control channels, predictable session handling and internet-exposed management have all surfaced in recent DER advisories — and that coordinated manipulation of enough inverter capacity could stress grid stability. For utilities integrating third-party DER, the takeaway is that inverter fleets are OT assets that need the same segmentation, monitoring and vendor-security scrutiny as any control system.

High Threat Intel — Nation-State

Dragos: VOLTZITE and SYLVANITE are quietly pre-positioning inside US electric-utility networks

Dragos’ ongoing 2026 electric-grid threat reporting warns that the sector’s most consequential activity is patient, quiet access rather than loud disruption. VOLTZITE — which overlaps with the China-linked Volt Typhoon activity — continues to silently pre-position inside US electric-utility IT and OT networks, harvesting network diagrams, GIS data and operational detail that would enable a future disruptive attack. SYLVANITE is described as a stage-one access broker: it establishes footholds and hands them to ICS-capable actors, and its targeting spans both electric and water utilities. As foundational context, the ELECTRUM/Sandworm intrusion that hit Poland’s distributed-energy grid in December 2025 shows the disruptive end-state these footholds can lead to. For grid defenders the message is to hunt for pre-positioning now — look for the access, not just the attack.

Critical Active Exploitation — KEV

Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day exploited at CVSS 10 — the SD-WAN that links substations and remote generation (CVE-2026-16812)

CISA added CVE-2026-16812 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 27 July after confirming it was abused as a zero-day. The flaw is an unauthenticated OS command-injection bug in Arista’s VeloCloud Orchestrator (On-Prem) rated CVSS 10.0: a remote attacker with no credentials can run arbitrary commands on the appliance that centrally manages an SD-WAN fabric. For utilities, VeloCloud-class SD-WAN is exactly what stitches substations, remote generation and field sites back to control centers — compromising the orchestrator hands an attacker control over the network paths carrying OT traffic and a pivot toward the sites themselves. A patch is available; treat it as emergency, given confirmed in-the-wild exploitation.

High Active Exploitation — KEV

CISA adds three exploited flaws to KEV — N-able N-central RMM and Apache Tomcat on the utility management/IT boundary (CVE-2026-18556, CVE-2026-34486)

On 4 August CISA added three actively-exploited CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a 7 August federal remediation deadline: an authentication-bypass in N-able N-central remote monitoring & management (CVE-2026-18556, affecting versions through 2026.1), an Apache Tomcat cluster-encryption bypass (CVE-2026-34486, CVSS 7.5), and a Langflow AI code-execution flaw (CVE-2026-9198). N-central is the RMM console MSPs and some utilities use to administer endpoints en masse; an auth bypass there hands an attacker management reach into every environment it touches — and it follows the incompletely-patched CVE-2026-18577 already exploited in the wild (added to KEV 3 Aug). Tomcat underpins countless web apps and vendor management consoles on the IT side. For power operators the read-across is the recurring one: the management and web-app tooling that fronts OT is the entry path, not the field controllers.

Regulation & Policy
Info Guidance — CI Fortify

CISA and Australia’s ACSC publish OT-isolation guidance for critical infrastructure — plan to run the grid offline

On 29 July, CISA, Australia’s ACSC and international partners released joint guidance on isolating vital operational-technology and enabling systems, under CISA’s CI Fortify initiative. It tells critical-infrastructure operators to identify the systems that deliver their most vital services, classify and segment them into zones, document every connection to non-critical networks (including vendor and cloud access), and build and rehearse graduated plans to isolate OT and keep operating — potentially for extended periods — if a network is compromised. The guidance speaks directly to utilities and dispatch/scheduling operators, acknowledging that isolating one utility ripples to its peers. Landing in the middle of the active PLC-targeting campaign, it reframes segmentation and manual-operation readiness from best practice to expectation.

Info Regulation — NERC CIP

FERC-approved CIP virtualization standards and INSM monitoring set the grid’s compliance runway

FERC Order 919 approved an updated set of CIP standards (CIP-002-7 through CIP-013-3) covering virtualized environments, effective 26 May 2026 with mandatory compliance by 1 July 2028. Separately, CIP-015-1 (Order 907) requires Internal Network Security Monitoring for applicable control centers by 2 September 2028 and other entities by 2 September 2030. Nothing changes operationally today, but planning for INSM and virtualization compliance — visibility inside the electronic security perimeter — is work that starts now.

Policy — no exploitation FERC / NERC
Compliance milestones — plan now Action: Map current EMS/SCADA virtualization and monitoring against the new CIP requirements, and stand up an INSM plan for control centers ahead of the 2028 deadline.
