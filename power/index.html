Info Policy — Federal incident reporting

117 federal reporting rules across 27 agencies — and the first hours of an incident are when they collide

The McCrary Institute and the US Chamber of Commerce published analysis on 8 September, drawing on Government Accountability Office work, counting 117 separate federal cyber incident reporting regulations spread across 27 agencies, 48 of which apply to private industry. Their argument is operational rather than ideological: duplicative notifications with different thresholds, formats and clocks pull the same small group of people away from containment during the hours when containment is most effective. Electricity operators recognise the shape of this — a single event at a registered entity can trigger E-ISAC reporting, an OE-417 to the Department of Energy, NERC CIP-008 obligations, state utility commission notification, and, for entities with federal contracts or regulated data, more besides. The analysis lands as CIRCIA rules are expected to be finalised this autumn, which is the point at which harmonisation either happens or does not.
