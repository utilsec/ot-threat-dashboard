High Incident — Aviation: air traffic control network failure; FAA says no indication of a cyberattack; root-cause investigation ongoing

The FAA’s nationwide ground stop traced to data flooding its telecom network during a router deployment — the FAA says it has no indication of a cyberattack

The FAA halted departures at every US airport from about 3:30 PM ET on 13 November after its legacy air traffic systems became unstable at 17 of 22 en-route centres and then several approach controls. Its 15 November update says data began flooding the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure network, about 1,000 connection attempts every 4–5 seconds, traced initially to a radar site in Amarillo, Texas, and a radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where provider L3Harris was deploying a new router. The FAA says it has “no indication that this was a cyberattack” and the root-cause investigation continues. The lesson: one network change can stop safety-critical operations nationwide (editorial assessment).
