Critical Advisory — Actively exploited, CISA KEV, historian and MES databases

A seven-year-old SQL Server flaw is now confirmed exploited — and it is the database under your historian

CISA added six vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 26 August, and the one that matters most on the plant floor is CVE-2019-1068, a remote code execution flaw in Microsoft SQL Server patched in July 2019 and only now confirmed as exploited in the wild. Successful exploitation runs code under the SQL Server Database Engine service account; where that account is over-privileged — which is common on systems installed by an integrator years ago — the attacker moves off the database and onto the Windows host. At an airport the exposure sits under the airport operational database and the reporting tier around it — baggage handling event and tracking data, gate and stand allocation, access-control and CCTV management servers, building management historians, and the fuelling and de-icing records that facilities teams keep. On the airline and MRO side it is the maintenance and parts systems. These are commonly SQL Server instances installed by a systems integrator and never revisited. No public write-up of the in-the-wild technique has appeared, so detection guidance is thin; the federal remediation deadline is 29 August. Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-8452 was added in the same batch.

Two unauthenticated flaws in Veeam ONE 13 — the monitoring layer that watches the backups you would restore from

Veeam disclosed vulnerabilities in Veeam ONE 13 in knowledge-base articles published on 25 August. Info Policy — EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting obligation

From 11 September 2026 exploited vulnerabilities in digital products must be reported within 24 hours

The EU Cyber Resilience Act’s reporting obligations begin on 11 September 2026. Manufacturers of products with digital elements placed on the EU market must notify an actively exploited vulnerability in their product, or a severe incident affecting its security, within 24 hours of becoming aware, with fuller reporting to follow. Airports and airlines are mainly buyers here rather than obligated manufacturers, and that is the useful angle: the suppliers of baggage systems, common-use platforms, jet bridges, lighting controls and building management equipment sold into Europe acquire a defined notification duty that can be written into support contracts.
