Citrix NetScaler campaign widens: a third exploited flaw joins CISA KEV with a 7 October deadline, and access planted earlier survives patching

Citrix confirmed on 27 September that two NetScaler ADC and Gateway flaws, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS 9.5 each), were exploited before patches existed, and CISA added both to KEV that day. The first lets an unauthenticated attacker run commands in any configuration, including the default; the second is a DTLS memory overflow reachable on VPN virtual servers. A third exploited flaw, CVE-2026-88779, a memory overflow on appliances configured for SAML, entered KEV on 4 October with a 7 October deadline. Citrix says it observed targeted attacks causing denial of service and no impact on customer data; watchTowr reproduced it as a crash-only bug and suspects it is used to knock appliances over to speed up CVE-2026-88771 exploitation. One researcher reported signs of code execution on a patched honeypot, but watchTowr and Citrix describe a crash. Mandiant’s CTO attributes the campaign to “advanced and suspected state-sponsored” actors without naming a group, and Mandiant counts dozens of victims in government, education, technology, finance, telecom and legal services; Arctic Wolf counts at least 78 organizations and Kevin Beaumont over 100 by his own tracking. No manufacturer has been named. GTIG and LevelBlue describe the WHIPSHOT web shell, a rogue superuser account, theft of the appliance configuration and log tampering, and Mandiant warns that upgrading alone does not remove that access or address stolen credentials. Unit 42 traces activity back to 21 August. NetScaler Gateway commonly fronts contractor, integrator and remote-engineer access into plant DMZs, which is why it leads this page.

Regulation & Policy
Medium Regulation — EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting duties in force

EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting duties took effect on 11 September: manufacturers must report exploited product vulnerabilities within 24 hours

Since 11 September, manufacturers of products with digital elements sold in the EU must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents affecting product security, according to the European Commission. The steps are an early warning within 24 hours, a fuller notification within 72 hours, and a final report no later than 14 days after a fix is available (for vulnerabilities) or one month after the 72-hour notification (for incidents). Reports go once through a single reporting platform run by ENISA, which became operational on 11 September, and the receiving national CSIRT shares them with the others. For machine builders and component makers that put controllers, HMIs, gateways and connected equipment on the EU market, this turns vulnerability handling into a legal deadline; for plant owners, expect supplier notifications on this timetable.
