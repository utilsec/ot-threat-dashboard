Medium Threat Intelligence & Research — Nation-state espionage at an automotive company, IT-side, no OT impact

A North Korea-linked implant lived inside the HAProxy load balancer at a South Korean automotive firm — where TLS ends and EDR never looks

Rapid7 Labs research, reported by Dark Reading on 16 September, describes a previously undocumented Linux espionage toolkit that Rapid7 attributes with medium confidence to a North Korean state actor, possibly APT37, and that was found at two South Korean organisations, one in media and one in automotive manufacturing. The core component, which Rapid7 calls TED, is a malicious plugin built into HAProxy, the open-source load balancer that terminates TLS in front of web applications. From that position it can read decrypted traffic, steal session cookies and credentials, inject scripts into pages and run commands, all while the load balancer keeps doing its job. A companion implant, curlRAT, is a trojanised copy of ordinary system daemons such as crond and sshd that talks to its operators over libcurl. Rapid7 says the tooling generates no unusual processes, no unexpected outbound connections and no log entries of its own, and that it scrubs logs and connection counters selectively. Initial access is not confirmed; Rapid7 judges it was most likely through exposed groupware or mail portals. Nothing published indicates that production systems or control networks were touched, and the stated objective on the automotive side is manufacturing-technology intelligence rather than disruption. The relevance for a plant is architectural: the reverse proxies and load balancers in front of MES portals, vendor-access gateways and historian web front-ends occupy exactly this position, they are typically Linux appliances outside endpoint-detection coverage, and they are trusted precisely because they are boring.
