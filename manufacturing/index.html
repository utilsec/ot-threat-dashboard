Top Story

Siemens ships a maximum-severity, unauthenticated code-execution flaw in its SIMATIC IoT2050 edge gateway — the strongest single advisory of the window

Siemens ProductCERT advisory SSA-834709 discloses CVE-2026-58115, a missing-authentication vulnerability (CWE-306) rated CVSS 10.0, in the Node-RED component bundled with SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced units (6ES7647-0BA00-1YA2) running Industrial OS. Node-RED’s HTTP interface enforces no authentication at all on the affected configuration, so a remote attacker who can reach the device needs no credentials to create new flows and execute arbitrary code with the device’s highest privilege level. Siemens has fixed the issue in firmware V4.3.4.1; until that update is applied, the interim options are removing Node-RED from the device entirely or hardening it according to the Node-RED project’s own security guidance. No exploitation had been reported as of publication, and the flaw is not yet listed in the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue. It matters to manufacturers specifically because IoT2050 is an edge-computing gateway sold into exactly the use case that keeps expanding on plant floors: pulling machine and line data for OEE dashboards, condition monitoring and digital-twin projects, which means it is routinely installed with one interface on the production network and another reaching somewhere far less trusted. Those installs are frequently commissioned by an analytics or engineering initiative rather than through OT procurement, so the device can sit outside the plant’s asset register even though it has a network foot on both sides. This is the single most severe item in this window’s advisory volume — a CVSS 10 from a dominant automation vendor in a widely deployed product class — and it is why a CVE, rather than an incident, leads this page: no manufacturing operator has confirmed an OT-disruptive compromise in this window, so the top driver of the threat level is a critical but unexploited flaw. That distinction also caps how far the level can move; see the Why line for the calibration rule that applies.

High Incident — Nation-state

The Iran-linked campaign against water utilities is exploiting the same Rockwell controller family common on manufacturing lines

Since 26–27 Jul 2026, actors have reached internet-exposed Rockwell Automation / Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 controllers at water and wastewater utilities in at least 12 states, changing device IP addresses and setting unauthorised passwords to lock operators out of monitoring and control; New Jersey and Alabama were the most recent states to confirm impact, as of mid-August reporting. Joint federal advisory AA26-097A assesses the activity to a group of Iranian-affiliated APT actors, naming CyberAv3ngers (also known as the Shahid Kaveh Group, affiliated with the IRGC Cyber Electronic Command) — officials use the word “assess,” not proof, and the newest individual state incidents have not each been separately attributed. No CVE was exploited: the method is exposure plus default, weak or shared credentials, using the manufacturers’ own PLC programming software once inside. Officials say drinking-water safety itself was not compromised. This is a water-sector incident, not a manufacturing one, but manufacturers should not file it under water and move on: MicroLogix 1100/1400 is a general-purpose controller family that shows up on packaging lines, conveyors and OEM-supplied skids across discrete and process plants, frequently commissioned with a routable address by a machine builder for remote support and never revisited by the plant’s own network team.

Active — confirmed OT disruption at water operators, using a controller family common in manufacturing No CVE exploited — internet exposure plus default/weak credentials on Rockwell MicroLogix 1100/1400
No patch applies — exposure removal and credential hygiene per Rockwell SD1790 Action: Run your own exposure sweep for controller protocols across every external address range you own, then ask every machine builder and integrator that supports your equipment how their remote-access connections work, and whether any of it touches a controller with a routable address. Skids and OEM-supported units are where exposed PLCs live in manufacturing, because someone else commissioned the connection.

High Incident — Ransomware

CISA details Gunra ransomware tradecraft the same week Dragos confirms manufacturing is the most-hit industrial sector

CISA, the FBI and international partners published a joint #StopRansomware advisory (AA26-222A) on 10 Aug covering Gunra, a Conti-derived ransomware-as-a-service operation running double-extortion campaigns against government and critical-infrastructure victims. Initial access typically comes through unpatched internet-facing VPN gateways and exposed RDP; the advisory cites Fortinet flaws CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472 as observed entry points, and notes Gunra affiliates give victims a five-to-seven-day payment deadline before publishing stolen data. No manufacturing victim is named in the advisory itself, but it lands in the same window as Dragos’s Q2 finding that manufacturing absorbed 747 of 1,140 industrial ransomware incidents — the fuller data is in the dedicated Threat Intelligence card below. The access pattern the advisory documents — internet-facing remote services and stolen credentials for entry, then encryption of servers carrying business and, indirectly, production systems — matches how Dragos describes most of this quarter’s manufacturing incidents reaching the plant floor: through IT, not through direct control-system manipulation.

Medium Incident — Supply chain

A single contract-logistics breach at CEVA Logistics shows how inbound materials and outbound dispatch can stall without any OT ever being touched

CEVA Logistics disclosed an intrusion dated 29 Jul 2026 that disrupted European contract-logistics operations and stopped outbound shipments from eight warehouses; customers were notified 1 Aug, and the scale became public the week of 10 Aug. Air, ocean, ground and rail transport were unaffected, and CEVA says payment and credential data were not compromised, but personal data — names, addresses, phone numbers, emails and order details — was. Downstream customers named in reporting include Bol, De Bijenkorf, Ace & Tate, Pokémon Center (UK and Germany, with some orders cancelled), Valve/Steam, Ajax and ING; the Dutch data protection authority is investigating, and no group has claimed responsibility. Nothing here is an OT compromise, and CEVA is not itself a manufacturer, but the operational shape is directly relevant to manufacturing: a single third-party logistics provider going IT-dark for days can stall inbound raw-material receipt or outbound finished-goods dispatch on a just-in-time schedule just as effectively as a plant-floor incident would, without anyone ever reaching a controller.

Most manufacturing risk registers do not name a single logistics vendor by criticality — this is the prompt to start.

Critical Advisory — Haiwell

Haiwell’s IoT Cloud HMI gateway has an unauthenticated, root-level command-injection flaw rated CVSS 10

CVE-2026-19188, published 14 Aug 2026 and confirmed at CVSS 10.0 under both v3.1 and v4.0, is an OS command-injection flaw (CWE-78) in the Net Check feature’s cmdPing Socket.io event, reachable via the gateway’s /setting endpoint. It requires no authentication and grants root-level remote code execution. Version 3.40.1.12 is confirmed vulnerable; Haiwell has fixed it in 3.50.1.19 or later. Public reporting could not independently confirm a formal ICSA advisory number for this one, so none is cited here — the CVE record on NVD is the source. Haiwell’s cloud-connected HMI gateways are documented as deployed mainly in energy and water/wastewater environments, but the same product class — a cloud-linked HMI gateway sitting between plant-floor devices and remote monitoring — is common in manufacturing visualization and remote-support setups, so the exposure is worth checking even where Haiwell is not a named vendor on your asset list.

No confirmed exploitation reported at publication Not in CISA KEV as of this edition
Available — upgrade to firmware 3.50.1.19 or later Action: Inventory any Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI gateways on your network, and more broadly any cloud-linked HMI or gateway product that bridges plant-floor visualization to remote access — that architecture pattern is what this flaw exploits, regardless of vendor. Upgrade past 3.40.1.12 immediately where found, and take the /setting management interface off any internet-reachable path in the meantime.

Critical Advisory — Johnson Controls

Johnson Controls patches two unauthenticated deserialization flaws that could hand over a facility’s physical access control

Johnson Controls updated its C-CURE 9000 / victor advisory (Update A, 11 Aug) covering CVE-2026-21655 and CVE-2026-21653 — two critical, unauthenticated deserialization vulnerabilities — alongside CVE-2026-34496, a high-severity issue, with CVSS scores reaching 9.6. A network-adjacent attacker exploiting the deserialization flaws could execute arbitrary code and take over the access-control system outright, no credentials required. C-CURE 9000 and victor are widely deployed physical access-control and video-management platforms, including at manufacturing sites, where they run badge readers, gates and building-access logging. A takeover is not a production-line compromise, but it is a physical-security and safety issue: an attacker could unlock restricted areas, disable access logging, or otherwise defeat the controls a plant relies on to keep people out of hazardous or high-value areas.

High Advisory — Rockwell

Rockwell updates its MicroLogix hardening guidance and warns that changing the password alone will not stop the campaign

Rockwell Automation updated advisory SD1790 on 10 Aug with recovery and hardening steps for MicroLogix 1400 and 1100 controllers, after actors were found remotely changing device IP addresses and setting unauthorised passwords on internet-exposed units during the ongoing water-sector campaign. Rockwell explicitly warns that resetting the password by itself is not sufficient mitigation — the guidance calls for removing the controller from the internet, reviewing all remote-access paths, and validating logic and configuration against a known-good backup. MicroLogix 1400/1100 is not water-specific hardware: it is one of the most widely deployed small-PLC families in industrial history, and manufacturing plants run it on packaging equipment, conveyors, and OEM-built skids as often as water utilities run it on pump stations. Forescout’s exposure count (see the Threat Intelligence section) makes the same point from a different angle — this is a shared exposure problem across sectors, not one confined to water.

High Advisory — CISA KEV

A Cisco ASA/FTD flaw exploited to crash firewalls enters CISA KEV with a three-day federal deadline

Cisco disclosed and patched CVE-2026-20349 (CVSS 8.6), a heap-inspection denial-of-service flaw in Secure Firewall ASA and Firepower Threat Defense software, reachable by a remote, unauthenticated attacker sending a crafted HTTP request to the Remote Access SSL VPN service. Cisco confirmed active exploitation in the wild, and CISA added the CVE to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 11 Aug with a federal remediation deadline of 14 Aug. Hotfixes are available across all affected 9.x, 7.x and 10.x branches; there is no workaround short of patching. ASA and FTD appliances are commonly deployed as the IT/OT boundary device in manufacturing environments, or to carry OEM remote-support VPN tunnels into the plant — a forced reload does not expose data by itself, but it interrupts the segmentation and monitoring the device is there to enforce, and creates a visibility gap while it recovers.

Critical Advisory — CISA

CISA’s 15-advisory day covers Siemens video management, license servers and CAD tools alongside four other manufacturing-relevant vendors

CISA published 15 ICS advisories in a single day on 13 Aug, spanning Siemens, AVEVA, Hitachi Energy, ANDRITZ, Schneider Electric and Phoenix Contact. The most severe is Siemens Siveillance Video (CVE-2026-3014, CVSS 9.1), an OS command-injection flaw in the video-management server that lets a user with edit permissions run arbitrary code in the VMS service; a patch is available. Siemens also fixed two License Server issues — CVE-2026-69109, an insecure sudoers policy enabling local privilege escalation, and CVE-2026-69108, a path-traversal bug allowing remote arbitrary file reads, both around CVSS 7.5 — and CVE-2026-64629 (CVSS 7.8), an out-of-bounds read in Parasolid, Simcenter Femap and Solid Edge triggered by a malicious X_T CAD file, fixed in Parasolid V38.0.235/V38.1.230 and later. ANDRITZ HIPASE-250 and 250 SCALA carry four vulnerabilities, including a fixed VNC password baked into the provisioning script and an unauthenticated, permissively configured API exposing live process data; a patch is available but no CVSS could be verified. Phoenix Contact PLCnext (advisory VDE-2025-056) has multiple input-validation flaws that an unauthenticated attacker could use to cause denial of service, unexpected controller behaviour or malicious SQL execution; patched, no verified CVE/CVSS. Schneider Electric shipped fixes for NetBotz 5 (750/755) — command injection and SQL injection in versions 5.5.2 and earlier — and for PowerChute Serial Shutdown’s authentication-attempt handling; both are environmental/power-monitoring appliances with patches available and no verified CVSS.

High Research — Ransomware

Dragos: manufacturing absorbed nearly two-thirds of all industrial ransomware incidents in Q2, and none involved direct control-system manipulation

Dragos’s Q2 2026 Industrial Ransomware Analysis, published 10 Aug, counted 1,140 ransomware incidents against industrial organisations, up 12% on Q1. Manufacturing took 747 of those 1,140 incidents — 65% of the total, more than every other industrial vertical combined — with food & beverage a distant second at 70, and transportation & logistics at 95 overall (maritime 8, aviation 6, rail 3). Qilin, Akira, The Gentlemen, DragonForce and LockBit 5.0 were the most active groups. The analytically critical finding is what did not happen: Dragos found that most intrusions cascaded into OT disruption through IT-layer encryption, not through direct manipulation of control systems, and observed no Stage 2 ICS Kill Chain activity in the quarter’s manufacturing cases. Dragos names West Pharmaceutical Services and Foxconn as examples — both from May 2026, outside this reporting window, cited here as illustrative prior incidents rather than new activity. The operational reading for a plant is that a production line can stop completely without an attacker ever touching a controller: the report’s pattern is that ransomware hits the ERP instance releasing work orders, the historian’s hypervisor, or the MES database, and the line stops because it depends on one of those, not because anyone reached the PLC.

Active — 747 confirmed manufacturing ransomware incidents in Q2 2026 alone Not a single-CVE finding — aggregate ransomware-incident analysis
Not applicable — the finding is about IT/OT dependency, not a patchable flaw Action: Take one production line and map exactly what it needs from outside the cell to run a shift — work orders, recipes, label and barcode data, quality release, dispatch paperwork, licence checks. For each, note the offline fallback and how long it holds, then actually test it: disconnect that line from the plant network for one shift with the fallback in place, rather than running it as a tabletop exercise.

High Research — Exposure

Forescout counts thousands of exposed Rockwell controllers worldwide, including the legacy models used in the water attacks

Forescout’s Vedere Labs found 4,407 internet-exposed Rockwell Automation / Allen-Bradley controllers worldwide, 2,844 of them in the US, dominated by discontinued legacy MicroLogix 1400 and 1100 units — the exact controller family targeted in the ongoing water-sector campaign, with 22 of the exposed devices sitting in cities already confirmed hit. Forescout’s researchers note that the intruders reconfigured devices directly rather than exploiting any new software flaw, reinforcing that this is an exposure and credential problem, not a patch problem. MicroLogix has been sold into manufacturing for decades as a general-purpose small-PLC platform, well beyond water and wastewater applications, which means a meaningful share of this exposed population is very likely sitting on manufacturing equipment that has never been assessed for internet reachability, commissioned by an OEM or system integrator years ago and left unreviewed since.

Info Policy — Water & wastewater

The legislative and funding response to the water attacks is a preview of what regulators could reach for in manufacturing next

In direct response to the water-utility campaign, Sens. Klobuchar and Schiff introduced the Water Cyber Shield Act on 13 Aug, which would give EPA authority to require mandatory cybersecurity assessments, fund $300 million a year through state revolving funds, and add a cyber-risk requirement to resilience planning for large water systems. Separately, New York awarded up to $9 million in SECURE grants to 153 drinking-water and wastewater systems — up to $50,000 per system for assessments and $100,000 for improvements — following the attack wave and the state’s March cybersecurity mandates, and a new Water Watch Center has launched to help small, resource-constrained utilities detect and respond to intrusions. None of this creates a direct obligation for manufacturers. But the shape of the response — mandatory assessments tied to federal or state funding, and a dedicated sector support centre for organisations too small to run their own security programme — is the template regulators have reached for after other critical-infrastructure sectors had a bad enough incident, and manufacturing currently has no equivalent despite carrying the largest ransomware share of any industrial vertical this quarter.
