Clop’s PLM extortion campaign has named 43 victims including Shell, GE and Philips — the stolen material is engineering data

Clop has begun naming victims of its campaign against internet-exposed PTC Windchill and FlexPLM instances, with reporting on 15 August putting the count at 43 and including Shell, GE and Philips. The entry point is CVE-2026-12569, an improper input-validation flaw allowing unauthenticated remote code execution, which Clop affiliates exploited as a zero-day in early June before PTC patched on 18 June. CISA added it to the KEV catalogue on 25 June — the first PTC product to be listed. Attackers drop JSP web shells for persistence, then stage and exfiltrate. What makes this a manufacturing story rather than a data-breach story is the content: Windchill and FlexPLM hold product designs, CAD files, bills of materials and supplier data for aerospace, automotive, heavy machinery and medtech firms.

Then hunt rather than assume: look for JSP web shells in web application directories, unexpected outbound transfers of large archives, and new service accounts created since early June.

High Incident — Mass exploitation

A suspected China-nexus actor mass-exploited a patched vCenter flaw and deployed Babuk-derived ransomware

Exploitation of CVE-2026-59310, a directory-traversal flaw in Broadcom VMware vCenter rated CVSS 9.8 and patched on 29 July, has been attributed to a suspected China-nexus actor. German incident-response firm QUIRSO tracked roughly 361 victim IP addresses across 47 countries, with 343 appearing between 3 and 5 August, and assesses with moderate confidence that a Chinese-speaking operator is behind it. The chain persists through cron, systemd and reverse_ssh, drops a Linux backdoor and in some cases deploys Babuk-derived ransomware. For manufacturers the exposure is concentrated: MES, historians, engineering workstations, licence servers and OT DMZ services are routinely consolidated onto one VMware cluster, and Dragos’ Q2 data shows precautionary hypervisor shutdowns are already a leading cause of production disruption.

Then document which production-critical virtual machines share a cluster with enterprise workloads, because that list is your shutdown blast radius.

High Incident — Supply chain

A breach at Ceva Logistics halted shipments from eight European warehouses and rippled into customers

A cyber intrusion disrupted part of Ceva Logistics’ European contract-logistics operation from 29 July, halting shipments at eight warehouses and delaying deliveries for customers whose inventory sat there. Customers were notified on 1 August. Personal data including names, addresses, phone numbers, emails and order details was exposed; Ceva says payment and credential data was not. Dutch retailers Bol and De Bijenkorf, ING, Ace & Tate, Ajax and Valve have been reported as affected downstream. The Dutch Data Protection Authority is investigating and no group has claimed responsibility. The manufacturing lesson is upstream: a contract logistics provider is a single point of failure for inbound components as much as outbound finished goods, and you will usually learn about their incident from a customer, not from them.

Add a supplier-outage branch to your continuity plan that assumes you find out late and have to source manually.

High Research — Ransomware

Manufacturing took 747 of 1,140 industrial ransomware incidents in Q2 — 65 percent of the total

Dragos recorded 1,140 ransomware incidents at industrial organisations in the second quarter of 2026, up 12 percent from 1,020 in Q1. Manufacturing absorbed 747 of them, 65 percent of the total; organisations that support ICS environments took 117 and transportation and logistics 95. North America accounted for 514. Qilin remained the most active brand, a position it has held since March 2025. The finding worth internalising is that Dragos observed no ransomware operator directly manipulating a control system during the quarter: production stopped because enterprise IT was encrypted, or because operators pre-emptively shut down virtualisation and shared infrastructure. Your line-down risk is therefore mostly a question of IT dependency and shutdown policy rather than PLC security.

High Threat Intelligence — Ransomware

Gunra ransomware is walking in through unpatched VPNs and firewalls, with manufacturing named in scope

The FBI, CISA, DoD Cyber Crime Center, NSA, US Secret Service and the Republic of Korea’s National Police Agency issued #StopRansomware advisory AA26-222A on Gunra, a Conti-derived double-extortion operation that moved to a structured affiliate model in early 2026. Manufacturing sits at the top of its named victim base alongside healthcare, finance, transport, government and utilities. Initial access comes from exposed edge infrastructure — VPN gateways, firewall appliances and RDP — including the Fortinet FortiOS and FortiProxy authentication-bypass flaws CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472. Nothing about the tradecraft is advanced. Read next to the Dragos numbers, it describes the most probable path to a stopped line this quarter: a known-vulnerable appliance, then enterprise encryption, then a precautionary shutdown.

High Research — Process refrigeration

Claroty found 23 flaws in Copeland refrigeration controllers — and showed the display can lie while cooling stops

Claroty’s Team82 disclosed 23 vulnerabilities in the Copeland XWEB Pro supervisory controller platform, 21 of them rated high, affecting the XWEB300D PRO and XWEB500D PRO families. Chained together, authentication bypass, predictable administrator credentials and command injection give an unauthenticated attacker root-level remote code execution. CVE-2026-21718 covers a deterministic password generator that derives administrator SSH and web credentials from the date, the unit’s MAC address and hard-coded firmware material. In a proof of concept the researchers turned off cooling while continuing to display the legitimate temperature. Copeland has released firmware 1.13; Claroty also reported issues to Danfoss, which shipped R4.3.1. Any plant with process cooling, chilled storage or environmental control should treat this as in scope.

No known exploitation reported at generation time Not in CISA KEV
Patches available — Copeland firmware 1.13, Danfoss AK-SM 800A R4.3.1 Action: Locate XWEB Pro and Danfoss AK-SM units across process cooling, chilled storage and utilities, and update firmware. Because the attack demonstrated is a silent one, add an independent temperature check — a separate sensor or a manual round — that does not read from the same controller as the display.

Critical Advisory — HMI gateway

Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway has an unauthenticated root command-injection flaw rated CVSS 10.0

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an operating-system command-injection vulnerability in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway rated CVSS 10.0. The Net Check feature reachable through the /setting endpoint passes unsanitised input to the cmdPing Socket.io event, allowing arbitrary commands to run as root. CISA names Critical Manufacturing first among the affected sectors, alongside Energy and Water and Wastewater, and Haiwell has released fixed firmware. Gateways in this class arrive with machines rather than through procurement: an OEM or system integrator fits one so the machine can be supported remotely, and it never enters the plant asset register. No public exploitation has been reported to CISA.

No known public exploitation reported to CISA at generation time Not in CISA KEV
Patch available — Haiwell Scada-v3.50.1.19 Action: Ask your machine builders and integrators directly whether Haiwell gateways are installed on any line, because they will not show up in your own records. Remove internet reachability today and apply the vendor firmware, then take the opportunity to inventory every OEM-supplied remote-support device on the floor.

High Advisory — Physical security

A critical deserialisation flaw in Johnson Controls C•CURE 9000 could hand over plant access control

CISA advisory ICSA-26-204-01, since revised as Update A, covers CVE-2026-21655, a deserialisation vulnerability in the Johnson Controls C•CURE 9000 and victor application servers rated CVSS v3 8.8 and v4 8.7. An unauthenticated attacker on an adjacent network can achieve arbitrary code execution on the application server and on connected clients, including the workstations used by security staff, which would put physical access controls at risk. The flaw sits in a deserialisation path on TCP port 8999. Johnson Controls has published version 3.20. Manufacturers should note that badge systems commonly share network segments with plant systems and are administered by facilities teams outside the OT patching process, and that access control is what keeps people out of hazardous areas.

No known exploitation reported at generation time Not in CISA KEV
Patch available — upgrade to C•CURE 9000 / victor 3.20 or later Action: Upgrade C•CURE 9000 and victor application servers to 3.20 or later, and restrict inbound access to TCP 8999 with both network and Windows firewalls so only required hosts can reach it. Add physical access control and video systems to your OT asset register if they are not already there.

Info Regulation — EU

EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting duties start in September 2026 — and manufacturers are on both sides of it

From September 2026, manufacturers covered by the EU Cyber Resilience Act must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and serious security incidents in their products. Industrial firms sit on both sides of this: as buyers you should get faster, more consistent disclosure from equipment and software vendors, and as makers of connected products you may be in scope yourself. It layers on NIS2, under which manufacturing of certain products is an important entity, and the NIS2 Cooperation Group adopted common incident-reporting templates on 26 May 2026. The practical question for most plants is whether anyone has determined which of the company’s own products carry digital elements that bring them into CRA scope — that assessment is usually nobody’s explicit job.
