Top Story

FBI warns attackers are hijacking internet-facing PLCs — the same controllers on your plant floor

A confirmed, active campaign is remotely tampering with internet-facing programmable logic controllers, and the FBI’s warning lands squarely on the plant floor. In a 30 July public service announcement, the FBI and EPA said that since 27 July, water and wastewater utilities in at least seven states reported attackers reaching internet-exposed Rockwell/Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 PLCs, changing their IP addresses and passwords to knock out monitoring and control — and in at least one case altering ladder logic across several sites. Reported effects included loss of pressure and flooding. The victims are water utilities, but the FBI stressed the same exposure applies to other branded PLCs — the internet-reachable Rockwell, Siemens and Schneider controllers manufacturers run every day. Any plant with a PLC or cellular modem reachable from the internet should treat this as a direct warning.

Active — confirmed OT disruption across seven states FBI / EPA Public Service Announcement (30 Jul)
No single patch — remove PLCs from internet, key-switch to RUN, verify logic Action: Pull every PLC and cellular field modem off the public internet behind a secure gateway/jump host; set physical and soft key switches to RUN to block logic and firmware changes; enforce strong, unique device passwords and ACLs limiting comms to expected control devices; and validate PLC project files and ladder logic against known-good baselines.
Suggested SLA: Internet-exposure sweep — 72h; logic-integrity & password/ACL review — 7 days Owner:

Iran-affiliated actors are rewriting plant-floor PLC logic and disabling safety functions

The updated federal advisory AA26-097A (22 July) confirms Iran-affiliated actors reaching internet-exposed PLCs — Rockwell CompactLogix and Micro850, Siemens S7-1200, Schneider Modicon M340 — extracting project files and modifying or deleting control logic, including Add-On Instructions, and manipulating HMI/SCADA displays. For manufacturers that is the nightmare case: an engineering workstation or exposed controller becomes a path to silently altered process logic and defeated interlocks.

Coca-Cola’s Fairlife restarts US production after ransomware halt; data theft confirmed

Coca-Cola’s Fairlife subsidiary has resumed most production at its four US plants after a roughly ten-day shutdown triggered by a 16 July ransomware attack — a reminder that on the plant floor an IT compromise fast becomes an operations problem. Manufacturing — and food and beverage especially, with just-in-time perishable output — offers attackers exactly the operational leverage that drives a fast payout.

Medium Threat Intel — Ransomware

Manufacturing stays the #1 ransomware target — and attackers now hit the backups first

Manufacturing remains the most-targeted industrial sector for ransomware in 2026, and Sophos’ latest State of Ransomware finds the vast majority of attacks now deliberately target backups — turning the last line of defense into a primary objective and driving recovery costs sharply higher when backups are compromised. Manufacturing’s recovery problem is structural: OT and control-system configurations rarely restore from IT-style backups, so an IT-to-OT ransomware event can idle production lines for weeks. RaaS crews such as Qilin, Akira and SafePay run the same double-extortion playbook — valid credentials, exposed remote access, and data theft before encryption.

Trend data — active ransomware ecosystem Sophos / Industrial Cyber / Dragos
No single patch — credentials, segmentation, protected backups Action: Prioritize the controls that break the common ransomware path: MFA everywhere, no internet-exposed RDP/remote access, segmented OT with monitored crossings, and offline, immutable, tested backups for production and control-system configurations.

High Active Exploitation — KEV

N-able N-central auth-bypass (CVE-2026-18577) exploited to reach managed endpoints — CISA KEV

N-able confirmed that CVE-2026-18577, an authentication-bypass in its widely used N-central RMM platform, has been exploited in the wild since 1 August; CISA added it to the KEV catalog on 3 August. The flaw (CVSS 8.2) lets an unauthenticated attacker take administrative control of an on-prem N-central server, and it is an incomplete-fix bypass of an earlier patch (CVE-2026-18556). After takeover, attackers abused N-central’s own “Take Control” remote-access feature to reach managed endpoints and dropped Cloudflare Tunnel for persistence. Manufacturers and the MSPs that support them routinely manage engineering workstations, MES/ERP hosts and shop-floor PCs through RMM tooling like this, so a compromised N-central is a ready-made pivot from IT onto the production network.

Active — exploited in the wild since 1 Aug CISA KEV — added 3 Aug 2026
Fixed in 2026.3.1 Hotfix 1 — patch now Action: If you or your MSP run on-prem N-able N-central, update to 2026.3.1 Hotfix 1 (2026.3.1.7) immediately and remove the console from direct internet exposure; review Take Control sessions and hunt for cloudflared/Cloudflare Tunnel persistence; rotate admin credentials and confirm no RMM path reaches engineering workstations or the OT cell. Require written patch confirmation from managed-service providers.
Suggested SLA: Patch & exposure — 24-72h; MSP confirmation — 72h Owner:

High Active Exploitation — KEV

Cisco Secure FMC has hard-coded credentials — exploited as a zero-day, now in CISA KEV

CISA added CVE-2026-20316 to its KEV catalog on 29 July after Cisco warned the flaw — hard-coded static credentials for a low-privileged account in the Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC) web interface — was exploited as a zero-day. An unauthenticated remote attacker can log in with the built-in credentials and read sensitive data; Cisco rates the impact high because it can be chained with other FMC bugs to escalate. FMC manages the firewalls that often enforce IT/OT segmentation, so a compromised management plane is a direct threat to the boundary protecting the plant floor. Base CVSS is 5.3, but active exploitation makes patching urgent.

Active — exploited as a zero-day CISA KEV — added 29 Jul 2026
Cisco fixes available — patch now Action: Inventory FMC instances, apply Cisco’s fixed release immediately, remove the management interface from any untrusted network, review authentication logs for the static account, and confirm firewall segmentation between IT and OT is intact.

Fresh CISA advisory: DoS flaw in Rockwell CompactLogix / ControlLogix comms modules

In its 30 July ICS advisory batch, CISA flagged a vulnerability (ICSA-26-211-05) in Rockwell Automation’s CompactLogix 5380, ControlLogix 5580 and 1756-EN4TR communication modules that a network attacker could use to force a denial-of-service condition, knocking a controller or its EtherNet/IP comms offline. These controllers sit at the heart of discrete and process lines, so an availability bug there translates directly to lost production or a tripped process. No public exploitation is reported; apply Rockwell’s fixes and keep these modules on a segmented, monitored control network.
