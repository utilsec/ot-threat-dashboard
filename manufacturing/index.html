Top Story

Clop wrote a web shell just for PTC Windchill — and it hides inside the application’s own identity

ReliaQuest has published analysis of the Java/JSP web shell Clop deployed in its PTC Windchill and FlexPLM data-theft campaign, and it is not an off-the-shelf implant. It is written against Windchill’s internal APIs, database schema, keystore and file-vault layout, and it does four things: decrypt stored credentials, enumerate repositories, retrieve files in bulk, and execute arbitrary Java. The detection problem is the part worth taking to your SOC — because the implant runs under the application’s own service identity, database telemetry can read the theft as ordinary application behaviour. There is no encryption payload and no observed reach into plant-floor systems. The campaign now stands at 43-plus named victims, including Shell (Clop claims about 89 GB), GE, and Philips, which says the intrusion was contained with no customer-environment impact. The flaw is CVE-2026-12569; PTC began patching on 17 June and CISA added it to KEV on 25 June, but exploitation is assessed to have started weeks earlier.

Patch available — PTC Windchill and FlexPLM updates released from 17 Jun 2026 Action: Confirm no Windchill, FlexPLM or comparable PLM instance answers from the internet, then hunt rather than assume. High Incident — Nation-state / PLCs

The Iranian PLC campaign now covers Siemens and Schneider controllers — and at one victim the safety logic was disabled

Joint advisory AA26-097A, issued 7 April by the FBI, CISA, NSA, EPA, DOE, US Cyber Command’s Cyber National Mission Force and Treasury and expanded on 22 July, describes Iranian-affiliated actors reaching internet-exposed programmable logic controllers across US critical infrastructure. Two points matter on a plant floor. The observed manufacturer scope has widened from Rockwell Automation to Schneider Electric and Siemens, which covers most of the installed base in discrete and process manufacturing. And at one confirmed US victim the actors modified ladder logic to disable safety shutdown and alarm functions, allowing unsafe conditions to develop without alerting operators — a different and more serious outcome than lockout. The agencies also documented project-file exfiltration and Dropbear SSH persistence. No CVE is involved; exposure and weak credentials are the way in.

Confirmed active campaign — federal assessment of Iranian-affiliated (IRGC-CEC) actors No CVE exploited — internet-exposed controllers with weak or default credentials
No patch — exposure removal, credential hygiene and logic-integrity verification Action: Diff the current logic on every controller that implements an interlock, permissive or shutdown against your last known-good baseline, with particular attention to add-on instructions and reusable routines. Then check whether any controller, gateway or cellular router at a remote or leased site answers from the internet.

High Incident — Cross-sector

Attackers crossed between two unrelated operators over a private cellular APN and stopped a turbine

CERT Polska has disclosed that attackers reached the control network of a Polish combined heat and power plant serving about 50,000 residents, stopping a steam turbine and the plant’s process-water treatment system. The path in was neither the internet nor the corporate network: a compromised FortiGate firewall at an unrelated wind farm, then a Teltonika cellular router that reached a private APN operated by the distribution system operator, on which devices could reach one another. CERT Polska believes this is the first observed real-world use of a private APN as a route into an industrial control network. Manufacturers should read it as a warning about shared bearers generally — the private APNs and machine-builder cellular links used for remote diagnostics on packaging, robotics and utility skids follow the same pattern.

Confirmed OT-disruptive incident — turbine and process-water treatment stopped No new CVE — compromised edge firewall plus a flat private cellular network
No patch — architectural: segment the APN, treat carrier networks as untrusted Action: List every cellular router and machine-builder remote-access box on the plant floor, including those the OEM installed and still manages. Ask the carrier whether devices on your APN can reach each other, and put a firewall with an explicit allow-list between each machine and the wider control network.

High Threat Intel — Ransomware

CISA and the FBI now count more than 500 Medusa victims — and the group weaponises new flaws inside a day

The #StopRansomware advisory on Medusa, AA25-071A, was refreshed on 18 August. The cumulative victim tally passed 500 critical-infrastructure organisations as of April 2026, up from roughly 300 in the March 2025 version — about 200 new victims in a year. Critical manufacturing is named among the core target sectors, alongside healthcare, the defense industrial base, government facilities, IT and financial services. The operationally load-bearing finding is speed: Medusa has been observed weaponising newly disclosed vulnerabilities within roughly 24 hours, and in some cases using exploits up to a week before public disclosure, because it buys capability rather than developing it. Impact is enterprise-IT encryption plus data theft; no control-system involvement is alleged. For a plant that means the patch window normally negotiated with production is, against this actor, already closed.

Confirmed active campaign — 500+ critical-infrastructure victims counted by CISA and the FBI No single CVE — exploits newly disclosed flaws, occasionally pre-disclosure

Then run the advisory’s indicators and detection content against your estate, and check that plant-floor recovery does not depend on a domain that the same intrusion would encrypt.

High Research — Ransomware trends

Manufacturing took 747 of 1,140 industrial ransomware incidents in Q2 — 65 percent of the total

Dragos recorded 1,140 ransomware incidents against industrial organisations in the second quarter of 2026, up 12 percent from 1,020 in the first. Manufacturing absorbed 747 of them, about 65 percent. Construction accounted for 176, the engineering firms and system integrators that support control environments for 117, equipment manufacturing for 114 and food and beverage for 70. The United States alone recorded 431, 38 percent of the global total, and the most active claimants were Qilin with 140, Akira with 129 and The Gentlemen with 125. The finding worth carrying into a plant meeting is the negative one: across all 1,140 incidents Dragos identified zero cases in which an operator reached Stage 2 of the ICS Cyber Kill Chain, and zero cases of direct control-system manipulation. Where production stopped, it stopped because virtualisation or ERP was encrypted — not because anyone touched a controller.

High Threat Intel — Virtualisation

CISA added the exploited vCenter flaw to KEV yesterday — 361 hosts compromised across 47 countries

CISA added CVE-2026-59310, a path-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter Server, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August. Broadcom patched it on 29 July; exploitation began about five days later. German incident-response firm QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence that a Chinese-speaking actor operates the campaign, counting roughly 361 affected addresses across 47 countries, with Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran and France most represented. The actor gains root on the vCenter appliance, plants a backdoor via cron and a reverse SSH binary, and in at least one case deployed Babuk-derived ransomware. In most plants the historian, MES servers, SCADA nodes and engineering workstation images all live on one vSphere cluster — which makes the management plane a single point of compromise for the whole OT estate.

Critical Advisory — Siemens

SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced exposes Node-RED with no authentication — maximum-severity remote code execution

Siemens ProductCERT advisory SSA-834709 covers CVE-2026-58115, rated CVSS 10.0 under both v3.1 and v4.0, in SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices running Industrial OS with Node-RED installed. The Node-RED HTTP interface enforces no authentication at all, so any remote unauthenticated attacker can create flows using programming nodes that execute system commands, giving arbitrary code execution at maximum privilege on the gateway. All versions before V4.3.4.1 are affected. Two things make it easy to miss: there is no corresponding CISA ICS advisory, so estates that track ICS-CERT rather than vendor PSIRT feeds will not see it; and IoT2050 gateways typically arrive in manufacturing as edge collectors and protocol converters feeding an OEE, historian or cloud analytics platform — devices the control network trusts, commissioned during a digitalisation project and then left out of the patch cycle entirely.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV — and no corresponding CISA ICS advisory published
Patch available — update to V4.3.4.1, or uninstall Node-RED where it is not required Action: Find every IoT2050 on site, check whether Node-RED is installed, and restrict its HTTP interface to a dedicated management host. Update to the fixed release; if a line stoppage is needed first, block the interface at the network layer today. Ask your OEE or analytics integrator which gateways they installed — they will not all be in your asset register.

Critical Advisory — HMI gateway

A CVSS 10.0 command injection gives unauthenticated root on Haiwell’s IoT Cloud HMI gateway

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an OS command injection in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway rated 10.0. The Net Check feature reached through the /setting endpoint passes user input from the cmdPing Socket.io event to the operating system unsanitised, letting an unauthenticated remote attacker execute commands as root. Version 3.40.1.12 is affected; Haiwell has published Scada-v3.50.1.19. Low-cost HMI gateways of this type reach plants inside packaged machinery — a filler, a palletiser, a compressor skid, a chiller — specified by the machine builder rather than by your controls standard, which is why they rarely appear in an asset inventory until someone scans for them.

High Advisory — Remote access

A Windows IKE flaw entered KEV on 18 August with a three-day deadline — and IKE is how vendors reach your plant

CISA added CVE-2026-33824, a double free in the Microsoft Windows IKE extensions service (IKEEXT) that allows remote code execution, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August with a federal remediation deadline of 21 August. A three-day deadline is a signal in itself; CISA reserves that compression for exploitation it assesses as widespread. The manufacturing relevance is the protocol rather than the product. IKE and IPsec terminate the site-to-site tunnels and vendor remote-access links that machine builders, systems integrators and corporate support use to reach plant networks, so a host running this service usually sits on a boundary that matters. Three further flaws were added the same day, including the VMware vCenter path traversal covered elsewhere on this page.

Confirmed active exploitation — the basis for the KEV listing In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-33824 added 18 Aug 2026, remediation due 21 Aug
Patch available — apply Microsoft’s fix Action: Identify the Windows hosts running the IKE and AuthIP IPsec Keying Modules service that terminate site-to-site or vendor tunnels into plant networks and patch them this week. Where the service is running but no IPsec tunnel actually depends on it — common on engineering workstations and jump hosts — disable it. Then review VPN authentication logs for sessions outside an approved maintenance window.

Medium Advisory — OT monitoring

Six flaws in CISA’s own Malcolm OT monitoring suite, including an 8.8 upload-to-code-execution

ICSA-26-230-01, published 18 August, discloses six vulnerabilities in Malcolm, CISA’s free open-source network traffic analysis suite for OT. The most serious is CVE-2026-55676 at CVSS 8.8, an unrestricted file upload that allows PHP to be uploaded and executed. A compromised sensor is a map of the environment and a foothold in it. No exploitation is reported.

Low Policy — National strategy

The new National Security Science and Technology Strategy names OT and ICS resilience as a priority

The National Security Science and Technology Strategy, released on 19 August, sets out four pillars and names operational technology and industrial control system resilience, together with technology supply chains, as explicit priorities. For manufacturers the supply-chain framing has the longer reach: it covers the security of the components, software and equipment that arrive on the plant floor, not only the operator’s own network. Nothing changes for an operator today — this is a strategy document rather than a rule, and no reporting obligation or standard follows from it. It is a reasonable indicator of where federal attention is heading, and that has historically preceded procurement conditions and sector guidance.
