Top Story

Manufacturing took 747 of 1,140 industrial ransomware hits last quarter — and not one attacker touched a PLC

Dragos counted 1,140 ransomware incidents at industrial organizations in the second quarter, up 12% from 1,020 in Q1, and manufacturing absorbed 747 of them — 65% of the total. Construction added 176 and equipment manufacturing 114; ICS-adjacent firms including engineering companies and system integrators accounted for 117, and food and beverage 70. North America led with 514 incidents, Europe 316. The finding that should shape plant planning is a negative one: Dragos observed no case during the quarter in which a ransomware operator directly manipulated an industrial control system. Production stopped anyway. The damage ran through IT, ERP and virtualization — scheduling, material movement, order execution and the hypervisors hosting MES and historian servers. For most plants the realistic scenario is not encrypted PLCs but an ERP that cannot tell the line what to build.

High Campaign — Nation-State

Federal advisory: attackers are using TIA Portal, Studio 5000 and EcoStruxure to rewrite PLC logic

The 22 July update to joint advisory AA26-097A describes exactly the toolchain a controls engineer uses. Iran-affiliated actors connect to internet-exposed controllers with the vendors’ own software — Rockwell Studio 5000 Logix Designer, Schneider EcoStruxure Control Expert and Siemens TIA Portal — over ports 44818, 2222, 102, 502 and 22, from leased third-party infrastructure. They extract and exfiltrate project files, then modify and delete logic. In one US case the FBI found a downloaded project file that preserved ladder logic for downstream function while adding logic overriding the instruction sets that maintained safe operating parameters, and disabling shutdown and alarm logic so equipment could enter unsafe states silently. Named targets now include Rockwell CompactLogix and Micro850, Schneider Modicon M340 (BMX P34) and Siemens S7-1200 — the standard fleet on a discrete or process line.

Active — confirmed hands-on manipulation of control logic; manufacturing gear targeted CISA/FBI/NSA/EPA joint advisory AA26-097A — refreshed IOCs and detection guidance
No single patch — segmentation, credentials and logic integrity Action: Confirm no controller in the named families answers from outside the plant, including via a machine-builder’s cellular router; move engineering software onto hardened workstations with named accounts, MFA and allowlisting, and stop sharing the ‘engineering’ login; take a current export of every project file and diff it against your last known-good, looking specifically for added add-on instructions and removed interlock, trip or alarm rungs; make that diff a recurring maintenance task.
Suggested SLA: Exposure and engineering-workstation review — 7 days; project-file diff on safety-relevant lines — 14 days Owner:

High Incident — Ransomware

Coca-Cola halted US production at fairlife after ransomware reached production-related systems

Coca-Cola disclosed on 16 July that its fairlife dairy subsidiary identified unauthorized third-party access to part of its systems, including production-related systems, in connection with a ransomware event, and that production operations at fairlife in the United States were temporarily suspended as a result. Reporting on the incident centred on the ERP layer carrying scheduling, material movement and order execution — the same dependency Dragos identifies as the main disruption path across the sector. It is the cleanest recent example of a plant losing output without an attacker ever addressing a controller, and it happened at an operator with resources far beyond a typical mid-market manufacturer.

Confirmed — company-disclosed ransomware event with temporary production suspension No CVE — company disclosure; access method not published
No patch — ERP/MES segmentation, backup integrity and degraded-mode operations Action: Map exactly which ERP and MES functions must be available for a line to run and for how long it can run without them; hold a printed or offline copy of current recipes, work orders and label data for at least one shift; verify ERP and MES backups are immutable and restore-tested, not just scheduled; segment the ERP from plant-floor networks so a business-system encryption event cannot propagate; rehearse the decision to keep running versus stop, with quality and food-safety leadership in the room.

High Campaign — Ransomware

Six agencies publish Gunra ransomware advisory; manufacturing among the named victim sectors

Joint advisory AA26-222A, released 10 August by CISA, the FBI, DC3, NSA, the Secret Service and South Korea’s National Police Agency, covers Gunra ransomware. Gunra emerged in April 2025 built on leaked Conti source code and has since become a ransomware-as-a-service operation recruiting affiliates through dark-web forums. Affiliates break in through exposed edge infrastructure — VPN gateways, firewall appliances and RDP-reachable systems — and run double extortion, exfiltrating before encrypting. Manufacturing, transport, utilities and government are among the named victim sectors. The entry-point list matters more than the family name: the same appliance that lets a machine-builder dial in for support is the documented way in.

Active — ongoing RaaS campaign with named victims in manufacturing Joint advisory AA26-222A — IOCs and detection guidance published
No single patch — patch edge devices, enforce MFA, segment and test recovery Action: Inventory every internet-facing VPN concentrator, firewall and RDP endpoint across plants and shared services and confirm current patch level plus MFA; eliminate direct RDP exposure; require machine builders and integrators to come in through a brokered, time-boxed access path rather than their own always-on tunnel; verify OT and engineering backups are offline or immutable and that one has been restored this quarter.

High Research — Plant Utilities

Claroty finds 23 flaws in Copeland XWEB Pro controllers that chain to root — and a hidden login in Danfoss AK-SM 800A

Claroty’s Team82 disclosed 23 vulnerabilities in Copeland XWEB Pro supervisory refrigeration controllers, 21 of them high severity, which can be chained — authentication flaws plus predictable administrator credentials plus command injection — to bypass security controls and achieve root-level remote code execution. In a separate disclosure the team found three flaws in Danfoss AK-SM 800A controllers, including a hidden ‘code-of-the-day’ authentication mechanism that can be abused to bypass normal login and reach code execution. In a demonstration, a compromised controller remotely manipulated cooling fans and compressors while concealing the resulting temperature rise from operators. Copeland shipped firmware 1.13 and Danfoss R4.3.1. For process manufacturers the lesson generalises beyond refrigeration: facilities-purchased supervisory controllers on chillers, compressors and air handling sit on plant networks with web management enabled and no controls-engineering oversight.

No known exploitation reported — researcher-demonstrated, vendor-patched Not in CISA KEV
Patches available — Copeland XWEB Pro firmware 1.13; Danfoss AK-SM 800A R4.3.1 Action: Ask facilities, not controls, for a list of every web-managed supervisory controller on plant utilities and who administers it; apply Copeland 1.13 and Danfoss R4.3.1 where applicable; take all such management interfaces off any internet-reachable path and restrict them to a management VLAN or VPN; change default and vendor-set administrator credentials; add independent temperature or process alarming that does not depend on the same controller, since the demonstrated attack concealed the effect.
Suggested SLA: Firmware update — 30 days; management-interface isolation and independent alarming — 60 days Owner:

Medium Research — Exposure

4,407 controllers found answering EtherNet/IP on the public internet

A Shodan query run on 3 August returned 4,407 devices exposing port 44818, with roughly 65% in the United States, 12% in Canada and 3% in Spain. The research was prompted by the water-sector attacks, and of 22 controllers located in affected cities, 19 were on firmware susceptible to CVE-2017-16740 — a Modbus TCP buffer overflow in Rockwell MicroLogix 1400 Series B and C on firmware 21.002 and earlier, fixed in 21.003. Manufacturers rarely believe this applies to them, and the exposure usually is not the main plant: it is a machine builder’s remote-support router at a satellite line, a leased packaging skid with its own SIM, or a controller a contractor put on a DMZ during commissioning and nobody removed.

Active — devices in this exposed population were attacked in the current water campaign CVE-2017-16740 — not in CISA KEV; fixed firmware available since 2017
Patch available — MicroLogix 1400 firmware revision 21.003 or later Action: Scan your own public IP ranges and any cellular APN your machine builders use for 44818, 502, 102 and 2222; treat unlisted results as an inventory failure to fix as well as an exposure to close; add a contractual requirement that suppliers declare and route remote connectivity through your access broker; inventory MicroLogix firmware and schedule the 21.003+ upgrade at the next planned downtime.

Critical Advisory — CVSS 10

CVSS 10 unauthenticated root command injection in Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway — critical manufacturing named

CISA released 14 ICS advisories on 13 August, led by CVE-2026-19188 in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway at CVSS 10.0. The device’s ‘Net Check’ feature passes user-supplied input from the cmdPing Socket.io event to the operating system without sanitizing it, letting an unauthenticated remote attacker execute arbitrary OS commands as root with no user interaction. Version 3.40.1.12 is affected, and CISA names critical manufacturing alongside energy and water among the deployment sectors. These combined HMI/gateway/DTU units are the low-cost remote-monitoring boxes that arrive attached to OEM machinery, leased equipment and small packaging or ancillary lines — the tier of asset that is bought on a capex line rather than through controls engineering and rarely appears in a patch cycle.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV
See vendor advisory — isolate immediately if internet-reachable Action: Search purchasing and OEM commissioning records, not just the CMDB, for Haiwell gateways and cloud HMI units on packaging, ancillary and leased equipment; take any internet- or cellular-reachable unit offline or restrict it to a management VLAN today; apply vendor firmware when published; where the device cannot be updated, plan replacement, because unauthenticated root execution leaves nothing on the device to compensate with.
Suggested SLA: Inventory and isolate exposed units — 24h; remediate or replace — 7 days Owner:

High Advisory — ICS Patch Tuesday

August ICS Patch Tuesday: maximum-severity authentication bypass in Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050, plus CAD and simulation fixes

Siemens published 10 advisories on 11 August. The headline is a maximum-severity missing-authentication flaw in SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices (SSA-834709) that lets a remote, unauthenticated attacker execute arbitrary code on the underlying server with elevated privileges. A critical code-execution flaw was fixed in Siveillance Video Management Servers, and high-severity issues in Solid Edge, Simcenter Nastran, the Siemens License Server, Simcenter Femap, Parasolid and Logo! Soft Comfort — the CAD, CAE and licensing stack sitting on engineering desktops, exploitable to crash applications, execute code, elevate privileges or read arbitrary files. Medium-severity fixes landed in Ruggedcom devices and Desigo controllers. Schneider Electric covered NetBotz 5 and PowerChute Serial Shutdown; Phoenix Contact addressed multiple PLCnext firmware flaws exploitable without authentication; Honeywell issued several building-management advisories.

High KEV — Actively Exploited

Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD zero-day added to CISA KEV after denial-of-service exploitation

CISA added three vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August, including CVE-2026-20349, a heap-inspection flaw in Cisco Secure Firewall Adaptive Security Appliance and Firewall Threat Defense that Cisco patched after it was exploited as a zero-day for denial-of-service attacks. The others are a use-after-free in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock (CVE-2026-68820) and SQL injection in Metabase (CVE-2026-72898). Earlier in the month CISA added Progress LoadMaster CVE-2026-8037 and Langflow, Apache Tomcat and N-able N-central flaws. ASA and FTD appliances commonly form the boundary between enterprise and plant networks and terminate the VPNs machine builders use; the Gunra advisory names exactly this class of device as an entry point, which makes patching it a plant-availability issue rather than an IT chore.

Actively exploited — confirmed zero-day exploitation before patch In CISA KEV — added 11 Aug 2026; federal remediation deadlines apply
Patches available from Cisco, Microsoft, Metabase and Progress Action: Identify every ASA or FTD appliance carrying plant traffic or terminating vendor VPNs and schedule the Cisco fix as an emergency change, timed so no line loses its only support path; apply the Windows AFD update to engineering workstations and jump hosts; check whether any Metabase or LoadMaster instance fronts production reporting; confirm N-able N-central and similar RMM tooling used by your IT provider is patched.
Suggested SLA: Cisco ASA/FTD patch — 7 days (KEV); workstation and supporting-system updates — 14 days Owner:
