High Supply-chain compromise — ConnectWise Cloud Backup app identities used against Microsoft 365 tenants; Microsoft names manufacturing, automotive, chemicals and semiconductor among targeted sectors

Microsoft ties the ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise to China-aligned Storm-0940 and lists manufacturing, automotive, chemicals and semiconductors among its targets

Microsoft said on 21 October that it assesses Storm-0940, which it calls a China-state aligned actor active since 2021, was responsible for the recently disclosed ConnectWise supply-chain compromise. The attackers controlled the identities of several ConnectWise cloud applications tied to Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick) and used them to sign in to customers’ Microsoft 365 tenants as the application, so MFA did not apply; a separate password spray on 10–14 October did not succeed. Microsoft says attempts reached more than 2,250 tenants, that most of the targeting failed, and that in several cases privileged access followed in under three hours. It says the actor used ARTEX, an open-source penetration-testing framework that can drive any AI model, and lists aerospace and defense, automotive, chemicals, energy, manufacturing and semiconductors among targeted sectors. ConnectWise, which first detected the activity on 14 October, suspended Cloud Backup and shut down SaaS Security and Cloud Management; its chief executive said on 22 October that services are being restored in controlled phases, and the company says it has no evidence that partner backup data was compromised. Huntress reported unauthorized sign-ins at 162 tenants it monitors. No manufacturing victim or OT impact has been named. Manufacturers whose MSP manages Microsoft 365 through these services should check their tenants (editorial assessment).

FBI and Secret Service: FortiBleed credential theft against FortiGate gateways is still running, with targets in all 16 critical-infrastructure sectors

The FBI and US Secret Service warned on 6 October, in joint advisory JCSA-20261006-01, that the FortiBleed campaign against internet-facing FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPNs remains an active threat. There is no CVE: the agencies describe abuse of reused or leaked credentials and legacy SHA-256 password storage, and researchers describe password spraying and stuffing, offline cracking of extracted hashes and a sniffer that captures authentication traffic. Attackers have added admin accounts and deleted or reset legitimate ones, locking owners out. The agencies say targets span all 16 US critical-infrastructure sectors, critical manufacturing among them, and the FBI ties the access to Payload ransomware; SOCRadar, which reports overlaps with INC and Lynx, says it has confirmed at least 12 ransomware deployments from this access and counted 86,644 working device credentials across 194 countries as of 19 June. No OT system compromise has been described. FortiGate often sits at the IT/OT boundary in plants, so a stolen admin login there is a path toward production networks (editorial assessment).

US, UK and German agencies warn that SafePay ransomware has hit at least 600 organizations since August 2024, getting in through stolen credentials and old edge-device flaws

CISA, the FBI and the US Secret Service, with Germany’s LKA Rheinland-Pfalz, the UK National Crime Agency and the North West Regional Organised Crime Unit, issued advisory AA26-293A on 20 October about SafePay, a closed ransomware group with no affiliates. The agencies count at least 600 victim organizations across multiple critical-infrastructure sectors in the Americas, Europe and Australia since August 2024. Medium Research — AI agents against OT: Booz Allen lab tests and Claroty Team82 exploit reproduction; no new CVE

In lab tests, an AI model found and moved a robotic arm within minutes, and an AI coding agent rebuilt a published controller exploit for about $6

Booz Allen’s 8 October report describes frontier AI models tested in its isolated, multi-vendor OT lab, which is modelled on a manufacturing facility. Across eight scenarios the models met every test objective; in one they went from a perimeter compromise to actions inside the industrial control network in just over 16 minutes, with a human approving every exploit and any action with a physical effect. One model identified a robotic arm, mapped its protection zones and motion limits and moved it within minutes; in another test the models found live, pre-authenticated connections to 14 OT devices. Team82 stresses this was not autonomous discovery and does not link the flaws to the French supermarket attacks. Both are lab results, not real-world attacks.
