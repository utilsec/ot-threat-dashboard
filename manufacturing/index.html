Info Guidance — CISA and G7 joint call to begin the post-quantum cryptography transition; no deadline attached

CISA and the G7 Cyber Security Working Group issued a joint call on 4 September for governments and organisations to begin the transition to post-quantum cryptography, setting out a phased, risk-based approach: inventory the cryptography in use and the dependencies that carry it, identify the data and systems whose exposure would outlast the migration, and hold a written transition plan rather than a procurement wish. The statement is explicit about store-now-decrypt-later — traffic captured today can be decrypted later — and about the second-order problem, which is that authentication and firmware-signing chains rest on the same public-key algorithms. It is guidance, not a rule, and no deadline attaches. For a plant the relevance is procurement: a controller or drive bought this year will still be in the line in the 2040s, and the signing and authentication cryptography baked into its firmware is decided by the vendor, not by you — unless you ask now.

From 11 September 2026 manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities within 24 hours

The EU Cyber Resilience Act’s reporting obligations begin on 11 September 2026. From that date, manufacturers of products with digital elements placed on the EU market must notify an actively exploited vulnerability in their product, or a severe incident affecting its security, within 24 hours of becoming aware — with a fuller report following.
