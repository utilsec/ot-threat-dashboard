Huntress published incident-response findings on 17 September on a new ransomware variant it calls Settra, drawn from two cases it investigated: a consumer-services and retail organisation in July and a manufacturing company in September 2026. Info Threat Intelligence & Research — Analysis, engineering practice

Cyber-informed engineering argues the useful question is not how to keep attackers out, but which consequences you can design away

An analysis published 15 September sets out the case for cyber-informed engineering in critical infrastructure: that industrial systems operate under a constraint IT does not share, because a compromised network does not automatically have to become a catastrophic physical event. The argument is to move the security question upstream into design — identifying the physical consequences that actually matter, then engineering them out with controls that do not depend on the digital system remaining trustworthy. For a plant this reframes the whole conversation. This is practitioner analysis rather than incident reporting; it names no victim and describes no attack, but it is the most useful lens available for operators whose detection budget will never match their consequence exposure.
