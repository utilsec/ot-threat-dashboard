Top Story

Citrix confirms two NetScaler zero-days exploited before patching, CVSS 9.5; CISA KEV deadline is 30 September

Citrix confirmed on 27 September that two NetScaler ADC and Gateway flaws, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS 9.5 each), were exploited before patches existed. The first lets an unauthenticated attacker run commands in any configuration; the second is a DTLS memory-overflow reachable on VPN virtual servers, where DTLS is on by default. Fixes shipped the same day in 14.1-73.37 and 13.1-64.23, and CISA added both flaws to KEV with a 30 September federal deadline. Shadowserver counts more than 20,000 exposed instances, and no actor or victim has been named. For manufacturers the concern is the front door: NetScaler Gateway commonly fronts contractor, integrator and remote-engineer access into plant DMZs. Because exploitation preceded disclosure, patching alone does not show whether an appliance was already compromised, and Citrix warns its IOC scan does not cover every technique.

High Incident — Confirmed IT breach stopped manufacturing; no SCADA compromise found

Boston Scientific confirms an intruder reached an external-facing network device and stopped manufacturing; SCADA systems were not reached

Boston Scientific disclosed on 25–26 August that a cyber incident had disrupted its network and business applications, hurting its ability to manufacture and to process and ship orders. In a 22 September update, the company said CrowdStrike’s investigation found the actor gained access to an external-facing network device and then to a limited part of its on-premises IT environment, with no evidence of compromise in manufacturing-maintenance, product and software development, medical-device maintenance or SCADA systems, and no evidence of data being staged or exfiltrated from those systems. The case shows an IT-side compromise of one internet-facing device can stop production without control systems ever being touched.

Medium Research — AI-assisted PLC exploit development

Forescout: an AI model ported a pre-authentication PLC exploit between WAGO models in about 8.5 hours, but needed heavy human guidance

Forescout’s Vedere Labs reported on 1 September that it used Claude to adapt an exploit for CVE-2021-31886, a pre-authentication stack overflow (CVSS 9.8) in the Nucleus FTP server, from the WAGO 750-852 PLC to the 750-831, reaching ARM shellcode execution without credentials. Forescout’s point is that AI lowers the cost of exploit work on embedded controllers, not that it removes the need for skill. Critical Vulnerability — Pepperl+Fuchs IO-Link master, CVSS 9.8, research disclosure

Nozomi finds 19 flaws in a Pepperl+Fuchs IO-Link master, including a CVSS 9.8 authentication bypass that chains to root

Nozomi Networks Labs published research on 24 September describing 19 vulnerabilities in the web management interface of the Pepperl+Fuchs ICE2-8IOL-K45P-RJ45 IO-Link master (EtherNet/IP firmware 1.7.3). The worst, CVE-2026-27546 (CVSS 9.8), lets an unauthenticated attacker obtain an admin session; chained with OS command injection flaws (CVSS 8.8) that run as root, it gives full control of the device, and an unauthenticated path-traversal flaw (CVE-2026-27557, CVSS 7.5) exposes SSH keys. Pepperl+Fuchs fixed the issues through coordinated disclosure with CERT@VDE (VDE-2026-014), and a Security Boulevard summary of the research points to firmware 1.7.8. No exploitation is reported. IO-Link masters bridge sensors and actuators on a machine to the plant’s EtherNet/IP network, so a compromised master is a foothold inside the cell.

Medium Regulation — EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting duties in force

EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting duties took effect on 11 September: manufacturers must report exploited product vulnerabilities within 24 hours

Since 11 September, manufacturers of products with digital elements sold in the EU must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents affecting product security, according to the European Commission. The steps are an early warning within 24 hours, a fuller notification within 72 hours, and a final report no later than 14 days after a fix is available (for vulnerabilities) or one month after the 72-hour notification (for incidents). Reports go once through a single reporting platform run by ENISA, which became operational on 11 September, and the receiving national CSIRT shares them with the others. For machine builders and component makers that put controllers, HMIs, gateways and connected equipment on the EU market, this turns vulnerability handling into a legal deadline; for plant owners, expect supplier notifications on this timetable.

Not an exploit advisory — regulatory obligation now in force Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: If you build or sell connected machinery, controllers or components into the EU, confirm you can meet the 24-hour, 72-hour and 14-day reporting steps and know who signs off on a report; if you buy them, ask key suppliers how they will notify you of exploited vulnerabilities.

Medium Guidance — Third-party ICS integrators; NIST OT security guide draft

CISA and the FBI tell operators to rein in third-party ICS integrators after a 2025 integrator breach exposed customer SCADA files

CISA and the FBI published joint guidance on 24 September for critical infrastructure operators working with third-party ICS integrators. It draws on a March–April 2025 compromise of a US industrial automation company in which a foreign actor searched for “customers” and “SCADA” and staged about 800 files of customer SCADA information, ICS device details and schematics in nine archives. The agencies urge contract security requirements, least privilege, monitored and on-demand remote access, a full inventory of integrator-supplied equipment, offline backups and the ability to operate manually if the integrator is compromised. The victim’s named customers were power and transportation entities, not manufacturers, but plants routinely give OEMs, machine builders and integrators standing access to PLCs, robots and MES interfaces. Separately, NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Rev. 4, its OT security guide, on 21 September, restructured around CSF 2.0; comments are due 30 November.

Not an exploit advisory — guidance grounded in a documented 2025 integrator compromise Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: List every OEM, integrator and machine builder with remote access to your PLCs, robots or MES/SCADA layer, and require time-boxed, logged access that you can revoke yourself.
