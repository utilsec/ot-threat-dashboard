Top Story

Citrix confirms two actively exploited NetScaler zero-days, CVSS 9.5, now added to CISA’s KEV catalog

Citrix confirmed on 27 September that two NetScaler ADC and Gateway vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS 9.5 each), are being actively exploited on unmitigated appliances. CVE-2026-88771 is an unauthenticated, improper input-validation flaw that lets an attacker run arbitrary commands in any default configuration; CVE-2026-88772 is a memory-overflow flaw reachable through DTLS, which is enabled by default on VPN virtual servers. Citrix shipped fixes the same day in versions 14.1-73.37 and 13.1-64.23, alongside six more flaws (CVE-2026-88773 through -88778, CVSS 7.0–9.3) disclosed but not reported exploited. CISA added both exploited CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 27 September with a federal deadline of 30 September. NetScaler Gateway is a common front door for contractor, vendor and remote-engineer access into manufacturing DMZs, and Citrix warns that exploitation predating disclosure means patching alone cannot rule out prior compromise.

High Ransomware — Multi-brand affiliate tradecraft, initial access unconfirmed

Microsoft: a ransomware affiliate rotating Qilin, DragonForce, Anubis and BERT has hit critical manufacturing and chemicals

Microsoft Threat Intelligence published a profile on 24 September of Storm-2570, a ransomware affiliate it has tracked since April 2025 that deploys Qilin, DragonForce, Anubis or BERT while its hands-on tradecraft stays the same. Affected organizations are in the US, Canada, the UK, Spain, the Netherlands and Puerto Rico, across 14 sectors that include critical manufacturing, chemicals, energy, and food and agriculture; Microsoft names no victims and gives no count. How the group gets in “remains unconfirmed.” Once inside, it uses remote-management tools (Atera, ScreenConnect, Splashtop, NinjaRMM, MeshAgent), PsExec, Impacket and NetExec to move laterally, Mimikatz, LaZagne and ntdsutil to harvest credentials, and Rclone or s5cmd to take data out. Qilin and DragonForce are the first- and fourth-most-active ransomware groups against manufacturers this year in Black Kite’s count.

High Incident — Confirmed breach disrupted manufacturing; no SCADA/OT compromise found

Boston Scientific confirms an attacker reached an external-facing network device and halted manufacturing for weeks; SCADA systems were not touched

Boston Scientific disclosed on 26 August that a threat actor had gained access to an external-facing network device and a limited part of its on-premises IT environment, forcing it to halt product manufacturing and queue orders for days while it restored core business systems. In a 22 September update, the medical-device maker said CrowdStrike’s investigation found no evidence the actor reached manufacturing-maintenance systems, product or software development, medical-device maintenance, or SCADA environments, and no data was staged or exfiltrated from those systems. The company has not said whether the incident will materially affect 2026 results. The case is a reminder that an IT-side compromise of a single internet-facing device can stop a production line even when engineering and control systems are never touched.

Critical Exploitation campaign — Cisco Secure FMC, CISA KEV

Three clusters are exploiting Cisco’s firewall manager; one uses tactics Talos says match Qilin ransomware affiliates

Cisco Talos, in an analysis published 9 September and last updated 21 September, describes three clusters exploiting two flaws in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center: CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0), which lets an unauthenticated attacker run scripts as root and is in CISA’s KEV catalog, and CVE-2026-20316 (CVSS 5.3), which lets a remote attacker log in with a low-privileged account. Talos names no victims or sectors, and no new development has been reported as of 28 September. FMC often manages the firewalls between corporate IT and plant-floor networks.

High Report — Manufacturing ransomware landscape

Manufacturing ransomware victims topped 1,183 through July, already ahead of all of 2024

Black Kite’s Manufacturing & Distribution Ransomware Report, published 18 September, counts 1,183 manufacturing ransomware victims globally from January through July 2026, up 39.7% year over year and already past 2024’s full-year total. Nearly half of these incidents came from groups that were absent in 2023 and 2024. European victims grew 85.4%, cutting the US share of victims from 52.3% to 34.8%; Germany alone rose more than 83%. Qilin (178 incidents), The Gentlemen (142), Akira (96), DragonForce (70) and INC Ransom (65) lead the field, and companies with $10–100 million in annual revenue made up 70.2% of manufacturing victims.

Critical Advisory — Shared field-I/O flaw set, 20 CVEs, no known exploitation

IO-Link masters from three vendors share 20 flaws; Nozomi shows how they chain to root on a Pepperl+Fuchs unit

CERT@VDE published three advisories on 16 September (VDE-2026-014, -027 and -028) covering Pepperl+Fuchs, Phoenix Contact and Carlo Gavazzi IO-Link master modules that share the same set of 20 CVEs, CVE-2026-27546 through CVE-2026-27565. Two are rated CVSS v3.1 9.8: CVE-2026-27546, an authentication bypass, and CVE-2026-27565, a malicious IODD upload. On 24 September Nozomi Networks Labs published a technical write-up of the Pepperl+Fuchs findings on an ICE2-8IOL-K45P-RJ45 master, covering 19 of the CVEs — most of them OS command injection — and showing that, chained, they give an unauthenticated attacker root on the device and the ability to manipulate sensor and actuator data. IO-Link masters sit at the field layer of EtherNet/IP and PROFINET lines. No exploitation has been reported.

Regulation & Policy
Medium Guidance — Third-party ICS integrators; NIST OT security guide draft

CISA and the FBI tell operators to rein in third-party ICS integrators after a 2025 integrator breach exposed customer SCADA files

CISA and the FBI released a joint fact sheet, “Considerations for Critical Infrastructure Operators Working With Third-Party ICS Integrators,” dated 23 September and publicised 24 September. It cites a March–April 2025 compromise of a US industrial automation company in which foreign actors searched for “customers” and “SCADA” and staged about 800 files of customer SCADA information, ICS device details and schematics for exfiltration. The agencies urge operators to write security requirements into integrator contracts, apply least privilege, route remote access through monitored, logged and preferably on-demand paths, get a full inventory of what the integrator supplied, and practise manual operation in case the integrator itself is compromised. The sheet names power utilities and transportation among the victim’s customers, not manufacturers, but plants routinely give OEMs, machine builders and integrators standing access to PLCs, robots and MES interfaces. Separately, NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Rev. 4, its Guide to OT Security, on 21 September; comments are due 30 November.

Not an exploit advisory — guidance grounded in a documented 2025 integrator compromise Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: List every OEM, integrator and machine-builder with remote access to your PLCs, robots or MES/SCADA layer, and require time-boxed, logged access with the ability to revoke it yourself.
Suggested SLA: Integrator access inventory — 14 days; conversion to on-demand access — 30 days Owner:
