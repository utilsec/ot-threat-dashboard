Top Story

Food and Ag-ISAC: federal agencies confirmed Iran-linked attacks on exposed processing and grain-handling PLCs; sector ransomware up 62%

The Food and Ag-ISAC’s State of the Threat: Food and Agriculture Sector Cyber Trends report, published 3 September and still the sector’s most current baseline, ranks nation-state actors as the second-largest category of adversaries after ransomware groups. In its words, Iran “blurs the line between state operations and hacktivism while showing willingness to directly target food security, with federal agencies confirming attacks on internet-exposed programmable logic controllers used in processing lines and grain handling systems.” It describes Chinese operators pre-positioning inside US critical infrastructure, Russian state-sponsored operators exploiting vulnerable networking equipment for persistent access, and North Korean IT workers posing as remote employees. Ransomware incidents against the sector rose 62% through July (227 vs. 140 in the same period of 2025); Qilin was most active with 47 listed attacks, followed by The Gentlemen (31) and Akira (14). The report warns that AI is shortening the time between disclosure and exploitation to hours and that exposed OT and ICS components deserve heightened scrutiny. No new development reported as of 27 September.

Confirmed — the Food and Ag-ISAC’s own report, data through July 2026; group counts are listed attacks, and the nation-state characterisations are the ISAC’s assessment, citing federal confirmation of the Iran-linked PLC attacks Not tied to a single CVE — sector-wide threat assessment
No single patch applicable Action: If you operate processing-line controllers, dosing systems or grain-handling PLCs, confirm none are reachable from the internet — the attacks the report cites were on internet-exposed PLCs. Prioritise exposed OT and ICS components over blanket patching, as the report advises, and verify your ransomware recovery plan covers engineering workstations and historians.

High Background — Foundational context for this year’s ransomware statistics

What “Qilin targets food and beverage” looks like in practice: 30 factories, six breweries, one week

Background (September 2025), not a new incident: Qilin’s ransomware attack on Asahi Group, Japan’s largest brewer, compromised data-center networks via stolen credentials, shut down 30 factories and suspended operations at six of seven breweries for a week, and cost over $31 million in lost revenue — roughly 42 million bottles of production. Order processing, shipments and accounting systems all failed alongside production. It remains the clearest illustration of what Qilin, the group the Food and Ag-ISAC now counts as this sector’s most active ransomware operator with 47 listed attacks through July 2026, can do to a food and beverage producer once inside. No confirmed OT-disruptive incident at a food, beverage or agriculture operator was reported between 24 and 27 September; this card is carried as context only.

Confirmed — Asahi Group Holdings confirmed the attack, factory shutdowns and data exposure in its own investor disclosures; Qilin claimed responsibility and published stolen data as proof Access vector: compromised credentials, not a specific disclosed CVE
Recovery: full containment and restoration took roughly two months Action: Use this as your tabletop scenario: if ransomware hit your order-processing, shipment and accounting systems simultaneously with production, could you keep shipping on manual processes for a week? Test credential hygiene on data-center and plant-network access specifically, since that was Asahi’s entry point.
Suggested SLA: Ransomware tabletop exercise — 90 days if not already run this year Owner:

Critical Active exploitation — Firewall management platform, CISA KEV

Cisco’s firewall manager: Talos says a ransomware operator with Qilin-consistent tactics got in through CVE-2026-20316

Cisco Talos (published 9 September, updated 21 September) tracks three clusters exploiting Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center. UAT-12197 exploited CVE-2026-20079, a CVSS 10.0 authentication bypass that gives an unauthenticated attacker root, which CISA added to KEV on 9 September. UAT-11823 used both CVE-2026-20079 and CVE-2026-20316, with tooling Talos says overlaps with Sandworm’s — an overlap, not an attribution. The third cluster, UAT-11988, logged in with static credentials via CVE-2026-20316 (CVSS 5.3, a low-privileged login flaw); Talos assesses with high confidence that it is a ransomware operator whose TTPs “were consistent with those of Qilin ransomware affiliates”, the group the Food and Ag-ISAC counts as this sector’s most active. Talos names no victims or sectors. No new development since 21 September.

Medium Threat actor profile — Cross-sector ransomware affiliate (IT-side)

Microsoft: one ransomware affiliate rotates Qilin, DragonForce, Anubis and BERT, and lists food and agriculture among its victim sectors

Microsoft Threat Intelligence published a profile on 24 September of Storm-2570, a ransomware affiliate that deploys Qilin, DragonForce, Anubis and BERT while keeping the same tradecraft. Microsoft lists food and agriculture among its victim sectors, alongside healthcare, government, energy, chemicals, critical manufacturing and others, in the US, Canada, the UK, Spain, the Netherlands and Puerto Rico. Initial access “remains unconfirmed”; after entry the group relies on remote-management tools (Atera, MeshAgent, ScreenConnect, Splashtop, NinjaRMM), PsExec, Impacket and NetExec to move laterally, and Rclone and s5cmd to exfiltrate data. This is a cross-sector, IT-side profile: Microsoft describes no OT impact and names no food or agriculture victim. Editorial assessment: it matters here because Qilin, one of the strains it deploys, leads ransomware against this sector in the Food and Ag-ISAC’s count.

Confirmed — intrusions observed by Microsoft Threat Intelligence; no food or agriculture victim named Not tied to a CVE — initial access unconfirmed
No single patch — behavioural and configuration controls Action: List the remote-management tools approved on plant and corporate networks, alert on unapproved Atera, ScreenConnect, Splashtop or MeshAgent installs, require MFA on approved RMM, and turn on tamper protection so attackers cannot switch off endpoint security.

High Guidance — Third-party ICS integrator access (CISA/FBI fact sheet)

CISA and the FBI tell critical-infrastructure operators to put third-party ICS integrators on least-privilege, on-demand access

CISA and the FBI released a joint fact sheet, “Considerations for Critical Infrastructure Operators Working With Third-Party ICS Integrators”, dated 23 September and publicised 24 September. It cites a March–April 2025 compromise of a US industrial automation firm in which foreign actors staged about 800 files of customer SCADA information, device details and schematics for exfiltration, and it names power utilities and transportation entities among the affected customers. It recommends least privilege, monitored and on-demand remote access, security requirements in contracts, a full inventory of supplied hardware and software, and practised manual operations. The fact sheet is addressed to critical-infrastructure operators generally and does not name co-ops. Editorial assessment: grain co-ops, dairy processors and packaging plants that rely on an integrator for dosing, refrigeration and line-control programming carry the same exposure, so the guidance applies to them equally.

Not an exploit advisory — access-control guidance grounded in a documented 2025 intrusion at an automation firm Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: List every integrator with remote access to dosing, refrigeration, packaging-line or grain-handling control systems, and move them to time-boxed, logged access you can revoke yourself.
Suggested SLA: Integrator access inventory — 14 days; conversion to on-demand access — 30 days Owner:

High Research — IT/OT/IoT network convergence

Forescout: only 13% of network segments containing OT devices are OT-only

Forescout’s Vedere Labs analysis, published 22 September, covers 47,700 network segments containing more than 2.5 million devices across 209 organizations. Only 13% of segments containing OT devices were OT-only; the other 87% also held other types of device, and almost half mixed OT with both IT and IoT assets. The average manufacturing segment held 29 devices, Forescout’s measure of how far one foothold can reach. Editorial assessment: in cold-chain and refrigeration environments, where building-management systems, IoT sensors and process control often share infrastructure, that flat architecture is what lets an IT-side ransomware foothold reach a production line.

Research finding — not tied to a specific exploited vulnerability or incident Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: Verify segmentation between refrigeration/cold-chain OT and building-management or office IT against actual traffic, not architecture diagrams.
Suggested SLA: Segmentation audit against live traffic — 30 days Owner:

Critical Advisory — Controller authentication bypass, no known exploitation

Schneider Modicon M580 authentication flaw rated CVSS v4.0 9.2 (v3.1 9.8)

Schneider Electric published SEVD-2026-251-04 on 8 September for CVE-2026-3869, an incorrectly implemented authentication algorithm (CWE-303) in Modicon M580 controllers below application level 4.00 and M580 Safety controllers below application level 4.20. Schneider rates it CVSS v4.0 9.2 and v3.1 9.8. Fixes: M580 firmware 4.10 or later with application level 4.00 or later (EcoStruxure Control Expert V15.2+), and M580 Safety firmware 4.21 or later with application level 4.20 or later (Control Expert V16.0 HF001+). No exploitation has been reported. Editorial assessment: the M580 is a common process-control platform, including on clean-in-place and pasteurization skids in food and beverage plants.

No known exploitation reported Not in CISA KEV

CERT@VDE published three advisories on 16 September — VDE-2026-014 (Pepperl+Fuchs ICE2/ICE3), VDE-2026-027 (Phoenix Contact IOL MA8) and VDE-2026-028 (Carlo Gavazzi YL212/YN115) — each listing the same 20 CVEs (CVE-2026-27546 through CVE-2026-27565). High Advisory — Industrial telemetry database denial-of-service, no known exploitation

One malformed packet can crash TDengine, a time-series database used for industrial telemetry

Ridge Security disclosed CVE-2026-42542 on 23 September, a pre-authentication denial-of-service flaw in TDengine: an integer underflow in RPC length handling on TCP port 6030 lets a single crafted packet crash the database server before any login, and repeating it can hold the service in a crash loop. Versions 3.4.0.0 through 3.4.1.5 are affected; 3.4.1.6 fixes it. It is rated CVSS 7.5 (version not stated). Ridge reports no in-the-wild exploitation and is not aware of public exploit code. Editorial assessment: where TDengine stores sensor telemetry for cold-chain or precision-ag monitoring, a sustained attack degrades monitoring and alarming rather than control itself.

No known exploitation reported — no public exploit code known Not in CISA KEV
Patched — TDengine 3.4.1.6 (affected: 3.4.0.0–3.4.1.5) Action: Confirm whether any cold-chain, refrigeration or precision-ag telemetry pipeline in your environment runs TDengine, upgrade to 3.4.1.6, and restrict TCP port 6030 to trusted hosts in the meantime.
Suggested SLA: Exposure check and port restriction — 7 days; upgrade — 30 days Owner:
