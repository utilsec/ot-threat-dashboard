Top Story

Researchers took over cold-chain refrigeration controllers and hid the temperature rise while food spoiled

Claroty’s Team82 disclosed 23 vulnerabilities in Copeland XWEB Pro supervisory refrigeration controllers, 21 of them high severity, which chain together — authentication flaws, predictable administrator credentials and command injection — to bypass security controls and reach root-level remote code execution. A separate disclosure covers three flaws in Danfoss AK-SM 800A controllers, including a hidden ‘code-of-the-day’ authentication mechanism that sidesteps normal login and leads to code execution. What makes this a food story rather than an HVAC one is the demonstration: researchers showed a compromised controller remotely manipulating cooling fans and compressors while concealing the resulting temperature rise from operators, so product degrades without an alarm ever sounding. Copeland has released firmware 1.13 and Danfoss R4.3.1. These controllers sit in cold storage, processing plants, distribution centres and retail back-of-house.

No known exploitation reported — researcher-demonstrated, vendors patched Not in CISA KEV — Claroty Team82 coordinated disclosure
Patches available — Copeland XWEB Pro firmware 1.13; Danfoss AK-SM 800A R4.3.1 Action: Ask facilities and refrigeration contractors — not IT — for a list of every XWEB Pro and AK-SM 800A unit across plants, cold stores and DCs, including leased and third-party-managed sites; apply Copeland 1.13 and Danfoss R4.3.1; take every management interface off any internet-reachable path and restrict it to a management VLAN or VPN; replace default and vendor-set administrator credentials; and add independent temperature monitoring and alarming that does not run through the same controller, since the demonstrated attack concealed the excursion from the system operators were watching.

High Incident — Ransomware

Coca-Cola suspended US fairlife production after ransomware reached production-related systems

Coca-Cola disclosed on 16 July that its fairlife dairy subsidiary identified unauthorized third-party access to a portion of its systems, including production-related systems, in connection with a ransomware event, and that production operations at fairlife in the United States were temporarily suspended. Reporting centred on the ERP layer carrying scheduling, material movement and order execution. It is the cleanest recent illustration of a food manufacturer losing output without an attacker addressing a controller — at an operator with resources far beyond a typical processor. The disclosure also landed within days of Japanese food and cold-chain logistics company Nichirei confirming a cyberattack that caused system failures across its group from 13 July, underlining that the exposure is not US-specific.

Confirmed — company-disclosed ransomware event with temporary production suspension No CVE — company disclosure; access method not published
No patch — ERP/MES segmentation, backup integrity and degraded-mode operations Action: Identify which ERP and MES functions a line must have to run, and for how long it can run without them; keep an offline copy of current recipes, allergen and label data, and open work orders for at least one shift; confirm ERP and MES backups are immutable and have actually been restored, not merely scheduled; segment business systems from processing and packaging networks; rehearse the run-versus-stop decision with quality and food-safety leadership present, because that call has regulatory consequences a CISO cannot make alone.

High Campaign — Ransomware

Six agencies warn on Gunra ransomware — VPN gateways and firewalls are the documented way in

Joint advisory AA26-222A, published 10 August by CISA, the FBI, DC3, NSA, the Secret Service and South Korea’s National Police Agency, covers Gunra ransomware. Gunra appeared in April 2025 built on leaked Conti source code and has grown into a ransomware-as-a-service operation recruiting affiliates through dark-web forums. Affiliates break in through exposed edge infrastructure — VPN gateways, firewall appliances and RDP-reachable systems — then exfiltrate before encrypting under double extortion. Named victim sectors include manufacturing, transport, utilities, retail and government. Food and beverage operators should read the entry-point list literally: the appliance a filler-line OEM or a refrigeration contractor dials through for support is the same class of device the advisory describes.

Active — ongoing RaaS campaign with named victims across food-adjacent sectors Joint advisory AA26-222A — IOCs and detection guidance published
No single patch — patch edge devices, enforce MFA, segment and test recovery Action: Inventory every internet-facing VPN concentrator, firewall and RDP endpoint across plants, cold stores and shared services and confirm patch level plus MFA; remove direct RDP exposure; require OEMs, refrigeration contractors and integrators to enter through a brokered, time-boxed access path instead of their own always-on tunnel; verify plant and engineering backups are offline or immutable and restore-tested this quarter.
Suggested SLA: Edge patch and MFA verification — 7 days; contractor remote-access rework — 60 days Owner:

Medium Report — Ransomware Trends

Dragos: 70 food and beverage ransomware incidents in Q2, none involving direct control-system manipulation

Dragos’ Q2 2026 industrial ransomware analysis, published 11 August, recorded 1,140 incidents at industrial organizations, up 12% from 1,020 in Q1. Food and beverage organizations accounted for 70; manufacturing overall took 747, transportation and logistics 95, and ICS-adjacent firms such as engineering companies and system integrators 117. North America led with 514 incidents, Europe 316. Dragos observed no case in the quarter where a ransomware operator directly manipulated an industrial control system, while noting that disrupting the IT systems supporting industrial environments is enough to interrupt production. For food and agriculture the 117 integrator and engineering-firm victims deserve attention too: those firms hold recipe libraries, line drawings and standing remote access to your plants.

Trend data — no direct ICS manipulation observed in the quarter Vendor threat research — not an advisory
Not applicable — supply-chain assurance and recovery planning Action: List the integrators, OEMs and engineering consultancies holding your recipes, line drawings or standing remote access, and confirm each has MFA, time-boxed access and a contractual breach-notification obligation; map which business systems the plant cannot lose for 72 hours and give each an offline recovery path; test a bare-metal restore of one MES or historian server rather than trusting the backup report.

High Advisory — Nation-State

AA26-097A update: engineering software used to strip shutdown and alarm logic from PLCs

The 22 July update to joint advisory AA26-097A describes Iran-affiliated actors connecting to internet-exposed controllers using the vendors’ own engineering software — Rockwell Studio 5000 Logix Designer, Schneider EcoStruxure Control Expert and Siemens TIA Portal — over ports 44818, 2222, 102, 502 and 22, then extracting, modifying and deleting project logic. In one US case investigators found a project file that preserved ladder logic for downstream function while adding logic that overrode safe operating parameters and disabled shutdown and alarm functions, letting equipment enter unsafe states without notifying operators. Targeted families include Rockwell CompactLogix and Micro850, Schneider Modicon M340 (BMX P34) and Siemens S7-1200. In a processing plant those controllers run pasteurisers, CIP skids, fillers and cooking or chilling steps where the alarm and interlock logic is the food-safety control.

Active — confirmed hands-on manipulation of control logic in other critical infrastructure sectors CISA/FBI/NSA/EPA joint advisory AA26-097A — refreshed IOCs and detection guidance
No single patch — segmentation, credentials and logic integrity Action: Confirm no controller in the named families is reachable from outside the plant, including through an OEM’s cellular router on a leased line; move engineering software onto hardened workstations with named accounts and MFA; export current project files for every CCP-relevant asset — pasteurisers, cookers, chillers, metal detection interlocks — and diff them against known-good backups for removed alarm or interlock rungs; make that diff part of the food-safety verification routine, not just the IT calendar.
Suggested SLA: Exposure and engineering-workstation review — 7 days; project-file diff on CCP-relevant logic — 14 days Owner:

Critical Advisory — CVSS 10

CVSS 10 unauthenticated root command injection in Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway (ICSA-26-225-02)

CISA released 14 ICS advisories on 13 August, led by CVE-2026-19188 in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway at CVSS 10.0. The device’s ‘Net Check’ feature passes user-supplied input from the cmdPing Socket.io event to the operating system without sanitizing it, allowing an unauthenticated remote attacker to run arbitrary OS commands as root without user interaction. Version 3.40.1.12 is affected; CISA lists critical manufacturing, energy and water among the deployment sectors. Food operators meet this class of device on OEM packaging skids, remote grain and silo monitoring, irrigation and pivot control, and cold-store telemetry — equipment bought as a machine rather than as an IT asset, often with a SIM card supplied by the vendor and no one on site who knows it is reachable.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV
See vendor advisory — isolate immediately if internet-reachable Action: Search purchasing, OEM and integrator records rather than the CMDB for Haiwell gateways and cloud HMI units on packaging lines, silos, irrigation and cold-store telemetry; take anything internet- or cellular-reachable offline or restrict it to a management VLAN today; apply vendor firmware when published; plan replacement where the device cannot be updated, since unauthenticated root execution leaves no compensating control on the device itself.
Suggested SLA: Inventory and isolate exposed units — 24h; remediate or replace — 7 days Owner:

High Advisory — ICS Patch Tuesday

August ICS Patch Tuesday: maximum-severity authentication bypass in Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050

Siemens published 10 advisories on 11 August, headed by a maximum-severity missing-authentication flaw in SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices (SSA-834709) that lets a remote, unauthenticated attacker execute arbitrary code on the underlying server with elevated privileges. Siemens also fixed a critical code-execution flaw in Siveillance Video Management Servers and high-severity issues in Solid Edge, Simcenter Nastran, the Siemens License Server, Simcenter Femap, Parasolid and Logo! Soft Comfort, with medium-severity fixes in Ruggedcom devices and Desigo controllers. Schneider Electric covered NetBotz 5 and PowerChute Serial Shutdown; Phoenix Contact addressed multiple PLCnext firmware flaws exploitable without authentication; Honeywell issued several building-management advisories. In a food plant the entries that bite are usually the quiet ones — NetBotz environmental monitoring in a server room, PowerChute on a UPS-backed SCADA host, Desigo running plant HVAC.

High KEV — Actively Exploited

Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD zero-day added to CISA KEV after denial-of-service exploitation

CISA added three vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August, including CVE-2026-20349, a heap-inspection flaw in Cisco Secure Firewall Adaptive Security Appliance and Firewall Threat Defense that Cisco patched after exploitation as a zero-day for denial-of-service attacks. The others are a use-after-free in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock (CVE-2026-68820) and SQL injection in Metabase (CVE-2026-72898). Earlier in August CISA added Progress LoadMaster CVE-2026-8037 and flaws in Langflow, Apache Tomcat and N-able N-central. The Gunra advisory names this exact class of appliance as an entry point, which is why a firewall patch belongs on the plant availability agenda: multi-site processors often route every DC and cold store through one pair of these boxes.

Patches available from Cisco, Microsoft, Metabase and Progress Action: Identify every ASA or FTD appliance carrying plant, DC or cold-store traffic or terminating vendor VPNs and schedule the Cisco fix as an emergency change, sequenced so no site loses its only monitoring path; apply the Windows AFD update to engineering workstations and jump hosts; check whether any Metabase or LoadMaster instance fronts production or cold-chain reporting; confirm your managed IT provider has patched N-able N-central.

Low Policy — Legislation

Senate farm bill markup ends without advancing, leaving sector cyber provisions in committee

The Senate Agriculture Committee held a markup of the Agricultural Act of 2026 — the Senate’s version of the 2026 Farm Bill — on 6 August. The motion to report the legislation to the full Senate failed 10–11, so the bill did not advance out of committee. Cybersecurity provisions attached to farm bill vehicles include support for precision-agriculture adoption through public-private partnerships on cybersecurity and interoperability in the House version. Separately, the Farm and Food Cybersecurity Act (S.754 / H.R.1604) would direct USDA, working with CISA, to conduct a biennial risk assessment of cyber threats to the food and agriculture sector and run annual crisis simulation exercises, authorizing $1 million a year from 2026 through 2030. Neither changes anything an operator must do today.

Not applicable — pending legislation No enacted requirement — provisions remain in committee
Not applicable — monitor and plan rather than comply Action: Do not wait on federal funding or mandate to justify the basics; use the pending provisions as a planning outline instead — a documented sector risk assessment, an annual crisis exercise involving operations and food safety rather than IT alone, and a security review of precision-agriculture and connected-equipment purchases before contracts are signed. Track the bills so a funding line can be used if it appears, and engage through Food and Ag-ISAC for sector-specific alerting in the meantime.
