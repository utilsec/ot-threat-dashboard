Top Story

Researchers found 23 flaws in one of the most widely deployed refrigeration controllers — and made the display lie

Claroty’s Team82 disclosed 23 vulnerabilities in the Copeland XWEB Pro supervisory controller platform, 21 rated high, affecting the XWEB300D PRO and XWEB500D PRO families used across commercial refrigeration, air conditioning and food retail. Chained together, an authentication bypass, predictable administrator credentials and command injection give an unauthenticated attacker root-level remote code execution. CVE-2026-21718 covers a password generator that derives admin SSH and web credentials from the date, the unit’s MAC address and hard-coded firmware material. The demonstration is what matters for food and agriculture: researchers turned off the cooling fans while continuing to display the correct temperature, so stock warms and spoils while the screen looks normal. Copeland has released firmware 1.13; Danfoss shipped R4.3.1 for related issues.

High Incident — Distribution & cold chain

A breach at Ceva Logistics halted shipments from eight European warehouses and rippled into retail customers

A cyber intrusion disrupted part of Ceva Logistics’ European contract-logistics operation from 29 July, halting shipments at eight warehouses and delaying deliveries for retail customers whose inventory was held there. Customers were notified on 1 August. Personal data including names, addresses, phone numbers, emails and order details was exposed; Ceva says payment and credential data was not. Dutch retailers Bol and De Bijenkorf, ING, Ace & Tate, Ajax and Valve have been reported affected downstream. The Dutch Data Protection Authority is investigating; no group has claimed responsibility. Food and beverage businesses should read this as a shelf-life problem as much as a delivery problem: when a 3PL cannot release stock, chilled and short-dated product is the first inventory to become unsellable.

Confirm you could authorise a release on paper with temperature records intact, because that is the food-safety half of the problem.

High Incident — Mass exploitation

A suspected China-nexus actor mass-exploited a patched vCenter flaw and deployed ransomware

Exploitation of CVE-2026-59310, a directory-traversal flaw in Broadcom VMware vCenter rated CVSS 9.8 and patched on 29 July, has been attributed to a suspected China-nexus actor. German incident-response firm QUIRSO tracked roughly 361 victim IP addresses across 47 countries, 343 appearing between 3 and 5 August, and assesses with moderate confidence that a Chinese-speaking operator is responsible. The chain persists via cron, systemd and reverse_ssh, drops a Linux backdoor and in some cases deploys Babuk-derived ransomware. Food and beverage plants concentrate risk here more than most: batch records, MES, recipe management, warehouse management and the historian that supports traceability are frequently virtual machines on one cluster, and losing them stops production even when the line equipment is untouched.

Actively exploited at scale — ~361 victim IPs across 47 countries, ransomware deployed Not listed in CISA KEV at generation time
Patch available — Broadcom fix released 29 Jul 2026 Action: Patch vCenter now; if an instance was reachable and unpatched after 3 August, hunt for unexpected cron and systemd units, reverse_ssh binaries and unfamiliar SSH keys before declaring it clean. Then confirm your traceability and batch records can be reconstructed if the cluster is unavailable for a week.
Suggested SLA: vCenter patch and compromise assessment — 7 days (Critical) Owner:

High Research — Ransomware

Dragos logged 1,140 industrial ransomware incidents in Q2 — and not one reached a control system

Dragos recorded 1,140 ransomware incidents at industrial organisations in the second quarter of 2026, up 12 percent on Q1, with 514 in North America. Manufacturing — the category that carries food and beverage processing — absorbed 747, or 65 percent. Qilin remained the most active brand. The finding that should shape food-sector planning is negative: Dragos observed no ransomware operator directly manipulating an industrial control system during the quarter. Production stopped because enterprise IT was encrypted, or because operators pre-emptively shut down virtualisation and shared infrastructure. For a processor, that means the realistic path to a stopped line runs through ERP, warehouse management, labelling and quality systems rather than through a PLC.

Then identify which systems must run for you to legally ship product — labelling, allergen control, batch traceability — and protect those first.

High Threat Intelligence — Ransomware

Gunra ransomware is entering through unpatched VPNs and firewalls, not novel exploits

The FBI, CISA, DoD Cyber Crime Center, NSA, US Secret Service and the Republic of Korea’s National Police Agency published #StopRansomware advisory AA26-222A on Gunra, a Conti-derived double-extortion operation that moved to a structured affiliate model in early 2026. Its named victim base spans manufacturing, retail, transport, healthcare, utilities and professional services — effectively the full food supply chain. Initial access comes from exposed edge infrastructure: VPN gateways, firewall appliances and RDP, including the Fortinet FortiOS and FortiProxy authentication-bypass flaws CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472. For food and agriculture businesses running lean IT teams across scattered sites, the uncomfortable part is that this requires no sophistication — only an appliance that missed a patch cycle.

Actively exploited — known Fortinet flaws used for initial access In CISA KEV — CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472
Patches available — Fortinet fixes released; rotate credentials after patching Action: Inventory every internet-facing VPN gateway, firewall and RDP entry point across processing sites, farms, distribution centres and co-packers, patch to current firmware and require MFA on admin accounts. Sites acquired or contracted in the last two years are the usual gap.

High Threat Intelligence — Extortion

Clop is naming victims in its PLM extortion campaign — and FlexPLM sits in consumer-goods supply chains

Clop has begun naming victims of its campaign against internet-exposed PTC Windchill and FlexPLM instances, with reporting on 15 August putting the count at 43 and including Shell, GE and Philips. The entry point is CVE-2026-12569, an unauthenticated remote code execution flaw exploited as a zero-day in early June before PTC patched on 18 June; CISA added it to the KEV catalogue on 25 June. Attackers drop JSP web shells, then stage and exfiltrate. FlexPLM in particular is used across retail and consumer-goods supply chains for specification, formulation and supplier management, so food and beverage businesses should check whether they run it — the exposure is recipe, specification and supplier-contract data, not process control.

Actively exploited — zero-day use from June, ongoing extortion and victim naming In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-12569, added 25 Jun 2026
Patch available — PTC fix released 18 Jun 2026 Action: Confirm whether Windchill or FlexPLM exists anywhere in your estate, including instances run by a design or product development team outside IT’s inventory. Verify they are patched and not internet-reachable, then hunt for JSP web shells and unexplained large outbound transfers since early June.

Critical Advisory — HMI gateway

Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway has an unauthenticated root command-injection flaw rated CVSS 10.0

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an operating-system command-injection vulnerability in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway rated CVSS 10.0. The Net Check feature reachable via the /setting endpoint passes unsanitised input to the cmdPing Socket.io event, allowing arbitrary commands to run as root. CISA lists Critical Manufacturing, Energy and Water and Wastewater among affected sectors, and Haiwell has released fixed firmware. Food and agriculture operations should still check: gateways in this class are common on packaging lines, grain handling, irrigation and environmental control, usually fitted by a machine builder or integrator so a system can be supported remotely, and almost never recorded in the site asset register. No public exploitation has been reported to CISA.

No known public exploitation reported to CISA at generation time Not in CISA KEV
Patch available — Haiwell Scada-v3.50.1.19 Action: Ask your machine builders, integrators and irrigation or environmental-control contractors whether Haiwell gateways are installed on any of your equipment. Remove internet reachability today, apply the vendor firmware, and use the exercise to inventory every OEM-supplied remote-support device on site.

Regulation & Policy
Info Regulation — EU

EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting duties begin in September 2026, which should improve equipment disclosure

From September 2026, manufacturers covered by the EU Cyber Resilience Act must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and serious security incidents in their products. For food and beverage processors this is mainly a supply-side improvement: the filling, packaging, refrigeration and weighing equipment you buy into the EU comes from vendors who will have to disclose on a defined timeline rather than at their discretion. It sits alongside NIS2, under which food production and distribution is an important entity in most member states, and the NIS2 Cooperation Group adopted common incident-reporting templates on 26 May 2026. The Copeland disclosure this week is a good illustration of why timely vendor reporting matters for equipment you cannot easily take out of service.

Not applicable — regulatory milestone Not applicable
No patch — procurement and vendor-management change Action: Add a clause to equipment purchase and service contracts requiring vendors to notify you of actively exploited vulnerabilities on the CRA timeline, and identify which of your existing suppliers fall in scope. If you place any connected product on the EU market yourself, assign an owner to determine whether you are in scope.
