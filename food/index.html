Top Story

Citrix NetScaler: a third exploited zero-day joins CISA’s KEV list, and responders warn patching alone will not evict intruders

Citrix confirmed on 27 September that two NetScaler ADC and Gateway flaws, CVE-2026-88771 (unauthenticated command execution) and CVE-2026-88772 (a memory overflow on appliances with DTLS enabled), both CVSS v4 9.5, were exploited before fixes shipped, and CISA added both to its KEV catalog that day. On 4 October CISA added a third, CVE-2026-88779 (CVSS v4 8.7), a memory overflow on appliances configured for SAML that Citrix says it has seen used in targeted attacks causing denial of service; federal agencies must fix it by 7 October. watchTowr reproduced it as a crash-only bug and suspects attackers use it to knock appliances over and speed up exploitation of CVE-2026-88771; one researcher reported signs of code execution, but Citrix says it has found no impact on data integrity. Mandiant describes the attackers as advanced and suspected state-sponsored, names no group, and reports dozens of victim organizations; Arctic Wolf counts at least 78, and researcher Kevin Beaumont tracked more than 100. Responders found web shells, a rogue superuser account and stolen appliance configurations, and Mandiant warns that upgrading alone will not remove that access. The victim sectors Mandiant names are government, education, technology, financial services, telecom, and legal and professional services; food and agriculture is not among them. Editorial assessment: food and beverage plants commonly let integrators, co-packers and equipment vendors reach line controllers, refrigeration monitoring and batch systems through gateways like these, so a compromised appliance can be the first hop into production networks.

Critical Active exploitation — Firewall management platform, CVE-2026-20079 in CISA KEV

Cisco Talos: crimeware, Sandworm-overlapping and Qilin-deploying clusters all exploited Secure Firewall Management Center

Talos reported on 9 September, updating on 21 September, that Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center is under active exploitation through CVE-2026-20079 (authentication bypass to root, CVSS 10.0) and CVE-2026-20316 (static credential, CVSS 5.3). It tracks three clusters: UAT-12197, crimeware using CVE-2026-20079 for web shells and credential theft; UAT-11823, which used both flaws to deploy Cyclops Blink and which Talos says overlaps in tooling with the Sandworm APT; and UAT-11988, a ransomware operator that used CVE-2026-20316 and deployed Qilin after living-off-the-land reconnaissance. Cisco released hotfixes for both flaws, with a fuller hardening release scheduled for mid-September. CISA added CVE-2026-20079 to KEV on 9 September; we did not verify a KEV listing for CVE-2026-20316. Editorial assessment: Qilin is the most active group against food and agriculture in the sector’s own data, and the management console holds the segmentation policy between IT and plant networks.

Ukraine’s largest grocery chain, ATB, confirms a cyberattack; a data-theft claim by DataSuckers is disputed

ATB, which runs more than 1,300 stores, confirmed on 5 October that it had been hit by a cyberattack and took some online services offline, describing the website disruption as technical maintenance. It said a temporary message shown on its website did not affect the security of customer data and that the site remains fully under its control. A group calling itself DataSuckers claims to hold 7.9 million customer records, employee passport data and more than 11 million order records, has demanded $400,000, and says it will sell the data rather than leak it; ATB denies that customer data was compromised, and the claim has not been independently verified. Carried as the only confirmed attack on a food-supply-chain business found this week; it is IT-side and retail.

Medium Threat actor profile — Cross-sector ransomware affiliate (IT-side)

Microsoft: one ransomware affiliate rotates Qilin, DragonForce, Anubis and BERT, and lists food and agriculture among victim sectors

Microsoft Threat Intelligence published a Storm-2570 profile on 24 September describing one affiliate that deployed four ransomware families — Qilin, DragonForce, Anubis and BERT — with largely the same toolkit: remote-management tools (MeshAgent, Atera, ScreenConnect, Splashtop, NinjaRMM), PsExec, Impacket and NetExec for lateral movement, Mimikatz-style credential theft, and s5cmd and Rclone for exfiltration. Anubis is also the brand that claimed July’s Fairlife attack; no link between the two is asserted.

Medium Research — IoT and edge-device botnet (Nozomi Networks Labs); no OT impact reported

Nozomi: the Cling botnet hides its command traffic in STUN requests and spreads through old router, camera and DVR flaws

Nozomi Networks Labs reported on 1 October that a botnet it calls Cling infects routers, access points, repeaters, DVRs and cameras, mainly through CVE-2021-35394 in the Realtek Jungle SDK, and carries exploits for seven other flaws, including CVE-2014-8361, CVE-2023-26801 and CVE-2024-3721. Infected devices contact public STUN servers, and the operators send commands encoded in STUN transaction IDs while spoofing source addresses so the traffic appears to come from Google’s STUN service. Nozomi saw the bots used for denial-of-service attacks on an internet provider, a university computing cluster and game servers; it gives no victim count and reports no OT or ICS compromise. Its advice is to patch the listed flaws, restrict inbound access and watch for unusual STUN patterns. Editorial assessment: low-cost routers, DVRs and cameras are common at farms, packhouses, grain sites and cold-storage warehouses, often on the same flat network as refrigeration or irrigation controllers.

Observed in the wild — Nozomi confirmed live command-and-control and DDoS use; no OT impact reported No new CISA KEV action tied to this report
Nozomi’s first advice: patch devices affected by CVE-2021-35394 and the other listed flaws Action: Inventory routers, DVRs and cameras at remote farm, packing and storage sites, move them off any segment shared with refrigeration, irrigation or process controllers, block inbound internet access to them, and replace devices that no longer receive firmware.

Critical Active exploitation — Email gateway and SD-WAN management, both in CISA KEV

Fortinet FortiMail and Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager zero-days are exploited in the wild; CISA lists both in KEV

Fortinet disclosed on 1 October that FortiMail CVE-2026-104286 (CVSS 9.8), a path-traversal flaw that lets an unauthenticated attacker write arbitrary files through crafted web requests, has been reported exploited in the wild; CISA added it to KEV the same day. Fortinet’s 5 October update lists fixed releases 7.4.9, 7.6.7 and 8.0.2 and tells 7.2 users to move to 7.4 or later; disabling the IBE feature and limiting webmail access to trusted networks are interim workarounds. Cisco disclosed on 30 September that Catalyst SD-WAN Manager CVE-2026-76504 (CVSS 9.8) lets an unauthenticated attacker bypass API authentication and act as an administrator. Cisco says its PSIRT learned of active exploitation in September and that there are no workarounds; CISA added it to KEV on 30 September. Neither vendor has named an attacker or victims. Editorial assessment: processors and distributors with many plants and warehouses often link them over SD-WAN, and the mail gateway is a common starting point for phishing that ends in ransomware.

High Advisory — WAGO controllers and CODESYS runtime (CERT@VDE), no known exploitation

CERT@VDE: a CVSS 9.8 flaw in the BACnet stack of WAGO controllers, plus CODESYS runtime fixes for WAGO and other PLCs

CERT@VDE published WAGO advisory VDE-2025-102 on 1 October for CVE-2025-41753 (CVSS 9.8), a path-traversal flaw in the BACnet/IP stack: by giving BACnet File Objects names that act as file paths, an unauthenticated attacker can read or overwrite arbitrary files, which can lead to full compromise of the device. It affects 750-811x, 750-821x, 751-9x01 and 752-8303 controllers and 762-series panels on firmware below 4.8.9 (FW30). A second WAGO advisory the same day (VDE-2025-081) covers CODESYS runtime flaws in the same families that let an unauthenticated attacker crash the device (the highest, CVE-2025-41738, is CVSS 8.3), plus a manipulated project file that can run code if a user opens it; the fix is firmware 4.10.0 (FW32). On 30 September CODESYS fixed a runtime race condition, CVE-2026-79625 (CVSS 8.1, needs authenticated monitoring access), in 3.5.22.40, with Linux-based variants due in 4.23.0.0 in the fourth quarter. No exploitation is reported for any of them. Editorial assessment: WAGO controllers and CODESYS-based PLCs are common in food-processing automation, refrigeration plant control and building management, where BACnet is the usual building-systems protocol.

No known exploitation reported Not in CISA KEV
Patched — WAGO firmware 4.8.9 (FW30) for the BACnet flaw and 4.10.0 (FW32) for the CODESYS issues; CODESYS 3.5.22.40, with Linux-based variants awaiting 4.23.0.0 (expected Q4 2026) Action: Inventory WAGO 750, 751, 752 and 762 devices and CODESYS-based controllers on processing lines and refrigeration plants, turn off BACnet/IP where it is not used, block BACnet traffic (UDP 47808) at the cell boundary, and schedule the fixed firmware at the next line-down window.

NIST’s draft SP 800-82 Rev. 4 adds food and agriculture to its OT security guide; comments due 30 November

NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Rev. 4, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, on 21 September. High Guidance — Third-party ICS integrator access (CISA/FBI fact sheet)

CISA and the FBI urge critical-infrastructure operators to put ICS integrators on least privilege and monitored remote access

CISA and the FBI released a fact sheet, dated 23 September and reported on 24 September, on the risks of working with third-party ICS integrators. It cites an FBI-analyzed intrusion at a US industrial automation firm in March–April 2025 in which foreign actors took roughly 800 files, including customer SCADA information, ICS device details and schematics. Recommendations: assess what data integrators hold, apply least privilege, secure and monitor remote connections, keep offline backups, write cybersecurity requirements into contracts, disconnect public-facing devices, and weigh geopolitical factors for foreign-owned providers. Coverage names power utilities and transportation as the firm’s customers; the fact sheet does not single out food and agriculture. Editorial assessment: food plants rely on integrators for line controls, batch systems and refrigeration, so the same exposure applies.
