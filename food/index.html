Top Story

Five federal agencies name food and agriculture in a warning about AI-scripted access to Siemens S7 controllers

The NSA, CISA, FBI, Department of Energy and EPA issued a joint advisory on 19 August warning that threat actors are combining the open-source snap7 and python-snap7 automation libraries with AI-assisted scripting to build custom tools that mimic legitimate OT monitoring software. Those tools give read and write access to Siemens S7 controller memory, configuration data and ladder logic over the S7comm protocol. Food and agriculture is named among the six most-targeted sectors. Actors locate exposed or poorly segmented devices using internet scanning services, then get in through default or weakly configured credentials rather than exploiting a vulnerability. Affected lines span the S7-200, S7-300, S7-400, S7-1200 and S7-1500 including F-series safety controllers. The agencies assess current activity as reconnaissance and capability development, with read access used to prepare for later disruptive write operations.

High Incident — Sector ransomware trend

Food and beverage ransomware volume is climbing, with production stoppages the recurring outcome

Trade reporting on the sector counts roughly 220 ransomware attacks against food and beverage organisations so far in 2026, carried out by 46 different groups, compared with 265 incidents across the whole of 2025. The most consequential recent case in the sector was the July attack on Fairlife, Coca-Cola’s dairy subsidiary, which suspended all US production after ransomware compromised critical production systems and forced manufacturing facilities offline while containment work proceeded. Separately, federal agencies have reported that since at least March an Iranian-affiliated group has disrupted the function of programmable logic controllers across multiple US critical infrastructure sectors including food processing facilities, causing operational disruption and financial loss. The pattern across both threads is the same: the sector’s just-in-time model and perishable product mean recovery time translates directly into scrapped inventory and missed delivery windows.

High Incident — Logistics / Cold chain

CEVA Logistics breach affects European warehouses and spills customer data through its clients

CEVA Logistics, one of the world’s largest shipping and logistics operators, was attacked between 29 July and 1 August, with the intrusion affecting at least eight warehouses across Europe. The France-headquartered company runs more than a thousand warehouses worldwide and reported 18.3 billion dollars in revenue last year. The consequences surfaced indirectly: several companies relying on CEVA for fulfilment disclosed that their customers’ personal information had been taken, including Dutch e-commerce group Bol, department store De Bijenkorf, eyewear company Ace & Tate and football club Ajax. High Research — Cold chain / refrigeration control

Refrigeration controllers can be switched off while the temperature display keeps reading normal

Claroty’s Team82 reported vulnerabilities in industrial refrigeration controllers from Danfoss and Copeland, with both vendors publishing advisories. The finding that matters operationally is not remote code execution — it is that an attacker able to reach the controller can disable cooling while the interface continues to report a normal setpoint and normal temperature. For a food processor or cold-storage operator that inverts the assumption every cold-chain procedure rests on: that the display, the logger and the alarm agree with the physical state of the room. A silent loss of refrigeration is a product-safety and recall event long before it is a security incident, and it can run for a full shift without triggering anything. There is no reported in-the-wild exploitation; both vendors have issued fixes and guidance.

Researcher-demonstrated — Claroty Team82 proof of concept; no in-the-wild exploitation reported Not in CISA KEV
Vendor advisories and fixes published by Danfoss and Copeland Action: Get an inventory of refrigeration and cold-storage controllers by make, model and firmware from your refrigeration contractor — most processors do not have one, because the racks were installed and are maintained outside the OT programme. Apply the Danfoss and Copeland fixes on the next planned maintenance visit, and remove any controller with a direct internet or cellular path. Independently of the patching, add one out-of-band temperature sensor per critical room that does not report through the same controller, so the display and physical reality can be cross-checked.

High Research — Ransomware trends

Dragos counts 70 food and beverage ransomware incidents in a single quarter

Dragos recorded 1,140 ransomware incidents involving industrial organisations in the second quarter of 2026, up 12 percent from 1,020 in the first, with manufacturing accounting for 747, construction 176, equipment manufacturing 114 and food and beverage 70. The analytical finding matters more than the count: ransomware operators do not need control system access to disrupt industrial production, because encrypting the IT systems that support the plant is regularly enough to stop the line. Extortion also continued shifting away from file encryption toward data theft. For a food processor the supporting systems are specific and unforgiving — warehouse and inventory management, label and date-code printing, lot traceability, quality and food safety records, and the systems that release product for shipment. Several of those are regulatory prerequisites for shipping at all, so an outage stops product moving even when every filler and palletiser is running normally.

Medium Research — Facility CPS exposure

Claroty finds one in five facility cyber-physical assets a single hop from internet-exposed systems

Team82 analysed more than 750,000 cyber-physical assets across large facilities and found nearly one in five sitting one hop from a system making outbound internet connections. Close to 90 percent of the building management systems examined communicated over insecure protocols and 40 percent ran outdated firmware, with around a third of HVAC and cooling systems one hop from an internet-exposed system. The study covers data centres, but the asset classes translate directly to a food plant: power distribution, building management, physical access control, fire protection and refrigeration. Refrigeration is the one that deserves separate attention here, because it is usually procured and maintained under a service contract with remote monitoring enabled by the contractor, sits outside the controls engineering asset register, and has a direct line to product safety rather than merely to uptime.

Critical Advisory — Remote access edge

Citrix is telling admins to patch NetScaler now — an unauthenticated bypass on the appliance that fronts most OT remote access

Citrix published bulletin CTX696939 on 20 August covering two NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway flaws. The serious one, CVE-2026-19490, is an authentication bypass carrying a CVSS score of 9.3: on Gateway and AAA virtual-server configurations an unauthenticated attacker can skip the login step entirely. The second, CVE-2026-19489, is a memory overflow that can produce unpredictable behaviour or a denial of service, and only applies where SIP ALG is enabled on a Large Scale NAT group. Fixed builds are 14.1-73.32 and 13.1-63.21 or later. Citrix, Rapid7 and Finland’s national cyber authority all said they were not aware of in-the-wild exploitation at publication, while urging immediate patching — NetScaler has a long record of exploited edge flaws. In food and beverage this appliance usually arrived with a managed-services contract and now fronts remote access for line OEMs, packaging and filling vendors and the corporate ERP that schedules production. An unauthenticated bypass there reaches the engineering path into filling, packaging and refrigeration control without needing a stolen password.

No known exploitation reported at publication — Citrix, Rapid7 and Finland’s NCSC all state none observed Not in CISA KEV as at 22 Aug 2026
Patch available — NetScaler ADC / Gateway 14.1-73.32 and 13.1-63.21 or later Action: Ask IT or your managed provider whether a NetScaler ADC or Gateway carries OEM, integrator or plant remote access, and get the build number rather than an assurance. Fixed builds are 14.1-73.32 and 13.1-63.21. Patch within the week; if a production window blocks it, restrict the Gateway virtual server to known source ranges immediately and require MFA. Check for authenticated sessions with no matching login event.

Medium Advisory — Life safety / facility systems

Fire-alarm management software stores its credentials in cleartext in memory, CISA warns

CISA published ICSA-26-232-01 on 20 August covering Johnson Controls Simplex Incident Manager, the software operators use to monitor and manage Simplex fire alarm panels across a property portfolio. Versions up to and including V2.01 keep user passwords and authentication tokens unencrypted in system memory while the application runs, tracked as CVE-2026-27875 and classified as CWE-316. A local attacker who already holds low-privileged access to the workstation can read those credentials out of memory and reuse them against the application and the systems it connects to. CISA lists critical manufacturing, commercial facilities, government facilities, transportation systems and energy among the affected sectors, with deployment worldwide. Johnson Controls has published remediation guidance in JCI-PSA-2026-28. Processing plants, cold stores and distribution centres run Simplex fire detection, and in food facilities the management workstation is typically a facilities or maintenance asset on the business network — shared login, physically reachable from the plant floor, and not in any OT inventory. That is the access this flaw turns into working credentials for every panel on site.

No known public exploitation — requires existing local low-privileged access Not in CISA KEV
Vendor guidance available — Johnson Controls advisory JCI-PSA-2026-28 Action: Ask maintenance or facilities whether Simplex Incident Manager runs at any of your sites and check the version against V2.01, then apply the guidance in JCI-PSA-2026-28. Until then remove shared local accounts from the host, limit interactive logon to named staff, and confirm the account it uses is not shared with your BMS, refrigeration monitoring or access control.

Critical Advisory — Exploited edge / KEV

Cisco ASA and FTD VPN zero-day exploited to reload firewalls, added to CISA KEV

Cisco has patched CVE-2026-20349, a flaw in the remote-access SSL VPN service of Secure Firewall ASA and Secure Firewall Threat Defense software that attackers are exploiting to force appliances offline. A specially crafted HTTP request to the vulnerable service causes an unexpected reload and a denial-of-service condition, requiring no authentication and no user interaction. Cisco’s PSIRT confirmed active exploitation in August, and CISA added the flaw to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue with a federal remediation deadline of 14 August. Hot fixes cover ASA 9.16, 9.18, 9.20, 9.22, 9.23 and 9.24 and FTD 7.0, 7.2, 7.4, 7.6, 7.7 and 10.0. For a processor the practical risk is losing remote support during a line stoppage: these appliances typically terminate OEM and integrator access to fillers, packaging machinery and refrigeration, and a night-shift breakdown with the machine builder unable to connect is an expensive extension of an ordinary fault.

Actively exploited — Cisco PSIRT confirmed in-the-wild attacks in August 2026 In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-20349, federal remediation deadline 14 Aug 2026
Patch available — Cisco hot fixes for ASA 9.16–9.24 and FTD 7.0–10.0 Action: Identify every ASA or FTD appliance carrying OEM, integrator or corporate access into plant networks and confirm it is on a fixed release. Where the maintenance window is weeks away, restrict the SSL VPN service to known source ranges now, and confirm your filler, packaging and refrigeration suppliers each have a documented alternative support route.

High Advisory — CISA KEV additions

Four more exploited flaws added to KEV on 18 August, including Windows IKE and VMware vCenter

CISA added four vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August: CVE-2026-33824 in Microsoft Internet Key Exchange, CVE-2026-55040 in Microsoft SharePoint, CVE-2026-59310 in Broadcom VMware vCenter and CVE-2026-65400 in Apple macOS. High Advisory — SCADA platform / deserialisation

AVEVA Enterprise SCADA can be pushed into executing code through tampered serialised data

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-01, published 13 August as part of a 15-advisory batch, covers a deserialisation flaw in AVEVA Enterprise SCADA. An attacker who already holds the “DNA Authority — Operator” privilege can tamper with serialised data and have it execute as code when the server deserialises it. The 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 release families are affected, along with certain Enterprise SCADA HMI versions. CISA reports no known public exploitation targeting this flaw. AVEVA’s guidance is to restrict operator rights to trusted users, disallow BLT test clients in production, and audit devices, network topology and perimeter defences; the vendor also points to KB117814 on removing the binary formatter. Process-side food and beverage plants and multi-site operations run Enterprise SCADA above the line controls, alongside iFIX and Ignition deployments. Code execution on that server puts an attacker level with the historians and alarm servers that batch records and cold-chain monitoring depend on. The operator-privilege requirement helps — provided control-room logins are individual rather than a shift password on a laminated card.

No known exploitation — CISA reports none targeting this flaw Not in CISA KEV
Vendor fix and mitigations available via bulletin AVEVA-2026-005 Action: Establish which AVEVA Enterprise SCADA version each plant runs and schedule the fix from bulletin AVEVA-2026-005. Until then, cut “DNA Authority — Operator” back to named individuals, remove vendor and contractor accounts that no longer need it, and confirm BLT test clients are absent from production nodes.

Low Policy — Japan / critical infrastructure

Japan drafts 150 cybersecurity measures for critical infrastructure, with recovery capability treated as the realistic goal

The Japanese government published draft guidance on 23 August setting out roughly 150 measures for operators across sixteen designated critical-infrastructure sectors, written in response to a recent run of ransomware against Japanese businesses. Two conclusions transfer cleanly to food and beverage. The draft states that closed networks disconnected from the internet “are not necessarily safe”, which is the assumption most processing plants still make about packaging lines, batching systems and refrigeration controls reached through OEM support links. And it accepts that complete prevention is unachievable, directing operators to build recovery capability and consider cyber insurance instead. For a sector where the loss is measured in scrapped product and missed retail commitments within hours rather than days, that reframing is the more useful half.

Medium Regulation — EU Cyber Resilience Act

From 11 September 2026 exploited vulnerabilities in digital products must be reported within 24 hours

The EU Cyber Resilience Act’s reporting obligations take effect on 11 September 2026. From that date, manufacturers of products with digital elements placed on the EU market must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents affecting those products: an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware, a full notification within 72 hours, and a final report within 14 days of a corrective measure being available, or within a month for a severe incident. Reporting flows through a single reporting platform to the relevant national CSIRT with simultaneous availability to ENISA, and the obligations cover products already on the EU market rather than only new releases. For food and beverage the effect is mostly on the supply side. Processing, filling and packaging equipment makers selling into the EU acquire a disclosure duty, so processors buying from European OEMs should expect faster and more formal vulnerability notification from September — and should have somewhere for it to land.

Low Policy — Joint guidance

US and Australian agencies publish guidance on isolating OT and running that way for an extended period

A joint publication from US and Australian agencies sets out how critical infrastructure operators should prepare to isolate vital operational technology and supporting systems from external connectivity, and to keep operating in that isolated state for an extended period rather than the few hours most continuity plans assume. It is guidance and creates no obligation. For a food processor its value is that it converts a confident claim — that the plant can keep running if corporate is compromised — into a dependency checklist. The items that break first are seldom drawn as control-system dependencies: time synchronisation, Active Directory authentication for HMI logins, licence servers for HMI and historian software, MES order release, and label and date-code printing. Given that product cannot legally ship without date coding and lot traceability, printing is frequently the binding constraint rather than anything mechanical.

Identify what fails after 24 hours with no corporate connectivity, then after a week, and pay specific attention to label and date-code printing and to lot traceability, since those determine whether finished product can leave the site at all.
