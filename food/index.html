High Incident — Production halt

Coca-Cola’s Fairlife suspended all US dairy production after a ransomware attack

Coca-Cola’s Fairlife dairy subsidiary suspended all US production on 16 July 2026 following a ransomware attack on production-related systems, disclosed via SEC filing. The company said product safety and quality were unaffected and engaged law enforcement and outside forensics. Whether attackers reached plant-floor control systems or only business IT was not confirmed at disclosure, and that distinction has not been publicly resolved. The Anubis ransomware group later claimed on its leak site to have locked Fairlife servers and stolen around 1 TB of data, with stolen VPN credentials cited as the suspected initial access; Coca-Cola has not confirmed the actor or the data theft, so treat the claim as unverified. Included as standing context — it is the reference case behind this month’s sector reporting.

High Incident — Cold chain

Ransomware at Nichirei disrupted a 7,000-vehicle refrigerated logistics network

A ransomware attack attributed to RansomHouse disrupted logistics and shipping systems at Japanese frozen-food group Nichirei in mid-July 2026. The company operates roughly 7,000 refrigerated vehicles across 141 sites, and the disruption caused supply shortages affecting around 5,000 clients including Japanese KFC franchises. Nichirei confirmed the breach and theft of personal data while coordinating with authorities; the RansomHouse attribution is the group’s claim rather than a confirmed finding. Cold-chain logistics is a concentration risk that rarely appears on anyone’s risk register: the physical assets are distributed across the country, but the dispatch and routing systems that make them useful are centralised, so one IT outage idles the whole fleet.

Two of Mackay Sugar’s three Queensland cane-processing mills were halted after a ransomware attack on 10 June 2026. The company restored the systems supporting cane supply, harvesting and mill operations within days; the group known as The Gentlemen, tracked by Microsoft as Storm-2697, claimed the incident but had not leaked data at the time of reporting. Agricultural processing has a hard seasonal window, which makes even a short outage disproportionately costly.

Critical Incident — OT disruption

Attackers took hands-on control of water-utility PLCs across a dozen states — locking out operators and knocking a treatment plant offline

Between 26–27 July 2026 a coordinated campaign disrupted automated controls at more than 30 Minnesota community water systems, and confirmed intrusions have since been reported across roughly a dozen states including Georgia, Michigan, South Dakota, Alabama and New Jersey. This is hands-on manipulation of control equipment, not a data breach: CISA says the actors changed PLC passwords and altered device IP addresses to lock operators out of their own equipment. In Braham, Minnesota a pump failure forced a treatment facility offline and prompted a resident conservation notice; Clayton County, Georgia issued a precautionary boil-water advisory after a pump-station disruption, later lifted. Dragos’s Rob Lee told NPR he could not recall “this many targets at once with operational impact.” Every affected utility reported that drinking water stayed safe, with staff falling back to manual operation. Federal officials have pointed to Iran-linked activity — a TLP:AMBER WaterISAC assessment reportedly found the evidence aligned with campaigns previously attributed to Iran — but no formal public attribution has been made, so treat the actor as suspected rather than confirmed.

Confirmed OT-disruptive incident — operator lockout, pump failure, plant offline across multiple utilities No single CVE — abuse of internet-exposed PLCs, default/absent credentials and weak remote access
No patch — architectural: remove OT from the internet, credential and remote-access hygiene Action: Today, not this quarter: find every controller, HMI and cellular router of yours reachable from the internet and take it off. Then change every default and shared PLC password, put remote access behind MFA and a broker, back up and hash your PLC project files and logic so you can prove and restore known-good state, and rehearse running the plant manually with the SCADA layer assumed hostile.

ABB Ability zenon ships an end-of-life MongoDB — and one of the 13 inherited CVEs is already being exploited

CISA advisory ICSA-26-218-01 bundles 13 vulnerabilities into ABB Ability zenon, all inherited from an outdated MongoDB 4.2 dependency shipped with the platform’s IIoT services. Scores run up to CVSS v3.1 7.5 / v4.0 8.7. A CVSS 10.0 command injection in Haiwell’s cloud HMI gateway hands an unauthenticated attacker root

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an OS command injection (CWE-78) in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway that scores the full CVSS 10.0. The flaw sits in the device’s Net Check function: an unauthenticated attacker can reach the cmdPing Socket.io event and execute arbitrary commands as root. No in-the-wild exploitation had been reported at publication, which is the good news — a maximum-severity, unauthenticated, internet-reachable root execution on a gateway product is exactly the profile that gets weaponised quickly once someone bothers.

An N-able N-central auth bypass was exploited, patched incompletely, then exploited again

CISA added two N-able N-central authentication-bypass flaws to KEV within two days: CVE-2026-18556 and CVE-2026-18577, both CVSS 8.2, the second being an incomplete-fix follow-on to the first. RMM platforms matter disproportionately in OT because they are how integrators, machine builders and managed-service providers reach plant equipment — often through a path that was never security-reviewed and does not appear on the network diagram.

High Threat Intel — Ransomware

Dragos counted 1,140 industrial ransomware incidents in Q2 — and not one reached stage 2 of the ICS kill chain

Dragos’s Q2 2026 industrial ransomware analysis recorded 1,140 incidents against industrial organisations, up 12% on Q1. Manufacturing absorbed 747 of them (65%), led by construction at 176 and equipment manufacturing at 114; food and beverage accounted for 70. The finding worth carrying into your own risk conversations is the negative one: Dragos found no case in Q2 where a ransomware operator reached stage 2 of the ICS Cyber Kill Chain. Production stopped because enterprise IT and ERP systems stopped — order entry, scheduling, shipping, labelling — not because anyone touched a controller. Named Q2 cases include West Pharmaceutical Services and Foxconn, the latter with a roughly two-week outage at Wisconsin and Texas sites. That pattern should shape where you spend: the loss is real, but the control is IT/OT dependency mapping and recovery, not deeper controller hardening.

High Research — Exposure

Researchers found 4,407 internet-facing Rockwell controllers — and 19 of 22 in already-attacked cities ran firmware vulnerable to a 2017 bug

A Cloud Security Alliance scan on 3 August 2026 identified 4,407 internet-facing Rockwell / Allen-Bradley controllers worldwide, 2,844 of them in the United States. The more uncomfortable finding is local: of 22 exposed controllers located in cities already hit by the July campaign, 19 were still running firmware vulnerable to CVE-2017-16740, a buffer-overflow flaw patched back in 2017. Separately, CISA Acting Director Nick Andersen said federal responders assisting with the recovery are still finding water-system PLCs reachable from the internet with factory-default credentials or no password at all. Broader counts cited during the response put roughly 10,000 Rockwell, Siemens and Schneider devices on the public internet sector-wide. The takeaway is not a novel zero-day; it is that a decade of unpatched, unauthenticated exposure is doing the work for the attacker.

High Advisory — Nation-state

The federal PLC advisory grew beyond Rockwell — Schneider Modicon and Siemens S7-1200 are now named too

CISA, the FBI, EPA and NSA updated joint advisory AA26-097A on Iran-affiliated exploitation of internet-exposed programmable logic controllers, widening the documented target set from Rockwell MicroLogix and CompactLogix to also include Schneider Electric Modicon M340 / BMX P34 and Siemens S7-1200 devices. The update adds July 2026 indicators of compromise, the MITRE ATT&CK exfiltration technique T1041, and detection guidance for malicious modification of reusable Rockwell Add-On Instruction code blocks — the logic building blocks that water-treatment and other process programs are assembled from. The advisory names energy and government facilities alongside water and wastewater as targeted sectors. It is the foundational document behind the multistate water-utility incidents, and the detection guidance is worth reading even if you run none of the three named vendors.

Active — exploitation of exposed PLCs documented by four federal agencies No CVE named in the advisory — exposure and weak authentication, not a single product flaw
Mitigations only — no universal patch; segmentation, credential and exposure controls Action: Pull the AA26-097A indicators into your monitoring and hunt against them. Specifically check whether any Add-On Instruction or reusable logic block in your controllers has been modified outside a change window, and compare running logic against your source-of-truth repository — most sites have never done this comparison and cannot currently tell.
