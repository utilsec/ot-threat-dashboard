Top Story

Fairlife restarts dairy production after ransomware halt — Coca-Cola confirms data was stolen

Coca-Cola’s Fairlife dairy has resumed the majority of production at its four US plants, closing a roughly ten-day shutdown that began with a 16 July ransomware attack — one of the year’s most consequential food and beverage incidents. On 28 July Coca-Cola said it had restored most operations and confirmed that attackers stole data during the intrusion; the Anubis ransomware group listed Fairlife on its leak site on 20 July, claiming server encryption and theft of roughly 1 TB of data. Production-related systems were among those hit, forcing a halt across US plants while Canadian operations continued and retail supply held on existing inventory. For food and ag operators the JBS lesson repeats: perishable, just-in-time output turns an IT intrusion into spoiled inventory and missed deliveries within hours. Whether ransomware crossed into OT or the stoppage was precautionary, the operational result was the same.

Medium Incident — Disruption

Cyberattack on Japanese food supplier Nichirei disrupts deliveries, including to KFC Japan

Nichirei, a major Japanese frozen- and processed-food company and logistics provider, disclosed a cyberattack that knocked out ordering and distribution systems and interrupted supplies to restaurant customers including KFC Japan. The company said it was bringing systems back online and would resume operations sequentially from 17 July, working through manual and backup processes in the interim. Nichirei has not detailed the intrusion method or confirmed ransomware, and reporting notes other Japanese food and beverage names — Asahi Group and Ezaki Glico among them — have been hit in recent months. For food and ag operators it is another reminder that an IT-side outage at a distributor or processor cascades straight into missed, time-critical deliveries.

High Incident — Nation-State

Iran-affiliated actors are disabling the PLC alarm and shutdown logic that guards food-safety interlocks

The updated federal advisory AA26-097A (22 July) confirms Iran-affiliated actors reaching internet-exposed PLCs — Rockwell, Siemens and Schneider — extracting project files and modifying or deleting control logic, and in some cases disabling shutdown and alarm functions so equipment can enter unsafe states without alerting operators. In food and beverage processing, that same class of controller enforces cook/pasteurization temperatures, CIP cycles and refrigeration interlocks. Defeated alarm logic is not just downtime — it is a food-safety exposure, which is why controller integrity belongs on the HACCP-adjacent risk list.

Active — confirmed control-logic tampering across sectors FBI/CISA/NSA — AA26-097A
No single patch — remove PLCs from internet, verify logic integrity Action: Remove processing and packaging PLCs from direct internet exposure; enforce MFA on remote/vendor access; baseline and hash PLC logic to detect tampering of safety interlocks; review logs against the July 2026 IOCs.

Medium Threat Intel — Ransomware

Ransomware on food & beverage keeps escalating — 220 tracked attacks in 2026 across 46 groups

A 3 August round-up pulls the year’s food-sector ransomware picture together: roughly 220 attacks tracked so far in 2026 across 46 distinct groups, on pace to exceed the 265 counted for all of 2025, with Qilin, Akira and CL0P among the most active and CL0P over-indexing on food and agriculture. The reporting strings together a run of high-profile hits — Coca-Cola’s Fairlife, Heineken, Campbell Soup, Dole, Krispy Kreme, Whole Foods distributor UNFI and Chick-fil-A — and Food and Ag-ISAC’s Jonathan Braley reiterates why the sector is a reliable pressure point: just-in-time, perishable supply chains feel a ransomware outage almost immediately. Most intrusions remain opportunistic — exposed systems, broker-bought access and phishing — not sector-specific targeting.

Medium Threat Intel — Sector Report

Food and Ag-ISAC: 265 ransomware attacks hit food & agriculture in 2025, led by Qilin and Akira

The Food and Ag-ISAC’s “Farm-to-Table Ransomware Realities” report (published February 2026) recorded 265 ransomware attacks against the food and agriculture sector in 2025 — an 82% year-over-year surge — with Qilin, Akira, CL0P, Play and Lynx the most active groups. The ISAC’s executive director has since confirmed awareness of the Fairlife (Coca-Cola) attack and characterized the sector as caught in broad, opportunistic ransomware targeting rather than bespoke campaigns (late-July 2026 commentary). Together the annual data and the fresh commentary make the same point: food and beverage operators are hit because exposed systems and just-in-time, perishable supply chains make them easy, high-pressure targets.

Critical Active Exploitation — KEV

FortiSandbox RCE flaws added to CISA KEV — exploited appliances on the plant IT/OT boundary

CISA added two critical Fortinet flaws, CVE-2026-39808 and CVE-2026-25089 (both CVSS 9.1), to the KEV catalog on 16 July after in-the-wild exploitation. They are OS command-injection bugs in FortiSandbox / Cloud / PaaS allowing unauthenticated command execution via crafted HTTP requests. In food and beverage plants, Fortinet appliances often guard the line between enterprise IT and processing OT; a compromised inspection appliance is a foothold and a blind spot. CISA set a 19 July federal remediation deadline.

Critical Active Exploitation — KEV

Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day (CVSS 10) exploited — added to CISA KEV

CISA added CVE-2026-16812 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 27 July after it was exploited as a zero-day. The flaw is an unauthenticated OS command-injection bug in Arista’s VeloCloud Orchestrator (on-prem SD-WAN management), rated CVSS 10.0, letting a remote attacker run arbitrary commands and seize control of the orchestrator; Arista has released fixes. For multi-plant food and beverage processors and cold-chain operators, VeloCloud commonly stitches together the WAN links between processing sites, distribution centers and remote refrigeration — so a compromised orchestrator is a single pivot into many facilities’ networks at once.

High Critical Advisory — CVE

Rockwell FactoryTalk Services Platform auth-bypass patched — food plants run Rockwell

In July’s ICS Patch Tuesday, Rockwell Automation fixed CVE-2026-10714 in the FactoryTalk Services Platform, a JWT/Okta authentication-bypass flaw carried in CISA advisory ICSA-26-202-07 (21 July) and rated CVSS v3 7.8 / v4 8.8. FactoryTalk is the backbone that ties HMI, historian and control applications together across many food and beverage lines, so an authentication bypass there could let an attacker reach production applications without valid credentials. No public exploitation has been reported; the fix is available.

Info Policy — Legislation

Farm and food cybersecurity legislation advances with risk assessments and crisis drills

Food-sector cyber provisions keep advancing on Capitol Hill: the Farm and Food Cybersecurity Act would direct USDA, with CISA, to assess sector cyber threats every two years and run annual crisis-simulation exercises for food-related emergencies, and the Senate Agriculture Committee’s 2026 farm-bill discussion draft, released 15 July, carries cybersecurity investments for agricultural facilities into farm-bill priorities alongside the Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026. For operators the direction of travel is clear: periodic risk assessment and exercised incident response are becoming baseline expectations.
