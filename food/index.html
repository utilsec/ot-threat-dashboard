Top Story

Citrix confirms two NetScaler zero-days were exploited before patches shipped; CISA adds both to KEV

Citrix confirmed on 27 September that two NetScaler ADC and Gateway remote-code-execution flaws, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (both CVSS v4 9.5), were exploited in the wild before fixes were released, and CISA listed both in its KEV catalog the same day. Neither has a workaround, so only upgrading to fixed builds closes the exposure, and CISA advises preserving forensic evidence first because patching cannot remove an earlier compromise. Food and beverage plants commonly let integrators, co-packers and equipment vendors reach line controllers, refrigeration monitoring and batch systems through gateways like these, so a compromised appliance can become the first hop into production networks. Citrix has not said who is exploiting the flaws or how widely.

Critical Active exploitation — Firewall management platform, CVE-2026-20079 in CISA KEV

Cisco Talos: crimeware, state-sponsored and Qilin-deploying clusters all exploited Secure Firewall Management Center

Talos reported on 9 September, updating on 21 September, that Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center is under active exploitation through CVE-2026-20079 (authentication bypass to root, CVSS 10.0) and CVE-2026-20316 (static credential, CVSS 5.3). It tracks three clusters: UAT-12197, crimeware using CVE-2026-20079 for web shells and credential theft; UAT-11823, described as state-sponsored with Sandworm links, which deployed Cyclops Blink; and UAT-11988, a ransomware operator that used CVE-2026-20316 and deployed Qilin after living-off-the-land reconnaissance. Cisco released hotfixes, with a fuller hardening patch scheduled for the week of 16 September. CISA added CVE-2026-20079 to KEV on 9 September; we did not verify a KEV listing for CVE-2026-20316. Editorial assessment: Qilin is the most active group against food and agriculture in the sector’s own data, and the management console holds the segmentation policy between IT and plant networks.

High Background — The sector’s clearest confirmed production-halting ransomware case this year

Coca-Cola’s Fairlife: ransomware suspended US dairy production for roughly 11 days and data was stolen

Background (July 2026), not a new incident: Coca-Cola disclosed on 16 July that fairlife had identified unauthorized third-party access to part of its systems, including production-related systems, and that US production was temporarily suspended; it said product quality and safety were not affected. The Anubis ransomware group claimed the attack, said it encrypted Nutanix systems and took about 1 TB, and its data was reported published when its deadline lapsed around 27 July, the day Coca-Cola confirmed data theft and said most US production had resumed. The initial-access route has not been disclosed, and the company has not said whether plant-floor control systems were reached. A 25 September Zscaler analysis (vendor blog) argues that perishability and uncertainty about system integrity, not equipment damage, drive food-plant shutdowns. Carried as context: no confirmed OT-disruptive incident at a food or agriculture operator was found in the past week.

NCC Group: August ransomware hit a 2026 high of 1,073 attacks, with industrials at 31% and Qilin leading

NCC Group’s review of August, reported by Industrial Cyber on 28 September, counted 1,073 ransomware attacks, up 12% from July’s 960 and the highest monthly total this year. Industrial organizations took 329 attacks (31%), North America 473 (44%), and Qilin led all groups with 164 attacks (15%), passing The Gentlemen. The public breakdown does not isolate food and agriculture, so treat the figures as backdrop rather than a sector count; the Food and Ag-ISAC’s own data below is the better yardstick. Medium Threat actor profile — Cross-sector ransomware affiliate (IT-side)

Microsoft: one ransomware affiliate rotates Qilin, DragonForce, Anubis and BERT, and lists food and agriculture among victim sectors

Microsoft Threat Intelligence published a Storm-2570 profile on 24 September describing one affiliate that deployed four ransomware families — Qilin, DragonForce, Anubis and BERT — with largely the same toolkit: remote-management tools (MeshAgent, Atera, ScreenConnect, Splashtop, NinjaRMM), PsExec, Impacket and NetExec for lateral movement, Mimikatz-style credential theft, and s5cmd and Rclone for exfiltration. Microsoft lists food and agriculture among the affected sectors. Its recommended controls include tamper protection, attack surface reduction rules blocking PsExec and WMI lateral movement, MFA on approved remote-management tools, and credential resets if unauthorized installs appear. Anubis is also the brand that claimed the Fairlife attack; no link between the two is asserted.

Observed activity — Microsoft profiles real intrusions; victims are not named and no single CVE is involved Not tied to a single CVE
No patch applicable — detection and hardening guidance Action: Alert on unapproved installs of remote-management tools and on PsExec use across IT servers that bridge to plant networks; enable tamper protection on endpoint security and restrict RMM tools to an approved, MFA-protected list.

High Research — Food and Ag-ISAC sector baseline

Food and Ag-ISAC: sector ransomware up 62% through July; federal agencies confirmed attacks on exposed processing and grain-handling PLCs

The Food and Ag-ISAC’s State of the Threat report (3 September) counted 227 ransomware incidents against the sector through July versus 140 a year earlier (+62%), led by Qilin (47), The Gentlemen (31) and Akira (14). It says federal agencies have confirmed attacks on internet-exposed PLCs used in processing lines and grain handling, and describes Iran as blurring state operations and hacktivism while willing to target food security. Other themes: AI shortening the gap between disclosure and exploitation, nation-state positioning in OT, hacktivist escalation, and ClickFix social engineering (up 108% in the first half). In an 8 September Industrial Cyber feature, the ISAC’s Jonathan Braley said 247 incidents had been tracked through August, none resulting in food supply contamination. Editorial note: the public federal Iran-affiliated PLC advisory coverage we reviewed names water, energy and government facilities, so the food and grain-handling detail rests on the ISAC’s report.

Critical Advisory — Field-I/O firmware, about 20 CVEs, no known exploitation

IO-Link masters from three vendors share about 20 flaws, two rated CVSS 9.8; Nozomi details the attack chain

CERT@VDE published three advisories on 16 September — VDE-2026-014 (Pepperl+Fuchs ICE2/ICE3), VDE-2026-027 (Phoenix Contact IOL MA8) and VDE-2026-028 (Carlo Gavazzi YL212/YN115) — covering the same firmware family (versions below 1.7.4) and roughly 20 CVEs each, including two rated CVSS 9.8: an authentication bypass (CVE-2026-27546) and a malicious file upload leading to root code execution (CVE-2026-27565); ten more score 8.8. High Advisory — Rockwell Automation software and controllers, no known exploitation

Rockwell patches over a dozen flaws in RSLinx Classic, Logix controllers and FactoryTalk software

Rockwell Automation’s early-September advisories, summarized by SecurityWeek on 2 September, cover RSLinx Classic (four critical and high-severity denial-of-service issues that crash the service until restart), a high-severity DoS flaw in ControlLogix and CompactLogix controllers (CVE-2026-9637), a high-severity remote code execution flaw in FactoryTalk Historian Machine Edition, a privilege flaw in FactoryTalk Activation Manager, and an arbitrary-code-execution issue in the ControlFLASH utility; ArmorStart motor controllers and 1756-ENBT modules are also listed. Info Policy — NIST draft OT security guide, comment period open

NIST’s draft SP 800-82 Rev. 4 adds food and agriculture to its OT security guide; comments due 30 November

NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Rev. 4, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, on 21 September. The draft broadens sector coverage to building automation, water and wastewater, food and agriculture, freight rail and maritime, plus industrial IoT and cloud convergence. It is restructured around Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, including the Govern function, and expands guidance on asset management, network monitoring, detection and zero-trust architecture, with an appendix aligning to the Risk Management Framework. It is guidance, not a mandate, and comments are open through 30 November 2026.

High Guidance — Third-party ICS integrator access (CISA/FBI fact sheet)

CISA and the FBI urge critical-infrastructure operators to put ICS integrators on least privilege and monitored remote access

CISA and the FBI released a fact sheet, dated 23 September and reported on 24 September, on the risks of working with third-party ICS integrators. It cites an FBI-analyzed intrusion at a US industrial automation firm in March–April 2025 in which foreign actors took roughly 800 files, including customer SCADA information, ICS device details and schematics. Recommendations: assess what data integrators hold, apply least privilege, secure and monitor remote connections, keep offline backups, write cybersecurity requirements into contracts, disconnect public-facing devices, and weigh geopolitical factors for foreign-owned providers. Coverage names power utilities and transportation as the firm’s customers; the fact sheet does not single out food and agriculture. Editorial assessment: food plants rely on integrators for line controls, batch systems and refrigeration, so the same exposure applies.
