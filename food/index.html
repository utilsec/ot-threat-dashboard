French supermarket refrigeration attack: CERT-FR still says campaigns are probably ongoing, and Forescout counts nearly 13,000 exposed refrigeration services worldwide

Forescout reported on 22 October that it had found 12,976 internet-exposed refrigeration management services on 6,017 IP addresses in 99 countries, most of them from the Danfoss and Copeland/Dixell product families named in the French supermarket attack. Over the nights of 11–13 October, attackers remotely took over refrigeration controllers and forced continuous or extended defrost at about 700 French stores, according to Finance Minister Roland Lescure; Carrefour, Coopérative U, E.Leclerc, Intermarché/Netto and Monoprix confirmed impact, stock spoiled, and the Paris prosecutor reports fires. CERT-FR’s alert CERTFR-2026-ALE-012 names Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC controllers and, since 13 October, Copeland and Dixell XWEB supervisors; its latest version, from 16 October, still says the campaigns are “probably still ongoing,” though no new attack nights have been reported since 13 October. About 80% of the Danfoss StoreView services Forescout observed run firmware older than 4.3.1. No group has claimed the attacks, no entry route has been confirmed, and the prosecutor’s cybercrime section is investigating with OFAC and ANSSI.

High Incident — Grocery retail and distribution (Netherlands, Belgium, US); company-confirmed malicious attack on IT systems; not a confirmed OT compromise

Ahold Delhaize confirms a malicious attack and restores online services; Albert Heijn home deliveries restart on 23 October

Ahold Delhaize said on 19 October that outside experts had confirmed the suspicious activity in its IT systems resulted from “malicious actions of others.” It notified the Dutch NCSC within 24 hours, has filed a police report and has no evidence so far that sensitive customer, associate or partner data was taken; all stores stayed open throughout. The company had taken systems offline as a precaution from 12 October, taking down websites and apps across most of its European and US brands, and an Albert Heijn statement reported by NL Times said its distribution centres had to be restarted on 13–14 October, leaving fresh-food shelves empty until deliveries resumed on 15 October. High Active campaign — FortiGate firewall and SSL VPN credential theft (FBI/USSS); food and agriculture among targeted sectors; no CVE

FBI and Secret Service: the FortiBleed credential-theft campaign against Fortinet firewalls is still active, and food and agriculture is among its targets

The FBI and US Secret Service warned on Tuesday 6 October that the FortiBleed campaign against internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPNs remains active. There is no CVE: the attackers reuse leaked or stolen credentials, crack passwords kept in legacy SHA-256 storage and intercept authentication traffic, then add admin accounts and sometimes delete or reset the owners’ accounts, locking them out. The agencies say the targets span all 16 US critical-infrastructure sectors, including energy and food and agriculture, and the FBI links the access to Payload ransomware; SOCRadar says it has confirmed at least 12 ransomware deployments from it. SOCRadar’s figure of 86,644 working device credentials across 194 countries dates from 19 June and is not a new count. No compromise of an OT system has been described. Editorial assessment: FortiGate firewalls often sit at the IT/OT boundary of processing plants and cold stores, so a hijacked admin account there can rewrite the segmentation that protects production.

Confirmed active campaign — FBI/USSS joint advisory; the SOCRadar figures and the Russian-speaking access-broker profile are researcher assessments, not agency attribution No CVE — credential abuse, not a product flaw
No patch — move admin credentials to PBKDF2 storage (FortiOS 7.2.11+), reset passwords and add MFA Action: Restrict FortiGate management to a dedicated admin network, end active sessions and reset VPN and admin passwords, enforce phishing-resistant MFA on SSL VPN, review admin accounts and API keys for unfamiliar names (reported examples include fortiAdmin and forticloud-sync), and move admin credentials to PBKDF2 storage.
Suggested SLA: Session reset and account review — 72 hours; MFA and credential-storage changes — 14 days Owner:

High Joint advisory — SafePay ransomware (#StopRansomware AA26-293A); cross-sector; no food-sector victim named

CISA, FBI and partners: SafePay ransomware has hit at least 600 organizations since August 2024, getting in through stolen VPN logins and edge-device flaws

Advisory AA26-293A, dated 20 October and co-sealed by CISA, the FBI, the US Secret Service, Germany’s LKA Rheinland-Pfalz, the UK National Crime Agency and the North West Regional Organised Crime Unit, says SafePay has hit at least 600 organizations in multiple critical-infrastructure sectors since August 2024, in the Americas, Europe and Australia. SafePay, reported to operate as a closed group without affiliates, steals data and threatens to leak it as well as encrypting. It gets in with exposed or brute-forced credentials, VPN logins (notably from Mullvad addresses), phished accounts and automated exploitation of older edge-device flaws, including CVE-2020-3259 and CVE-2023-20269; once inside it uses WinRM, PsExec and RDP, moves data out with tools such as RClone, WinSCP and FileZilla, deletes shadow copies and resets NAS backup devices. The published coverage names no food or agriculture victims and describes no OT impact. Editorial assessment: food and agriculture is one of the critical-infrastructure sectors, and the NAS-reset step matters to plants that keep line, recipe or HMI backups on network storage.

Confirmed campaign — joint government advisory; the 600-plus count and the tactics are the agencies’; no food-sector victim named and no OT disruption reported No new KEV action — the advisory cites older edge-device flaws (CVE-2020-3259, CVE-2023-20269)

Medium Research — IoT and edge-device botnet (Nozomi Networks Labs); no OT impact reported

Nozomi: the Cling botnet hides its command traffic in STUN requests and spreads through old router, camera and DVR flaws

Nozomi Networks Labs reported on 1 October that a botnet it calls Cling infects routers, access points, repeaters, DVRs and cameras, mainly through CVE-2021-35394 in the Realtek Jungle SDK, and carries exploits for seven other flaws, including CVE-2014-8361, CVE-2023-26801 and CVE-2024-3721. High Active exploitation — Siemens Software Center DLL side-loading flaw (CVE-2026-110936) used in a ClickFix campaign; in CISA KEV; Windows PCs

Siemens Software Center flaw is exploited in a ClickFix malware campaign; CISA adds it to KEV with a 3 November deadline

Siemens confirmed on 19 October that attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-110936 (CVSS v3.1 6.7; v4.0 8.4) in Siemens Software Center, its tool for installing and updating Siemens software. The program did not restrict where it looks for helper DLLs, so attackers bundle a genuine, validly signed copy with their own DLLs and get victims to run the bundle through a ClickFix lure; Siemens ties the activity to what it calls the Romulus multi-platform ClickFix campaign and names no final malware family. Siemens says no Siemens system, product or update channel was breached. Every version before V4.0.9.8 is affected; that version fixes the flaw, and existing installations update automatically on their next start. CISA added the CVE to KEV on 20 October with a 3 November federal deadline and republished Siemens’ advisory as ICSA-26-295-03 on 22 October. Editorial assessment: Siemens software is common on engineering workstations in food and beverage plants, where a signed Siemens program may not draw attention; the attack still needs a user to run the lure, so ClickFix awareness matters as much as the update.

PHP2Shell reaches OT products: Schneider, ABB and Moxa publish notices, and one Schneider engineering tool has no fix yet

Two unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaws in PHP, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 (“PHP2Shell”, CVSS v4.0 9.2 as rated by the PHP team), were fixed on 21 October in PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35 after details leaked early. Amazon says exploitation attempts began on 17 October, four days before the patches, and attempts now run into the millions, but no confirmed successful compromise has been reported and the flaws are not in CISA’s KEV catalog.
