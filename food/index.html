Top Story

The water-utility PLC campaign is the clearest preview yet of what food and agriculture’s own exposed controllers could face

Since around 26–27 July 2026, actors have reached internet-exposed Rockwell Automation and Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 controllers at water and wastewater utilities in at least 12 states, changing device IP addresses and setting unauthorised passwords to lock operators out of monitoring and control. More than 30 Minnesota community systems were affected, including a brief state of emergency in Maple Plain and a pump failure and conservation order in Braham; Clayton County, Georgia issued a boil-water advisory after a pump-station failure; Michigan (nine systems), Rapid City, South Dakota, and, most recently, New Jersey (Cape May, Woodbine) and Alabama (Childersburg water, sewer and gas) have also been hit. Officials say drinking-water safety has not been compromised at any affected utility. Joint Cybersecurity Advisory AA26-097A — FBI, CISA, NSA, EPA, DOE, USCYBERCOM’s Cyber National Mission Force and Treasury, updated 22 July — assesses that Iranian-affiliated CyberAv3ngers, also known as the Shahid Kaveh Group and affiliated with the IRGC’s Cyber Electronic Command, is conducting the activity; that is an official assessment, not proven attribution, and it does not individually cover the newest New Jersey and Alabama cases. No CVE was exploited — the method is exposure plus default, weak or absent credentials on internet-reachable controllers, using the vendor’s own PLC programming software.

No food or agriculture facility has been named as a victim in this campaign. It leads this edition anyway because the mechanism transfers directly: food and beverage plants, grain elevators, feed mills and dairy and meat processors run process water intake, clean-in-place (CIP) and effluent or wastewater treatment on the same discontinued Rockwell/Allen-Bradley MicroLogix family the water sector is being hit through, and Forescout counted thousands of exposed units of that exact family online worldwide. The same technique — find it on the internet, log in with a default or shared password, change the IP or lock out the operator — requires no new tooling and no food-sector-specific vulnerability to reach a plant’s own water or CIP controller instead of a utility’s. Nothing here establishes that a food or agriculture operator has been probed or targeted; it is a shared-exposure argument, not a reported intrusion into this sector.

Check your own IP ranges against Shodan/Censys, change every default or shared password this week, and put the controller behind a firewall — Rockwell’s own SD1790 advisory says a password change alone is not sufficient.

Medium Incident — Data breach

A logistics-provider breach at CEVA shows how one distribution partner can stall shipments across food and retail supply chains

CEVA Logistics disclosed an intrusion, dated 29 July 2026, that disrupted European contract logistics and stopped outbound shipments from eight warehouses; customers were notified 1 August and the full scale became public the week of 10 August. Air, ocean, ground and rail transport were unaffected, and CEVA says personal data — names, addresses, phone numbers, email and order details — was compromised, but payment and credential data were not. Downstream retailers named include Bol, De Bijenkorf, Ace & Tate, Pokémon Center (UK/Germany, some orders cancelled), Valve/Steam, Ajax and ING; the Dutch data protection authority is investigating and no group has claimed the intrusion. CEVA and comparable contract-logistics and 3PL providers also run cold-chain and food distribution warehousing across Europe, so an eight-warehouse outbound stoppage at one provider is a direct illustration of how a single logistics partner can halt shipments for food and beverage brands that outsource cold storage and distribution. Reporting describes IT systems and shipment processing being affected; no OT or warehouse-automation compromise has been confirmed, but it has not been explicitly ruled out either.

Confirmed intrusion, contained — customer data and shipments affected at eight warehouses No CVE confirmed as the entry point
No patch — third-party/vendor risk management, not a fix Action: Ask your cold-chain and distribution partners directly whether an IT outage at their warehouses would halt your outbound shipments, and put a contractual incident-notification SLA in place — CEVA’s public disclosure reportedly trailed the intrusion by more than a week.

Critical Advisory — Siemens

A maximum-severity flaw in Siemens’ SIMATIC IoT2050 edge gateway allows unauthenticated code execution at full privilege

CVE-2026-58115, CVSS 10.0, Siemens ProductCERT advisory SSA-834709: missing authentication (CWE-306) in the Node-RED interface on SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced (6ES7647-0BA00-1YA2) running Industrial OS. The Node-RED HTTP interface enforces no authentication at all, so an unauthenticated remote attacker can create flows and execute code at maximum privilege. Siemens has fixed it in V4.3.4.1; interim options are removing Node-RED or hardening it per Node-RED’s own user guide. IoT2050 is marketed as an edge-computing gateway for bridging PLC and plant-floor data to enterprise systems — exactly the role it would play on a packaging or filling line, grain-handling telemetry, or a dairy or meat plant’s data-collection network. No exploitation has been confirmed as of this window, and it is not yet listed in CISA KEV.

No confirmed exploitation — vendor-disclosed via ProductCERT Not listed in CISA KEV as of this edition

Critical Advisory — Haiwell

An unauthenticated command-injection flaw gives root access to Haiwell’s cloud HMI gateway

CVE-2026-19188, CVSS 10.0 (v3.1 and v4.0): OS command injection (CWE-78) in the Net Check feature’s cmdPing Socket.io event, reachable via the /setting endpoint, giving an unauthenticated attacker root-level remote code execution. Published 14 August 2026. Version 3.40.1.12 is vulnerable; fixed in 3.50.1.19 or later. NVD is the citable source — no ICSA advisory number could be independently confirmed for this one. Gateways like this sit directly between operators and process equipment, and grain elevators, feed mills and mid-size dairy or beverage lines that lean on low-cost, cloud-connected HMI/gateway products for remote monitoring are exactly the kind of internet-facing device this flaw targets — a compromised unit hands an attacker root, not just a view of the process.

Critical Advisory — Johnson Controls & Siemens

CISA’s 15-advisory day included critical flaws in the building-automation and access-control systems that guard plants and cold storage

CISA released 15 ICS advisories in a single day, 13 August 2026, spanning AVEVA, Hitachi Energy, ANDRITZ, Siemens and Johnson Controls. Two are directly relevant to food and agriculture facilities’ physical plant. Johnson Controls C-CURE 9000 and victor access-control software carry two critical unauthenticated deserialization bugs (CVE-2026-21655, CVE-2026-21653) plus a high-severity issue (CVE-2026-34496), CVSS up to 9.6 — a network-adjacent attacker could execute code and take over the system controlling badge readers and doors; the advisory was updated 11 August. Siemens Desigo DXR2 and PXC3/4/5/7 building controllers (CVE-2026-59693, CVSS 4.3) can be knocked unresponsive by a malformed BACnet packet until manually reset; firmware updates are available for both product lines. Desigo-class controllers also run HVAC and, at some plants, refrigeration zones — a stalled controller during a cold-chain-critical period is an operational problem, not just a security one.

No confirmed exploitation of either product line reported Neither listed in CISA KEV as of this edition
Fixed — JC C-CURE/victor updated 11 Aug; Desigo firmware updates available Action: If C-CURE 9000 or victor controls badge access to your plant floor or cold storage, patch it before anything else on this list — deserialization RCE on an access-control system is a physical-security failure. Separately confirm any Desigo controllers tied to refrigeration or HVAC zones are on fixed firmware and have a documented manual-reset procedure.

High Advisory — CISA KEV

An actively exploited Cisco ASA/FTD flaw crashing VPN firewalls sits on the perimeter of many food and beverage plant networks

CVE-2026-20349, CVSS 8.6: a heap-inspection denial-of-service flaw in the Remote Access SSL VPN service, actively exploited to crash firewalls, added to CISA KEV on 11 August 2026 with a 14 August federal remediation deadline. Hotfixes are available for all affected 9.x/7.x/10.x branches; there is no workaround. Cisco ASA/FTD is commonly deployed as the IT/OT boundary device and remote-access VPN concentrator at food and beverage plants, including the link refrigeration contractors and automation vendors use for remote support — a crashed firewall at that boundary can cut off both an attacker and the legitimate remote access someone needs to diagnose a chiller or line fault.

Medium Research — Ransomware

Dragos counted 70 food and beverage ransomware incidents in a record quarter for industrial ransomware

Dragos’ Q2 2026 Industrial Ransomware Analysis, published 10 August, logged 1,140 ransomware incidents against industrial organisations, up 12% on Q1; manufacturing accounted for 747 of the 1,140 (65%), food and beverage for 70. The most active groups were Qilin, Akira, The Gentlemen, DragonForce and LockBit 5.0. Dragos’ key finding: most intrusions cascaded into OT or production disruption through IT-side encryption, not direct control-system manipulation — no Stage 2 ICS Kill Chain activity was observed in the quarter. As a labelled example of that pattern, Dragos cited Mackay Sugar’s shutdown of two Queensland mills in June 2026 — outside this reporting window — where enterprise-IT ransomware halted production without the intrusion ever reaching the ICS layer. The same shape describes Coca-Cola’s fairlife dairy ransomware disruption, which halted US production in mid-July 2026: also outside this window, not itself one of Dragos’ cited examples, but the sector’s most visible recent instance of the pattern the report describes.

Active — sustained ransomware volume against the sector, 70 confirmed incidents this quarter No single CVE — multiple criminal groups, IT-side encryption is the mechanism
No patch — backup, segmentation and recovery-time planning Action: Use Dragos’ Stage-2-absent finding to your advantage: because these intrusions are not reaching ICS, a hardened IT/OT boundary plus tested offline backups of recipe, labelling and quality-release systems is what actually shortens recovery. Pressure-test that assumption at one plant this quarter rather than assuming segmentation alone will hold.

High Research — Exposure

Forescout counted 4,407 exposed Rockwell/Allen-Bradley controllers worldwide — the same legacy family the water campaign is exploiting

Forescout Vedere Labs found 4,407 internet-exposed Rockwell/Allen-Bradley controllers worldwide, 2,844 of them in the US, dominated by discontinued legacy MicroLogix 1400/1100 units — including 22 in cities that were hit by the water-sector attacks. Intruders in that campaign reconfigured devices directly rather than exploiting a new flaw, which means an exposure count like this maps closely to actual risk rather than theoretical risk. This is the same controller family food and agriculture operators rely on in grain elevators, feed mills and older processing lines, and the count is a floor, not a ceiling — it reflects only what internet-wide scanning found, not what a plant’s own network audit would turn up.

Medium Intel — Ransomware-as-a-Service

A joint advisory details Gunra, a Conti-derived ransomware operation that leans on unpatched VPN gateways

CISA, the FBI and international partners issued a #StopRansomware advisory on Gunra (AA26-222A, 10 August) — a Conti-derived ransomware-as-a-service operation running double extortion against government and critical infrastructure targets. Initial access is typically via unpatched internet-facing VPN gateways and exposed RDP; the advisory cites Fortinet flaws CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472 as examples. Reported victims face a five-to-seven-day payment deadline. No food or agriculture victim is named in the advisory; it is included here because VPN gateways and remote-access appliances are exactly what food and ag operators use for OEM and refrigeration-contractor remote support, and Gunra’s initial-access pattern is generic enough to reach any exposed instance.

Info Policy — Water & wastewater

A new federal water-cybersecurity bill would not directly cover food plants — but it signals where mandatory-assessment obligations may be headed

Senators Klobuchar and Schiff introduced the Water Cyber Shield Act on 13 August 2026 in direct response to the utility attacks: it would give EPA authority for mandatory cybersecurity assessments, provide $300 million a year in state revolving-fund support, and add a cyber-risk requirement to resilience planning for large water systems. The bill targets public water utilities, not food or beverage processors. But many large dairy, meat, beverage and grain-processing plants operate their own permitted water intake and wastewater or CIP-discharge systems, sometimes co-located with or drawing from the same municipal infrastructure this bill targets — and, per Rockwell’s SD1790 advisory and Forescout’s exposure count, the controller families and exposure patterns are identical. If EPA’s assessment authority and funding model take hold for water utilities, self-supplied industrial water systems are a plausible next target for similar obligations, though nothing currently pending applies to them directly.
