Top Story

Still the only confirmed water OT attack: hackers altered pump cycles and disabled alarms at two small Colorado systems

Colorado Gov. Jared Polis’s office confirmed on 18 September that foreign actors breached two small, privately owned water systems, each serving fewer than 200 people, in late August. The attackers changed equipment settings, disabled remote access and alarms, and altered pumping cycles. The office says the disruptions were brief and caused no known impact to water treatment, water quality or public safety. It cannot confirm which foreign actors were involved; it points to an Iranian-backed effort against US drinking water systems that federal agencies are tracking, but no Colorado link has been confirmed. Industrial Cyber cites EPA as reporting more than 100 drinking water and wastewater systems in 12 states targeted this year, and CISA’s 22 July update to advisory AA26-097A names Rockwell/Allen-Bradley, Schneider and Siemens PLCs as targets. No new victims, access method or attribution have been reported since 22 September. It leads this edition because it remains the one confirmed manipulation of water-system controls in the past 30 days.

High Incident — Ransomware intrusion at a water utility (IT-side), vendor-reported

Symantec: a China-nexus Warlock ransomware crew breached a water utility through on-premises SharePoint

Symantec’s threat hunters reported on 1 October that a China-nexus threat actor it calls Longlegs (also tracked as Storm-2603) attacked at least four organizations in the past two months: a water utility, a telecom provider, a regional government body and a university, in Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking countries across Europe, Africa and Latin America. None is named. The group typically gets in by exploiting on-premises Microsoft SharePoint Server; the only CVEs Symantec names are the 2025 “ToolShell” flaws, plus related SharePoint bugs. It then uses a vulnerable signed driver to switch off security tools, a Visual Studio Code tunnel for persistence and the domain SYSVOL share to push ransomware. In Symantec’s detailed case study, an intrusion at an unnamed critical-infrastructure organization, Warlock reached at least 33 hosts; the report does not say whether that victim was the water utility, and it describes no OT or operational impact. In our assessment, a domain-wide ransomware push is exactly where a flat or over-trusted link between utility IT and SCADA turns an office outage into an operations problem.

Low Incident — Municipal ransomware disrupts utility bill payments; no OT impact reported

Vicksburg, Mississippi ransomware takes city systems offline and disrupts utility bill payments

Vicksburg Mayor Willis Thompson said in a statement on 1 October that a ransomware attack forced the city to take systems offline temporarily, disrupting its utility payment systems; emergency services were not affected. He said no one’s service will be cut off and no late-payment penalties will be charged while the investigation runs, and that the city is still working out whether personal or confidential information was compromised. High Incident — Exploited network switch, CISA KEV

996 Zyxel GS1900 network switches compromised in 48 countries through a flaw now in CISA KEV

Help Net Security reported on 22 September that 996 Zyxel GS1900-series smart managed switches in 48 countries had been compromised through CVE-2026-7273, a stack-based buffer overflow in the web interface that lets an unauthenticated attacker on the local network run operating-system commands. Firmware 2.90(XXXX.1)C0 and earlier is affected; Zyxel shipped a fix in June. The attacks date to around mid-August, and stolen data included configuration files and hashed credentials, with 564 victims still on factory-default credentials. The actor is described only as Chinese-speaking and linked to earlier WordPress and Gitea exploitation. CISA lists the flaw in KEV with a 24 September federal deadline, now passed. In our assessment, low-cost managed switches like this are common at small utilities’ remote and treatment sites, where they network PLCs, HMIs and cellular modems.

Medium Research — Credential exposure in the water sector

Infostealer logs expose OT and remote-access logins at 258 US water utilities and vendors

SpyCloud published research on 20 September examining 10,000 water-sector organizations drawn from the 66,845 EPA-registered US water systems. It found 258 with credentials for OT or remote-access systems in infostealer data, including TeamViewer, LogMeIn and GoToMyPC logins, SonicWall and Fortinet management portals, roughly 167 metering-vendor accounts from one infected device at a metering technology provider, and one small district’s shared “scada@” mailbox with a saved remote-desktop credential. It is exposure, not confirmed intrusion, and SpyCloud ties it to no actor. For small systems that lean on vendor remote-access accounts, a password stolen from someone else’s computer is an entry point the utility cannot see.

High Guidance — CISA/FBI on third-party ICS integrators

CISA and FBI warn operators to tighten control of ICS integrators’ access

CISA and the FBI released a fact sheet on 24 September on the risks of working with third-party industrial control system integrators. It draws on an FBI analysis of a 2025 intrusion at a US automation firm serving power utilities and transportation customers, in which actors searched for SCADA and customer records and staged nine archives holding about 800 files of network schematics, device configurations and customer details. Water is not among the named sectors, but small utilities that rely on one integrator for PLC and SCADA work share the same exposure. The agencies recommend least-privilege access, cybersecurity terms in contracts, monitored on-demand remote access, and disconnecting devices from the public internet.

Critical Advisory — Three exploited NetScaler zero-days, CISA KEV; remote-access edge

Citrix NetScaler: a third exploited zero-day enters CISA KEV with a 7 October deadline, and last week’s patch does not evict intruders

Citrix confirmed on 27 September that two NetScaler ADC and Gateway flaws, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS v4 9.5 each), were exploited before patches existed, and CISA added both to KEV the same day. Investigators at Google’s threat intelligence group and LevelBlue found web shells, a rogue superuser account and stolen configuration archives, and Mandiant warns that upgrading alone does not remove that access or undo stolen credentials. Mandiant counts dozens of victim organizations, Arctic Wolf at least 78, and researcher Kevin Beaumont over 100 by his own tracking; Mandiant calls the actors suspected state-sponsored and names no group. A third flaw, CVE-2026-88779, is a memory overflow on appliances configured for SAML; Citrix confirmed targeted attacks that can crash appliances when it released fixes, and CISA added it to KEV on 4 October with a 7 October deadline. One researcher reported signs of code execution on patched honeypots; Citrix and watchTowr describe a crash, and watchTowr suspects attackers use it to knock appliances over and speed up exploitation of CVE-2026-88771. No water-sector victim has been reported; WaterISAC alerted members on 28 September. NetScaler often fronts VPN and vendor remote access into utility networks, so an unhunted appliance is a possible foothold toward SCADA (editorial assessment).

High Advisory — Two more exploited edge zero-days in CISA KEV: FortiMail and Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager

FortiMail and Cisco SD-WAN Manager zero-days join CISA’s exploited list; Fortinet now lists fixed releases

Two more internet-facing products were confirmed exploited in the past week. Fortinet advisory FG-IR-26-175, published 1 October, covers CVE-2026-104286 (CVSS 9.8), a path-traversal flaw in the web interface of FortiMail’s identity-based encryption feature that lets an unauthenticated attacker write arbitrary files on the system. Fortinet says it has been reported exploited in the wild, and CISA added it to KEV on 1 October with a 4 October deadline. Fortinet’s 5 October update lists fixed releases 7.4.9, 7.6.7 and 8.0.2; the 7.2 branch must move to 7.4 or later. Cisco disclosed on 30 September that CVE-2026-76504 (CVSS 9.8) in Catalyst SD-WAN Manager lets a remote attacker bypass an authentication rule on an API endpoint and gain administrator access; Cisco says it saw active exploitation in September, CISA listed it the same day with a 3 October deadline, and Cisco revised its advisory on 2 October. Neither vendor has named an actor or victims. In our assessment, FortiMail sits in the corporate mail path that utility staff and vendors rely on, and an SD-WAN manager can control the links to treatment plants and remote pumping sites.

MikroTik RouterOS: three flaws now in CISA KEV, and a new pre-authentication CVSS 9.8 bug in a 29 September CISA advisory

MikroTik routers are widely used at small utilities’ remote sites and as cellular or VPN gateways (editorial assessment).

NIST’s draft OT security guide adds water and wastewater; comments open until 30 November

NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Rev. 4, “Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security”, on 21 September 2026.
