Remote Access Contractor and provider tools keep turning up as the way in

Microsoft said on 21 October that it assesses Storm-0940, which it calls China-state aligned, was behind the ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise, reaching customers’ Microsoft 365 tenants through the vendor’s own application identities; a separate password-spray campaign did not succeed. Microsoft Security
AI & Exploits AI makes skilled attackers faster, and OT defenders lose their time cushion

Waterfall Security’s Andrew Ginter argued on 25 October, using PHP2Shell as his example, that frontier AI now handles the slow exploit-building work that once made old memory-handling flaws look harmless, amplifying skilled attackers rather than replacing them. Attacks such as Ukraine’s in 2015 took months of target study, giving defenders time to notice; he expects AI to shrink that window and recommends Cyber-Informed Engineering to remove attack paths that could lead to unacceptable physical consequences. Claroty’s Team82 showed on 13 October that an AI coding agent, steered by a researcher, could rebuild known Danfoss controller exploits in about 28 minutes for roughly $6.

High Incident — Cross-sector OT attack on remotely managed controllers (food retail, France); confirmed by French authorities

Attackers took over internet-reachable refrigeration controllers at about 700 French supermarkets; France’s cyber agency says the campaign is probably still running

Over the nights of 11–13 October, attackers remotely changed settings on web-managed refrigeration controllers in French supermarkets, forcing long or continuous defrost cycles that delayed alarms and pushed some cases to around 50 °C. Finance Minister Roland Lescure told Parliament on 13 October that about 700 stores were hit, at chains including Carrefour, Coopérative U, E.Leclerc, Intermarché/Netto and Monoprix; the Paris prosecutor has reported fires, and its cybercrime section has opened an inquiry. CERT-FR’s alert named Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC controllers on 12 October, added Copeland and Dixell xweb units on 13 October, and says in its 16 October update that the campaigns are probably still ongoing; no new attack nights have been reported since 13 October, and the alert was still at that third version on 28 October. No group has claimed it, no ransom was demanded and the entry route has not been established; Danfoss says most affected units are end-of-life. Les Mousquetaires, which runs Intermarché and Netto, has said the intrusion came through a company that maintained refrigeration at some of its stores; that is the retailer’s statement, not an official finding. No water system is involved. In our assessment the method, remote setpoint changes on controllers left reachable from the internet, mirrors this year’s attacks on US water systems, and Nozomi’s 13 October analysis ties the same exposed-access weakness to them.

High Incident — Ransomware intrusion at a water utility (IT-side), vendor-reported

Symantec: a China-nexus Warlock ransomware crew breached a water utility through on-premises SharePoint

Symantec’s threat hunters reported on 1 October that a China-nexus threat actor it calls Longlegs (also tracked as Storm-2603) attacked at least four organizations in the past two months: a water utility, a telecom provider, a regional government body and a university, in Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking countries across Europe, Africa and Latin America. None is named. The group typically gets in by exploiting on-premises Microsoft SharePoint Server; the only CVEs Symantec names are the 2025 “ToolShell” flaws, plus related SharePoint bugs. It then uses a vulnerable signed driver to switch off security tools, a Visual Studio Code tunnel for persistence and the domain SYSVOL share to push ransomware. In Symantec’s detailed case study, an intrusion at an unnamed critical-infrastructure organization, Warlock reached at least 33 hosts; the report does not say whether that victim was the water utility, and it describes no OT or operational impact. In our assessment, a domain-wide ransomware push is exactly where a flat or over-trusted link between utility IT and SCADA turns an office outage into an operations problem.

High Incident — Active credential-theft campaign against Fortinet firewalls and VPNs; FBI and Secret Service warning

FBI and Secret Service: the FortiBleed campaign against Fortinet firewalls is still active, and their warning covers the water sector

In a joint advisory dated 6 October, the FBI and US Secret Service said the FortiBleed campaign against internet-facing FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways remains an active threat. There is no CVE: the attackers log in with reused or leaked credentials, crack passwords stored with legacy SHA-256 hashing and sniff authentication traffic. Once in, they create their own administrator accounts and sometimes reset or delete the originals, locking owners out, and the access is resold; the advisory says brokers have passed it to INC/Lynx and Payload ransomware affiliates, and SOCRadar says it has confirmed at least 12 ransomware deployments. The advisory covers all 16 US critical-infrastructure sectors and names water and wastewater among them; no water utility or control-system compromise has been described. SOCRadar’s count of 86,644 working device credentials in 194 countries dates from 19 June and is not a new figure. FortiGates often sit at a utility’s IT/OT boundary or carry vendor VPN access (editorial assessment).

Medium Research — Internet exposure of remotely managed controllers after the French attacks; firewall guidance for small sites

After France’s supermarket attacks, Forescout counts nearly 13,000 exposed refrigeration control services worldwide, and Waterfall sets out a minimum firewall checklist for small sites

Forescout’s Vedere Labs reported on 22 October that internet searches for ten refrigeration-controller product families returned 12,976 exposed service observations on 6,017 public IP addresses in 99 countries and territories; it counts services, not devices. About 80% of the Danfoss StoreView observations ran firmware older than 4.3.1, which carries known flaws, and Forescout counts more than 20 hacktivist claims of attacks on refrigeration systems since January, mostly in Spain and Italy (claims, not confirmed incidents). It says the entry route, actor and persistence in the French attacks are still unknown. Two days earlier, Waterfall Security’s Andrew Ginter, noting that the attacks look similar to recent ones on small US water utilities, set out a minimum setup for sites with little IT staff: a commercial-grade firewall with VPN, MFA and automatic updates; deny all traffic by default and allow only fixed addresses such as the service provider’s office, never home or mobile ones; back up and document the configuration; and replace the firewall about every four years. His theories about how the French attackers got in are his own and unconfirmed.

Medium Research — MITRE ATT&CK v20 documents the 2026 US water-utility PLC campaign

MITRE ATT&CK v20 catalogues the July attacks on more than 30 Minnesota water utilities as campaign C0066

MITRE’s ATT&CK v20, released this month, adds campaign C0066, “2026 U.S. Water Utilities Attacks” (entry created 8 October, last modified 13 October). It covers intrusions from 27 July at more than 30 municipal water and wastewater utilities in Minnesota, part of wider activity the FBI and EPA reported in at least seven states. The attackers reached internet-facing Rockwell/Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 PLCs through cellular gateways at water towers and sewer lift stations and changed their IP addresses and passwords to lock operators out. At least one victim’s program logic was altered, and reported effects included loss of water pressure, flooding and one treatment plant taken offline. MITRE says the campaign has not been formally attributed. The release also adds CyberAv3ngers’ IOControl malware, a Mexican campaign (C0064) that reached a municipal water utility, and a new ICS scripting technique, T1701. C0066’s technique list, from default credentials to modified parameters and program logic, is a ready-made checklist for detection and tabletop work.

Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator: an exploited flaw that lets any logged-in user take operator control is now in CISA KEV

Arista disclosed CVE-2026-114625 on 22 October in Security Advisory 0199 and says it is known to be actively exploited. Info Regulation & Policy — Federal cyber incident-reporting rule (CIRCIA)

CISA’s final cyber incident-reporting rule reaches White House review; text and start dates are not yet public

The White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs logged receipt of CISA’s final rule under the Cyber Incident Reporting for Critical Infrastructure Act (RIN 1670-AA04) on 1 October, and the review is pending. The statute set an October 2025 deadline, and CISA’s later targets of May and September 2026 slipped; HIPAA Journal reports publication is expected before year-end. The law calls for covered entities to report covered cyber incidents within 72 hours and ransom payments within 24 hours, but no obligation applies until the final rule sets its dates. The rule text, including which water and wastewater systems it covers, is not public, and the review can still change it. No publication of the final rule had been reported as of 28 October.
