Critical Incident — Water & wastewater

The campaign produced real operational consequences: a pressure drop, a boil-water advisory, a lift-station incident

Beyond locked-out controllers, the campaign has caused documented physical effects. Clayton County Water Authority in Georgia, which serves about 300,000 customers in metro Atlanta, reported cyber activity linked to a pump-station failure that caused a water-pressure drop and a precautionary boil-water advisory, since lifted after testing. Minnesota IT Services reported coordinated activity against more than 30 community water systems from 28 July, with Plymouth, South St. Paul, Maple Plain and Braham confirming operational impacts. A wastewater lift station in Rapid City, South Dakota was also affected, and Michigan, New Jersey and Alabama have reported incidents — Cape May and Woodbine in New Jersey and Childersburg, Alabama among the systems added on 27 July. In several cases utilities lost remote control of pumps, valves and pressure and reverted to manual operation. Officials maintain treated-water safety was not compromised.

High Incident — Attribution

AA26-097A now covers Schneider and Siemens PLCs too, and adds detection guidance for tampered code modules

The joint advisory behind this campaign was issued on 7 April by the FBI, CISA, NSA, EPA, DOE, US Cyber Command’s Cyber National Mission Force and Treasury, and updated on 22 July. The update matters for water operators in two ways. First, the observed manufacturer scope expanded beyond Rockwell Automation to include Schneider Electric and Siemens PLCs, so a utility that replaced its MicroLogix fleet is not automatically out of scope. Second, the agencies added guidance for detecting malicious changes inside reusable code modules used within Rockwell PLC programs — a reminder that the risk is not only lockout but altered logic. At one confirmed victim the actors disabled safety shutdown and alarm functions. Reported techniques also include Dropbear SSH persistence and project-file exfiltration. No further update to the advisory has been published since; agencies last publicly assessed the campaign active on 12 August, and there has been no new agency statement in the five days since the most recent confirmed victim.

High Incident — Cross-sector

A Polish plant lost its process-water treatment when attackers crossed in over a private cellular APN

CERT Polska has disclosed that attackers reached the control network of a Polish combined heat and power plant serving about 50,000 residents and stopped both a steam turbine and the plant’s process-water treatment system. The route in was not the internet or the corporate network: a compromised FortiGate firewall at an unrelated wind farm, then a Teltonika cellular router that reached a private APN operated by the distribution system operator, on which devices were permitted to talk to one another. CERT Polska believes this is the first observed real-world use of a private APN as a path into an industrial control network. Water utilities should read it closely, because private APNs are exactly how many of them connect remote lift stations, wells and booster sites — and the isolation policy is usually the carrier’s, unseen.

Confirmed OT-disruptive incident — turbine and water treatment stopped No new CVE — compromised edge firewall plus a flat private cellular network
No patch — architectural: segment the APN, treat carrier networks as untrusted Action: Ask your cellular carrier in writing whether devices on your private APN can reach each other, and whether the APN is shared with other subscribers. Then put a firewall with an explicit allow-list between every field router and the SCADA network, so a compromised remote site cannot reach the plant.
Suggested SLA: Private APN isolation review and field-router firewall rules — 30 days Owner:

High Research — Exposure

4,407 Rockwell controllers are still reachable from the internet — 22 of them in cities already hit

A Shodan-based scan by Forescout Vedere Labs on 3 August found 4,407 internet-facing Rockwell Automation and Allen-Bradley programmable logic controllers worldwide, 2,844 of them in the United States. Twenty-two sit in cities where water and wastewater utilities have reported attacks since late July, and 19 of those 22 appear vulnerable to CVE-2017-16740, a buffer overflow in MicroLogix 1400 controllers that Rockwell patched nine years ago. Forescout attributes much of the exposure to small and mid-sized utilities connecting controllers to cellular routers for remote monitoring, which keeps staffing costs down but frequently leaves EtherNet/IP or Modbus TCP answering from the open internet. Total exposure is down 47 percent from a 2020 peak of 7,814 — real progress, and still far too many.

Directly linked to active campaign — exposed devices in affected cities CVE-2017-16740 not in CISA KEV; exposure is the primary risk
Patch available since 2017 for CVE-2017-16740; exposure removal is the priority Action: Run your own external scan this week rather than assuming: check every public IP your utility owns, plus the addresses your cellular carrier assigns to field routers, for EtherNet/IP (TCP 44818), Modbus (TCP 502) and web interfaces. Anything that answers goes behind a firewall or VPN before the end of the month.

High Threat Intel — Virtualisation

CISA added the exploited vCenter flaw to KEV yesterday — 361 hosts compromised across 47 countries

CISA added CVE-2026-59310, a path-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter Server, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August. Broadcom patched it on 29 July and exploitation began about five days later. German incident-response firm QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence that a Chinese-speaking actor operates the campaign, counting roughly 361 affected addresses across 47 countries. The actor gains root on the vCenter appliance, plants a backdoor through cron and a reverse SSH binary, and in at least one case deployed Babuk-derived ransomware. Plenty of mid-sized water utilities now run SCADA servers, historians and engineering workstations as virtual machines on a single vSphere cluster; if that cluster’s management plane falls, every one of those systems is reachable at once, regardless of how well the plant network is segmented.

Confirmed active exploitation — suspected China-nexus actor, ransomware in at least one case In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-59310 added 18 Aug 2026
Patch available — Broadcom fix released 29 Jul 2026 Action: Patch any vCenter that hosts SCADA, historian or engineering virtual machines, and check the appliance for unexpected cron entries and a “linuxFile” binary. The vCenter management interface should be reachable only from a dedicated administrative host, never from the business network.

High Threat Intel — Ransomware

Medusa has passed 500 critical-infrastructure victims — and weaponises new flaws inside 24 hours

CISA and the FBI refreshed #StopRansomware advisory AA25-071A on 18 August. The cumulative Medusa victim count among critical-infrastructure organisations passed 500 as of April 2026, up from roughly 300 in the March 2025 edition — about 200 new victims in a year. The named sectors are healthcare, critical manufacturing, the defense industrial base, government facilities, information technology and financial services; no control-system involvement is alleged, and the impact described is enterprise-IT encryption plus data theft. The finding that matters operationally to a water utility is the tempo: the agencies say Medusa turns newly disclosed vulnerabilities into working intrusions within about 24 hours, and has been observed using exploits up to a week before public disclosure, because the group buys access and exploits rather than developing them. A 30-day patch cycle on an internet-facing appliance is not a patch cycle against that.

Confirmed active operation — 500+ critical-infrastructure victims, IT-side impact No single CVE — opportunistic use of newly disclosed and pre-disclosure exploits
No single patch — shrink the exposure window on internet-facing and remote-access services Action: Agree an emergency patch path now for anything of yours that answers from the internet — VPN, firewall, remote-access gateway, webmail, file transfer — measured in days, not in the next maintenance window. Then confirm that billing, work-order, laboratory and compliance-reporting data has an offline restorable backup, because that is what actually gets encrypted at a utility.

Critical Advisory — HMI gateway

The advisory of the cycle for water: a CVSS 10.0 command injection gives unauthenticated root on Haiwell’s IoT Cloud HMI gateway

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an OS command injection in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway rated 10.0. The Net Check feature reached through the /setting endpoint passes user input from the cmdPing Socket.io event straight to the operating system, letting an unauthenticated remote attacker run arbitrary commands as root. Version 3.40.1.12 is affected; Haiwell has published Scada-v3.50.1.19. This is the same device class the current campaign is exploiting through exposure rather than through a flaw — a low-cost cloud-connected HMI gateway of the kind small utilities install at wells, lift stations and booster pumps because it is cheaper than extending the SCADA network. Here the risk is worse, because it needs no credentials at all. With a live campaign running against internet-facing water OT, a maximum-severity flaw in an internet-oriented HMI gateway is the highest-priority advisory of this cycle for the sector.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV
Patch available — Haiwell Scada-v3.50.1.19 Action: Search your inventory for Haiwell devices, including anything a system integrator installed at an outlying site. Update to Scada-v3.50.1.19 and, whatever the version, confirm the gateway is not answering from a public or cellular-carrier address — that check protects you against this CVE and the current campaign at the same time.

Critical Advisory — Siemens

SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced exposes Node-RED with no authentication — maximum-severity remote code execution

Siemens published ten advisories in its August Patch Tuesday round; the most severe (SSA-834709) affects SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices running Industrial OS with Node-RED installed. The Node-RED HTTP interface enforces no authentication, so a remote unauthenticated attacker can reach programming nodes that execute system commands and run arbitrary code at maximum privilege. A fixed version is available. IoT2050 units are used as edge gateways and protocol converters — in water, typically bridging plant equipment to a telemetry or reporting platform. They tend to be commissioned by an integrator and then left alone, which is how a device trusted by the process network ends up outside the patch cycle entirely.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV
Patch available — update to the fixed SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced release Action: Find every IoT2050 in the estate, check whether Node-RED is installed, and make sure its HTTP interface answers only to a dedicated management host. Update to the fixed release; if a shutdown window is needed first, block the interface at the network layer today as an interim control.

High Advisory — Remote access

A Windows IKE flaw went into KEV on 18 August with a three-day deadline — and IKE is what terminates OT remote access

CISA added CVE-2026-33824, a double free in the Microsoft Windows IKE extensions service (IKEEXT) that leads to remote code execution, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August with a federal remediation deadline of 21 August. A three-day deadline is CISA’s signal for exploitation happening at scale rather than in isolated cases. For a small utility the placement is what matters: IKE and IPsec are what terminate the site-to-site tunnels and remote-access connections that link a plant to an outstation, an integrator or an on-call operator, and the Windows host carrying them frequently sits on the boundary between the business network and SCADA. Three other flaws were added the same day — the VMware vCenter path traversal covered elsewhere on this page, a SharePoint authentication bypass (CVE-2026-55040) and a macOS Screen Sharing authentication flaw (CVE-2026-65400).

Confirmed active exploitation — basis for the KEV listing In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-33824 added 18 Aug 2026, federal deadline 21 Aug
Patch available — apply the Microsoft update Action: Identify every Windows host that terminates an IPsec or IKE tunnel for OT remote access or an outstation link and patch it this week, matching the federal 21 August date. If a host cannot be patched immediately, restrict UDP 500 and 4500 to known peer addresses and review VPN authentication logs for sessions that do not match a named engineer or an approved vendor window.

High Advisory — OT monitoring

Six flaws in CISA Malcolm — the free OT monitoring suite small water systems are pointed at

Advisory ICSA-26-230-01 covers six vulnerabilities in Malcolm, CISA’s own open-source network traffic analysis suite for OT. The worst is CVE-2026-55676, CVSS 8.8, an unrestricted file upload that allows a PHP file to be planted and executed. The set also includes a path traversal during archive extraction (CVE-2026-63177, 7.1), a role-based access control bypass through unnormalised URI paths and a percent-encoding authorisation bypass (5.4 each), and resource-exhaustion and compression-bomb denial-of-service conditions. Versions below 26.06.1, below 26.07.0 and up to 26.07.1 are affected depending on the CVE, with fixes in Malcolm 26.07.0 and 26.08.0. No exploitation is reported. It belongs on this page because Malcolm is free, and free is why it is a common choice for a system serving a few thousand people — usually sitting on a SPAN or TAP port with visibility of the entire process network, which makes it a poor host to leave unpatched.

No known exploitation reported as of this edition Not in CISA KEV
Patch available — Malcolm 26.07.0 and 26.08.0 depending on the CVE Action: If you run Malcolm as an OT sensor, check its version today and schedule the update to 26.08.0. Whatever the version, confirm the sensor’s web and management interfaces answer only to a dedicated administrative host and not to the plant network it monitors, and that its capture interface stays receive-only.

Medium Advisory — Vendor round-up

What else from the August advisory cycle a water utility should actually check for

Beyond the Haiwell gateway, the 13 August CISA batch carried several items that turn up in water estates. ICSA-26-225-01 covers AVEVA Enterprise SCADA and Enterprise HMI (CVE-2025-7639, CVSS 7.1), where an authenticated operator-level user can tamper with serialised data and potentially execute code; AVEVA also advises moving serialisation away from BinaryFormatter, which is a change-controlled project rather than a patch. Medium Regulation — Water sector

The Water Cyber Shield Act would give EPA assessment and corrective-action authority, plus $300 million a year

Senators Adam Schiff and Amy Klobuchar introduced the Water Cyber Shield Act on 13 August, in response to the coordinated attacks on more than 30 Minnesota community water systems. The bill would expand EPA’s authority to conduct cybersecurity assessments and require corrective action where significant vulnerabilities are found; require large drinking-water and wastewater systems to fold cybersecurity assessments into existing risk and resilience planning; direct EPA to develop tiered standards in consultation with CISA, NIST, states and sector stakeholders; authorise an additional $300 million a year through the Drinking Water and Clean Water State Revolving Funds; and extend federal cyber incident reporting to the state and locally owned systems currently exempt, with flexibility and funding priority for smaller systems. It is an introduction, not a law, and mandatory authority for EPA has failed before. The tiered approach matters most for small systems, which is where the exposure actually sits.

Medium Sector support — Small systems

Free managed detection for systems serving under 10,000 people, and the sector gets a shared view of what is happening

DEF CON Franklin and the National Rural Water Association announced the Water Watch Center at DEF CON on 10 August. It offers managed detection and response at no cost to community water systems serving fewer than 10,000 people — roughly nine in ten of the approximately 50,000 US community water systems, and the same size class that dominates the victim list in the current PLC campaign. Five providers are delivering the service: Defendify, Legato Security, L1 Secure, Rapid7 and Sentinel Technologies. Anonymised telemetry from participating utilities is shared with WaterISAC, which is the quietly significant part: a sector that has never had visibility across its smallest operators would begin to get one. Funding is philanthropic, with seed money from Craig Newmark, and organisers have been open that philanthropy is not a durable basis for the programme and that federal funding will ultimately be needed.

New York put a further $9 million into cybersecurity at 153 water systems

New York announced more than $9 million in SECURE grants on 3 August for cybersecurity improvements at 153 local water systems, administered through the state Environmental Facilities Corporation. It is a small sum measured against the sector’s need and a meaningful one measured against what a village water system can raise from its own rate base, which is the point: the utilities carrying the most exposure in the current campaign are overwhelmingly the ones with no cybersecurity line in the budget at all.
