The Justice Department and FBI announced court-authorised seizures on 26 August of two linked platforms, QScan and QTRouter, operated by a PRC state-sponsored group the government calls QTFY. Critical Advisory — Actively exploited, CISA KEV, historian and MES databases

A seven-year-old SQL Server flaw is now confirmed exploited — and it is the database under your historian

CISA added six vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 26 August, and the one that matters most on the plant floor is CVE-2019-1068, a remote code execution flaw in Microsoft SQL Server patched in July 2019 and only now confirmed as exploited in the wild. Successful exploitation runs code under the SQL Server Database Engine service account; where that account is over-privileged — which is common on systems installed by an integrator years ago — the attacker moves off the database and onto the Windows host. Water utilities meet SQL Server in the historian and the SCADA reporting server — Ignition, AVEVA and iFIX deployments routinely sit on an instance an integrator stood up years ago and nobody has touched since, and at a small utility that server is frequently reachable from the business network and sometimes from the internet. No public write-up of the in-the-wild technique has appeared, so detection guidance is thin; the federal remediation deadline is 29 August. Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-8452 was added in the same batch.

Patched — Microsoft fixed this in July 2019; the exposure is unpatched or end-of-support SQL Server instances still running in OT Action: Find your OT SQL Server instances before you do anything else: the historian, the reporting or compliance database, the alarm archive, and any vendor package that quietly installed SQL Express. Info Policy — Draft US legislation, not enacted

Draft legislation would give the EPA authority to assess water utilities and require fixes

A Senate bill introduced in August would give the Environmental Protection Agency mandatory assessment authority over water-system cybersecurity, with reported funding of around $300 million a year to support utilities in closing the gaps it finds. It is a direct response to the current campaign and to the long-standing observation that the water sector has guidance and voluntary programmes but no enforceable baseline. The bill has been introduced, not enacted, and its provisions may change substantially in committee — so treat it as a signal of direction rather than a compliance obligation. Separately, industry groups have been pressing Congress for a water-sector reliability organisation modelled on NERC.

Not applicable — draft legislation No CVE

If funding follows, utilities that can already describe their gaps in specific terms will be positioned to use it; those that cannot will spend the first year producing the inventory.

Info Policy — Procurement-driven security certification

The NSA is putting an ISA/IEC 62443 certificate in front of the controllers it buys

ISASecure announced a partnership with the National Security Agency to develop a High Criticality Component Security Assurance certification scheme, derived from its Component Security Assurance programme and grounded in ISA/IEC 62443-4-2. Once accepted, the NSA intends to use it when adding OT components to the National Security Systems product compliant list. Water utilities are not the buyer here, but they are downstream of the effect: when a large customer makes certification a purchase condition, vendors build to it and the requirement language migrates into everyone else’s specifications. That gives a utility drafting a SCADA or instrumentation tender a defensible standard to cite rather than inventing security requirements from scratch.

Not applicable — this is a certification scheme announcement, not a vulnerability No CVE
No patch applies — the item is procurement policy and component assurance Action: Add ISA/IEC 62443-4-2 component security levels and ISASecure certification as an evaluated criterion in your next SCADA, RTU or instrumentation tender rather than waiting for a mandate. Ask your current vendors and integrator which products already hold certification and at what level; the answer varies within a single catalogue and is useful at renewal. For small utilities this is one of the few security improvements that costs nothing to specify and shifts the burden onto the supplier.
