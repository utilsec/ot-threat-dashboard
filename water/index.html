Citrix NetScaler zero-days under active attack: WaterISAC tells utilities to check for compromise before patching

Citrix confirmed on 27 September that two critical NetScaler ADC and Gateway flaws, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS 9.5 each), were exploited before patches existed, and CISA added both to its exploited-vulnerabilities list the same day. NetScaler commonly supplies the VPN and remote-access path into water utilities and the IT-support firms that serve them, so a compromised appliance can hand an attacker a trusted foothold from which to reach SCADA and engineering networks. Reporting puts more than 20,000 instances online; no actor has been named and no water-sector victim has been reported. WaterISAC issued a notification on 28 September urging members to look for signs of compromise first, then upgrade to fixed builds 14.1-73.37 or 13.1-64.23, and to ask outsourced IT providers to do the same.

Critical Incident — Confirmed manipulation of water-system OT

Unattributed hackers altered pump cycles and disabled alarms at two small Colorado water systems

Colorado Gov. Jared Polis’s office confirmed on 18 September that foreign actors breached two small, privately owned water systems, each serving fewer than 200 people, in late August. The attackers changed equipment settings, disabled remote access and alarms, and altered pumping cycles. The office says treatment and water quality were, to its knowledge, unaffected, and state health officials helped the providers and alerted other utilities through the state response network. It says it cannot confirm which foreign actors were involved, though it points to the Iranian-backed effort against US water systems that federal agencies are tracking; no Colorado link has been confirmed. Industrial Cyber cites EPA as reporting more than 100 drinking water and wastewater systems in 12 states targeted this year. Background: CISA’s 22 July update to advisory AA26-097A names Rockwell/Allen-Bradley, Schneider and Siemens PLCs as targeted. No new victims, access method or attribution have been reported since.

High Incident — Exploited network switch, CISA KEV

996 Zyxel GS1900 network switches compromised in 48 countries through a flaw now in CISA KEV

Help Net Security reported on 22 September that 996 Zyxel GS1900-series smart managed switches in 48 countries had been compromised through CVE-2026-7273, a stack-based buffer overflow in the web interface that lets an unauthenticated attacker on the local network run operating-system commands. Firmware 2.90(XXXX.1)C0 and earlier is affected; Zyxel shipped a fix in June. The attacks date to around mid-August, and stolen data included configuration files and hashed credentials, with 564 victims still on factory-default credentials. The actor is described only as Chinese-speaking and linked to earlier WordPress and Gitea exploitation. CISA lists the flaw in KEV with a 24 September federal deadline, now passed. In our assessment, low-cost managed switches like this are common at small utilities’ remote and treatment sites, where they network PLCs, HMIs and cellular modems.

Medium Research — Credential exposure in the water sector

Infostealer logs expose OT and remote-access logins at 258 US water utilities and vendors

SpyCloud published research on 20 September examining 10,000 water-sector organizations drawn from the 66,845 EPA-registered US water systems. It found 258 with credentials for OT or remote-access systems in infostealer data, including TeamViewer, LogMeIn and GoToMyPC logins, SonicWall and Fortinet management portals, roughly 167 metering-vendor accounts from one infected device at a metering technology provider, and one small district’s shared “scada@” mailbox with a saved remote-desktop credential. It is exposure, not confirmed intrusion, and SpyCloud ties it to no actor. For small systems that lean on vendor remote-access accounts, a password stolen from someone else’s computer is an entry point the utility cannot see.

High Guidance — CISA/FBI on third-party ICS integrators

CISA and FBI warn operators to tighten control of ICS integrators’ access

CISA and the FBI released a fact sheet on 24 September on the risks of working with third-party industrial control system integrators. It draws on an FBI analysis of a 2025 intrusion at a US automation firm serving power utilities and transportation customers, in which actors searched for SCADA and customer records and staged nine archives holding about 800 files of network schematics, device configurations and customer details. Water is not among the named sectors, but small utilities that rely on one integrator for PLC and SCADA work share the same exposure. The agencies recommend least-privilege access, cybersecurity terms in contracts, monitored on-demand remote access, and disconnecting devices from the public internet.

High Advisory — Confirmed exploited router flaw chain, CISA KEV

CISA adds a second MikroTik RouterOS flaw, CVE-2026-67279, to KEV as the “MikroTrick” takeover chain is confirmed

The Hacker News reported on 25 September that CISA added CVE-2026-67279, an authentication bypass in RouterOS, to KEV with a 28 September federal deadline. Chained with CVE-2026-86060, already in KEV since 10–11 September, it lets an attacker take administrative control of an internet-exposed router without a password. CERT Polska corrected the chain on 22 September, so earlier reports that named other CVE IDs in it were wrong. Fixes came on 3 September in RouterOS 7.24.2, 7.23.4 and 6.49.21, and exploitation is reported from as early as 2 September, before the fix. MikroTik routers are widely used at small utilities’ remote sites and as cellular or VPN gateways (editorial assessment).

High Advisory — Industrial telemetry database denial-of-service, no known exploitation

A single malformed packet can crash TDengine, a time-series database used for industrial telemetry

Industrial Cyber reported on 25 September that Ridge Security found CVE-2026-42542, a pre-authentication denial-of-service flaw in the TDengine time-series database, rated CVSS 7.5. An integer underflow in how the server handles RPC message lengths on TCP port 6030 lets one crafted packet crash the taosd service before any login, and repeating it can hold the database in a crash loop. Version 3.4.1.6 and later fix it. Where a utility’s SCADA historian or monitoring pipeline uses TDengine, an attack would blind trending and alarming rather than change control (editorial assessment).

No known exploitation reported Not in CISA KEV
Patched — TDengine 3.4.1.6 and later Action: Confirm whether any telemetry, historian or monitoring pipeline in your environment runs TDengine, upgrade to 3.4.1.6, and restrict TCP port 6030 to trusted hosts in the meantime.

Info Regulation & Policy — NIST draft OT security guide

NIST’s draft OT security guide adds water and wastewater; comments open until 30 November

NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Rev. 4, “Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security”, on 21 September 2026. It expands coverage to water and wastewater systems alongside building automation, food and agriculture, freight rail and maritime, reorganises around the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, adds zero-trust principles, and expands guidance on asset management and network monitoring. It is guidance rather than a mandate, but it is a likely reference point for utility programs and state audits. Public comments are due by 30 November 2026.

Info Regulation & Policy — Threat-information sharing law extended to 11 Dec

Congress extends the cyber threat-sharing law only to 11 December, keeping legal cover for sharing water-sector incident data temporary

The stopgap funding law signed on 3 September extends the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 and the Federal Cybersecurity Enhancement Act through 11 December 2026, according to Nextgov/FCW and GovConWire; the 2015 law had been due to lapse on 30 September. It gives companies liability protection when they share cyber threat information with government, which matters to water utilities and vendors that report incidents through WaterISAC and to CISA. The Operational Technology Cybersecurity Coalition has called for long-term reauthorization after the summer’s water attacks. Until Congress acts, the protection carries a hard end date.
