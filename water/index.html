Info Research — Isolation readiness, practitioner guidance

Six steps to being able to disconnect — and an assumption you may run isolated for three months

Nozomi Networks published a practitioner summary on 2 September of the Five Eyes “CI Fortify” guidance released in late July, reducing it to six steps: identify vital systems and their dependencies; establish trust levels and connection maps; pre-build the separation points; create and test graduated isolation plans; retain visibility while isolated; and run post-isolation checks before reconnecting. Two points matter for water. The first is graduated isolation — the realistic response to a compromise is rarely a single switch, it is cutting remote access while keeping plant SCADA, then cutting SCADA while keeping local control. The second is the planning horizon: Nozomi suggests assuming isolated operation could last up to three months, which for a utility means asking whether you have the staff to run lift stations and dosing manually for that long, not merely whether you can pull the cable.

Confirmed — Nozomi Networks analysis published 2 Sep 2026, summarising the Five Eyes CI Fortify guidance released 28 Jul 2026 No CVE — this is preparedness guidance, not a product vulnerability
No patch applies — the work is dependency mapping, pre-built separation points and tested isolation plans Action: Do the first two steps and the rest become tractable. Write down, per system, what plant SCADA and your remote-site telemetry depend on that lives outside the control network — directory services, DNS, NTP, licence servers, cellular APNs, the integrator’s remote-access path. That list is the isolation plan. Then pre-build the separation point so cutting external connectivity is a documented action rather than an improvised firewall change at 2am. Test the cheapest graduated tier first: disconnect vendor remote access for an hour during a low-demand period and confirm the plant still runs and you still have visibility. If you cannot do that without an outage, that is the finding.

High Research — AI-assisted exploit porting against PLCs

An AI model re-targeted a working PLC exploit onto a sibling controller in a day — and destroyed one in the process

Forescout’s Vedere Labs set a large language model the task of taking a working remote-code-execution exploit for one WAGO controller — the 750-852, via CVE-2021-31886, a pre-authentication buffer overflow in its Nucleus FTP server — and re-targeting it at a related model, the 750-831. It worked, but not cheaply or unattended: the final stage alone ran more than eight hours and over $500 in API costs, with a researcher steering throughout. Draft legislation would give the EPA authority to assess water utilities and require fixes

A Senate bill introduced in August would give the Environmental Protection Agency mandatory assessment authority over water-system cybersecurity, with reported funding of around $300 million a year to support utilities in closing the gaps it finds. It is a direct response to the current campaign and to the long-standing observation that the water sector has guidance and voluntary programmes but no enforceable baseline. The bill has been introduced, not enacted, and its provisions may change substantially in committee — so treat it as a signal of direction rather than a compliance obligation. Separately, industry groups have been pressing Congress for a water-sector reliability organisation modelled on NERC.

No patch applies — this is proposed regulation at the introduction stage Action: Use the direction of travel rather than the bill text. Whatever passes, an assessment regime will ask the same first questions: what assets do you have, what is exposed, who has remote access, and can you restore. Getting a defensible asset inventory and an exposure baseline in place now costs the same as doing it under a deadline and gives you the evidence either way. If funding follows, utilities that can already describe their gaps in specific terms will be positioned to use it; those that cannot will spend the first year producing the inventory.
