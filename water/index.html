High Incident — Cross-sector OT attack on remotely managed controllers (food retail, France); confirmed by French authorities

Attackers took over internet-reachable refrigeration controllers at about 700 French supermarkets; France’s cyber agency says the campaign is probably still running

Over the nights of 11–13 October, attackers remotely changed settings on web-managed refrigeration controllers in French supermarkets, forcing long or continuous defrost cycles that delayed alarms and pushed some cases to around 50 °C. Finance Minister Roland Lescure told Parliament on 13 October that about 700 stores were hit, at chains including Carrefour, Coopérative U, E.Leclerc, Intermarché/Netto and Monoprix; the Paris prosecutor has reported fires, and its cybercrime section has opened an inquiry. CERT-FR’s alert named Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC controllers on 12 October, added Copeland and Dixell xweb units on 13 October, and says in its 16 October update that the campaigns are probably still ongoing; no new attack nights have been reported since 13 October, and the alert was still at that third version on 31 October. No group has claimed it, no ransom was demanded and the entry route has not been established; Danfoss says most affected units are end-of-life. Les Mousquetaires, which runs Intermarché and Netto, has said the intrusion came through a company that maintained refrigeration at some of its stores; that is the retailer’s statement, not an official finding. Separately, Reuters reported on 29 October, citing four unnamed officials, that US Army and Air Force investigators, since joined by US Cyber Command, are treating freezer failures at at least six US military commissaries since late August as a suspected cyberattack, and that investigators have spoken to French police; no cause has been confirmed and no link to the French attacks established. No water system is involved. In our assessment the method, remote setpoint changes on controllers left reachable from the internet, mirrors this year’s attacks on US water systems, and Nozomi’s 13 October analysis ties the same exposed-access weakness to them.

High Incident — Active credential-theft campaign against Fortinet firewalls and VPNs; FBI and Secret Service warning

FBI and Secret Service: the FortiBleed campaign against Fortinet firewalls is still active, and their warning covers the water sector

In a joint advisory dated 6 October, the FBI and US Secret Service said the FortiBleed campaign against internet-facing FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways remains an active threat. There is no CVE: the attackers log in with reused or leaked credentials, crack passwords stored with legacy SHA-256 hashing and sniff authentication traffic. Once in, they create their own administrator accounts and sometimes reset or delete the originals, locking owners out, and the access is resold; the advisory says brokers have passed it to INC/Lynx and Payload ransomware affiliates, and SOCRadar says it has confirmed at least 12 ransomware deployments. The advisory covers all 16 US critical-infrastructure sectors and names water and wastewater among them; no water utility or control-system compromise has been described. SOCRadar’s count of 86,644 working device credentials in 194 countries dates from 19 June and is not a new figure. FortiGates often sit at a utility’s IT/OT boundary or carry vendor VPN access (editorial assessment).

High Research — China-linked relay network and management-platform exploitation; water among the named sectors; no OT intrusion described

Lumen: Volt Typhoon’s “Silent Train” network has reached targets since 2023 through hijacked routers and flaws in management platforms, and lists water among the sectors hit

Lumen’s Black Lotus Labs reported on 28 October on Silent Train, a framework it attributes to Volt Typhoon, the group US agencies link to the Chinese state, and has seen in consistent use since November 2023. For each operation the operators build a single-use chain, from virtual private servers outside the US through hijacked home-office routers and IoT devices abroad and then in the US, sometimes in under two minutes, and take it apart afterwards, which makes the traffic hard to trace or block. The chains are used to reach targets and exploit IT and network management platforms: Lumen ties the group to flaws in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN (CVE-2026-20127, CVSS v3.1 10.0, Cisco), VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-16812), N-able N-central (CVE-2025-8875 and CVE-2025-8876) and Versa Director, and likely an undisclosed Quest KACE flaw. Its key findings list water among the critical-infrastructure sectors affected, alongside energy, transportation, maritime, rail, aviation and emergency services; Lumen says it has notified hundreds of victims over three years, and Hawaii, California and Texas were the most targeted states. No victim is named and no intrusion into OT is described. These are different flaws from the VeloCloud and Cisco SD-WAN Manager bugs on other cards on this page. In our assessment, these platforms are what a utility’s IT or network provider uses to manage every site at once, so a compromise there reaches all of them.
