Top Story

Unattributed hackers altered pump cycles and disabled alarms at two small Colorado water systems

Colorado Gov. Jared Polis’s office confirmed on 18 September that foreign cyber actors breached two small, privately owned water systems in late August. The attackers changed equipment settings, disabled remote access and alarms, and altered pumping cycles. Officials say the disruption was brief and caused no known impact on water treatment, water quality or public safety. The governor’s office says it cannot confirm which foreign actors were involved; federal officials are separately tracking an Iranian-backed effort against US water systems, but no link to Colorado has been confirmed. No new development — further victims, the access method or attribution — has been reported since 18 September. It remains the only confirmed OT-disruptive water incident in this edition’s window.

High Incident — Exploited network switch, CISA KEV

996 Zyxel GS1900 network switches compromised in 48 countries through a flaw now in CISA KEV

GreyNoise reported on 21 September that 996 Zyxel GS1900-series switches in 48 countries had been compromised through CVE-2026-7273 since about mid-August. The flaw is a stack-based buffer overflow in the switches’ web (CGI) interface that can let an unauthenticated attacker with network access run operating-system commands; firmware 2.90(XXXX.1)C0 and earlier is affected, and Zyxel released a fix in June. GreyNoise describes the actor as Chinese-speaking and links it to the actor, or a related one, behind earlier WordPress and Gitea exploitation. CISA added the flaw to KEV on 21 September with a 24 September federal deadline, now passed. In our assessment, low-cost managed switches like the GS1900 line are common at small utilities’ remote and treatment sites, where they network PLCs, HMIs and cellular modems, so this is a device class worth inventorying.

Patched — Zyxel firmware update available since Jun 2026 Action: Inventory Zyxel GS1900 switches at every remote and treatment site, apply Zyxel’s June firmware update, keep switch management interfaces off untrusted networks, and change any default administrative credentials.

Medium Background — Context for the Colorado incident, not a new story

Background: July’s campaign against internet-exposed water-sector PLCs across a dozen states

Background, not a new incident: the FBI and EPA warned on 30 July that malicious actors were remotely accessing internet-exposed Rockwell Automation MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 controllers, changing IP addresses and passwords and, in some cases, altering ladder logic. Utilities in at least a dozen states were affected, with Minnesota alone counting more than 30 community water systems hit in a late-July wave; some experienced loss of monitoring, brief flooding or pressure loss before switching to manual operation. A group calling itself “APT Iran” claimed collaboration with CyberAv3ngers, an IRGC-linked unit, though officials stopped short of formal attribution. No boil-water advisories resulted. It is carried here only as context for the Colorado incident; no link between the two has been confirmed.

Confirmed — FBI/EPA alert and state health departments confirm exploitation of internet-exposed PLCs; the “APT Iran”/CyberAv3ngers claim is self-asserted, not a government attribution CVE-2021-22681 (Rockwell Logix authentication bypass) implicated; not a new 2026 flaw
Mitigated by removing internet exposure and patching per Rockwell’s existing advisory Action: If you have not audited internet-facing PLCs and cellular modems since this alert, do so now: remove any MicroLogix, or other PLC, from direct internet exposure, put cellular modems behind a VPN with MFA, and change default credentials on every field device.
Suggested SLA: Internet-facing PLC audit — 30 days (if not already done) Owner:

Medium Research — Infostealer credential exposure, not an intrusion

Infostealer logs hold OT or remote-access credentials tied to 258 US water utilities and vendors

SpyCloud published an analysis on 20 September of stolen-identity data linked to 10,000 US water and wastewater utilities and their technology vendors. It found active infostealer exposure at 1,787 of those organizations, and credentials for OT or remote-access systems at 258. Tools named include TeamViewer, LogMeIn and GoToMyPC, plus SonicWall and Fortinet management portals. In one case, a single infected device at a major advanced-metering technology provider held saved logins for about 167 utility metering portals. SpyCloud stresses that it measures identity exposure, not confirmed intrusion, and ties the findings to no threat actor or campaign; exposure was concentrated in larger operators and vendors, with small utilities underrepresented. The practical risk for a small system is a vendor’s or employee’s infected computer leaking the same remote-access login the utility depends on (editorial assessment).

Exposure research — SpyCloud measures stolen credentials, not confirmed intrusions; no actor or campaign identified Not KEV-related
No patch applicable — a credential-hygiene and MFA issue Action: Rotate passwords for every remote-access tool and vendor portal your utility uses, turn on MFA wherever it is offered, remove remote-access tools you no longer need, and ask your integrator and metering vendor how they protect saved utility logins on their own staff devices.
Suggested SLA: Remote-access credential rotation and MFA check — 14 days; vendor credential-handling review — 30 days Owner:

High Guidance — Third-party ICS integrator access

CISA and the FBI urge tighter control of ICS integrators’ remote access after a 2025 integrator breach

A joint CISA/FBI fact sheet, “Considerations for Critical Infrastructure Operators Working With Third-Party ICS Integrators” (dated 23 September, publicised 24 September), describes how malicious foreign cyber actors compromised a US industrial automation company in March–April 2025 and staged nine archives holding about 800 files of customer SCADA information, device details and schematics for exfiltration. It recommends least-privilege access for integrators, remote access that is monitored, logged and granted on demand where possible, security requirements written into contracts, an inventory of everything an integrator supplies, and practised manual operations with offline backups. The downstream customers it names are power utilities and transportation entities, not water, but small utilities that rely on one integrator for all PLC and SCADA work face the same exposure (editorial assessment).

Not an exploit advisory — guidance grounded in a documented 2025 integrator compromise Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: List every integrator and vendor with remote access to your control systems, move them from standing accounts to time-boxed, approved sessions you can log and revoke yourself, and find out what copies of your SCADA configuration and network drawings each one holds, and where.
Suggested SLA: Integrator access inventory — 14 days; conversion to on-demand access — 30 days Owner:

High Research — IT/OT/IoT network convergence

Only 13% of network segments with OT devices are OT-only, Forescout study finds

Forescout’s Vedere Labs analysed 47,700 network segments containing more than 2.5 million devices across 209 organizations, Industrial Cyber reported on 24 September. Only 13% of the segments that contained OT devices were OT-only; the remainder held other device types, and almost half mixed OT with both IT and IoT devices. Utilities averaged nine devices per segment, against 29 in manufacturing and 72 in oil and gas. At small or unstaffed water sites, where one switch often carries SCADA alongside cameras, building systems and office equipment, a single compromised laptop or exposed switch can put pump and dosing controls within reach (editorial assessment).

Research finding — not tied to a specific exploited vulnerability or incident Not KEV-related
No patch applicable Action: Verify your own segmentation claims against actual network traffic, not architecture diagrams, particularly at small or unstaffed sites where IT and OT gear are most likely to share a switch.

High Advisory — Exploited remote-site router flaws, CISA KEV

CISA lists a third exploited MikroTik RouterOS flaw; the routers often sit at small utilities’ remote sites

CISA added CVE-2026-67279, an SSH flaw in MikroTik RouterOS rated CVSS 6.9, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 25 September with a 28 September federal deadline. CISA says it lets an unauthenticated client open a session channel and send an exec request, and can be chained with CVE-2026-86060 for unauthenticated exploitation. CERT Polska reported in-the-wild exploitation of a two-flaw SSH chain it calls “MikroTrick” from about 2 September; its 22 September technical analysis names these two flaws as the chain, giving full administrative control without a password. CVE-2026-86060 and CVE-2026-67277 were added to KEV on 10 September. Fixed builds are RouterOS 7.24.2, 7.23.4 and 6.49.21 (CVE-2026-67278 needs 7.24.3 or 7.23.6). Shadowserver counted more than 122,500 MikroTik devices with SSH reachable from the internet in early September. In our assessment, MikroTik routers are a common low-cost remote-access and cellular edge at small water and wastewater sites.

Confirmed exploited — three RouterOS flaws in CISA KEV; CERT Polska reported in-the-wild exploitation of the chain from about 2 Sep In CISA KEV — CVE-2026-67279 added 25 Sep 2026 (due 28 Sep); CVE-2026-86060 and CVE-2026-67277 added 10 Sep; CVE-2026-67276 not listed
Patched — RouterOS 7.24.2, 7.23.4, 6.49.21 (7.24.3 / 7.23.6 for CVE-2026-67278) Action: Find every MikroTik router at pump stations, lift stations and other remote sites, including any an integrator installed; upgrade RouterOS; block SSH and other management access from the internet; and check for CERT Polska’s indicators, such as an unexpected privileged account named “ops” added over SSH.

Critical Advisory — Controller authentication flaw, no known exploitation

Schneider Modicon M580 controllers have a critical authentication flaw (CVSS v4.0 9.2)

Schneider Electric published security notice SEVD-2026-251-04 on 8 September for CVE-2026-3869, an incorrectly implemented authentication algorithm (CWE-303) rated CVSS v4.0 9.2 (v3.1 9.8). It affects Modicon M580 controllers with application level below 4.00 and M580 Safety controllers below 4.20. No exploitation has been reported and it is not in KEV. The M580 is used for process control in water and wastewater among other sectors, including treatment and pump-station automation (editorial assessment). The fix is a firmware update plus an application-level change, which on a running treatment process needs a maintenance window and validation, so expect it to take longer than an IT patch.

High Advisory — Industrial telemetry database denial-of-service, no known exploitation

A single malformed packet can crash TDengine, a time-series database used for industrial telemetry

Industrial Cyber reported on 25 September that Ridge Security found CVE-2026-42542, a pre-authentication denial-of-service flaw in the TDengine time-series database rated CVSS 7.5 (version not stated). An integer underflow in how the server handles RPC message lengths on TCP port 6030 lets one crafted packet crash the taosd service before any login, and repeating it can hold the database in a permanent crash loop. Versions 3.4.0.0 to 3.4.1.5 are affected; 3.4.1.6 fixes it. Ridge says it has seen no in-the-wild exploitation and knows of no public exploit code. Where a utility’s SCADA historian or monitoring pipeline uses TDengine, an attack would blind trending and alarming rather than change control (editorial assessment).

No known exploitation reported — no public exploit code Not in CISA KEV
Patched — TDengine 3.4.1.6 (affected: 3.4.0.0–3.4.1.5) Action: Confirm whether any telemetry, historian or monitoring pipeline in your environment runs TDengine, upgrade to 3.4.1.6, and restrict TCP port 6030 to trusted hosts with firewall/ACL rules in the meantime.
Suggested SLA: Exposure check and port restriction — 7 days; upgrade — 30 days Owner:
