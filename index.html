Critical Incident — Engineering data theft

Clop’s PLM extortion campaign now names Shell, GE and Philips — the stolen material is plant drawings and blueprints

Clop has listed 43 new victims tied to its exploitation of internet-exposed PTC Windchill and FlexPLM, the product lifecycle management platforms used across aerospace, defence, automotive, heavy machinery, medtech and retail. On 13 August Shell confirmed it is investigating a “potential incident” after the gang claimed 89 GB of data; on 17 August GE said it is assessing the claim and Philips confirmed a contained compromise of one enterprise server with no customer impact. The gang advertises backups, project plans, facility photographs, drawings, diagrams and blueprints. The entry point is CVE-2026-12569, an unsafe-deserialisation flaw in Windchill and FlexPLM rated CVSS 9.3 by ReliaQuest, exploited for unauthenticated remote code execution and JSP web-shell deployment. PTC began patching on 17 June and warned of heightened threat activity on 26 June; CISA added the flaw to KEV on 25 June with a three-day federal remediation deadline, and Germany’s BSI phoned customers overnight to push patching. No OT or control-system impact has been reported at any victim — this is confidentiality loss, not disruption. The OT relevance is what was taken: PLM holds the as-designed record of plants, machines and safety systems, and it is rarely inventoried as an OT-adjacent asset or covered by an OT threat model.

Confirmed active exploitation — 43 claimed victims; Shell investigating, Philips confirms contained breach, GE assessing. No OT disruption reported CVE-2026-12569 — in CISA KEV since 25 Jun 2026 (CVSS 9.3, ReliaQuest)
Patched by PTC from 17 Jun 2026 — patch, then hunt; exploitation predates the fix Action: Find out today whether your Windchill or FlexPLM instance answers from the internet, and put it behind a VPN or access gateway regardless of patch state. Then hunt rather than assume: look for JSP web shells under the Windchill login path, unexpected outbound transfers, and new service accounts, and rotate any credentials the platform holds. Separately, add PLM, CAD vaults and engineering document stores to your OT asset register and decide who owns them — a stolen P&ID or safety-system drawing is reconnaissance for whoever buys it next.

Critical Incident — Nation-state

A confirmed nation-state actor pivoted through a shared private APN to stop a Polish CHP plant’s steam turbine

CERT Polska’s follow-up report on a 29 December 2025 intrusion describes an OT lateral-movement path most defenders have not modelled. The attacker started from a FortiGate appliance at a wind farm, used there as both firewall and VPN concentrator, and scanned the carrier-managed private APN for VNC, HTTP, Modbus and Siemens S7 services. That scan surfaced a WAGO PFC200 controller at an unrelated combined heat and power plant with a WAN-facing management interface and default credentials. The attacker enabled SSH, tunnelled into the plant’s OT network, and stopped the steam turbine and the process water treatment system; several ICS devices were permanently bricked during cleanup. The plant serves roughly 50,000 residents; heat and electricity deliveries were not interrupted. Polish reporting links the wider campaign, which reportedly targeted some 30 other energy sites, to Russia-linked activity. Researchers describe it as the first publicly documented use of a private APN as an OT lateral-movement path.

Confirmed OT-disruptive incident — steam turbine and water treatment system stopped; disclosed after the fact No CVE — exposed edge appliance, default credentials on a field controller, flat shared private APN
No patch — architectural: segment the APN, disable field-device management interfaces, monitor east–west OT traffic Action: Ask your cellular carrier, in writing, what else shares your private APN, and treat the answer as untrusted until tested. Require client isolation between all APN members, disable SSH and web management on internet- or WAN-facing PLCs and routers, rotate every default credential on field gear, and confirm your PLC logic backups are current and actually restorable — that is what got this plant running again.

Critical Exploitation — Virtualisation under OT

A suspected China-nexus actor is mass-exploiting a patched vCenter flaw — the layer most OT DMZs now run on

CVE-2026-59310 is a directory-traversal flaw in the VMware vCenter Syslog server, CVSS 9.8, that lets a remote attacker with network access to vCenter execute arbitrary code. Broadcom patched it on 29 July; exploitation began on 3 August, five days later. German incident-response firm Quirso counted 361 victim IP addresses across 47 countries, with roughly half in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran and France, and 343 of them already connected to attacker infrastructure by 5 August. Quirso assesses with moderate confidence a Chinese-speaking operator working in UTC+08:00. After compromise the actors drop the open-source reverse_ssh framework to hold an outbound channel that inbound filtering does not see; reporting describes at least one case ending in Babuk-derived ransomware. Victim IPs include hosting and cloud ranges, so the organisation count is unknown and no OT operator has been named. The OT relevance is structural rather than confirmed: at most modern plants vCenter hosts the historian, the HMI servers, the engineering jump host and the OT DMZ itself, so losing it stops production without an attacker ever touching a controller — the same pattern behind Dragos’s Q2 ransomware numbers.

Confirmed active exploitation since 3 Aug — 361 victim IPs in 47 countries; no OT operator named to date CVE-2026-59310 — not listed in CISA KEV as of this edition; exploitation confirmed by incident responders

Then hunt: look for reverse_ssh binaries and unexpected outbound connections from vCenter hosts — Quirso published a YARA rule, but validate hits, since the tool is also used legitimately. Longer term, put your hypervisors in the OT asset inventory and decide who owns their patch window before the next one.

Medium Research — Vehicle & fleet control systems

A heavy-truck brake controller’s safety recall quietly doubled as a security fix — and nobody told the fleets

Researchers at the National Motor Freight Traffic Association reported that a safety recall issued for the Bendix EC80 electronic brake controller also closed remote code execution and denial-of-service weaknesses, without the security content being called out to operators. Fleets that treated the recall as a routine mechanical action would have had no reason to prioritise it as a cyber remediation, and any vehicle that skipped the campaign is still carrying the flaws. The pattern is the one OT asset owners keep meeting in a different costume: a safety-certified component gets a fix through a safety channel, the security implications never reach the people who track vulnerabilities, and the two remediation programmes never reconcile. The same dynamic applies to firmware shipped inside process skids, packaged equipment and vendor-maintained safety systems — and it means a clean vulnerability-management report can be entirely accurate and still miss patched-but-unapplied safety-critical defects.

No confirmed exploitation reported — researcher disclosure; fix already shipped through the safety-recall channel Not in CISA KEV
Remediated by the recall — exposure is unapplied recalls, not an unpatched defect Action: Reconcile your safety-recall and service-bulletin backlog against your vulnerability register, for vehicles and for plant equipment alike. Ask each OEM and packaged-equipment vendor, in writing, whether their safety bulletins ever carry security content and to flag it explicitly in future. Then confirm someone actually owns the join: in most organisations recalls sit with maintenance or fleet, vulnerabilities sit with security, and no one is accountable for the overlap.

High Research — Cold chain & refrigeration

Claroty Team82 found 23 flaws in Copeland refrigeration controllers — and showed a compromised unit can spoil stock while the temperature display stays normal

Team82 disclosed 23 vulnerabilities in Copeland XWEB Pro supervisory controllers, 21 of them rated high severity, plus multiple remote-code-execution flaws in Danfoss AK-SM 800A refrigeration controllers. High Advisory — Enterprise platform at industrials

A CVSS 10.0 SAP Commerce Cloud flaw is being probed within days of the patch — and industrial operators run SAP everywhere

CVE-2026-58231 is an improper-authorisation flaw in the Data Hub Adapter for SAP Commerce Cloud, rated CVSS 10.0, that lets an unauthenticated attacker with network access abuse a default authentication client and submit crafted input to functions that do not validate it — ending in arbitrary code execution and compromise of internal components. SAP shipped the fix in its 11 August Security Patch Day alongside 27 other notes. Threat-intelligence firm Defused Cyber saw exploitation attempts hit its honeypots three days later, on 14 August, while noting the flaw has no public proof-of-concept and is not known to have been successfully exploited. SAP security firm Onapsis says an IP Filter Set restricting access to the vulnerable endpoint works as a temporary workaround. This is an enterprise-side flaw with no direct control-system reach, and it is here because SAP is the system of record sitting immediately upstream of production scheduling, spares, maintenance and shipping at most industrial operators — the same enterprise layer whose loss stopped plants in Dragos’s Q2 figures without an attacker ever reaching a controller.

Senators Klobuchar and Schiff introduced the Water Cyber Shield Act on 13 August, three weeks into the multistate PLC campaign. Info Policy — Water & wastewater

A volunteer-built “Water Watch Center” launched at DEF CON to put paid monitoring into the smallest utilities — the ones the campaign is hitting

DEF CON Franklin and the National Rural Water Association launched the Water Watch Center, aimed squarely at utilities serving fewer than 10,000 people — about 91% of the roughly 50,000 US community water systems, and the segment with no security staff at all. Five firms — Defendify, Legato Security, L1 Secure, Rapid7 and Sentinel Technologies — will deliver managed detection and response directly to participating utilities, with threat intelligence shared back through the NRWA. Seed funding came from Craig Newmark. The programme builds on a two-year pilot that paired roughly 450 volunteer practitioners with utilities in seven states, is expanding into Maryland, and is working with Vanderbilt on digital twins of water environments under DARPA’s CASTLE research.
