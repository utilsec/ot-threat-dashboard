Critical Incident — Engineering data theft

Clop’s PLM extortion campaign now names Shell, GE and Philips — the stolen material is plant drawings and blueprints

Clop has listed 43 new victims tied to its exploitation of internet-exposed PTC Windchill and FlexPLM, the product lifecycle management platforms used across aerospace, defence, automotive, heavy machinery, medtech and retail. On 13 August Shell confirmed it is investigating a “potential incident” after the gang claimed 89 GB of data; on 17 August GE said it is assessing the claim and Philips confirmed a contained compromise of one enterprise server with no customer impact. The gang advertises backups, project plans, facility photographs, drawings, diagrams and blueprints. The entry point is CVE-2026-12569, an input-validation flaw in Windchill and FlexPLM exploited as a zero-day from early June for unauthenticated remote code execution and JSP web-shell deployment. PTC began patching on 17 June; CISA added the flaw to KEV on 25 June with a three-day federal remediation deadline. No OT or control-system impact has been reported at any victim — this is confidentiality loss, not disruption. The OT relevance is what was taken: PLM holds the as-designed record of plants, machines and safety systems, and it is rarely inventoried as an OT-adjacent asset or covered by an OT threat model.

Critical Incident — Confirmed OT sabotage

An attacker crossed from a wind farm to a district heating plant through a shared private APN — and stopped a steam turbine

CERT Polska’s follow-up on the December 2025 Polish energy-sector intrusion, disclosed at DEF CON on 9 August and published on 11 August, describes what it calls the first observed case of an attacker reaching an OT network through a private cellular APN. The intruder compromised a FortiGate firewall at a wind farm, used a Teltonika cellular router on that network to enter a private APN operated by the distribution system operator, and from there reached a combined heat and power plant supplying roughly 50,000 residents — an entirely unrelated organisation. A steam turbine and the process-water treatment system were shut down, interrupting cogeneration. Operator response kept it to a short outage with no loss of heat supply. CERT Polska attributes the activity to a Russia-linked actor and notes the root cause was configuration, not exploitation: devices on the shared APN could reach each other freely, a pattern it found was common across Poland.

Confirmed hands-on-OT sabotage — turbine and water treatment stopped. Historic (Dec 2025); technical detail newly published 11 Aug 2026 No single CVE — edge-firewall compromise plus flat private-APN routing

If yes, you do not have a segmentation control, you have a flat WAN shared with everyone else on that APN — including organisations you have no relationship with. Demand device-to-device isolation or per-site APNs, put a firewall behind every cellular router rather than treating the router as the boundary, and log east-west traffic on the APN so a pivot is visible rather than inferred three months later.

Critical Incident — Mass exploitation

CISA put the mass-exploited vCenter flaw in KEV today — and virtualisation is where OT lives now

CISA added CVE-2026-59310 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August, formalising what responders had been reporting for a fortnight. Broadcom published VMSA-2026-0006 on 29 July for the flaw, a directory-traversal issue in the vCenter syslog service rated CVSS 9.8 with no workaround. German incident-response firm QUIRSO observed the first victims connecting to attacker infrastructure on 3 August, five days later; by 5 August roughly 95% of an eventual 361 victim IP addresses across 47 countries had already appeared, concentrated in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran and France. The actor gains root execution on vCenter, plants a cron job establishing reverse-SSH persistence, and in at least some cases deploys ransomware derived from leaked Babuk source. QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence that the operator is Chinese-speaking, based on language artefacts in attacker scripts, tooling choices, victimology that excludes mainland China, and working hours consistent with UTC+08:00. No OT or industrial victim has been named. The relevance is structural: HMIs, historians, engineering workstations and SCADA servers increasingly run as guests, so root on vCenter is root on the plant’s supervisory layer.

Confirmed mass exploitation — 361 victim IPs in 47 countries; reverse-SSH persistence and ransomware deployment observed. No named OT victim Added to CISA KEV 18 Aug 2026 — federal remediation deadline applies
Patched — Broadcom fixes available since 29 Jul 2026 Action: If any vCenter instance hosting OT-supporting guests was reachable from the corporate network or internet and was unpatched between 29 July and 5 August, treat it as compromised until proven otherwise: review syslog-service logs, check for unexpected root shells and new SSH keys, and validate hypervisor-level integrity before trusting the guests. Then fix the durable problem — management interfaces for hypervisors that run process-critical VMs should not be reachable from the enterprise network, and a hypervisor outage should have a documented manual-operations fallback.

Dragos’ second-quarter industrial ransomware analysis records 1,140 victims, up 12% from 1,020 in Q1. Refrigeration controllers can be switched off while the temperature display keeps reading normal

Claroty Team82 examined two supervisory controller platforms that run commercial and industrial refrigeration — Danfoss AK-SM 800A and Copeland XWEB Pro — and found serious flaws in both. Six agencies profile Gunra ransomware — and its way in is the VPN and firewall your integrators dial through

CISA, the FBI, DC3, NSA, the Secret Service and the Republic of Korea’s National Police Agency published joint advisory AA26-222A on 10 August under the #StopRansomware programme. Critical Advisory — HMI gateway

Haiwell’s IoT Cloud HMI gateway lets an unauthenticated attacker run OS commands as root

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02, published 13 August, describes an OS command injection flaw in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway. The Net Check feature reachable through the /setting endpoint passes user-supplied input to a cmdPing Socket.io event without sanitisation, so an attacker can inject and execute arbitrary operating-system commands with root privileges. Haiwell has released Scada-v3.50.1.19 to address it. The product is a cloud-connected HMI and remote-access gateway — the class of device that exists precisely to be reachable, which is what makes an unauthenticated root-command flaw in it worth treating as urgent even though no exploitation has been reported. Haiwell equipment is common in smaller integrator-built machine and process installations, where it is often commissioned once and never revisited.

Fixed versions landed on 14 August (GeoServer 3.0.1, 2.28.5, 2.27.6; GeoTools 35.1, 34.5, 33.6) under GHSA-mqjf-5f49-2fjh, reported at CVSS 9.8; no CVE had been assigned at the time of writing. It is a regression of CVE-2023-25158, patched in 2023. Against PostGIS-backed stores an attacker-supplied value is written into SQL unescaped; where GeoServer connects as a PostgreSQL superuser or a role holding pg_execute_server_program, that becomes OS command execution on the database host. Without those privileges it still reads whatever the database user can. No confirmed compromise and no OT impact have been reported. The OT relevance is where these servers sit: water and electric utilities, ports, pipelines and municipalities run GeoServer to publish network maps, asset registers, outage and leak data — frequently internet-facing for a public viewer, frequently owned by a GIS team with no security programme, and frequently pointed at a database holding a detailed picture of the physical estate. Earlier GeoServer flaws are already in CISA KEV.

Patched 14 Aug 2026 — GeoServer 3.0.1 / 2.28.5 / 2.27.6 Action: Ask your GIS team a question OT security rarely asks: is GeoServer running anywhere, and is it reachable from the internet? Patch to a fixed release, then fix the more durable problem — the database role. GeoServer almost never needs PostgreSQL superuser or pg_execute_server_program; strip both and the same injection stops being remote code execution on your GIS database. Put a public map viewer behind a reverse proxy rather than exposing GeoServer directly, and treat the asset data behind it as sensitive infrastructure detail, because that is what it is.

The Water Cyber Shield Act would give EPA mandatory-assessment authority and $300M a year for utility cybersecurity

Senators Klobuchar and Schiff introduced the Water Cyber Shield Act on 13 August, three weeks into the multistate PLC campaign. A volunteer-built “Water Watch Center” launched at DEF CON to put paid monitoring into the smallest utilities — the ones the campaign is hitting

DEF CON Franklin and the National Rural Water Association launched the Water Watch Center, aimed squarely at utilities serving fewer than 10,000 people — about 91% of the roughly 50,000 US community water systems, and the segment with no security staff at all. Five firms — Defendify, Legato Security, L1 Secure, Rapid7 and Sentinel Technologies — will deliver managed detection and response directly to participating utilities, with threat intelligence shared back through the NRWA. Info Guidance — All critical infrastructure

“CI Fortify”: five agencies now tell operators to plan for cutting OT loose and running it alone

CISA, Australia’s ACSC, the FBI, the UK NCSC and the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security published joint guidance on isolating vital operational technology from every other network during a disruption or crisis, and keeping it running that way for an extended period. The document asks operators to map every interconnection into vital systems — corporate IT, vendor remote access, cloud platforms, third-party and carrier networks — with architecture diagrams, routing, encryption and ownership documented, then to pre-plan the cut. Physical separation is named the strongest control, including dedicated fibre or CWDM paths for distributed sites rather than shared infrastructure. It also asks for tested manual or alternative SCADA paths so essential service continues in a degraded state. The water utilities that reverted to manual operation this month are the live example of what this guidance is asking you to rehearse.

Voluntary guidance — no compliance deadline, no exploitation associated Not applicable
Not applicable — architecture, dependency mapping and continuity planning Action: Treat this as the checklist for the question your executives are already asking after the water campaign: can we run without IT, and for how long? Produce a one-page list of every path into your vital OT — including the ones a carrier or integrator owns, which is exactly where the Polish CHP pivot came from — and mark which you could sever within an hour. Then run the drill: disconnect a non-critical site’s enabling systems and see what actually stops. Manual fallback that has never been exercised is a plan, not a capability.
