The Iran-linked water-utility PLC campaign is now formally attributed — and internet-exposed controllers are still the way in

Since 26–27 July, actors have reached internet-exposed Rockwell Automation / Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 controllers at water and wastewater utilities in at least 12 states, changing device IP addresses and setting unauthorised passwords to lock operators out of monitoring and control. More than 30 Minnesota community systems were affected, including a brief state of emergency in Maple Plain and a pump failure with a conservation order in Braham. Clayton County, Georgia issued a boil-water advisory after a pump-station failure; Michigan reported nine affected systems; South Dakota reported a wastewater lift station in Rapid City; and the campaign's newest confirmed incidents, in Cape May and Woodbine, New Jersey and Childersburg, Alabama, hit water, sewer and gas utilities. Officials maintain drinking-water safety itself was not compromised. The joint advisory AA26-097A — FBI, CISA, NSA, EPA, DOE, the USCYBERCOM Cyber National Mission Force and Treasury, originally published 7 April and updated 22 July — is the correction that matters this week: the agencies have formally assessed this activity to a group of Iranian-affiliated APT actors, naming CyberAv3ngers (also known as the Shahid Kaveh Group), affiliated with the IRGC Cyber Electronic Command. That is a federal attribution, hedged with “assess” rather than proven, not an absence of one. The individual August incidents in New Jersey and Alabama have not each been separately attributed in agency statements, though press reporting describes them as Iran-linked. No CVE was exploited: AA26-097A describes actors using leased third-party infrastructure and the manufacturers' own PLC programming software to reach controllers that were internet-accessible and lacked adequate access control — exposure plus default, weak or absent credentials, not a software flaw.

Critical Incident — Nation-state

A confirmed nation-state actor pivoted through a shared private APN to stop a Polish CHP plant’s steam turbine

CERT Polska’s follow-up report on a 29 December 2025 intrusion describes an OT lateral-movement path most defenders have not modelled. The attacker started from a FortiGate appliance at a wind farm, used there as both firewall and VPN concentrator, and scanned the carrier-managed private APN for VNC, HTTP, Modbus and Siemens S7 services. That scan surfaced a WAGO PFC200 controller at an unrelated combined heat and power plant with a WAN-facing management interface and default credentials. The attacker enabled SSH, tunnelled into the plant’s OT network, and stopped the steam turbine and the process water treatment system; several ICS devices were permanently bricked during cleanup. The plant serves roughly 50,000 residents; heat and electricity deliveries were not interrupted. Polish reporting links the wider campaign, which reportedly targeted some 30 other energy sites, to Russia-linked activity. Researchers describe it as the first publicly documented use of a private APN as an OT lateral-movement path.

Confirmed OT-disruptive incident — steam turbine and water treatment system stopped; disclosed after the fact No CVE — exposed edge appliance, default credentials on a field controller, flat shared private APN
No patch — architectural: segment the APN, disable field-device management interfaces, monitor east–west OT traffic Action: Ask your cellular carrier, in writing, what else shares your private APN, and treat the answer as untrusted until tested. Require client isolation between all APN members, disable SSH and web management on internet- or WAN-facing PLCs and routers, rotate every default credential on field gear, and confirm your PLC logic backups are current and actually restorable — that is what got this plant running again.
Suggested SLA: APN isolation review and field-device management lockdown — 30 days Owner:

High Incident — Ransomware

US and South Korean agencies warn Gunra ransomware is walking in through unpatched edge appliances, not novel exploits

CISA, the FBI and South Korean counterparts published a joint #StopRansomware advisory, AA26-222A, on the Gunra operation, a Conti-derived ransomware-as-a-service group running double extortion against government and critical-infrastructure victims.
