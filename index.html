Medium Nozomi Networks — Time-Sensitive Networking (CC-Link IE TSN) protocol research

The protocol that guarantees your safety signals arrive on time can be used to forge them — and to drift the clock quietly

Nozomi Networks researchers showed that Time-Sensitive Networking, the family of IEEE Ethernet amendments industrial sites are adopting to give control and safety traffic deterministic delivery, can be turned against the processes it protects. Working on CC-Link IE TSN, one of the more widely deployed implementations, the team reverse-engineered the synchronisation behaviour, predicted valid timing values and injected crafted frames into the correct time slot so they were accepted as legitimate scheduled communications. In a lab demonstration they manipulated process variables directly — starting and stopping a robotic arm and making it release the object it was holding. A quieter variant injects a small, hard-to-detect drift into OT synchronisation clocks, so scheduling errors accumulate long after the intrusion and are difficult to trace back. The protocol flaw was covered by a CISA ICS advisory on 30 July.

Vendor research presented at Black Hat USA, reported 21 Aug 2026 by Dark Reading, with the underlying protocol issue covered by CISA advisory ICSA-26-211-07 (30 Jul 2026). No in-the-wild exploitation is reported. The attack is not remote: it requires the ability to see and inject traffic inside the TSN segment. The researchers reached that position by finding previously unknown bugs in Phoenix Contact TSN switches that allowed them to pivot from the switch’s remote management interface to its process port — so the realistic precondition is an exposed or reachable switch management plane, not an attacker already sitting on the control network. Nozomi is a wholly owned Mitsubishi Electric subsidiary and CC-Link IE TSN is a Mitsubishi Electric protocol; the researchers say Nozomi operates independently and maintains a vendor-neutral roadmap. Confirmed vs claimed: the technique is demonstrated in the researchers’ own lab, corroborated by a CISA advisory; the broader claim that other TSN protocols share the weakness is the team’s stated hypothesis, not yet tested Not in CISA KEV — no evidence of exploitation; CISA advisory ICSA-26-211-07 covers the CC-Link IE TSN issue

For new builds, put deterministic-network segmentation and management-plane isolation into the design review while the cabling is still on paper — segregation is currently the only real control.

FERC signs off on CIP-014-4 — a tighter, more consistent test for which substations count as critical

FERC issued an order on 10 September 2026 approving NERC Reliability Standard CIP-014-4, published in the Federal Register on 15 September. CIP-014 is the physical security standard for the bulk-power system, and this revision targets the part registered entities have found most contestable: how transmission stations, transmission substations and primary control centres are identified and assessed as critical to reliable operation. The revision pushes toward a more consistent, repeatable identification methodology across entities rather than adding a new control set.
