Two opposite motions are running at once, and practitioners have noticed. On one side the lever is getting heavier: late amendments to the UK Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, reported on 3 September, would let ministers stop critical-sector organisations using technology suppliers deemed high risk — improving critical infrastructure not by demanding more of the operator but by disconnecting it from suppliers judged inadequate (see Regulation & Policy). Critical Incident — Confirmed OT disruption, energy; first documented private-APN pivot

Attackers walked from a wind farm’s firewall to another plant’s PLCs across a shared cellular APN — and stopped a turbine

CERT Polska’s follow-up report on attacks against Poland’s energy sector documents an intrusion path that maps almost exactly onto the exposure CISA and the NCSC are now warning about, and it is the reason this older item belongs on the page. Attackers compromised a FortiGate firewall at a wind farm, moved to a Teltonika cellular router, and then crossed a private cellular APN that had been provisioned without client isolation — meaning every SIM on that APN could reach every other. On the far side sat a WAGO PFC200 at a separate combined heat and power plant, still carrying default administrator credentials, which the attackers used to build an SSH bridge into the OT network. From there they forced three Siemens PLCs into STOP and set password protection on them, shutting down a steam turbine and the process-water treatment serving a town of roughly 50,000 people. CERT Polska assesses this as the first documented real-world OT intrusion via lateral movement across a private APN, and notes the shared-APN-without-isolation configuration is common in Poland and likely well beyond it.

Confirmed by CERT Polska — the national CERT’s energy-sector incident follow-up report, published 10 Aug 2026, describes the full chain and the operational outcome, including the turbine and water-treatment shutdown at a second facility. The incident itself occurred in 2025 and is disclosed here for the first time in this detail. Not attributed: CERT Polska publishes the intrusion path and tooling without naming an actor No single CVE drives this — the chain ran on an internet-reachable edge firewall, an unisolated carrier APN and a default controller password
Patching alone would not have stopped it — the decisive weaknesses were architectural: APN client isolation and a default credential Action: Treat every private APN as an untrusted external network, not as an extension of your plant LAN. Ask your carrier, in writing, whether client isolation is enabled on your APN and whether any SIM on it can reach any other; if the answer is no or unclear, that is a finding, not a query. Then put the APN behind the same boundary you would give a vendor VPN — an allow-list of exactly which field devices may talk to exactly which OT hosts on exactly which ports, terminated on a firewall you control rather than on the carrier’s. Separately, run a credential sweep across every field controller and gateway reached over cellular: a WAGO, Teltonika or comparable device still holding its shipped administrator password is the single cheapest thing an attacker can find. Finally, note where the damage actually landed — at a facility the wind farm’s operators did not consider connected to them. Map which of your sites share a carrier, an integrator or a management platform, because that shared plane is the blast radius nobody drew.
Suggested SLA: Carrier APN isolation confirmation and allow-list enforcement — 30 days; default-credential sweep on all cellular-reached field devices — 14 days Owner:

Then take the two mitigations the NSA actually wrote down: firmware updates on IoT and network devices, and isolating critical systems from edge devices so a boundary compromise does not become a control-network one. Finally, look at your own IoT population — the routers, cameras, printers and cellular gateways at unstaffed field sites are exactly what QTRouter recruited, and yours can be conscripted into someone else’s operation without any effect you would ever notice.

How to read the indicators. Exploitation and CISA KEV tags reflect status at generation time — “No confirmed exploitation reported / Not in CISA KEV” means none was reported as of this edition, not that risk is absent. Suggested SLAs are starting points (Critical ≈ 7 days, High ≈ 30 days) to adapt to your own risk tolerance. Owner and status notes you enter are saved only in your local browser and are not transmitted anywhere.
