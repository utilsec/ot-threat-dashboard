Geopolitics Iran’s sabotage campaign moves from attribution to arrest warrants and EU sanctions

On 16 November EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said new sanctions on Iran are being prepared, after France asked partners to act over the October refrigeration attack and the Paris prosecutor sought arrest warrants for three IRGC-linked officers. AttackIQ research released on 17 November maps 20 Iranian state-backed groups and documents how they hand network access from spying teams to destructive ones, sometimes through contractors and suppliers. The debate now is how far allies follow France, and whether exposed controllers come offline faster than the campaign widens.

AI & Speed AI is shrinking the gap between finding a flaw and using it

Unit 42 tied Cairn-driven intrusions to 10 organizations, including an industrial-automation maker. Defenders are using the same tools: Forescout said its AI harness found 99 flaws in embedded TCP/IP stacks used in OT in 90 hours.

Supply Chain Buyers want proof of what is inside the box

Days later Finite State raised $27 million to analyse the firmware actually shipped in industrial and utility devices, arguing it often differs from what the source code says. On 18 November Microsoft’s chief executive told the New York Times DealBook Summit that much critical infrastructure, naming chiller and HVAC firmware, is effectively unpatchable yet connected to the internet, which is why buyers now ask about support lifetimes before they sign (editorial assessment).

Remote Access Remote-management tools remain the busiest attack path

N-able shipped a third N-central hotfix on 16 November after exploits reached servers that took the second, and ConnectWise said attacks on unpatched ScreenConnect continue after rogue clients spread through hijacked support panels at 48 organizations. CISA added exploited SonicWall VPN, Apache Axis 1.x and Cisco Expressway flaws to KEV this month, and Mandiant said on 19 November that a suspected China-linked group got in through a BeyondTrust Remote Support flaw (see Critical Advisories). SonicWall Gen 6 firewalls, still common at small sites, will get no further security patches and should come off the internet edge. These tools are how many integrators and MSPs reach plant-side hosts, so a supplier’s remote-access hygiene is part of the plant’s own attack surface (editorial assessment).

Resilience Can plants really run without IT? Leaders and engineers disagree

SANS’s 2026 ICS survey, published 12 November, found every security leader expects plants to ride out a full IT compromise, while only 15% of practitioners say they could run safely by hand beyond 48 hours. Recent outages sharpened the point: Brazil’s Santos port moved to manual handling, Osaki Electric halted smart-meter production and a Chilean solar-and-battery plant is still off the grid (see Incidents & Attacks), and Sweden’s Kinnarps stopped three factories after an IT intrusion. A German hotel pool’s chlorine-doser alarm was stuck in the Microsoft 365 outage, injuring guests. On 17 November CISA’s acting director said outages from bad patches deserve the same resilience planning as attacks. Safety alarms need a path that does not depend on the cloud, and recovery plans need a realistic reconnection timeline.
