Six agencies publish the seventeen ways attackers take Active Directory — which is how most of them reach a control network

CISA, the NSA and allied cyber agencies issued joint guidance cataloguing seventeen techniques used to compromise Active Directory, with detection and mitigation advice for each. Info Regulation — FERC order approving NERC Reliability Standard CIP-014-4; effective 1 October 2028

The substation risk assessment just got a method, a 36-month clock and a 1,500-foot neighbour test

FERC issued an order on 10 September approving NERC Reliability Standard CIP-014-4, with the Federal Register notice published on 15 September and an effective date of 1 October 2028. The revisions target the part of CIP-014 that NERC concluded lacked rigour and consistency: the R1 risk assessment. Transmission owners must now identify applicable facilities existing or planned to be in service within a 36-month window, aligning the cycle with transmission planning; use a documented risk assessment methodology performing both steady-state and dynamic simulations on at least one system peak load case and one off-peak case; and identify proximate bulk electric system stations and substations within 1,500 feet, fence line to fence line, to evaluate the effect of an attack on adjacent equipment. It is a physical security standard, not a cyber one — but the assessment work, the ownership questions and the budget cycle all land on the same teams.

Regulatory action, not a threat — confirmed: FERC issued its order approving Reliability Standard CIP-014-4 on 10 Sep 2026, with the Federal Register notice published 15 Sep 2026 and an effective date of 1 Oct 2028. The standard provides a consistent approach for identifying and assessing transmission stations, transmission substations and primary control centres critical to bulk-power system operation. Key changes: the risk assessment cycle aligns to a 36-month timeframe covering facilities existing or planned to be in service; a documented risk assessment methodology is mandated, specifying both steady-state and dynamic simulations using at least one system peak load case and one system off-peak load case; and a new provision requires identification of proximate existing bulk electric system transmission stations and substations within 1,500 feet (457 metres) measured fence line to fence line, to evaluate the impact of a physical attack on adjacent equipment. Info Regulation — EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting obligations now in force

The clock has started: anyone selling a connected product into the EU now owes a 24-hour warning on exploited vulnerabilities

The reporting obligations under the EU Cyber Resilience Act took effect on 11 September, with ENISA operating a Single Reporting Platform as the entry point. Manufacturers of products with digital elements placed on the EU market must notify an actively exploited vulnerability within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, followed by fuller reporting, and must report severe incidents affecting product security on the same initial clock. For OT asset owners the direct obligation is limited — this falls on manufacturers — but the second-order effects are not. Expect more advisories, published faster, from vendors who previously disclosed quietly or not at all; expect procurement and legal teams to start asking vendors for CRA conformity evidence; and expect the vendors who cannot meet it to quietly stop selling certain legacy lines into Europe, which is a supportability question for anyone running them.
