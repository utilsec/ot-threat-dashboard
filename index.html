Critical Incident — Confirmed OT disruption

CERT Polska: an attacker crossed a shared private cellular network from a wind farm into a heating plant and stopped its turbine

After an investigation running more than three months, CERT Polska disclosed on 8 August what it assesses to be the first real-world case of an attacker reaching an OT network through a private APN. The intrusion began at a wind farm’s edge, moved onto a cellular router, and crossed a distribution system operator’s private access point name — a dedicated mobile network the DSO had provisioned with a carrier — into a controller at an entirely unrelated combined heat and power plant. At the CHP plant the attacker stopped a steam turbine and the water treatment system that produces process water, interrupting cogeneration at a facility supplying heat to roughly 50,000 residents. The incident itself occurred on 29 December 2025; the disclosure and the technical detail are new this month, and the design pattern it breaks is one agencies have actively recommended.

Critical Incident — Engineering data theft

Clop’s PLM extortion campaign names Shell, GE and Philips — and the stolen material is plant drawings and blueprints

Clop has listed more than forty organisations tied to its exploitation of internet-exposed PTC Windchill and FlexPLM, the product lifecycle management platforms used across aerospace, defence, automotive, heavy machinery, medtech and process industry. Shell confirmed on 14 August that it is investigating a potential incident after the gang claimed 89 GB; GE said it is assessing a claim of 391 GB; Philips confirmed a contained compromise with no impact on customer environments against a claim of 13.5 GB. The advertised inventory is backups, project plans, facility photographs, drawings, diagrams and blueprints. The entry point is CVE-2026-12569, an unauthenticated remote code execution flaw in Windchill and FlexPLM rated CVSS 9.8 and reached through deserialisation of untrusted data. No OT or control-system impact has been reported at any victim — this is confidentiality loss, not disruption. The OT relevance is what was taken: PLM holds the as-designed record of your plants, machines and safety systems, and in most organisations it is neither inventoried as an OT-adjacent asset nor covered by an OT threat model. An adversary who has your drawings does not need your network to plan against you. Two developments since: Clop began publishing full victim names on 12 August and has now listed more than forty organisations, among them Ingersoll Rand, Zebra Technologies, Mindray and Largan Precision, while GE has quietly disappeared from the leak site. More consequential for defenders, ReliaQuest reported on 19 August that the implant is purpose-built rather than a generic web shell: it maps the application’s vault, decrypts every credential held in the Windchill keystore and carries a custom Java class loader that lets the operator run further code inside the application process. Those keystore credentials are how PLM talks to the systems around it — directories, file shares, ERP, sometimes engineering workstations — so this stops being only a data-theft story and becomes a credential-compromise one.

CISA published AA26-237A on 25 August, comparing two red-team assessments run with near-identical tradecraft: one against a government services and facilities organisation, one against a water and wastewater systems entity. High Threat intelligence — Control device security

Team82 chained 23 flaws in Copeland refrigeration controllers to root, then warmed a fridge while the display kept reading normal

Claroty’s Team82 disclosed 23 vulnerabilities in the Copeland XWEB Pro supervisory controller family — XWEB300D PRO and XWEB500D PRO — 21 of them rated high severity. The chain starts with a logic flaw in the device’s Lua-based authentication middleware: because any Lua table, including an empty one, evaluates as true, supplying an unrecognised auth_mode returns an empty-but-truthy object and the router waves the request through to the administrative API. A separate predictable password-generation routine yields a deterministic daily administrator credential, giving a second way in. From there Team82 found 19 OS command-injection issues across API and CGI endpoints, all running with elevated privileges, for root-level remote code execution. The part that should interest engineers is the physical demonstration: from a compromised XWEB the researchers drove a serially connected XR60CX field controller over Modbus, disabled the compressor and cooling fans, and simultaneously fed the display the probe’s legitimate reading — so the load spoiled while the temperature record, the same record kept for regulatory compliance, looked clean. Copeland worked with the researchers and shipped firmware 1.13.

No in-the-wild exploitation reported — coordinated disclosure, vendor-patched. The physical impact was demonstrated in Team82’s own test rig, not observed at a victim site Not in CISA KEV. Individual CVE IDs are not consistently published across the 23 findings — treat firmware version, not a CVE list, as the tracking key
Patched — Copeland XWEB Pro firmware 1.13 Action: Find your refrigeration and environmental supervisory controllers before you go looking for CVEs — on most sites they were installed by a refrigeration contractor, sit on a flat store or plant network, and appear in no asset register. CISA adds an N-able N-central authentication bypass to KEV after real customer compromises — the RMM that manages your plant IT

CVE-2026-18577 is an authentication bypass in N-able N-central that lets a remote, unauthenticated attacker reach administrative access on the server without valid credentials. It carries a CVSS score of 8.2 and is an incomplete-patch bypass of an earlier fix, CVE-2026-18556 — the second time this login path has had to be closed. Exploitation is confirmed, not theoretical: N-able’s own managed-detection team caught the activity in a customer environment on 31 July, Huntress reported a further victim on 3 August, and CISA has since added the flaw to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue. The reason this belongs on an OT dashboard is what sits downstream. N-central is a managed-service platform, and an attacker with administrative access can abuse the built-in Take Control remote-session feature to pivot straight onto every endpoint the server manages, then install persistence. For a plant that outsources IT to an MSP, that is a pre-authorised software agent already running on engineering workstations, historians and jump hosts — a supply-chain route that bypasses your perimeter entirely. All versions before 2026.3.1.7 are affected; the fix is 2026.3 HF1, and N-able has since shipped a second hotfix.

Info Regulation — EU Cyber Resilience Act

Two CRA dates land five weeks apart: standards adoption on 30 August, then 24-hour exploited-vulnerability reporting from 11 September

The standardisation programme underpinning the EU Cyber Resilience Act sets 30 August 2026 as the latest date for adoption of the horizontal Type A standard and the Type B standards covering vulnerability management. The harder date follows on 11 September 2026, when the CRA’s reporting obligations begin: manufacturers placing products with digital elements on the EU market — explicitly including OT and industrial equipment, embedded systems and networking gear — must notify ENISA and the relevant national CSIRT of an actively exploited vulnerability within 24 hours. Most of the wider CRA obligations do not bite until December 2027, which is why the September date is widely missed. For industrial equipment this is the point at which the CRA stops being an abstraction: IEC 62443 is emerging as the key technical reference for CRA compliance in industrial settings, which means the standard many operators have used as a voluntary maturity model becomes the yardstick their suppliers are measured against in a regulated conformity process. Asset owners are not the regulated party here, but they are the beneficiary — and the practical effect from September is that a vendor learning of exploitation in your installed base is on a clock to tell a regulator, which makes it far harder for that news to reach you late.
