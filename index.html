Top Story

Refrigeration hack hits about 700 French supermarkets; France’s cyber agency now names Copeland as well as Danfoss

Attackers remotely forced refrigeration controllers into defrost at French supermarkets on 11–13 October, pushing cabinets toward 50°C, starting fires at some stores and spoiling stock. Officials put the toll at about 700 of 10,000 supermarkets. CERT-FR named Danfoss AK-series, then Copeland and Dixell XWEB controllers; both makers say the affected generations are end-of-life, so no patches are coming. On 20 October The Record reported that the EU Cyber Resilience Act will not force fixes for products already on the market, leaving owners to disconnect or replace them. No group, ransom demand, entry route or CVE is confirmed, and the Paris prosecutor is investigating. Anyone running web-managed refrigeration or building controllers should treat this as a live warning.

The exploited flaws this cycle sit at the boundary between outside networks and the plant: three Citrix NetScaler zero-days now exploited (see top story), Check Point VPN and F5 BIG-IP flaws past their federal deadlines, Cisco’s fifth SD-WAN zero-day of 2026, and a FortiMail zero-day in KEV. On 7 October the FBI and Secret Service added that the FortiBleed credential campaign against Fortinet VPN gateways is still active, Ivanti disclosed on 13 October that three chained Connect Secure zero-days gave root on fully patched VPNs from at least 3 October, and SonicWall shipped fixes for a maximum-severity SMA1000 flaw that two threat-intel firms now report exploitation attempts (first seen 8 October). Palo Alto Networks added a PAN-OS management-interface zero-day (CVE-2026-0322) to the list on 14 October, with limited exploitation and a CISA KEV listing (see Critical Advisories). None of these devices runs a control loop, but each is a path a vendor, integrator or technician uses to reach one. On 20 October the CISA and FBI advisory on SafePay ransomware (see Incidents & Attacks) again pointed to VPN and firewall flaws among its common ways in.

Medium Incident report — Unit 42 analysis of a telecom managed-service-provider intrusion via FortiManager and FortiOS; suspected nation-state tradecraft

Unit 42 traces the Peerstitch implant to a FortiOS flaw used after a FortiManager compromise exposed more than 500 managed firewalls

Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 described an intrusion at a telecommunications managed service provider in which attackers exploited FortiManager (CVE-2024-47575) to take configurations, credentials and cryptographic material for more than 500 managed FortiGate firewalls. They then pushed packet-capture rules to downstream firewalls to collect HTTP, TFTP, RADIUS and LDAP traffic, and used a FortiOS auto-script path-traversal flaw (CVE-2025-61624, per the report) to load Peerstitch, a memory-resident ARM64 implant that leaves no disk binary and opens no listening port. Logs and rogue accounts were wiped. Unit 42 links some tradecraft to suspected nation-state actors; intrusions into the provider’s customer networks were not confirmed, but the stolen secrets expose them. The relayed account’s timeline is internally inconsistent, so check the original Unit 42 report for dates.

High Advisory — joint CISA/FBI ransomware advisory AA26-293A on SafePay, a group the agencies say has hit at least 600 organizations, many in critical infrastructure

CISA and the FBI warn that SafePay ransomware has breached more than 600 organizations since August 2024

The FBI, CISA and partner agencies in Germany and the UK describe SafePay as one of the most active ransomware groups of 2026, with victims across North America, South America, Europe and Australia and “multiple critical infrastructure sectors.” Entry is usually through exposed or guessed credentials, VPN access and known flaws in edge devices and internet-facing systems; the group then steals data, deletes shadow copies, resets network-attached storage that holds backups, and uses remote-access tools such as AnyDesk or TeamViewer to push the encryptor. The advisory carries indicators and ATT&CK mappings for activity from November 2024 to August 2026. Reporting on it does not describe any OT or manufacturing impact, so the OT risk here is the usual one: a ransomware crew reaching the IT network that plant systems depend on.

Info Regulation — FERC staff report on CIP audit lessons learned flags late registration of new generation, aggregated low-impact systems and AI tools inside CIP networks; no CVE

FERC staff publish CIP audit lessons: unregistered new generation, stacked low-impact systems and AI tools in CIP networks

On 13 October FERC released a staff report drawing on its fiscal-2026 audits of NERC-registered entities. Most cybersecurity processes reviewed met the mandatory CIP standards, but auditors found three recurring problems. Some generator owners registered new resources late or never identified their BES Cyber Systems, and several inverter-based resources were running commercially without the required cyber evaluation, one more than a year past the threshold. Two entities treated systems as low impact that together met the medium-impact criteria once affiliate and contractor control was counted. Auditors also saw what the report calls “super intelligence” (SI) tools — chatbots, assistants and automated analytics — appearing inside CIP-networked environments, where they can mimic user behavior and open unauthorized access or lateral-movement paths faster than compliance programs adapt. The report is staff guidance, not a new requirement, and it suggests NERC may consider further guidance or standards changes.
