Critical Incident — PHP2Shell plus Gaia root flaw CVE-2026-123039 used to backdoor Check Point gateways and wipe a VMware ESXi host (wave 1 from 30 Oct, Israel; wave 2 from 2 Nov, multiple countries); CVE-2026-123039 added to CISA KEV 13 Nov 2026

Attackers rooted unpatched Check Point gateways through PHP2Shell, planted backdoors and wiped a VMware host; the root flaw is now in KEV

Check Point Research said on 12 November that attackers exploited the PHP2Shell flaws on unpatched Check Point Mobile Access portals from 30 October, about a week after fixes shipped, then used a local flaw in the gateway’s DynamicCLI service (CVE-2026-123039) to become root. They planted web shells and backdoors named TSGA and TSProxy, scanned the internal network, moved over SSH to a VMware ESXi host and ran a wiper that overwrites and deletes files. The first wave hit Israeli organizations, some destructively; a second wave from 2 November reached gateways in several countries and sectors with no destruction seen. The backdoors resemble tooling linked to the China-nexus group UNC5221, but Check Point does not attribute the campaign with confidence. CISA added CVE-2026-123039 to KEV on 13 November. No OT victim is reported, but these firewalls often sit at the IT/OT boundary and ESXi hosts often run HMIs and historians (editorial assessment).

On 11 November Maersk confirmed that attackers extracted data from a single IT system it inherited through an earlier acquisition, which it did not name. Info Research — SANS 2026 State of ICS/OT Security survey (released 10 Nov 2026), nearly 700 respondents; no CVE

SANS survey: a third of industrial organizations reported a cyber incident this year, and most of those incidents disrupted operations

The SANS Institute’s 2026 State of ICS/OT Security survey, released on 10 November, found that 34% of nearly 700 industrial respondents had a cyber incident, up from 22% last year and the largest one-year jump in the survey’s history; 72% of those incidents disrupted operations. Shared IT/OT credentials and ransomware tied as the most common incident types (31% each), and incidents affecting safety rose from 8% to 13%. Resilience is thin: only 15% could run critical operations manually for more than 48 hours, 40% do not know, and 58% say they would be unable to operate within a year if their main automation vendor disappeared. A follow-up analysis on 12 November points to a confidence gap: every responding security chief expects plants to keep running through a full IT compromise, yet only 14% of leaders report routine technical validation such as ICS-specific threat hunting, and only 41% of incidents were detected within 24 hours. These are self-reported survey results, not incident data.

Volt Typhoon has used a rotating “Silent Train” relay network since 2023 to break into management platforms, with water, energy and transport among the victims, Lumen reports

Lumen’s Black Lotus Labs reported on 28 October that Volt Typhoon has used “Silent Train”, a covert network of rapidly rotating, single-use chains of compromised small-office routers, IoT and enterprise devices, since November 2023 to reach targets and keep access to management and control-plane systems. Lumen ties the group to four confirmed zero-day campaigns and one likely one: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN (CVE-2026-20127, CVSS v3.1 10.0, Cisco), VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-16812), N-able N-central (CVE-2025-8875 and CVE-2025-8876), Versa Director (no CVE given) and, with moderate confidence, Quest KACE (no CVE). These are older flaws, not the VeloCloud flaw CVE-2026-114625 that CISA added to KEV on 23 October. Victims include telecoms, government, defence contractors, IT firms and MSPs, and universities, and Lumen lists water, energy, transportation, maritime, rail, aviation and emergency services among the critical-infrastructure sectors hit; Hawaii, California and Texas recur as focal points (Zero Day). No victim is named and no OT or ICS intrusion is described. Lumen points defenders to CISA’s Volt Typhoon guidance (AA24-038A) and its advisory on China-nexus covert networks (AA26-113A). Management and orchestration platforms often hold trusted paths into plant networks, which makes them the asset to protect here (editorial assessment).

High Threat Intel — Binarly research (reported 5 Nov 2026) on a Zyxel GS1900 switch zero-day, CVE-2026-107441; earlier GS1900 flaw CVE-2026-7273 in CISA KEV; activity tied to a Chinese-speaking group

Zyxel GS1900 managed switches: a new zero-day slipped past June’s fix, after attackers had already pulled data from about 1,000 of them

Binarly says CVE-2026-107441, an out-of-bounds write in the web-request handler of Zyxel’s GS1900 switches, gives root command execution to anyone who can reach the management interface without logging in, and that attackers already had a working exploit when it reported the bug on 29 September. Zyxel scores it 8.8 (Binarly 9.8). The June firmware that fixed the earlier GS1900 flaw, CVE-2026-7273 (in CISA KEV since 21 September), does not stop it; firmware 2.90 Patch 3, released 23 October, covers 10 models. GreyNoise linked theft of configuration data from 996 GS1900 switches around 17 August to the earlier flaw, and 564 of them still used factory passwords. Acronis ties the activity to a Chinese-speaking group it calls Red Heron, reported to deploy a backdoor and rootkit.
