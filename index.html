Medium Research — Forescout Vedere Labs analysis of 47,700 network segments across 209 organizations; only 13% of OT-containing segments are dedicated exclusively to OT; sector-aggregated statistics, not a specific incident; no CVE

Forescout: only 13% of OT network segments are actually OT-only

Forescout’s Vedere Labs examined 47,700 network segments holding more than 2.5 million devices across 209 organizations, publishing findings on 22 September. Just 13% of segments containing OT devices were dedicated exclusively to OT, and only 6% of medical-device segments were isolated solely for that equipment; 62% of segments overall mixed device categories, with the average segment holding 54 devices across four categories. The researchers conclude that OT and medical devices routinely share network space with general enterprise assets, widening the blast radius available to an attacker who lands anywhere on a converged network — a structural exposure rather than a single active campaign.

Sector-wide segmentation analysis, not an active campaign — Forescout aggregates telemetry from its own deployed sensors across 209 organizations. NIST’s core OT security guide gets a major rewrite — comments are open until 30 November

NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Revision 4, “Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security,” on 21 September. The draft expands coverage beyond classic ICS to include building automation, water and wastewater, food and agriculture, freight rail, maritime vessels and industrial IoT/cloud convergence; restructures the document around NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, with the risk section refocused on the Govern function; and expands guidance on asset management, network monitoring and zero-trust architecture for OT. Public comments are open through 30 November 2026, and it lands the same week as the FBI/CISA fact sheet on third-party ICS integrator risk (above).
