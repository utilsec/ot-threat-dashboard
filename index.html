Refrigeration hack hits about 700 French supermarkets; France’s cyber agency now names Copeland as well as Danfoss

Attackers remotely forced refrigeration controllers at French supermarkets into defrost over the nights of 11–13 October, pushing cabinets toward 50°C, starting fires at some stores and spoiling stock. France’s finance minister put the toll at about 700 of 10,000 supermarkets, across Intermarché, Carrefour, Monoprix, Coopérative U and E.Leclerc. CERT-FR named Danfoss AK-series controllers, then added Copeland’s Dixell XWEB systems; both vendors say most affected units are older, end-of-life models. SecurityWeek reports defrost cycles were stretched from one hour to as many as eight, delaying alarms. No group, ransom demand, entry route or CVE is confirmed. The Paris prosecutor is investigating, and on 16 October CERT-FR warned that campaigns are probably still ongoing, so operators of similar controllers elsewhere should treat this as a live warning.

Because NetScaler often fronts remote access into plant networks, an unhunted appliance is a possible foothold toward OT.

The FBI and US Secret Service warned on 6 October that attackers are still scanning internet-facing FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways with credentials harvested earlier in the campaign. Researchers describe a Russian-speaking operator that may be selling access, with overlaps to INC and Lynx ransomware; that attribution is reported, not confirmed. Fortinet gateways often sit at the IT/OT boundary.

FBI takes down domains behind Flax Typhoon’s scanning and phishing tools used against energy and airport targets

On 8 October the FBI announced it had seized domains used to run two hacking tools, MicroScan and FishHub, attributed to the China-linked group tracked as Flax Typhoon (also Ethereal Panda). MicroScan is a Python scanner with more than 1,300 testing scripts, used alongside a Mirai botnet; FishHub handles spear-phishing, malware delivery and data theft. A joint FBI, CISA and NSA advisory (AA26-281A) with international partners lists targets that include a South Carolina power company, airports in Japan and Poland, and Taiwanese gas and electricity firms, though the FBI did not confirm those were breached.
