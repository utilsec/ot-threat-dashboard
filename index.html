Critical Advisory — Active threat to controllers

The S7 advisory’s real finding: attackers are running AI-written exploitation scripts dressed up as OT monitoring tools

Joint advisory AA26-231A, published 19 August by NSA, CISA, the FBI, DOE and EPA, says threat actors are running AI-generated Python scripts — packaged to look like ordinary OT monitoring software — to find and reach Siemens S7 controllers across US critical infrastructure. The tooling is built on the snap7.dll and python-snap7 libraries and speaks S7comm, giving read and write access to PLC memory, configuration data and ladder logic. Targets are located through internet-wide scanning services such as Censys and ZoomEye, then reached through exposed interfaces, outdated firmware or weak authentication. S7-200, S7-300, S7-400, S7-1200 and S7-1500 are all named — including F-series safety controllers — across critical manufacturing, energy, water and wastewater, chemical, food and agriculture, and commercial facilities. The agencies assess the activity as persistent reconnaissance and capability development positioning actors for later disruption, say the targeting is broader than Siemens, and state plainly that this is not a theoretical risk. They also flag a blind spot worth acting on: where a system integrator or MSP holds remote access, the asset owner frequently does not know the controller is internet-reachable at all. The related campaign against water-sector controllers is where the confirmed damage sits — attackers modified or deleted project-file logic, manipulated HMI and SCADA display data, and disabled shutdown and alarm functions, creating unsafe conditions with no operator notification.

Critical Advisory — Industrial edge gateway

No public exploitation of CVE-2026-58115 has been reported.

Critical Advisory — Serial-to-Ethernet gateway

Ten flaws, CVSS 9.8, no login required and no patch coming — the Ebyte gateway advisory is a replacement decision

CISA published ICSA-26-237-06 on 25 August covering ten vulnerabilities in the Ebyte NE2-D11 serial-to-Ethernet gateway, firmware FW-9167-0-11. The web management interface does not enforce authentication on administrative functions (CVE-2026-73125, CVSS 9.8), relies on client-side authentication logic an unauthenticated user can simply reproduce to mint valid requests (CVE-2026-71187, CVSS 9.8), and sends MQTT credentials and control traffic in cleartext (CVE-2026-69658, CVSS 9.8). The remainder cover cleartext web transport, unprotected session tokens, cross-site request forgery, clickjacking, missing server-side authorisation and no login rate limiting. The practical impact is complete unauthorised administrative access, configuration modification, session hijack and loss of device availability. Info Policy — US water sector

Draft US legislation would give the EPA authority to assess water utilities and require fixes

The Water Cyber Shield Act was introduced in the US Senate on 13 August, directly in response to the coordinated attacks on more than 30 Minnesota community water systems and utilities in other states. The bill would amend the Safe Drinking Water Act and the Clean Water Act to give the EPA explicit authority to conduct cybersecurity assessments at water and wastewater systems and to require corrective action where significant vulnerabilities are found. The EPA would develop tiered cybersecurity standards in consultation with CISA, NIST, states and sector stakeholders — tiered by system size and risk rather than a single flat requirement. It authorises an additional $300 million annually through the Drinking Water and Clean Water State Revolving Funds earmarked for cybersecurity improvements, and extends federal incident-reporting obligations to state- and locally-owned systems previously outside scope. Nothing here is binding: the bill is at introduction stage and may change substantially or go nowhere. The direction of travel, however, is now unambiguous.

Legislative proposal at introduction stage — not law, and no obligations apply today. Reported alongside the ongoing multi-state water-sector campaign that prompted it No CVE — proposed legislation

Info Legislation — US bulk electric system

A Senate bill would put post-quantum migration on the grid’s regulatory agenda

Senators Chris Coons and Mike Rounds introduced the bipartisan Quantum-GUARD Act — the Quantum Grid Utility Assurance and Resilient Defense Act of 2026 — on 18 August. It would direct FERC to take quantum-enabled cryptographic risk into account, stand up a Department of Energy test environment for post-quantum cryptography adoption, and commission a study of quantum-related vulnerabilities across the bulk electric power system, with explicit attention to migrating both IT and operational technology. The premise is the “harvest now, decrypt later” problem: NIST finalised its first post-quantum standards in 2024, and long-lived grid assets will still be in service when those standards matter. It is an introduced bill, not law, and the OT half is the hard half — protective relays, RTUs and control-centre links have replacement cycles measured in decades, and many cannot be re-keyed in the field at all.

Legislative proposal — introduced in the US Senate 18 Aug 2026 by Senators Coons and Rounds. Not enacted; no compliance obligation exists today No CVE — this is cryptographic lifecycle policy, not a vulnerability

Info Standards — component procurement / 62443

The NSA is putting an ISA/IEC 62443 certificate in front of the controllers it buys — and the pattern will not stay inside government

ISA announced on 20 August that ISASecure is working with the US National Security Agency on a new High Criticality Component Security Assurance (HCSA) certification scheme for commercial OT components bought for National Security Systems. HCSA is derived from the existing Component Security Assurance scheme, which tests conformance to ISA/IEC 62443-4-2 component security levels, and layers on six additional technical requirements the NSA’s Operational Technology Assurance Partnership published in its 2025 report on smart controller security. Once the scheme is accepted, an HCSA certificate becomes evidence supporting a product’s inclusion on the NSA’s NSS OT Product Compliant List — in practice, a gate a smart controller passes through before it can be installed. Nothing here obliges a commercial operator. The reason it matters anyway is precedent: a government buyer with real leverage has chosen an existing 62443 conformance scheme rather than writing its own, and vendors who build to HCSA will sell those same product lines everywhere else.

Confirmed by ISA press release, 20 Aug 2026 — scheme is in development, not yet issuing certificates, and NSA acceptance is still pending. No security incident or vulnerability is involved No CVE — a conformance-certification programme, not a product flaw
No patch applies — a procurement and specification item Action: Use this to sharpen your own component specifications rather than waiting for a certificate to exist. Pull the ISA/IEC 62443-4-2 component requirements and decide, per asset class, what security level you actually need — SL 1 for a device inside a well-segmented cell, higher where a controller sits at a remote unstaffed site reachable over a carrier network.
