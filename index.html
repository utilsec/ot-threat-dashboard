Critical Incident — Engineering data theft

Clop’s PLM extortion campaign now names Shell, GE and Philips — the stolen material is plant drawings and blueprints

Clop has listed 43 new victims tied to its exploitation of internet-exposed PTC Windchill and FlexPLM, the product lifecycle management platforms used across aerospace, defence, automotive, heavy machinery, medtech and retail. On 13 August Shell confirmed it is investigating a “potential incident” after the gang claimed 89 GB of data; on 17 August GE said it is assessing the claim and Philips confirmed a contained compromise of one enterprise server with no customer impact. The gang advertises backups, project plans, facility photographs, drawings, diagrams and blueprints. The entry point is CVE-2026-12569, an input-validation flaw in Windchill and FlexPLM exploited as a zero-day from early June for unauthenticated remote code execution and JSP web-shell deployment. On 18 August ReliaQuest published analysis of the implant itself, and it is not commodity tooling: a Java/JSP web shell written against Windchill’s own internal APIs, database schema, keystore and file-vault layout, with functions for credential access and decryption, repository enumeration, bulk file retrieval and arbitrary Java execution. It was engineered for PLM estates specifically. The detection problem is the part worth carrying into your own environment — the web shell runs as the application’s own service identity, so database telemetry can misattribute the activity as normal application behaviour, and query or file-access logs alone will not separate the theft from ordinary use. PTC began patching on 17 June; CISA added the flaw to KEV on 25 June with a three-day federal remediation deadline. No OT or control-system impact has been reported at any victim — this is confidentiality loss, not disruption. The OT relevance is what was taken: PLM holds the as-designed record of plants, machines and safety systems, and it is rarely inventoried as an OT-adjacent asset or covered by an OT threat model.

Then ask the harder question — who outside engineering can pull a P&ID, safety-instrumented-function specification or network drawing out of your PLM, and does that export leave a log? A stolen design package is reconnaissance material for whoever buys it next. Critical Incident — Confirmed OT sabotage

An attacker crossed from a wind farm to a district heating plant through a shared private APN — and stopped a steam turbine

CERT Polska’s follow-up on the December 2025 Polish energy-sector intrusion, disclosed at DEF CON on 9 August and published on 11 August, describes what it calls the first observed case of an attacker reaching an OT network through a private cellular APN. The intruder compromised a FortiGate firewall at a wind farm, used a Teltonika cellular router on that network to enter a private APN operated by the distribution system operator, and from there reached a combined heat and power plant supplying roughly 50,000 residents — an entirely unrelated organisation. A steam turbine and the process-water treatment system were shut down, interrupting cogeneration. Operator response kept it to a short outage with no loss of heat supply. CERT Polska attributes the activity to a Russia-linked actor and notes the root cause was configuration, not exploitation: devices on the shared APN could reach each other freely, a pattern it found was common across Poland.

Critical Incident — Mass exploitation

CISA put the mass-exploited vCenter flaw in KEV today — and virtualisation is where OT lives now

CISA added CVE-2026-59310 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August, formalising what responders had been reporting for a fortnight. High Incident — Electronics manufacturing

A chip-programming supplier pulled its own production and shipping systems offline to contain ransomware — and told the SEC it has no restoration date

Data I/O, the Redmond, Washington maker of the programming systems that load firmware onto automotive and consumer electronics, disclosed in an 8-K filed 21 August that it detected ransomware on 16 August. Containment meant taking systems offline, and the company states the outage reached internal and external communications, shipping, receiving, manufacturing production and other support functions. More than 65% of its business is automotive electronics programming, and it names Amazon, Apple, Google and Microsoft among its customers. As of the filing it could not offer a restoration timeline, and the scope and data impact remain under third-party investigation. No group has claimed the attack, no vector has been disclosed, and nothing published points to control-system compromise — which is precisely the pattern Dragos documented for the quarter and which is carried elsewhere on this board: production stops because the systems that schedule, route, program and document it stop. For anyone buying programmed microcontrollers, the exposure is a supply-chain one with two faces. The obvious face is delivery — a device programmer that cannot ship is a line-down risk for its customers. The less obvious face is code integrity: a firm whose function is writing firmware into parts you will install in your plant or product is a provenance dependency, and an intrusion at that firm is worth asking about explicitly rather than assuming was ruled out.

High Incident — Building automation / facility OT

Ransomware took out door access, elevators and HVAC monitoring at Manitoba’s largest hospital — and a week later they were still restoring it

Shared Health discovered ransomware on the facility-management systems at Winnipeg’s Health Sciences Centre on 10 August, and confirmed in a 17 August update that recovery was still under way across HSC and CancerCare Manitoba. The systems affected were not clinical: central monitoring of heating, ventilation and cooling equipment went dark, door access control and elevators were disrupted, and the site security office could not issue or update staff ID access cards. The plant itself kept running — HVAC equipment stayed operational and was monitored locally at the panels, some exterior entrances were closed or redirected, and Shared Health has said throughout that patient care was not affected and it has no indication patient data was involved. No group has claimed the attack and the initial access vector has not been made public. For OT readers the interest is not the sector but the asset class. Building automation, access control and elevator management are the systems most likely to sit outside both the IT programme and the OT programme — commissioned by facilities or a contractor, remotely supported by that contractor, rarely in the asset inventory, and almost never in the incident response plan. Claroty’s Team82 has been making the same point with data: it found roughly three-quarters of organisations it studied running building management systems carrying known exploited vulnerabilities, with about half of those also insecurely connected to the internet. The consequence here was manual workarounds, degraded monitoring and a hospital that had to fall back on local control and physical processes for a week — the same failure mode a plant would see if its BMS, badge readers and gates went down together during a shift change.

Critical Advisory — Remote access & VPN

The Windows service that terminates a lot of OT vendor VPNs has a pre-authentication RCE — and it is being used

CVE-2026-33824 is a double-free memory-corruption flaw in the Windows IKE Extension, the IKEv2/IPsec service behind Windows Routing and Remote Access VPN endpoints. It allows remote code execution over the network with no authentication and no user interaction — an attacker only needs to reach the IKE listener with a crafted packet. Microsoft shipped the fix in the April 2026 security updates, but exploitation is what changed the priority: Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 reported an operator hand-driving reverse-shell callbacks out of three IKE VPN endpoints, and CISA added the CVE to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August with a 21 August remediation deadline for federal agencies under BOD 26-04. The OT relevance is the placement, not the product: Windows-hosted IPsec concentrators are a common way integrators, OEMs and remote-support contracts reach plant networks, and a compromised concentrator already sits on the boundary you were relying on.

Confirmed exploitation — reverse-shell callbacks observed from three IKE VPN endpoints; no OT-specific victim named In CISA KEV — added 18 Aug 2026, federal deadline 21 Aug 2026
Patched — Microsoft April 2026 security updates and later cumulative updates Action: Find every Windows host running RRAS or the IKE Extension that terminates a VPN into or alongside an OT network — including the ones a vendor stood up years ago and nobody re-inventoried — and confirm each is on an April 2026 or later cumulative update. Where a box cannot be patched inside the week, restrict UDP 500 and 4500 at the perimeter to known peer addresses only. Then hunt: unexpected child processes spawned by the IKE service and outbound connections from the concentrator are the tells. High Advisory — Ransomware exploiting remote-access appliances

The reissued Medusa advisory puts a number on it: 500+ critical infrastructure victims, and exploits weaponised inside 24 hours

The FBI, CISA and HHS reissued #StopRansomware advisory AA25-071A on 18 August, updating the February 2025 original with investigative findings current to April 2026. The headline count rose from roughly 300 victims to more than 500 across healthcare and public health, the defence industrial base, critical manufacturing, government facilities, information technology and financial services. Two things in the update matter more than the total. High Advisory — Remote access

Citrix is telling admins to patch NetScaler now — an unauthenticated bypass on the appliance that fronts most OT remote access

Citrix published a bulletin on 19 August covering two flaws in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway, and followed it with an unusually direct instruction to upgrade immediately. The serious one is CVE-2026-19490, an authentication bypass a remote attacker can reach without credentials where the appliance runs as an AAA virtual server or as a Gateway — SSL VPN, ICA Proxy, CVPN or RDP Proxy — with exposure depending on firmware version and whether a SAML action is configured. ShadowServer currently sees more than 22,000 NetScaler ADC and roughly 1,800 Gateway instances answering from the internet. Medium Advisory — Fire & life-safety management

CISA’s newest ICS advisory covers a fire-alarm incident manager that keeps passwords and tokens in cleartext in memory

CISA published ICSA-26-232-01 on 20 August, covering CVE-2026-27875 in Johnson Controls Simplex Incident Manager at version 2.01 and earlier. Medium Policy — NERC CIP

NERC has to file the expanded internal network monitoring standard by 2 September, and it pulls EACMS and PACS into scope

SERC’s August compliance update sets out the CIP standards currently in flight, and one of them has a date close enough to plan around: NERC must submit a modified CIP-015-2 to FERC by 2 September 2026 extending internal network security monitoring to Electronic Access Control or Monitoring Systems and Physical Access Control Systems that sit outside the Electronic Security Perimeter. That is a meaningful widening. The first version of the standard concentrated monitoring inside the perimeter, and the systems being added — jump hosts, authentication and access-control infrastructure, badge and door controllers — are exactly the assets an attacker reaches first and the ones that grant onward access to everything else. The update also notes that CIP-003-9 became enforceable on 1 April 2026, that FERC approved the Virtualization Reliability Standards on 19 March 2026 — eleven modified CIP standards plus four new and eighteen modified glossary definitions — and that CIP-002-8 adopts a revised definition of “control center”. Registered entities that categorised assets under the old definitions should expect the scoping exercise to move.
