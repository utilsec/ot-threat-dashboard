Top Story

Russia-aligned hackers are manipulating exposed industrial controls across NATO countries, Bloomberg reports

Bloomberg’s 27 October investigation describes a loose network of pro-Russian hacktivist crews, chiefly CARR and Z-Pentest, tampering with exposed industrial controls across NATO countries since late 2024. Documented cases include a Danish water-pump controller set to zero pressure and then surged, bursting pipes and cutting water to about 450 homes (password “1234”); a Los Angeles-area meatpacker whose refrigeration was shut off, causing an ammonia leak; and Spanish crematorium, winery and transformer-factory incidents. Attribution of individual cases is mostly unconfirmed and CISA says these groups exaggerate, yet US charges and sanctions treat CARR as GRU-directed. The common thread is internet-exposed controllers with weak passwords, not sophisticated malware, so basic exposure and password hygiene blocks most of it.

Medium Incident — CERT-UA warns that internet-connected Dahua cameras and recorders are being compromised, apparently through the Easy4IP cloud feature and 2021 authentication-bypass flaws; actor, victims and device counts undisclosed

Ukraine’s CERT-UA warns that attackers are taking over Dahua surveillance cameras and recorders

In a warning issued late in the week before The Record reported it on 26 October, CERT-UA said attackers had compromised internet-connected Dahua cameras and network video recorders, changed their settings and created unauthorized user accounts. They appear to abuse Dahua’s Easy4IP cloud remote-access feature: serial numbers follow predictable patterns, so cloud-connected units can be found by guessing and then opened with weak or default passwords or through outdated firmware; CERT-UA flagged the 2021 authentication bypasses CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045. The agency did not say how many devices were hit, when, by whom, or which organizations own them. Cameras and recorders are common on plant perimeters, substations, pumping stations and terminals, and they often sit on the same networks as operations gear, so an exposed unit is both a surveillance risk and a possible foothold (editorial assessment).

High Incident report — Unit 42 update: ransomware crews (Qilin, Inc and Settra) exploiting the PAN-OS GlobalProtect authentication bypass CVE-2026-0257, in CISA KEV since 29 May 2026

Ransomware crews are now using the PAN-OS GlobalProtect authentication bypass to get past MFA and into networks, including utilities, energy and manufacturers

Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 updated its brief on 19–20 October to report that ransomware operators are exploiting CVE-2026-0257, an authentication bypass in the PAN-OS GlobalProtect portal and gateway, to start VPN sessions without valid credentials and bypass MFA. Unit 42 ties the activity to Spikey Scorpius (Qilin) and Tarnished Scorpius (Inc), and public reporting also names Settra. Exploitation was first seen in June, after a proof of concept appeared on 29 May; the flaw was fixed in May and added to CISA’s KEV catalog on 29 May. Targeted sectors include utilities and energy, manufacturing, and transportation and logistics. After entry the crews use remote-management tools, move over RDP and SMB, and in one case pivoted to vCenter, disabled security controls on ESXi hosts and dumped credentials from a domain controller’s virtual disk. No OT impact is reported.

High Advisory — CISA ICSA-26-300-01 (27 Oct): four EPICS Base vulnerabilities, fixed in 7.0.11; no exploitation reported

EPICS Base control-system toolkit: four flaws, one rated critical, found by an AI agent in under four hours

CISA published an advisory on 27 October for EPICS Base through 7.0.10, the open-source control-system toolkit used at scientific facilities and some industrial sites. The Register reports that Causal Security’s autonomous agent “Cipher” found and validated all four flaws in 3 hours 49 minutes with one human instruction; the worst, an integer overflow leading to a heap write and possible code execution, is rated CVSS v3.1 9.8. Exploitation needs network reach to the vulnerable services, so flat or poorly segmented control networks carry the risk. The maintainers warn that older Channel Access clients may not work with 7.0.11.

Palo Alto Networks’ advisory, raised to Critical on 27 October and updated on 28 October, reports active exploitation targeting PAN-OS management interfaces exposed to the internet on PA-Series, VM-Series and Panorama (web UI and XML API; GlobalProtect is not affected); it names no victims, and hotfixes are rolling out 21–30 October. Sophos and Unit 42 withdrew earlier reports of successful exploitation, including Unit 42’s link to a European government agency compromise, and CYFIRMA’s tie between a scanning campaign and North Korea is a single-vendor assessment. High Advisory — Siemens Software Center uncontrolled search-path (DLL side-loading) flaw CVE-2026-110936 (CVSS v3.1 6.7 / v4.0 8.4) exploited in the “Romulus” ClickFix campaign; in CISA KEV since 20 Oct 2026

Siemens confirms a Software Center flaw is being exploited to deliver malware, and CISA adds it to the KEV catalog

Siemens first published CVE-2026-110936 on 15 October and on 19 October confirmed it is being exploited. Software Center, the tool engineers use to install and update Siemens software, did not restrict where it searches for helper DLLs, so attackers paired a genuine, validly signed copy with their own malicious libraries in an installer package. Siemens ties the activity to the “Romulus” multi-platform ClickFix campaign, which tricks users into running pasted commands, and names no final malware family. It says no Siemens system, product or update channel was breached, to its knowledge. Version 4.0.9.8 fixes the flaw, and installed copies update on next start. CISA added the flaw to KEV on 20 October with a 3 November deadline and has republished Siemens’ advisory as ICSA-26-295-03. Exploitation needs local access and a user who runs the lure, so engineering workstations running Siemens tooling are the exposure.

Regulation & Policy
Info Regulation — NERC CIP-003-9 vendor remote access controls for low-impact BES systems (in effect since 1 April 2026); audit-readiness warning; no CVE

Centrii warns low-impact grid sites to prove NERC CIP-003-9 vendor remote access controls before auditors ask

In a 26 October analysis, compliance firm Centrii says CIP-003-9, in effect since 1 April 2026, now requires owners of low-impact bulk electric system assets that allow vendor remote access to have three processes: identify vendor remote access, disable it, and detect known or suspected malicious communications tied to it. The standard does not define “vendor”, and ReliabilityFirst expects auditors to read it broadly, including staff augmentation. Centrii flags records as the weak spot: evidence must be kept for three calendar years, audits of low-impact requirements can reach back six, and the proof is often scattered across email, equipment-maker support portals and contractor systems. The owner stays accountable even when operations are outsourced. Centrii recommends one named person to approve each vendor access path, one to disable it and one place for records. This is a vendor’s compliance commentary, not a regulator statement.
