High Investigation — Bloomberg review (27 Oct) of Russia-aligned hacktivists tampering with exposed industrial controls in NATO countries since 2024; most cases date from 2024–2025; the Danish waterworks attack is officially attributed

Russia-aligned hackers have been manipulating exposed industrial controls across NATO countries since 2024, Bloomberg reports

Bloomberg’s 27 October investigation describes a loose network of pro-Russian hacktivist crews, chiefly CARR and Z-Pentest, tampering with internet-exposed industrial controls across NATO countries since 2024. The review is new, but most cases date from 2024 and 2025, with Spanish incidents running into summer 2026. The best-established is the December 2024 attack on the Tureby Alkestrup waterworks in Denmark, where pressure control was reset to zero and then surged, bursting pipes (the utility’s password was reportedly “1234”). Denmark’s Defence Intelligence Service publicly attributed that attack to Z-Pentest in December 2025; Danish officials put the impact at about 50 households without water for seven hours and about 450 for one hour, while Bloomberg says 450 homes. Bloomberg also reports a Texas public water system whose settings were changed in 2024, a Los Angeles-area meatpacker whose refrigeration was shut off, spoiling meat and causing an ammonia leak, and, in Spain, a crematorium, a transformer-factory fire and two wineries whose heating and ventilation were disrupted; who confirmed each case is not always stated. US charges and US and EU sanctions target members of these groups, and US authorities treat CARR as GRU-linked. The common thread is internet-exposed controllers with weak passwords, not sophisticated malware, so removing exposure and fixing passwords blocks most of it.

Volt Typhoon has used a rotating “Silent Train” relay network since 2023 to break into management platforms, with water, energy and transport among the victims, Lumen reports

Lumen’s Black Lotus Labs reported on 28 October that Volt Typhoon has used “Silent Train”, a covert network of rapidly rotating, single-use chains of compromised small-office routers, IoT and enterprise devices, since November 2023 to reach targets and keep access to management and control-plane systems. Lumen ties the group to four confirmed zero-day campaigns and one likely one: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN (CVE-2026-20127, CVSS v3.1 10.0, Cisco), VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-16812), N-able N-central (CVE-2025-8875 and CVE-2025-8876), Versa Director (no CVE given) and, with moderate confidence, Quest KACE (no CVE). No victim is named and no OT or ICS intrusion is described. Lumen points defenders to CISA’s Volt Typhoon guidance (AA24-038A) and its advisory on China-nexus covert networks (AA26-113A). Management and orchestration platforms often hold trusted paths into plant networks, which makes them the asset to protect here (editorial assessment).

High Advisory — OpenSSH “SSHole” authentication bypass CVE-2026-123158 (CVSS 8.1) on musl-based Linux without PAM; exploited within hours; added to CISA KEV (9 Nov 2026)

OpenSSH “SSHole” lets an over-long password open password-less accounts on musl-based Linux; exploited within hours and now in KEV

OpenSSH 10.6p2 fixes CVE-2026-123158 (CVSS 8.1). On Linux builds that use the musl C library with PAM turned off, a password of 257 to 1,024 characters is accepted for accounts that have no usable password, including root on default Alpine setups. Previdian saw the first attempt less than nine hours after disclosure on 6 November and confirmed root on an Alpine honeypot; by 8 November it counted 20 attempts from 13 addresses, and watchTowr says only botnets and crypto miners have used it so far. CISA has added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. Glibc-based distributions, Dropbear and systems that check passwords through PAM are not affected. musl-based embedded builds such as Buildroot, OpenWrt and Yocto sit inside many industrial gateways, routers and appliances, but no OT vendor has yet published an advisory, so whether a given product is exposed depends on how it was built (editorial assessment).

Exploited in the wild — Confirmed vs claimed: exploitation is confirmed by Previdian honeypot data, GreyNoise, Defused and CISA’s KEV listing; observed use is botnets and miners; no OT product or victim has been confirmed In CISA KEV (added 9 Nov 2026); CVE-2026-123158
Upgrade to OpenSSH 10.6p2 or the distribution’s patched package and restart sshd; or disable password authentication Action: Ask suppliers of Linux-based gateways, routers, edge servers and appliances whether their images use musl with OpenSSH and no PAM, and get their patch dates in writing. November’s first ICS fixes: a 9.9 flaw in Axis camera management, a dozen bugs in Helmholz’s myREX24 remote-access server, and PHP2Shell reaching Weidmüller routers

Four advisories landed on 10 November. Axis rates CVE-2026-13312 in AXIS Camera Station and Camera Station Pro at 9.9: a network attacker with low privileges could compromise the server; fixes are Pro 6.17 and 5.59 SR 2, camera firmware alone does not fix it, and Axis knows of no exploitation. Helmholz fixed 12 flaws in its myREX24V2 remote-access server in version 2.21.0, including cross-site scripting (9.3) and request forgery (8.1) that could let an outsider trick an administrator’s browser into shutting the server down or restoring a backup, five command-injection flaws for high-privileged users and a static backup password.
