Critical Incident — Engineering data theft

Clop’s PLM extortion campaign now names Shell, GE and Philips — the stolen material is plant drawings and blueprints

Clop has listed 43 new victims tied to its exploitation of internet-exposed PTC Windchill and FlexPLM, the product lifecycle management platforms used across aerospace, defence, automotive, heavy machinery, medtech and retail. On 13 August Shell confirmed it is investigating a “potential incident” after the gang claimed 89 GB of data; on 17 August GE said it is assessing the claim and Philips confirmed a contained compromise of one enterprise server with no customer impact. The gang advertises backups, project plans, facility photographs, drawings, diagrams and blueprints. The entry point is CVE-2026-12569, an input-validation flaw in Windchill and FlexPLM exploited as a zero-day from early June for unauthenticated remote code execution and JSP web-shell deployment. On 18 August ReliaQuest published analysis of the implant itself, and it is not commodity tooling: a Java/JSP web shell written against Windchill’s own internal APIs, database schema, keystore and file-vault layout, with functions for credential access and decryption, repository enumeration, bulk file retrieval and arbitrary Java execution. It was engineered for PLM estates specifically. The detection problem is the part worth carrying into your own environment — the web shell runs as the application’s own service identity, so database telemetry can misattribute the activity as normal application behaviour, and query or file-access logs alone will not separate the theft from ordinary use. PTC began patching on 17 June; CISA added the flaw to KEV on 25 June with a three-day federal remediation deadline. No OT or control-system impact has been reported at any victim — this is confidentiality loss, not disruption. The OT relevance is what was taken: PLM holds the as-designed record of plants, machines and safety systems, and it is rarely inventoried as an OT-adjacent asset or covered by an OT threat model.

Confirmed active exploitation — 43 claimed victims; Shell investigating, Philips confirms contained breach, GE assessing. No OT disruption reported CVE-2026-12569 in CISA KEV since 25 Jun 2026
Patched — PTC fixes available since 17 Jun 2026 Action: Patch or take offline any internet-facing Windchill or FlexPLM instance today, then assume compromise and hunt: look for JSP files written into web-application directories since early June, unexpected outbound transfers of large archives, and new local accounts. Then ask the harder question — who outside engineering can pull a P&ID, safety-instrumented-function specification or network drawing out of your PLM, and does that export leave a log? A stolen design package is reconnaissance material for whoever buys it next. Do not close the case on database telemetry alone: the implant queries Windchill as Windchill, so “normal application behaviour” is exactly what it looks like.

Critical Incident — Confirmed OT sabotage

An attacker crossed from a wind farm to a district heating plant through a shared private APN — and stopped a steam turbine

CERT Polska’s follow-up on the December 2025 Polish energy-sector intrusion, disclosed at DEF CON on 9 August and published on 11 August, describes what it calls the first observed case of an attacker reaching an OT network through a private cellular APN. The intruder compromised a FortiGate firewall at a wind farm, used a Teltonika cellular router on that network to enter a private APN operated by the distribution system operator, and from there reached a combined heat and power plant supplying roughly 50,000 residents — an entirely unrelated organisation. A steam turbine and the process-water treatment system were shut down, interrupting cogeneration. Operator response kept it to a short outage with no loss of heat supply. CERT Polska attributes the activity to a Russia-linked actor and notes the root cause was configuration, not exploitation: devices on the shared APN could reach each other freely, a pattern it found was common across Poland.

Confirmed hands-on-OT sabotage — turbine and water treatment stopped. Historic (Dec 2025); technical detail newly published 11 Aug 2026 No single CVE — edge-firewall compromise plus flat private-APN routing

If yes, you do not have a segmentation control, you have a flat WAN shared with everyone else on that APN — including organisations you have no relationship with. Critical Incident — Mass exploitation

CISA put the mass-exploited vCenter flaw in KEV today — and virtualisation is where OT lives now

CISA added CVE-2026-59310 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 18 August, formalising what responders had been reporting for a fortnight. Broadcom published VMSA-2026-0006 on 29 July for the flaw, a directory-traversal issue in the vCenter syslog service rated CVSS 9.8 with no workaround. German incident-response firm QUIRSO observed the first victims connecting to attacker infrastructure on 3 August, five days later; by 5 August roughly 95% of an eventual 361 victim IP addresses across 47 countries had already appeared, concentrated in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran and France. The actor gains root execution on vCenter, plants a cron job establishing reverse-SSH persistence, and in at least some cases deploys ransomware derived from leaked Babuk source. No OT or industrial victim has been named. The relevance is structural: HMIs, historians, engineering workstations and SCADA servers increasingly run as guests, so root on vCenter is root on the plant’s supervisory layer.

High Incident — Ransomware campaign

Medusa has now hit 500+ critical-infrastructure organisations — and it weaponises new vulnerabilities inside a day

CISA and the FBI reissued their #StopRansomware advisory on Medusa (AA25-071A) on 18 August. The cumulative victim count passed 500 critical-infrastructure organisations as of April 2026, up from roughly 300 in the March 2025 edition — about 200 new victims in a year. Named sectors include healthcare, critical manufacturing, the defense industrial base, government facilities, information technology and financial services. The operationally important finding is tempo: the agencies assess that Medusa affiliates turn newly disclosed vulnerabilities into working intrusions within roughly 24 hours, and have been observed using exploits up to a week before public disclosure — the group buys access and exploits rather than developing them. Impact at victims is enterprise-IT encryption plus data theft for double extortion; no control-system involvement is alleged in the advisory. For OT owners the relevance is not the encryptor but the assumption underneath most patch programmes: a 30-day cycle on an internet-facing appliance is not a control against an adversary working a one-day — sometimes negative-day — window, and production stops when the enterprise systems that schedule, supply and record it stop.

Haiwell’s IoT Cloud HMI gateway lets an unauthenticated attacker run OS commands as root

CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-02, published 13 August, describes an OS command injection flaw in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway. High Advisory — OT monitoring tooling

Six flaws in Malcolm, CISA’s own OT traffic-analysis suite — the sensor that watches your whole plant network

ICSA-26-230-01, published 18 August, covers six vulnerabilities in Malcolm, the free open-source network-traffic-analysis suite CISA develops and recommends for OT visibility. The headline issue is CVE-2026-55676 at CVSS 8.8: an unrestricted file upload that accepts a PHP file and leads to arbitrary code execution. Behind it sit CVE-2026-63177 (7.1), a path traversal during archive extraction; CVE-2026-63133 and CVE-2026-19671 (6.5 each); CVE-2026-63134 (5.4), an RBAC bypass using unnormalised URI paths; and CVE-2026-19670 (5.4), an authorisation bypass via percent-encoding — plus resource-exhaustion and compression-bomb conditions that can deny service. A monitoring sensor with full network visibility is a high-value foothold, and it is rarely in anyone’s patch schedule.

No confirmed exploitation reported Not in CISA KEV

High Advisory — Geospatial platform

An unauthenticated GeoServer SQL injection was probed within hours of disclosure — the utility map server nobody patches

A researcher published an unauthenticated SQL injection in GeoServer’s jsonArrayContains filter function on 12 August with no vendor patch available. watchTowr reported exploitation attempts within hours — hundreds of probes from a small pool of addresses, so far fingerprinting rather than exploiting. Fixed versions landed on 14 August (GeoServer 3.0.1, 2.28.5, 2.27.6; GeoTools 35.1, 34.5, 33.6) under GHSA-mqjf-5f49-2fjh, reported at CVSS 9.8; no CVE had been assigned at the time of writing. It is a regression of CVE-2023-25158, patched in 2023. Against PostGIS-backed stores an attacker-supplied value is written into SQL unescaped; where GeoServer connects as a PostgreSQL superuser or a role holding pg_execute_server_program, that becomes OS command execution on the database host. Without those privileges it still reads whatever the database user can. No confirmed compromise and no OT impact have been reported. The OT relevance is where these servers sit: water and electric utilities, ports, pipelines and municipalities run GeoServer to publish network maps, asset registers, outage and leak data — frequently internet-facing for a public viewer, frequently owned by a GIS team with no security programme, and frequently pointed at a database holding a detailed picture of the physical estate. Earlier GeoServer flaws are already in CISA KEV.

Patch to a fixed release, then fix the more durable problem — the database role. GeoServer almost never needs PostgreSQL superuser or pg_execute_server_program; strip both and the same injection stops being remote code execution on your GIS database. Put a public map viewer behind a reverse proxy rather than exposing GeoServer directly, and treat the asset data behind it as sensitive infrastructure detail, because that is what it is.

A volunteer-built “Water Watch Center” launched at DEF CON to put paid monitoring into the smallest utilities — the ones the campaign is hitting

DEF CON Franklin and the National Rural Water Association launched the Water Watch Center, aimed squarely at utilities serving fewer than 10,000 people — about 91% of the roughly 50,000 US community water systems, and the segment with no security staff at all. Five firms — Defendify, Legato Security, L1 Secure, Rapid7 and Sentinel Technologies — will deliver managed detection and response directly to participating utilities, with threat intelligence shared back through the NRWA. Info Policy — Federal strategy & legislation

A new national science and technology strategy names OT/ICS resilience a priority — and a Senate bill would put grid quantum risk under FERC

The National Security Science and Technology Strategy, released on 19 August, sets out four pillars and names operational-technology and industrial-control-system resilience, alongside technology supply chains, as explicit national priorities rather than a sub-heading under IT security. A day earlier Senators Coons and Rounds introduced the Quantum-GUARD Act — the Quantum Grid Utility Assurance and Resilient Defense Act — which would direct FERC to assess quantum-related vulnerabilities under its existing reliability authority, require the Department of Energy to study quantum risk to the bulk electric system with operational technology explicitly in scope alongside IT, and establish a DOE test environment for post-quantum cryptography migration. The bill is introduced only, not enacted, and neither item creates an obligation today. Together they are a reasonable read on where federal attention — and eventually requirements and funding — is heading: OT named on its own terms, and cryptographic migration treated as a grid-reliability question rather than an IT refresh.

Strategy document and introduced legislation — no exploitation, no compliance deadline Not applicable
Not applicable — strategy and proposed legislation Action: The quantum item has a long lead time and a short procurement window, which is the awkward combination. Anything that cannot be is a replacement decision, not a patch, so it belongs in a capital plan now.
