High Investigation — Bloomberg review (27 Oct) of Russia-aligned hacktivists tampering with exposed industrial controls in NATO countries since 2024; most cases date from 2024–2025; the Danish waterworks attack is officially attributed

Russia-aligned hackers have been manipulating exposed industrial controls across NATO countries since 2024, Bloomberg reports

Bloomberg’s 27 October investigation describes a loose network of pro-Russian hacktivist crews, chiefly CARR and Z-Pentest, tampering with internet-exposed industrial controls across NATO countries since 2024. The review is new, but most cases date from 2024 and 2025, with Spanish incidents running into summer 2026. The best-established is the December 2024 attack on the Tureby Alkestrup waterworks in Denmark, where pressure control was reset to zero and then surged, bursting pipes (the utility’s password was reportedly “1234”). Denmark’s Defence Intelligence Service publicly attributed that attack to Z-Pentest in December 2025; Danish officials put the impact at about 50 households without water for seven hours and about 450 for one hour, while Bloomberg says 450 homes. Bloomberg also reports a Texas public water system whose settings were changed in 2024, a Los Angeles-area meatpacker whose refrigeration was shut off, spoiling meat and causing an ammonia leak, and, in Spain, a crematorium, a transformer-factory fire and two wineries whose heating and ventilation were disrupted; who confirmed each case is not always stated. US charges and US and EU sanctions target members of these groups, and US authorities treat CARR as GRU-linked. The common thread is internet-exposed controllers with weak passwords, not sophisticated malware, so removing exposure and fixing passwords blocks most of it.

Confirmed vs claimed: the Danish waterworks attack is officially attributed to Z-Pentest by Denmark’s Defence Intelligence Service; US and EU charges and sanctions on CARR and Z-Pentest members are official; the Texas, Los Angeles and Spanish cases are as Bloomberg reports them and are mostly unattributed; the groups’ own claims are not independently verified No CVE — weak or default passwords and exposed remote access (such as VNC) on HMIs and controllers; not in CISA KEV

Confirm operators can run the process manually if a controller is tampered with.

Info Research — ICS-Sniper, a lab-demonstrated timing attack on encrypted cloud links to water and energy control systems (University of British Columbia, 3 November 2026); no CVE

UBC researchers show a router-level attack that delays water-plant commands over encrypted cloud links without breaking encryption

University of British Columbia researchers describe “ICS-Sniper,” an attack on facilities that send monitoring and control traffic to cloud platforms over encrypted connections. An attacker who has already taken over a router on that path studies message timing and size to guess which messages carry critical commands, then drops a small share of them. On a water-treatment testbed the team delayed clean-water pumping by about 15 minutes and left a simulated valve open more than nine minutes too long. Defences are still in development. This is laboratory research, not an observed attack, and it needs a compromised router first, but it shows that encryption alone does not protect the timing of control traffic.

Research finding — Confirmed vs claimed: results come from the researchers’ own testbed; no real-world use has been reported No CVE — not applicable
No patch applies — defences still in development Action: Where plant data goes to a cloud service, harden and monitor the routers and gateways on that path, keep safety and critical control logic local rather than dependent on cloud round trips, and alarm on missing or delayed commands.

UK NCSC publishes a Core Profile that sets a minimum cyber-resilience baseline for essential services and CNI operators

On 30 October 2026 the UK National Cyber Security Centre published a Core Profile built on CAF version 4.0. It sets the minimum level of cyber security and resilience expected of organisations that provide essential services or support critical national infrastructure, framed around moderately sophisticated adversaries and common threats such as ransomware, supply chain compromise and legacy technology. Each contributing outcome is rated “Partially achieved” or “Achieved”, and most require “Achieved”; a handful, including supply chain (A4), data security (B3), design for resilience (B5.b), security culture (B6.a), alert triage (C1.d) and threat hunting (C2.a), need only “Partially achieved”.
