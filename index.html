A third Citrix NetScaler zero-day is now exploited, days after two others were patched

Citrix has confirmed a third NetScaler zero-day under attack, days after patching two others that were exploited for weeks before disclosure. Medium Research — Nozomi Networks Labs report on the “Cling” botnet, which hides command traffic in STUN-like exchanges and exploits old flaws in routers, DVRs and access points, led by CVE-2021-35394 in the Realtek Jungle SDK; no confirmed OT impact

Cling botnet hides its command traffic inside STUN and preys on unpatched edge and IoT gear

Nozomi Networks Labs reported on 1 October a botnet that disguises its command channel as STUN traffic, the protocol used to set up voice and video calls, encoding commands in STUN transaction IDs and spoofing source addresses to look like a Google STUN server. It spreads by exploiting years-old flaws in networking and IoT devices, led by the Realtek Jungle SDK bug CVE-2021-35394, plus bugs in Linksys, LB-LINK, FiberHome and several DVR products. Nozomi confirmed the infrastructure by sending its own registration messages to suspected controllers and receiving operator commands back. Observed DDoS targets included a South Korean ISP and a university computing cluster. The report shows no OT impact, but unpatched internet-facing routers and cameras at remote sites are a plausible foothold, and STUN-disguised traffic can slip past simple firewall rules.

NIST spotlights its secure remote access guide for water and wastewater OT

A 1 October NIST Cybersecurity Insights blog highlights SP 1800-45, “Cybersecurity for the Water and Wastewater Sector: Build Architecture (Operational Technology Remote Access),” which NIST finalized on 24 June 2026. The guide is not new, but its three reference architectures remain a practical yardstick: a conventional firewall and remote-access server design, a cloud-based, provider-managed option for resource-constrained utilities, and encrypted system-to-system links. The blog adds that NIST’s National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence will soon issue a call for collaborators on its OT asset management and visibility project. The architectures apply well beyond water to any site that lets vendors or staff reach controls remotely.

Existing guidance, not a new release — SP 1800-45 has been final since 24 Jun 2026; the 1 Oct NIST blog spotlights it. Confirmed vs claimed: voluntary reference architectures; no new regulatory requirement or deadline No CVE — guidance document, not a product vulnerability

CISA launches “Securing the Next 250” campaign aimed at critical infrastructure resilience

For Cybersecurity Awareness Month, CISA has launched “Securing the Next 250,” a campaign urging critical infrastructure owners and operators and individuals to harden their defenses. It frames the effort around three Rs — reduce attack surfaces, replace end-of-support devices, and recover quickly to keep operations running — alongside four basic practices: avoiding and reporting phishing, strong passwords, multifactor authentication with password managers, and keeping software updated. It is voluntary guidance rather than a new rule, but it is a useful hook for leadership conversations about replacing unsupported OT and funding recovery planning.

Confirmed publication — campaign announced by CISA and reported by Industrial Cyber. Confirmed vs claimed: voluntary guidance; no new regulatory requirement or deadline No CVE — awareness campaign, not a product vulnerability
