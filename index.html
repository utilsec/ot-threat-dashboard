Critical Enforcement — PRC intrusion infrastructure

US seizes the Chinese state-sponsored scanning and relay network built out of other people’s IoT devices

The Justice Department and FBI announced on 26 August that they had seized the domains behind QScan and QTRouter, two linked platforms operated by a PRC state-sponsored group DOJ tracks as QTFY and ties to a Nanjing technology company. QScan automatically scans for and infects internet-facing IoT devices worldwide; QTRouter then enrols those devices, alongside commercial proxy services and leased virtual private servers, into a network that hides the Chinese origin of intrusions. Critical Advisory — Active threat to controllers

The S7 advisory’s real finding: attackers are running AI-written exploitation scripts dressed up as OT monitoring tools

Joint advisory AA26-231A, published 19 August by NSA, CISA, the FBI, DOE and EPA, says threat actors are running AI-generated Python scripts — packaged to look like ordinary OT monitoring software — to find and reach Siemens S7 controllers across US critical infrastructure. The tooling is built on the snap7.dll and python-snap7 libraries and speaks S7comm, giving read and write access to PLC memory, configuration data and ladder logic. Targets are located through internet-wide scanning services such as Censys and ZoomEye, then reached through exposed interfaces, outdated firmware or weak authentication. S7-200, S7-300, S7-400, S7-1200 and S7-1500 are all named — including F-series safety controllers — across critical manufacturing, energy, water and wastewater, chemical, food and agriculture, and commercial facilities. The agencies assess the activity as persistent reconnaissance and capability development positioning actors for later disruption, say the targeting is broader than Siemens, and state plainly that this is not a theoretical risk. They also flag a blind spot worth acting on: where a system integrator or MSP holds remote access, the asset owner frequently does not know the controller is internet-reachable at all. The related campaign against water-sector controllers is where the confirmed damage sits — attackers modified or deleted project-file logic, manipulated HMI and SCADA display data, and disabled shutdown and alarm functions, creating unsafe conditions with no operator notification.

Active threat declared 19 Aug against Siemens S7 PLCs across six sectors — agencies assess persistent reconnaissance, not confirmed S7 manipulation. The underlying campaign has confirmed OT disruption at water utilities in at least 12 states: logic deleted, alarms and shutdown functions disabled, operators locked out and forced to manual operation No CVE — internet-exposed controllers and default or weak credentials, not a software flaw
No patch applies — architectural: remove internet exposure, reset credentials, broker remote access Action: This week, not this quarter, and now for Siemens estates too: inventory every controller, RTU, HMI, cellular router, satellite terminal and historian that answers from the public internet and take it off. Ask your integrator, in writing, which of your sites they can reach and how. Then add one detection you probably lack: alert on S7comm write operations from any source that is not your engineering workstation.

Critical Incident — Confirmed OT disruption

CERT Polska: an attacker crossed a shared private cellular network from a wind farm into a heating plant and stopped its turbine

After an investigation running more than three months, CERT Polska disclosed on 8 August what it assesses to be the first real-world case of an attacker reaching an OT network through a private APN. The intrusion began at a wind farm’s edge, moved onto a cellular router, and crossed a distribution system operator’s private access point name — a dedicated mobile network the DSO had provisioned with a carrier — into a controller at an entirely unrelated combined heat and power plant. The APN was configured to let arbitrary devices on it talk to one another, so what everyone involved treated as a segmentation boundary behaved as a flat shared LAN. At the CHP plant the attacker stopped a steam turbine and the water treatment system that produces process water, interrupting cogeneration at a facility supplying heat to roughly 50,000 residents. The incident itself occurred on 29 December 2025; the disclosure and the technical detail are new this month, and the design pattern it breaks is one agencies have actively recommended.

CISA ran the same attack against a water utility and a government body — one caught it, the other never noticed

Critical Advisory — Serial-to-Ethernet gateway

Ten flaws, CVSS 9.8, no login required and no patch coming — the Ebyte gateway advisory is a replacement decision

CISA published ICSA-26-237-06 on 25 August covering ten vulnerabilities in the Ebyte NE2-D11 serial-to-Ethernet gateway, firmware FW-9167-0-11. The web management interface does not enforce authentication on administrative functions (CVE-2026-73125, CVSS 9.8), relies on client-side authentication logic an unauthenticated user can simply reproduce to mint valid requests (CVE-2026-71187, CVSS 9.8), and sends MQTT credentials and control traffic in cleartext (CVE-2026-69658, CVSS 9.8). The remainder cover cleartext web transport, unprotected session tokens, cross-site request forgery, clickjacking, missing server-side authorisation and no login rate limiting. The practical impact is complete unauthorised administrative access, configuration modification, session hijack and loss of device availability. The part that changes your response: CISA records that Ebyte acknowledged the reports and said a patch was under development, then stopped responding. CISA advisory ICSA-26-225-04, published 13 August, describes a Linux kernel vulnerability affecting Hitachi Energy APM Edge versions 6.10 and earlier. Info Policy — US water sector

Draft US legislation would give the EPA authority to assess water utilities and require fixes

The Water Cyber Shield Act was introduced in the US Senate on 13 August, directly in response to the coordinated attacks on more than 30 Minnesota community water systems and utilities in other states. The bill would amend the Safe Drinking Water Act and the Clean Water Act to give the EPA explicit authority to conduct cybersecurity assessments at water and wastewater systems and to require corrective action where significant vulnerabilities are found. The EPA would develop tiered cybersecurity standards in consultation with CISA, NIST, states and sector stakeholders — tiered by system size and risk rather than a single flat requirement. It authorises an additional $300 million annually through the Drinking Water and Clean Water State Revolving Funds earmarked for cybersecurity improvements, and extends federal incident-reporting obligations to state- and locally-owned systems previously outside scope. Nothing here is binding: the bill is at introduction stage and may change substantially or go nowhere. The direction of travel, however, is now unambiguous.

Legislative proposal at introduction stage — not law, and no obligations apply today. Reported alongside the ongoing multi-state water-sector campaign that prompted it No CVE — proposed legislation

Info Policy — United Kingdom

The UK’s Cyber Security and Resilience Bill is in its final stages — and it brings a 24-hour reporting clock

The UK Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill has cleared the Commons and is in its final stages in the House of Lords as of late August. It updates the UK’s critical-national-infrastructure cyber regime chiefly by amending the Network and Information Systems Regulations 2018. Data centres become classed as essential services and data infrastructure a NIS sector; managed service providers and data-centre operators are regulated for the first time; and a new category of “designated critical suppliers” captures firms that support essential services without being essential services themselves. On the energy side, entities managing the flow of electricity to smart appliances at 300 MW or more of aggregate control are brought in as operators of essential services. The change most likely to bite OT teams is the reporting clock: an initial notification to both the regulator and the NCSC within 24 hours, followed by a full report within 72 hours. Read this alongside DESNZ’s stated intent to update energy-sector rules after the generator attack.

Bill in final parliamentary stages as of 25 Aug 2026 — not yet law, and commencement dates will follow Royal Assent. Introduced 12 Nov 2025; Commons stages completed earlier in 2026 No CVE — primary legislation amending the NIS Regulations 2018
