High Investigation — Bloomberg review (27 Oct) of Russia-aligned hacktivists tampering with exposed industrial controls in NATO countries since 2024; most cases date from 2024–2025; the Danish waterworks attack is officially attributed

Russia-aligned hackers have been manipulating exposed industrial controls across NATO countries since 2024, Bloomberg reports

Bloomberg’s 27 October investigation describes a loose network of pro-Russian hacktivist crews, chiefly CARR and Z-Pentest, tampering with internet-exposed industrial controls across NATO countries since 2024. The review is new, but most cases date from 2024 and 2025, with Spanish incidents running into summer 2026. The best-established is the December 2024 attack on the Tureby Alkestrup waterworks in Denmark, where pressure control was reset to zero and then surged, bursting pipes (the utility’s password was reportedly “1234”). Denmark’s Defence Intelligence Service publicly attributed that attack to Z-Pentest in December 2025; Danish officials put the impact at about 50 households without water for seven hours and about 450 for one hour, while Bloomberg says 450 homes. Bloomberg also reports a Texas public water system whose settings were changed in 2024, a Los Angeles-area meatpacker whose refrigeration was shut off, spoiling meat and causing an ammonia leak, and, in Spain, a crematorium, a transformer-factory fire and two wineries whose heating and ventilation were disrupted; who confirmed each case is not always stated. US charges and US and EU sanctions target members of these groups, and US authorities treat CARR as GRU-linked. The common thread is internet-exposed controllers with weak passwords, not sophisticated malware, so removing exposure and fixing passwords blocks most of it.

Confirmed vs claimed: the Danish waterworks attack is officially attributed to Z-Pentest by Denmark’s Defence Intelligence Service; US and EU charges and sanctions on CARR and Z-Pentest members are official; the Texas, Los Angeles and Spanish cases are as Bloomberg reports them and are mostly unattributed; the groups’ own claims are not independently verified No CVE — weak or default passwords and exposed remote access (such as VNC) on HMIs and controllers; not in CISA KEV
