FBI and CISA: your ICS integrator’s access to your plant is your risk too, not just theirs

The FBI and CISA published a joint fact sheet, “Considerations for Critical Infrastructure Operators Working With Third-Party ICS Integrators,” prompted by a 2025 compromise of a US industrial automation solutions company that provided system integration, engineering consulting and SCADA programming. Between March and April 2025, the agencies say, foreign intruders searched the company’s systems for terms including “customers” and “SCADA” and staged nine archives of roughly 800 files of customer SCADA information, ICS device details and schematics for exfiltration; the downstream customers named are power utilities and transportation entities. The fact sheet recommends least-privilege access for integrators, on-demand and auditable remote access, a full inventory of supplied hardware and software, security requirements in contracts, and the ability to operate manually if the integrator is compromised.

NIST’s core OT security guide gets a major rewrite — and the comment window is open until 30 November

NIST released the initial public draft of SP 800-82 Revision 4, “Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security,” on 21 September. The draft expands coverage beyond classic ICS to include building automation, water and wastewater, food and agriculture, freight rail, maritime vessels and industrial IoT and cloud convergence; restructures the document around NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, with the risk section refocused on the Govern function; and expands guidance on asset management, network monitoring and zero-trust architecture for OT. Public comments are open through 30 November 2026. It landed the same week as the FBI and CISA fact sheet on third-party ICS integrator risk (above).
